# Round O1 - flow and ownership pressure: hostile feasibility screen

**Verdict: no credible candidate currently meets the requested evidence bar.** I found one recent, close match with an annualized long-short Sharpe of 0.75 over a long full sample, below the roughly 0.8 post-cost Dow screen in decision 76. It is a working paper, the result is not isolated to post-2010 or the largest U.S. stocks, and its turnover/costs are not demonstrated for a 30-name Dow book. A second older common-owner result reports high annual excess returns but no Sharpe and ends before 2010. Neither is a credible R6 candidate as-is.

This is a literature and file-availability review only. I did not load prices, signals, returns, portfolio outputs, or holdout material, and computed no project outcomes.

## Ranked lead (not recommended for a new variant)

### 1. Institution-level buy/sell execution asymmetry and within-quarter ownership seasonality - FAIL-GATE

**Proposed mechanism.** Institutional managers may execute routine purchases more aggressively than sales, temporarily lifting prices of holdings even without net buying. A quarterly ownership/turnover state could therefore predict short-lived support and later reversal. The candidate is a close translation of Xiao Zhang's working-paper strategy, rather than a new mechanism.

**(a) Literature - partial pass for mechanism, fail for qualifying evidence.** Zhang's 2025 working paper, revised February 2026, reports that institutional buys have greater price impact than sells, high-institutional-ownership stocks earn 5.7% cumulative four-factor alpha over 18 months before reversing over five years, and a within-quarter long-short strategy earns 13.2% annualized alpha with a 0.75 Sharpe over 1980-2023. That is directly relevant, but it is not a peer-reviewed paper and the full-sample Sharpe misses the requested ~0.8 bar. [Zhang, *When Buys Move Markets More than Sells* (SSRN)](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5630970).

The basic mechanism has stronger peer-reviewed antecedents: mutual-fund inflows/outflows induce price pressure in common holdings and can make forced trading predictable ([Coval & Stafford 2007, JFE](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2006.09.007)); predictable mutual-fund flow-induced trading forecasts returns before later reversal ([Lou 2012, RFS](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs103)). However, a major measurement warning matters for any replication: Wardlaw shows the conventional outflow-pressure measure mechanically includes realized returns; after removing that component, the quarterly return decline is negligible and there is no subsequent reversal ([Wardlaw 2020, Journal of Finance](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12962)).

**(b) Largest U.S. stocks / post-2010 - FAIL.** Zhang's sample runs through 2023, but the reported 0.75 Sharpe is for the full 1980-2023 sample; the paper's abstract and cited result do not establish a separate 2010-2023 estimate for the largest stocks. A long sample that includes recent years is not evidence that the edge survives in mega-caps in those years. The proposed execution-asymmetry mechanism is plausible where managers care about reported marks, but it is not enough to infer a durable, executable Dow edge.

**(c) Mechanism power - FAIL / weak.** A monthly strategy over the project window yields at most 72 monthly observations; the underlying ownership snapshots update quarterly, about 24 reporting quarters before filing delay, serial dependence, or missing eligibility. At the reported strategy Sharpe of 0.75 (not a Sharpe of active returns versus EW30), the optimistic iid six-year signal-to-noise is only `0.75 * sqrt(6) = 1.84`, below the approximate t=2 cutoff. Treating every month as an independent observation is generous for a quarterly ownership signal. The paper supplies no post-2010 mega-cap effect size with which to justify higher power. Do not spend a variant on this lead without a genuinely post-2010 large-cap estimate and an input-only coverage/power gate.

**(d) Free data / input feasibility - FAIL for the published construct; partial for a proxy.** The workspace has staged development PIT 13F position and roster files under `r4_L4/data/`, including `13f_development_pit_positions.csv`, `13f_top50_pit_roster.csv`, and filing-coverage files. SEC 13F disclosures are free and can support lagged ownership or holdings-change proxies. They do not provide monthly mutual-fund subscriptions/redemptions, actual trade dates, or the complete CRSP/Morningstar-style fund TNA/flow history used by the literature. Position changes between quarterly filings are also not pure flows and can reflect discretionary trading or valuation. A Dow-only proxy is constructible, but not an equivalent input to Zhang's flow-based measure; no additional proprietary input is allowed by this task.

**(e) Novelty - FAIL / low.** The source mechanism and within-quarter ownership seasonality are already the substance of Zhang's paper. Applying a lagged 13F proxy to the Dow is a universe change, not a compelling new economic mechanism. R6 has also already screened ownership-linked pressure and flow variants; avoid relabeling that work as new without a material, pre-specified identification change.

### Criterion-4 feasibility estimate (best-case transfer, not a forecast)

- **Best documented Sharpe to transfer to the Dow:** 0.75 annualized gross, from Zhang's full 1980-2023 strategy. It is already below decision 76's approximately 0.8 expected post-cost Dow hurdle; the paper does not show the Dow/mega-cap/post-2010 slice. With the required 0.05% per traded dollar, turnover capped at 20% per rebalance would cost 0.01% NAV per rebalance (1 bp); over 12 monthly rebalances that is at most 0.12% NAV/year under the harness's total-traded-notional convention. Zhang's exact turnover, costs, borrow, and Dow volatility are not reported in the accessible result, so a net Sharpe cannot honestly be backed out; 0.75 is only an optimistic gross ceiling, not an expected net Sharpe.
- **Six-year p-value screen:** if one nevertheless transfers 0.75 as an optimistic proxy for the required annualized Dow excess Sharpe (the paper reports a strategy Sharpe, not EW30-relative Sharpe) and assumes 72 iid monthly returns, the normal approximation gives `z = 1.84`, one-sided raw `p ~= 0.033`. If this were the only discovery and the other eight R6 Holm slots entered as p=1, the approximate Holm-adjusted p would be `min(9p, 1) ~= 0.30`. Serial dependence, the quarterly source data, transaction costs, and the lack of an eligible Dow subsample would make the actual 10-session block-bootstrap p less favorable or simply not estimable from the published summary. This is not a bootstrap run or a project result: the paper's Sharpe alone does not encode an EW30-relative return path from which to calculate the required block-bootstrap p.
- **Criterion-4 decision:** **FAIL.** The generous gross Sharpe proxy is below 0.8, post-cost performance is unreported, the approximate family-adjusted p is far from 0.05, and no large-cap post-2010 series establishes feasibility. Do not advance this direction.

## Why I do not rank a second candidate

Anton and Polk's common-owner connected-stock strategy is the nearest ownership-network comparator: the published work documents shared-owner comovement and a cross-stock reversal strategy, while an institutional summary reports over 9% annual excess return. But its U.S. sample is 1980-2008, its summary does not give a comparable Sharpe or cost-adjusted result, and it does not satisfy the requested post-2010 screen ([Anton & Polk 2014, Journal of Finance](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12149); [IESE research summary with sample and return description](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/connect-stocks-improve-trading/)). It also overlaps the previously screened D4 common-owner network direction; Round 6 decisions mark D4-D7 dead. It is therefore a rejection comparator, not a new candidate.

Other published flow-pressure papers are useful mechanisms but do not repair the screen. Coval and Stafford's core sample is 1980-2004; Lou's work studies 1980s-2000s mutual fund flows; and Wardlaw's 2020 correction weakens a popular flow-pressure measurement. These support why flow pressure might exist, not a documented >=0.8 net Sharpe among post-2010 mega-caps.

## Recommendation

**Keep O1 empty unless new qualifying evidence appears.** The highest reported lead has Sharpe 0.75 before any demonstrated Dow implementation costs, the large-stock post-2010 effect is not reported, and available 13F inputs cannot reconstruct the published monthly fund flows. The evidence does not support an expectation of the criterion-4 Sharpe and Holm-adjusted block-bootstrap hurdle; continuing with this family would spend a variant on an unqualified near-miss rather than a credible pass candidate.
