# Synthesis — candidate price-pressure directions

**Status:** Research-only dossier; no backtests or outcome data used. The L1 postmortem file was checked on 10 Nov 2026 and is not present yet. These candidates therefore respond to the project diagnosis already in `RESEARCH_WORKFLOW_PLAN.md`—return correlation is co-movement, portfolios came before mechanism tests, and costs/breadth matter—but should be reconciled with L1's root-cause findings when that report arrives.

## Executive view

The literature does not support one universal rule that signed buying pressure persists. The sign depends on the source: noninformational liquidity demand usually reverses; informed or persistent demand can continue while it is still being deployed; ETF arbitrage causes short-horizon constituent pressure and reversal. Large-cap evidence is mixed and often smaller than small-cap results. Daily Dow OHLCV can test a rough conditioned pressure proxy, but cannot identify true buyer/seller flow, ETF/fund exposure or network links. The best substantive candidates require intraday TAQ or point-in-time holdings; do not silently substitute candle proxies for missing flow data.

## Direction 1 — retail marketable-order imbalance: a direct, signed-flow continuation test

- **Mechanism/evidence:** Marketable retail net buying predicts positive next-week returns in Boehmer et al. (2021), beyond a sizeable contribution from flow persistence and public news; the source calls the information explanation suggestive. Campbell, Grossman and Wang (1993) and Llorente et al. (2002) provide the competing mechanism: high-volume noninformational pressure can reverse, especially for liquid large stocks. The retail effect is smaller but still present in their largest-cap tercile (about 2.6 bp weekly IQR return difference, t=3.68).
- **Predicted sign/horizon:** Positive signed retail imbalance forecasts positive close-to-close returns over 1–5 trading days; older results report decay by roughly 6–8 weeks, but the 2016–21 re-examination finds much weaker significance and only short-lived overall predictability. Expect a smaller signal for Dow mega-caps than the full cross-section. Ardia, Aymard and Cenesizoglu (2024) find that the 2016–21 signal disappears for large caps and their retail long-short strategy is no longer profitable; this is a major adverse update. The result must also be decomposed into overnight and intraday returns following Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019).
- **Required data/access:** Historical TAQ trades and quotes, market-center identifiers, trade size and price improvement; CRSP/FactSet adjusted returns and point-in-time Dow roster. UNSW FactSet access should be checked for TAQ or a retail flow feed; otherwise this direction is infeasible with the stated OHLCV.
- **Test sequence:** First, preregister a single retail-imbalance construction and test Fama–MacBeth/rank prediction at 1, 5 and 20 days with Newey–West errors, controlling for current return, volume, size and market move; report overnight/intraday separately. Add a falsification using non-retail/overall imbalance and shuffled trade signs. Only if the signed prediction survives, test a weekly long-short portfolio: long top retail-flow names and short bottom names, dollar-neutral, with 0.05%/side costs, 50%+ active share, turnover/cost disclosure, and a small predefined top/bottom basket rather than an EW30 reweighting.
- **Known failure mode:** TAQ classification error and survivorship/coverage; strongest evidence is in small caps, while the effect can be only a few basis points weekly in mega-caps and may not cover costs. The 2024 re-examination found in search needs review before any preregistration.
- **Priority:** Mechanism is measurable if data exists, but current literature makes this a replication/decay check, not a favored alpha candidate. Stop rather than emulate retail flow with CLV.

## Direction 2 — forced mutual-fund flow across common holdings: a networked fire-sale / reversal test

- **Mechanism/evidence:** Coval and Stafford (2007) show fund outflows force sales concentrated in common holdings, producing pressure and subsequent liquidity-provider returns; Lou (2012) shows predictable flow-induced trading forecasts returns during deployment and reverses later. This directly replaces the correlation graph with an ownership-exposure network. ETF arbitrage in Ben-David et al. (2018) is a separate, reinforcing example of linked holdings transmitting nonfundamental pressure.
- **Predicted sign/horizon:** Holdings-weighted predicted demand (FIT) has a positive sign while the fund flow is being deployed; an extreme realized forced-sale episode has negative same-event return followed by positive reversal over the next days/weeks. For positive forced buys, signs reverse. Do not collapse these event stages into one blended signal. A 1–20-day response is a first pass; Lou motivates a longer, 12–24-month unwind horizon where monthly data exists.
- **Required data/access:** Point-in-time fund/ETF holdings, assets/flows, stock returns and total market cap, plus fund return/flow dates; FactSet or licensed mutual-fund holdings. SEC 13F is free but quarterly, delayed, incomplete for non-13F assets and unsuitable as contemporaneous daily ownership. Archived holdings must be point-in-time to avoid lookahead.
- **Test sequence:** First freeze a holdings-weighted flow exposure formula, source/neighbor definition, event threshold and window. For each fund-flow shock, measure treated Dow constituents versus low/no-exposure names and estimate cumulative abnormal response at 1, 5, 20 days (and monthly if feasible), with Newey–West inference and calendar/sector controls. Placebos: shuffle holdings within size/sector, randomize event dates, and use unrelated assets. Then, only for a signed predictive response, test a long-short/high-vs-low exposed basket or long-short event portfolio with active share >=50%, costs and turnover. Avoid selection based on which event threshold looks best.
- **Known failure mode:** 30 Dow names offer little cross-sectional breadth; holdings are quarterly and stale, and measured “forced” activity may actually be informed trading. A large gross reversal can be costly and crowded. Common ownership can capture factor exposure rather than causal pressure, so placebo networks and sector/market controls are essential.
- **Priority:** Strongest fit with the propagation thesis, but depends on genuinely usable point-in-time flow and holdings access.

## Direction 3 — ETF creations/redemptions and constituent arbitrage pressure

- **Mechanism/evidence:** Ben-David, Franzoni and Moussawi (2018) find ETF ownership increases daily volatility, and ETF mispricing/flows map into constituent returns with subsequent reversals (for S&P 500 names, next-20-day return response to ETF flow is negative after same-day pressure). Greenwood and Sammon's index evidence (working paper) cautions that classic index-demand price effects have nearly disappeared in recent years; this candidate should therefore use actual ETF flow/mispricing shocks, not generic index additions.
- **Predicted sign/horizon:** A positive ETF creation/demand shock gives same-day positive constituent pressure, followed by negative 1–20-day reversal; redemptions reverse the signs. The response should scale with ETF ownership and be strongest where arbitrage is active. This is a reversal hypothesis for nonfundamental demand, not a momentum tilt.
- **Required data/access:** Daily ETF creations/redemptions or reliable flow estimates, dated ETF holdings/weights, NAV-premium or mispricing, and Dow member returns/roster. FactSet, Bloomberg/CRSP or a licensed ETF holdings source; Yahoo prices alone cannot identify creation flows or holdings changes.
- **Test sequence:** First compute holdings-weighted signed ETF flow shocks, then event-study constituent response over 0–1, 2–5 and 6–20 days, controlling for same-day return, volume and broad market. Shuffle ETF-to-constituent links and flow dates as placebos; compare high-ownership and zero/low-ownership names. If effects survive, make a dollar-neutral long-short portfolio that fades recently pressured constituents, active share >=50%, rebalanced only after the prespecified event, with 5 bp/side costs and turnover. Do not test generic price/volume proxies as a stand-in for ETF demand.
- **Known failure mode:** ETF ownership is not itself a daily flow shock; creation/redemption data may be stale or lack intraday timing. Effect may be swamped by news, and modern price elasticity/index-effect decay means a historical mechanism can fail in 2020–26. The Dow is a narrow universe and may have little ETF exposure variation.
- **Priority:** Second network-mechanism option if ETF holdings and flow files are available at daily frequency.

## What daily Dow OHLCV can and cannot answer

- **Can:** Separate overnight (prior close to open) from intraday (open to close) returns; compute daily turnover and Amihud-style illiquidity; test a preregistered abnormal-volume/price-shock condition as a *proxy* for liquidity pressure; observe event-window return and volume response.
- **Cannot:** Observe aggressor buy/sell flow, quote depth/cancellations, bid-ask spread reliably, retail identity, ETF ownership/creation flow, or mutual-fund forced trades. CLV ( (2C−H−L)/(H−L) ) says where the close sits in the day's range, not who initiated trades. `sign(return) × volume` has the same limitation. Neither is a validated signed-order-flow measure.
- **Horizon read:** Intraday order-book pressure effects are primarily contemporaneous; daily flow effects can show next-day through next-week continuation or reversal depending on source; monthly fund-flow pressure can persist into a year and reverse over later years. Literature gives no defensible universal effect horizon.
- **Tradability:** Avramov, Chordia and Goyal (2006) is an explicit warning that short-horizon reversal profits can fall below costs, especially where the signal is largest. Boehmer's retail result and Coval–Stafford's distressed flow results are gross literature findings, not a promise of net edge in 30 mega-caps. Test mechanisms first and calculate trading costs from the actual rebalance turnover.

## Decision support

If TAQ and point-in-time holdings are available, pursue Directions 1 and 2 as the main competing tests; Direction 3 is a distinct ETF-specific pathway. If only daily OHLCV is available, do not claim to test signed order flow or network propagation. At most, preregister one limited hypothesis that separates overnight/intraday returns and tests high-volume liquidity-shock reversal, explicitly labeled as a proxy test. Bring all directions to the user gate after L1's postmortem and L4's data availability report; no portfolios should run before the predictive/event tests are frozen.
