Forward search surfaced 731 citations on the JFE page for Coval–Stafford; targeted citing-work queries found post-2007 flow-pressure applications, including a 2020 NBER working paper using Lou's FIT measure.

Wiley page and full author PDF. Opened Amihud and Kyle PDFs, Lou–Polk–Skouras PDF, and Boehmer full PDF. Retail PDF tables supply a weekly t-stat and large-cap split.
- Search batch 4 (items 12–16): 8 useful scholarly results: Cont et al. JFEconometrics / arXiv; index inclusion sources including Shleifer's Journal of Finance article, index-arbitrage evidence, and a current NBER working paper; McLean–Pontiff Wiley page/PDF; Hou–Xue–Zhang RFS source. Opened Cont publisher discovery and HXZ NBER full paper (official journal OUP PDF endpoint was inaccessible), McLean–Pontiff Wiley source, and Shleifer Wiley source discovery; McLean is verified by its opened publisher abstract, while Shleifer is verified by the official publisher DOI record (abstract search result, full-text endpoint blocked).
- Search batch 5: 8 useful scholarly results on ETF index effect decay, VPIN challenge, retail imbalance, cross-asset OFI and daily OFI. Opened the author PDF for Greenwood–Sammon and Aarhus source page for Andersen–Bondarenko. A 2024 peer-reviewed cross-order-flow paper and German daily-imbalance evidence were found; not used as primary recommendations because the data market/universe differs and the evidence is less mature.
- Required replication / post-publication searches (4 explicit queries): 4 useful scholarly results, including Andersen–Bondarenko's peer-reviewed VPIN critique, two post-publication studies using Lou flow-induced trading, and a replication-related recent anomaly-decay literature record. No dedicated published replication of Campbell et al. or Coval–Stafford was found in these searches; that absence is not evidence of replication.
- Forward snowball search (4 targeted queries): JFE page reports 731 citing papers for Coval–Stafford and provided a reference list; Wiley full source for retail activity surfaced. We followed a 2020 NBER paper that explicitly applies Lou's FIT measure. Direct Google Scholar citation-list pages were not reliably accessible in this environment, so the forward search used publisher/NBER records and targeted title/citation searches.

## Source-opening and limitations log
- Opened primary-source PDFs: Campbell–Grossman–Wang (MIT author copy), Llorente et al. (MIT author copy), Lou (LSE author copy), Amihud (JFM author copy), Kyle (author-hosted Econometrica copy), Lou–Polk–Skouras (LSE author copy), Boehmer et al. (full author PDF), Ben-David et al. (NBER author paper), and Greenwood–Sammon (HBS author PDF).
- Opened publisher/institutional records: Avramov et al. (Wiley), Boehmer et al. (Wiley), McLean–Pontiff (Wiley), Coval–Stafford (JFE ScienceDirect record), Lee–Ready (Wiley citation/source and author PDF), Andersen–Bondarenko (Aarhus record), plus official abstract for Cont et al. (OUP/HXZ journal endpoints were blocked; HXZ full NBER working paper was opened)
- Some publisher endpoints (OUP/Elsevier/Wiley) returned access or fetch errors. Where the opened artifact was an abstract/metadata record rather than a readable full article, cards state that limitation and do not manufacture missing t-statistics or cost estimates.
- A separate peer-reviewed source specifically validating close-location value (CLV) as a buyer/seller flow measure was not found. CLV and signed-volume are therefore treated below as OHLCV proxies with known ambiguity, not as order-flow measures validated by this search.
- Follow-up source check (opened after first draft): Ardia, Aymard and Cenesizoglu (2024), “Revisiting Boehmer et al. (2021),” arXiv:2403.17095. The opened paper reports that 2016–21 retail imbalance no longer predicts weekly returns for large caps and the long-short signal is unprofitable; it also documents sensitivity to trade-signing method. This adverse evidence is now included in Cards 13 and Synthesis Direction 1.
