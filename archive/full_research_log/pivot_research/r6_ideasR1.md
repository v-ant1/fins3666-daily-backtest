# R1 — Own-stock predicted dividend-month buying pressure

**Status: design only; no return, signal, or portfolio outcomes computed.** This is a new idea for the R family, not an extension of a tested portfolio. It is a weak criterion-4 candidate, not a claim that the Dow implementation inherits the literature Sharpe.

## Mechanism and literature evidence

Hartzmark and Solomon (2013) find positive abnormal returns in months when a company is predicted to pay a dividend, both versus other companies and versus the same dividend-paying companies in their non-dividend months. Their monthly CRSP sample runs January 1927–December 2011, so only its final two years are post-2010 and it does not establish that the effect survives in a modern post-2011 stock-level test. Their prediction uses earlier dividend timing (quarterly lags of 3, 6, 9, and 12 months, and analogous semiannual/annual lags). The paper reports 41 bp monthly abnormal returns for a portfolio of predicted payers, 53 bp monthly four-factor alpha for the between-company long-short portfolio, a 0.421 annual Sharpe for the long-only dividend-month portfolio, and a 0.195 Sharpe for the within-company portfolio. Results hold value-weighted as well as equal-weighted and are not confined to small or illiquid firms. The proposed mechanism is dividend-seeking/reinvestment demand creating temporary buying pressure before the ex-date, followed by reversal; this motivates a one-month exposure, not a permanent dividend-yield tilt. [Hartzmark & Solomon (2013), *The dividend month premium*, JFE, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.02.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.02.015).

The post-2010 evidence is supportive but incomplete for this exact trade. Hartzmark and Solomon's later market-wide paper uses CRSP daily data from 1926–2018; it finds that one standard-deviation higher daily dividend payments predict 3.2 bp higher value-weighted market returns, and the relationship is stronger when reinvestment is more likely. It establishes a mechanism in very liquid broad-market assets and includes the post-2010 years through 2018, but it does not report a standalone post-2010 Sharpe for an own-stock dividend-month strategy. It is also a market-level payment-date design, not this cross-sectional monthly Dow signal. [Hartzmark & Solomon (2022), *Predictable Price Pressure*, NBER Working Paper 30688](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30688).

## Proposed preregistered design

At each month-end, use dividend event dates observed no earlier than 2020-06-01 and strictly before the formation date. Mark a Dow stock eligible for the coming month if its prior dividend dates establish a recurring quarterly, semiannual, or annual schedule that predicts an ex-dividend event in that month. Use the same lag logic as the 2013 paper; do not use a current-month event to decide eligibility, revise the rule after seeing returns, or include special dividends. Require a full year of permitted trailing event history before assigning the schedule, so early formations without that history stay in EW30. Long-only portfolio: equal-weight the five eligible Dow names with the nearest predicted ex-dates; if fewer than five qualify, fill remaining slots from EW30. If more than five qualify, retain incumbents when event dates are similarly near. The sole forecast is the expected dividend month; there is no price or volume component.

Prediction is public, mechanical calendar information, not a forward outcome. This proposal intentionally does not load pre-2020-06 Dow dividend, price, or return history. It uses a 12-month trailing window entirely within the allowed period, sacrificing early sample months to preserve that boundary. Dividend-event dates are not payments or returns, and the event-date audit below read only event dates; it did not use Yahoo's price-bar fields.

## Gates (a)–(e)

**(a) Mechanism and evidence gate.** Keep only if the pre-ex-date buying-pressure pattern is economically present in the allowed development sample and is directionally stronger for predicted dividend months than matched non-event months. The design must also show the predicted timing was knowable by the month-end formation date. A post-ex-date reversal is a mechanism check, not an extra trade. The published 0.421 Sharpe and 41 bp monthly abnormal return are full-sample literature facts, not projected Dow excess returns. The later 2022 working paper supports broad-market flow pressure but cannot validate this cross-sectional trade on its own.

**(b) Power gate.** The question is monthly cross-sectional rank IC of the ex-ante schedule indicator versus next-month stock returns on the S&P 500 development panel, with SIC2-clustered inference. SIC2 mapping in the staged PIT panel has 58 groups across 622 roster tickers (about 8.6 names per group at a 500-name cross-section). For planning, use an ICC of 0.10 as an explicit assumption, producing design effect 1 + (8.6 − 1)×0.10 = 1.76 and null IC SD ≈ sqrt(1.76/499) = 0.0594. `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with 500 assets, 72 monthly observations, one-month horizon, rho=0, planted IC=0.020, 5,000 simulations and seed 20261111 gives detection probability 0.8156 (MC SE 0.0055) and 80% MDE 0.0199. This is a planning sensitivity, not estimated power for the paper's 41 bp portfolio alpha: the paper reports no rank IC, and converting its alpha into IC would require return dispersion and a matched benchmark not provided in that study. Treat 0.02 IC as an aspirational gate, not a literature-calibrated effect. The simulation uses a single effective IC SD to approximate SIC2 clustering; confirm with actual SIC2 block resampling if the design reaches an authorized test.

**(c) Predictive and placebo gate.** Use only formation-time eligible names and next-month returns, with the predeclared SIC2 cluster inference. Require positive IC and positive mean return spread; compare against (i) the same firms in their non-predicted months and (ii) month-shifted schedules as placebo. Report raw and Holm-adjusted results, and report nulls. No portfolio should be considered if the schedule signal does not clear this mechanism/predictive gate. Do not treat the market-level 3.2 bp result as evidence that the individual-stock gate passes.

**(d) Criterion-4, turnover, and costs.** Criterion 4 remains the binding test: net annualized Dow excess Sharpe versus EW30 ≥0.8 over roughly six years, with one-sided bootstrap p<0.05 after the required Holm correction. A rough feasibility estimate for this design is expected net Dow excess Sharpe **0.10** (planning range 0–0.25), giving a one-sided six-year bootstrap p proxy ≈0.40 at the central estimate (Sharpe×sqrt(6) normal approximation; not a resample of project returns); even the literature's 0.421 long-only Sharpe, if optimistically treated as an excess Sharpe, implies p≈0.15 under the same proxy. The 0.421 comparison is before translating the effect from all-stock or within-stock portfolios to five Dow names and before costs; the 0.10 estimate is already a judgmental post-cost haircut. The cited Sharpe is not against EW30, so the 0.421 comparison is deliberately generous. At Sharpe 0.8 the same proxy p is about 0.025 before Holm; the estimated central effect is far short. **Estimated probability of clearing criterion 4: 5%; after Holm, about 1%.** The 0.10 central estimate is a large haircut to 0.421 because it is benchmark-relative to EW30, concentrated in five Dow names, subject to the 20% turnover cap, and has no clean post-2011 own-stock validation. These are subjective feasibility probabilities, not measured outcomes; the key uncertainty is whether the strong historical effect survived and transfers to the modern Dow.

Cap total traded notional at 20% of NAV per monthly rebalance by staging changes: replacing a 20%-weight slot fully would count as 40% turnover (sell plus buy), so no more than half a slot is rotated in a month. This can dilute event timing and is included in the low expected Sharpe. For a transparent transaction-cost hurdle, `break_even_rank_ic(turnover=.20, return_dispersion=.08, cost_per_side=.0005, top_fraction=1/6)` returns IC 0.000866 (0.087 bp in rank correlation); at 1 bp/side it returns 0.000173. These are cost-only thresholds under the helper's linear mapping, assuming 8% monthly cross-sectional return dispersion; they are not forecasts of alpha. Gross predictive IC must exceed the cost threshold and the strategy must still pass the net Sharpe gate. Low turnover makes costs manageable by design but cannot solve the benchmark-relative effect-size gap.

**(e) Novelty and data feasibility.** This is a single-stock dividend-calendar demand signal, distinct from all pressure, peer-network, earnings, overnight, passive-ownership, pairs, ETF-flow, and short-interest ideas in R5/R6. Its pressure source is predictable cash distributions and dividend-seeking/reinvestment; the signal is the issuer's own recurring payment schedule; the holding window is the predicted ex-dividend month. It is not a dividend-yield or quality factor. Only free dividend event dates plus the already-staged Dow/S&P development data are needed. No holdout data are needed or permitted.
