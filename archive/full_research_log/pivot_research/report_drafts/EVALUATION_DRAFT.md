## What was tested

The original pressure-plus-heat design asked whether a five-day blend of close-location value, signed volume, and intraday-minus-overnight return predicts continuation, and whether diffusing that score across a 63-session return-correlation graph improves it. On the development sample, the five-day **close-to-close** pressure rank IC was 0.0034 (Newey–West t=0.34); heat’s five-day close-to-close IC was −0.0015 (t=−0.12). Some individual components showed short-lived one-day continuation, but that did not carry through the fixed blend and five-day test. The graph was constructed from co-movement and smoothed peer pressure; it did not demonstrate directional pressure transmission. The unbuffered top-five heat portfolio returned +133.2% gross versus +102.6% for EW30, but after the registered 0.05%-per-side cost it returned +83.3% versus EW30’s +101.7%; net Sharpe was 0.64 versus 0.92. Average total traded notional was 161.2% of NAV per rebalance. These values are reported in `r4_L1/POSTMORTEM.md` (“Predictive power by horizon” and portfolio results); `report_drafts/TESTING_METHOD.md` summarizes the same portfolio’s gross active return (+30.5 percentage points) and after-cost shortfall (18.4 points). The close-to-close IC values are distinct from the next-open-to-close values in that testing-method summary. This is an example of a gross edge failing as a tradable result.

D5 tested a more specific mechanism: whether five-session CLV pressure at the highest-dollar-volume industry leader predicts same-direction next-week returns among its SIC followers. Its input-only gate passed on all 293 eligible fifth-session dates, with 11 directed pairs per date. The primary mean rank IC was 0.007455 (NW t=0.5644, one-sided raw p=0.286249), below both the 0.0183 mean-IC hurdle and t≥2. The randomized within-SIC leader comparison also failed: the observed IC was at the 69.4th percentile of the 500-draw null, below the required 95th percentile (randomization p=0.307385). The observed linked signal did not exceed the registered randomized-link threshold; this is not proof that its effect is exactly zero. Reverse-edge and fixed 20-session-shift controls met their registered |t|<2 checks, but non-significance does not establish that their effects are exactly zero. The 60-session sensitivity cannot replace variant 1. The independent reproduction matched D5’s mean IC and NW statistic; it found 1,463 daily IC observations, in addition to the 293 formation dates used for the input gate. D5 therefore failed before portfolio testing; returns, costs, Sharpe, turnover, DSR, and holdout are not run, not zero.

D4 tested whether five-session peer CLV on a dated common-owner network predicts next-week continuation among Dow stocks. The primary mean rank IC was −0.018074 across 1,479 dates (NW t=−2.4195, lag 4); the registered positive-direction raw p-value was 0.992229. This p-value tests the preregistered positive effect, so it is near one when the estimate is negative; the negative t-statistic points in the opposite direction. In this sample, stocks with stronger common-owner peer pressure tended to have lower subsequent returns in relative rank, a short-term reversal opposite to the prediction. It is reported as a failed mechanism, not as a trading signal. The degree-preserving, random-link, fixed-shift, and placebo-equivalence gates also failed. An independent calculation from the saved development bundle matched the primary mean IC and t-statistic exactly. D4 stopped before portfolio or holdout testing.

D6 tested whether the monthly 12-minus-1 Treasury-basket trend predicts SPY returns after controlling for SPY’s own trend. In the 62 monthly targets, the primary coefficient was 0.076921 (NW t=0.7663, raw one-sided p=0.221736), failing the registered positive-sign/statistical-strength gate. Its circular-shift placebo comparison was also short of the required 95th percentile: the observed coefficient was at the 94.6th percentile, with randomization p=0.055888. A six-minus-one sensitivity had a larger coefficient and t=2.623, but it is not a registered rescue for the failed 12-minus-1 primary. The accidental L1 run and official L2 run produced identical primary statistics, independently reproduced in the audit. D6’s portfolio endpoint remains not run, and no mechanism failure is upgraded by the sensitivity.

## Inference, costs, and limitations

The interim Holm table in `report_drafts/HOLM_INTERIM.md` applies the logged raw p-values across all 21 D4–D6 endpoints, counting the duplicate D6 run once and assigning p=1 to each unrun portfolio endpoint. No endpoint has a Holm-adjusted p-value below 0.05; the smallest is 0.0915 for the D6 six-minus-one sensitivity. Any final deflated Sharpe calculation must use the full trial count of 30; D4–D6 have no eligible portfolio Sharpe and therefore no DSR result. Their portfolios were gated off after mechanism failure, so net excess return, risk, turnover, and trading costs for these directions are unavailable rather than estimated losses.

A null or gate failure here does not prove that all pressure transmission is absent, nor that an effect of any size is impossible. It means the specified signal, network, horizon, and development sample did not produce evidence strong enough to meet the pre-registered advancement rule. This is more informative than changing definitions after seeing results: the signs, horizons, primary thresholds, placebo comparisons, and rule that sensitivities cannot rescue the primary were fixed before outcomes. Independent reproductions support the reported D4, D5 and D6 primary statistics, and both official/accidental D6 runs agree. Still, the sample has limited breadth, and non-significant estimates may reflect a weak effect, limited power, or both. The deterministic D5 placebo checks are diagnostics for their stated thresholds, not proof of no effect; the shuffled-link result is the more direct evidence that D5 did not outperform its registered network control.

**Disclosed data and process limitations:** the SIC panel has filing dates but no EDGAR acceptance timestamps, so the frozen prior-session cutoff was implemented as usable on the first NYSE session strictly after the filing date. This later cutoff was recorded as conservative against look-ahead. DD and pre-2019 DOW SIC lineage corrections remain holdout-only under the recorded decision. D4's missing AAPL and TRV position mappings were fixed and inputs rebuilt before the single mechanism run; a runner bug was corrected before outcomes, and a later formatting issue was finalized from saved results without rerunning. D4 and D6 freeze hashes were corrected to content hashes before their outcome runs, with frozen specification text and dates unchanged. D6 was also run accidentally by L1 after freezing; the artifacts were preserved and the official guarded run matched it exactly. The D6 duplicate log entries count once in the Holm family. No worker-side holdout result is reported. D7 remains pending and not ready for freeze until its gate chronology and endpoint manifest are resolved. The index-change proposal was assessed for input feasibility and ruled infeasible before outcomes; it was not an outcome test.

### Plain-English terms

**CLV** means close-location value: whether the close is near the day’s high or low. **IC/rank IC** is the date-by-date correlation between stocks’ signal ranks and their later-return ranks. **Common-owner network** links companies held by the same disclosed institutional managers. **Newey–West t-statistic** is a test statistic using standard errors adjusted for dependence across nearby dates. **SIC** is the industry code used to group companies. A **placebo** deliberately breaks the proposed link or timing to check whether similar results appear without the proposed mechanism. **Holm-adjusted p-value** accounts for testing a family of hypotheses. **DSR** means deflated Sharpe ratio, which adjusts a reported Sharpe for trying multiple strategies. **EW30** is the equal-weight return of the 30 Dow names. **Turnover** here is traded notional as a share of portfolio value.
