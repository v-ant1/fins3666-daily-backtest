# S2 strategy search — round 2 (L-S2B)

**Disposition: no candidate qualifies for a preregistration.** The S&P holdout now has about 1,215 sessions (2016-01-04–2020-10-30), which is substantially shorter than the earlier six-year S&P planning window. Re-running the R7 synthetic power method raises the only active-share-eligible weekly capped rank-weighted design to about **0.031 mean rank IC** for 80% power, and the comparable monthly design to about **0.069** at signal persistence ρ=0.8. The event-study screen also rejects the strongest new forced-flow lead: index additions have a credible, clear mechanism, but their post-2010 mean is below a realistic 80%-power event-CAR hurdle.

No signals, strategy returns, price/return values, or holdout files were accessed. Power calculations below use only the published R7 synthetic planning assumptions, the new holdout length supplied by the supervisor, literature magnitudes, and event-count metadata from cited publications. The stipulated holdout is sealed S&P 500 constituent OHLCV and point-in-time membership, 2016-01-04–2020-10-30. J2 and S2 must both be frozen before either opens it, and they share one Holm family.

## 1. Power gate rerun at T ≈ 1,215 sessions

I applied the R7 power-gate assumptions and scaled its effective observations to the new period: about **243 weekly** formations and **58 monthly** formations. To keep this an 80%-power, family-aware requirement, I used a conservative first Holm step with **26 tests** (the 24 existing R6/R7 endpoint slots plus one J2 and one S2 primary endpoint), one-sided α=0.05/26, and 80% power. Exact family membership/count must be fixed before any holdout access; additional preregistered endpoints can only make the first-step hurdle more stringent. R7's persistence-dependent effective-N ratios are held fixed. These are synthetic planning thresholds, not fitted effects.

Each cell below is the required mean rank IC at 80% power. Cap50 turnover remains 50% by construction; the separate 50% active-share requirement is shown in the eligibility column.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Top decile | .044 / .041 / .037 | 51.1% / 58.8% / 75.0% | .066 / .074 / .093 | 51.1% / 58.8% / 75.0% |

Only the high-persistence (ρ=.8) rank-weighted top-half portfolio clears active share in both frequencies. Its 80%-power requirement is about **.031 weekly** or **.069 monthly**. The weekly threshold is a little above the old .028 result; monthly remains much more demanding. At ρ=.2/.5, all rank-weighted top-half portfolios fail active share even before effect-size comparison. The other shapes either require larger effects or fail active share at lower persistence.

### Event-study hurdle

For a one-sided event-CAR test, the conservative 80%-power MDE is `(z[1−.05/26] + z[.80]) × SD(event CAR) / sqrt(N_eff)`, where the multiplier is 3.732. Event SDs below are stated planning assumptions, not estimated from holdout returns. No overlap/dependence correction is credited, so a clustered event calendar would raise the hurdle.

| Event design | S&P event count / year and approximate N over 4.8 years | Assumed event-CAR SD | 80%-power abnormal return needed per event | Credible published effect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| S&P 500 additions (positive demand) | About 20/year; N≈96, using the broad 2011–2021 S&P change rate | 5% | **1.90%** | +0.8% average in 2010–2020; below hurdle. Even at a 2% SD (optimistic for a multi-session announcement-to-effective CAR), hurdle is .76%, so only this highly optimistic noise assumption puts the effect near the boundary. |
| S&P 500 deletions (negative demand) | About 20/year; N≈96 | 5% | **1.90%** | −0.6% average in 2010–2020; below hurdle. Post-effective reversal magnitude is not established by this source. |
| Turn-of-month, four-session window | 12/year; N≈58 | 2.4% four-day CAR SD | **1.18%** | Approximately +0.10% per trading day in the narrow month-end window across markets in a 2026 study (roughly 0.4% over four sessions); below hurdle and not stock-specific. |
| Quarter-end rebalancing window | 4/year; N≈19 | 2.4% four-day CAR SD | **2.06%** | No post-2010 S&P stock-level forced-flow CAR of this size identified; event count is too small for this design absent an effect above 2%. |
| Secondary offering announcement | Planning range 5–15 issuer events/year (N≈24–72); must be counted from filings before any registration | 5% three-day CAR SD | **2.33%–3.81%** | A −2.5% three-day announcement CAR is reported for 1997–2006, not post-2010; it is an information/issuance event and does not establish a reversible forced-selling effect in current S&P names. |
| S&P deletions followed by reversal | Same deletion events as above; no independent event stream | 5% | **1.90%** for a 96-event sample | The post-2010 deletion announcement-to-effective effect is only −0.6%; a post-effective reversal estimate is not matched/available, so no credible expected reversal clears the hurdle. |

S&P's 2011–2021 sample records 226 additions and 222 deletions, approximately 20 per year each; this generous count is used above. If a narrower definition or same-date clustering reduces effective N, the required return increases. On that favorable count, event effects still need roughly 1.9% per event under the stated multi-session noise assumption.

## 3. Decision

**No survivors; no S2 preregistration drafted.** Index additions are the clearest forced-buying mechanism and have the best holdout inputs, but post-2010 effect size (+0.8%) falls below the realistic event-study MDE (about 1.9% per event). The other mechanisms either miss the capped weekly/monthly IC hurdle, have only older or mismatched evidence, lack an allowed direct input, or do not identify stock-level forced buying. The S&P holdout's existence does not cure these power/effect-size failures.

The next admissible search would need a substantially larger published post-2010 S&P effect or a much lower, independently justified event-level variance and a specific event count. Any future candidate intended to share this holdout must be frozen with J2 before either is opened; both tests belong to the same Holm family and all prior trials remain in DSR accounting. No holdout access, trading, or signal/outcome computation is authorized here.
