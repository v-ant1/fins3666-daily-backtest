# K1 — Own-firm earnings-announcement return drift

**Status: FROZEN — runner build authorized; outcome run coordinator-only.** This preregistration specifies one earnings-event pressure signal. It uses SEC Item 2.02 acceptance timestamps as a public-clock proxy, point-in-time membership, and development prices only; it does not use any sealed holdout data.

## Hypothesis and fixed design

**Mechanism.** A large positive market-adjusted return during a firm's first complete regular session after an earnings release may capture buying pressure that persists after investors process the report. The signal is the firm's abnormal return over that announcement window; the predicted sign is positive for its subsequent approximately 21-session abnormal return. This is an own-firm event-pressure test, not an industry peer-transfer test.

**Primary sign and horizon.** Positive: higher announcement-window abnormal return predicts higher subsequent 21-session abnormal return. For the S&P 500 mechanism, form one quarterly cross-sectional Spearman IC per calendar quarter from distinct eligible issuer events in that quarter; the test series has up to 24 quarterly observations. Use a one-sided Newey–West mean test with Bartlett weights and **lag 4 quarterly observations**. H16 passes only if the mean IC is positive and the NW t-statistic is ≥2.00. The cost-only IC floor below is a labelled planning diagnostic and is not an additional mechanism or progression gate. There is one signal, sign, horizon, portfolio design, and strategy variant.

## Event signal, timing, and data boundary

The event input is `r6_data/earnings_clock_sp500.csv`, which records Form 8-K/8-K/A filings whose SEC submissions metadata lists Item 2.02, with CIK, point-in-time ticker, filing date, report date, acceptance timestamp in ET and UTC, BMO/intraday/AMC classification, accession number, membership flag, and source link. SEC acceptance time is a conservative filing-availability clock, not verified first public-release time. Deduplicate by issuer CIK and reporting quarter, retaining only the earliest qualifying Item 2.02 acceptance; amended or duplicate filings do not create additional events. Use dated SIC2 and CIK/ticker lineage from `r6_data/sp500_sic_panel.csv` and the point-in-time S&P and Dow rosters.

Use adjusted daily open and close prices and a free broad-market benchmark series (SPY adjusted close). For each announcement, map the SEC acceptance timestamp to the first complete regular trading session whose open occurs strictly after acceptance. Define the event session `e` as that session and `p(e)` as the immediately preceding trading session. Thus a BMO filing accepted before 09:30 ET uses that same day's full session; an AMC filing accepted at or after 16:00 ET uses the next trading session. Exclude filings accepted from 09:30 through before 16:00 ET because the first public time and the partial-session boundary are ambiguous. For a filing on a non-trading date, use the next trading session whose open follows the timestamp. This rule uses exchange-calendar dates and the stored ET timestamp; do not infer timing from `filing_date` alone.

The event pressure score is the stock's close-to-close return over the first complete post-acceptance session minus the contemporaneous broad-market return:

```text
event_pressure[i,e] = (adj_close[i,e] / adj_close[i,p(e)] - 1)
                     - (adj_close[SPY,e] / adj_close[SPY,p(e)] - 1)
```

The forward outcome begins at the next session's adjusted open and ends at the adjusted close on the 21st trading session after event session `e`, less SPY's return over those same endpoints:

```text
fwd_abret21[i,e] = (adj_close[i,e+21] / adj_open[i,e+1] - 1)
                  - (adj_close[SPY,e+21] / adj_open[SPY,e+1] - 1)
```

This is a close-known signal traded no earlier than the next open. The signal and outcome require the event stock's prior close, event-session close, next-session open, 21st-session close, and matching SPY endpoints. Missing inputs make that event ineligible; do not fill prices or substitute a surviving ticker. The staged earnings clock begins 2020-06-01, but the permitted Dow development price panel begins only **2020-11-27**. No signal may use a Dow price before that panel start. The first event session eligible for Dow signal construction is therefore no earlier than 2020-11-30, requiring the prior close from 2020-11-27. Use event sessions from **2020-11-30 through 2026-09-30** for the mechanism so the 21-session forward endpoint fits by the staged price end of 2026-10-30. The portfolio's first monthly formation is **2020-12-31**, the first month-end that allows a 21-session signal-age window from the permitted Dow panel; continue through **2026-09-30**. Align the S&P mechanism's event-session window to this same development boundary and report quarter-by-quarter event, price, SIC2, and roster coverage before any outcome calculation.

For the mechanism, include only point-in-time S&P 500 member events with known SIC2, the complete event and forward inputs above, and eligible BMO/AMC timing. Within each calendar quarter, keep at most one event per CIK (the earliest qualifying event in that quarter after the deduplication rule); calculate the Spearman correlation across eligible issuers between `event_pressure` and `fwd_abret21`. Report quarter-level event counts, SIC2 coverage, event timing class, and all input gaps. Do not choose quarters based on observed ICs or returns. If a quarter has fewer than two eligible issuer events or an undefined rank correlation, label that quarter unavailable based only on input eligibility and report it; do not impute it.

The existing clock covers 13,443 Item 2.02 filings across 603 filing issuers, with 7,185 BMO, 415 intraday, and 5,843 AMC classifications for 2020-06-01 through 2026-10-30. Those are filing-index counts, not verified earnings-announcement counts; 8-K/A duplicates, non-filers, 6-K foreign private issuers, and acceptance/release timing differences are limitations. The Dow event and price intersection is smaller and must be disclosed rather than projected from the S&P count.

## Mechanism, cost diagnostic, and SIC2 placebo

The primary mechanism time series is the sequence of quarterly date-wise ICs defined above. Do not pool all issuer-event rows into one independent sample. Report the mean IC, NW t-statistic, raw one-sided p-value, usable quarters, event counts, and SIC2 coverage. The mechanism gate is **positive mean IC and NW t≥2.00 only**.

For a cost-only planning diagnostic, use `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` with monthly total-traded-notional turnover of 0.50, assumed 10% monthly cross-sectional return dispersion, transaction cost 0.0005 per traded dollar, and the fixed reference top fraction 5/30:

```text
break_even_rank_ic(turnover=0.50, return_dispersion=0.10,
                   cost_per_side=0.0005, top_fraction=5/30)
    = 0.0017320508
```

The dispersion and top fraction are planning assumptions, not estimates from project outcomes. The helper's linear rank-return approximation covers explicit trading costs only. The result is diagnostic and does not add a threshold to H16 or H17; actual selection is `K=min(5,floor(n/2))` based on the execution-date roster, and actual costs are measured from realized traded notional.

For the placebo, within each quarter and SIC2, permute the `event_pressure` labels across eligible issuer-event outcomes while keeping the event outcomes, event dates, and eligible issuer set fixed. Leave groups of one event unchanged. Recalculate the quarterly IC series and its mean for exactly **500 draws** with seed **20261111**, retaining every draw and its masks. The one-sided randomization p-value is:

```text
p = (1 + count(shuffled_mean_IC >= observed_mean_IC)) / 501
```

H17 passes only if observed mean IC exceeds the 95th percentile of this positive-direction null and p<0.05. Do not alter the quarter/SIC2 groups, eligible mask, draw count, seed, or statistic after outcomes are observed. H18 is not run unless the raw H16 and H17 gates both pass.

## Honest power statement

Power is low because issuer events cluster in four earnings seasons per year and SIC2 peers share shocks; 13,443 filings do not provide 13,443 independent tests. The K idea's existing synthetic planning analysis treated the six-year period as 24 season clusters, used 54 effective S&P SIC2 groups, an IC-SD stress of 0.13736, temporal AR(1)=0.50 and NW lag 4. At the 0.0183 R6 reference IC (a benchmark, not a literature-estimated K1 effect), simulated power was **17.77%**; the 80%-power MDE was **0.11885**. This planning estimate is not based on K1 or project returns and is not an outcome calculation. It is a low-power design: a plausible smaller continuation effect can readily fail the mechanism gate. The exact monthly Dow portfolio result is not forecast by this S&P mechanism simulation.

The literature supplies evidence for post-earnings announcement drift but does not establish that this EDGAR-clocked one-session abnormal-return score predicts 21-session abnormal returns in the post-2020 Dow or clears its portfolio costs. This distinction is central: an abnormal price change is not a direct signed order-flow measure, and broad-market adjustment does not identify the source of buying pressure.

## Dow long-only portfolio, decision-63 missing prices, and inference

After the S&P mechanism and placebo raw gates pass, trade the same event-pressure score on the point-in-time Dow roster. At each month-end formation close, each Dow issuer may use its latest qualifying event score only if its event session ended within the previous 21 trading sessions and all signal inputs were available by that close. At the next month's first session open, intersect those signal-valid names with the point-in-time Dow roster effective at that open and names with an available execution open. This intersection is the eligible strategy cohort. Let `n` be the count of point-in-time Dow constituents with an available execution open at that same open; use those same names as the EW30 benchmark roster. Set `K=min(5,floor(n/2))`, select the highest `K` eligible event scores equally weighted, or all qualifying names if fewer than `K` are available; if none qualify, hold cash. Break signal ties by ticker ascending. Never use the execution session's close for membership, selection, or buy availability.

At each monthly rebalance, move pro rata from the preceding weights toward the target subject to total buys plus sells divided by NAV of at most **50%**, including the initial transition. Charge **0.05% per dollar of actual traded notional**, counting buys and sells once in turnover. Hold between monthly formations. Compare against point-in-time EW30 built on the same execution-date available-name roster. Report gross and net excess return, turnover, cap-binding months, costs, cost/gross-edge ratio, active share, Sharpe relative to EW30, drawdown, and all portfolio coverage. The portfolio must satisfy the goal's active-share threshold of at least 50%; a turnover cap does not itself demonstrate an edge.

Apply the coordinator's decision-63 rule identically to strategy and EW30. If a held name has no close on a day, carry its last valid close for marking and record zero return until a valid close resumes; then mark from the last valid close. Keep a stale holding at that carried value until the next scheduled monthly rebalance. At that rebalance, sell at its next valid open; if no later valid open/price ever occurs, liquidate at its last valid close. Mark its value at the carried close between the gap and sale/liquidation, and include any return realized at the actual sale price. Do not make an unscheduled rebalance or silently replace the holding.

For daily net portfolio excess returns versus EW30, use a circular block bootstrap with **10-session blocks, 5,000 draws, seed 20261111**. Report raw and full-R6 Holm-adjusted p-values and DSR at the cumulative R6 variant count. Criterion 4 requires positive net excess return, net Sharpe above EW30, active share ≥50%, and one-sided block-bootstrap p<0.05 after Holm correction across the full R6 family; turnover must be ≤50% per rebalance or costs must be <25% of gross edge. Here the registered 50% turnover cap is the chosen route. No alternative cost rule may rescue a failure.

## Evidence basis and limitations

- Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara, and Venkatachalam, [“Earnings Announcements are Full of Surprises”](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/santa_clara_surprises.pdf), a working paper, report continuation in earnings-announcement-return-sorted portfolios, including a reduced but positive effect in the top 1,000 CRSP firms. This supports an event-return continuation hypothesis, not the exact SEC-timestamped one-session signal.
- Chan, Jegadeesh, and Lakonishok, [“Momentum Strategies,” *The Journal of Finance* 51 (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb05222.x), find earnings-surprise-related drift after controlling for past return. Their predictor is earnings surprise, not a realized abnormal announcement return.
- Chordia, Subrahmanyam, and Tong, [“Have Capital Market Anomalies Attenuated in the Recent Era of High Liquidity and Trading Activity?” *Journal of Accounting and Economics* 58 (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2014.06.001), report broad attenuation evidence. Fink's [2021 PEAD review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbef.2020.100446) summarizes concerns that classic drift may be weak or absent in large, liquid U.S. firms. None establishes post-2010 Dow mega-cap persistence for K1.
- SEC filing-clock metadata and Item 2.02 are obtainable from the [SEC EDGAR API](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces). Acceptance time is not necessarily the first time earnings information became public; this timing error can attenuate or misdate the proposed event signal.

The event-return continuation literature motivates the positive sign, but this remains a low-probability and low-power replication. The sample, abnormal-return proxy, clock limitations, top-five concentration, and Holm-adjusted portfolio criterion all make a complete pass substantially less likely than a raw event-study association.
