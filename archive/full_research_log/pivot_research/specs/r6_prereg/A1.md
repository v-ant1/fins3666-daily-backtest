## Hypothesis and prediction

Extreme recent buying pressure measured by close-location value (CLV) times relative dollar volume is partly non-informational liquidity demand. It should be followed by a short-horizon reversal as liquidity providers absorb that demand. The score is defined so that higher values mean stronger expected reversal: a more negative recent pressure score means a stronger long candidate. The one primary prediction is **positive** cross-sectional Spearman rank IC between the reversal score at close t and each stock's sector-residualised return from the open of t+1 through the close of t+5. The primary mean IC must be at least the cost-derived threshold below and its Newey–West Bartlett t-statistic must be at least 2.00 at lag 4. This is a predictive mechanism test, not a causal claim.

## Fixed signal

For asset i and date d:

1. `CLV(i,d) = (2 × adjusted_close − adjusted_high − adjusted_low) / (adjusted_high − adjusted_low)`. Set CLV to 0 if adjusted high equals adjusted low.
2. `DV(i,d) = unadjusted_close × reported_share_volume` and `relative_DV(i,d) = DV(i,d) / median(DV(i,d−20:d−1))`. The median uses exactly the previous 20 sessions and must be positive and finite; otherwise the score is unavailable.
3. `pressure(i,d) = CLV(i,d) × relative_DV(i,d)`.
4. At close t, calculate `P5(i,t)` as the arithmetic mean of pressure over exactly t−4 through t. Calculate `mu252(i,t)` and `sd252(i,t)` from the 252 daily pressure observations t−252 through t−1, using the sample standard deviation. Require all inputs finite and `sd252 > 0`.
5. The sole primary reversal score is `score(i,t) = −(P5(i,t) − mu252(i,t)) / sd252(i,t)`. Rank the score cross-sectionally; no winsorisation, extra filters, sector balancing, or alternative lookbacks are permitted.

For each date t, construct the five-session forward return `r5(i,t) = adjusted_close(i,t+5) / adjusted_open(i,t+1) − 1`. The mechanism-test return is the stock's r5 minus the point-in-time GICS-sector equal-weight r5 for the same date; if sector has fewer than two eligible stocks, use the stock's r5 minus the point-in-time universe equal-weight r5. This residualisation and fallback are fixed. The date-wise primary IC is Spearman correlation between score(i,t) and that residual return across eligible assets. Report the IC series, mean IC, number of finite dates and pairs per date, and NW t with Bartlett lag 4. Do not replace the primary return definition if the score or result is weak.

## Pre-outcome feasibility and power gate

Before computing any future return, IC, event response or portfolio statistic, report only input coverage: valid point-in-time S&P roster dates and members, Dow roster dates and members, matched OHLCV rows, corporate-action and volume field availability, GICS coverage, and the number of names with 252-session signal history. Do not relax these gates or switch universe after viewing outcomes.

Planning power is assumption-based, not an observed result. About 1,500 development sessions yield approximately 150,000 S&P 100 stock-days (or 750,000 for S&P 500). Use 500 effective dates as a conservative planning value after serial dependence and HAC adjustment and daily rank-IC SD 0.29; the illustrative mean-IC threshold for t=2 is `2 × 0.29 / sqrt(500) = 0.0259`, approximately 0.026. Cross-sectional size improves the date-wise ranks but does not make stock-days independent. This calculation does not justify changing the registered sign, horizon, or threshold.

## Placebos

Use 500 cross-sectional score permutations per date with NumPy `default_rng(20261111)`. Independently permute the finite scores across that date's eligible stocks, preserving the observed return panel and score distribution; recalculate the mean IC for each draw. Report the complete null distribution, one-sided randomization p-value `(1 + count(null_mean_IC >= observed_mean_IC)) / 501`, and observed percentile. The observed mean IC must exceed the 95th percentile and the randomization p-value must be below 0.05.

Also calculate one fixed temporal negative control: align each date's returns with the score from exactly 20 trading sessions earlier (no wrapping at sample boundaries), then compute its mean IC and two-sided NW t with Bartlett lag 4. Report the result and require absolute NW t < 2.00. This is a placebo only; it cannot become an alternative signal or horizon. Report both placebo results whether or not the primary gate passes.

## Cost-derived IC hurdle

The portfolio is planned as a long-only top fifth of the Dow (five names of 30) versus EW30, rebalanced every fifth trading session. Use total strategy traded notional of 0.50 NAV per rebalance as both the planning turnover input and the hard turnover cap, a return-dispersion scale of 0.03007510102898867 (the documented R4 five-session reference, not an A1 estimate), per-side transaction cost 0.0005, and `top_fraction = 5/30`. The harness calculation is:

```text
r4_harness.break_even_rank_ic(
    turnover=0.50,
    return_dispersion=0.03007510102898867,
    cost_per_side=0.0005,
    top_fraction=5/30,
) = 0.0057590856
```

Thus the registered cost-only mean-rank-IC hurdle is 0.0057591. It is a transparent planning approximation, not an estimate of A1's alpha or actual turnover. Independently enforce the portfolio turnover cap and calculate actual costs from share-level trades; the cost helper does not replace realized cost accounting or the NW t-statistic gate.

## Portfolio, costs, and inference

Only after V1 passes the mechanism and both placebo gates, construct the Dow portfolio. At each fifth-session formation close, rank eligible Dow names by V1 score and set the long-only target to equal weights in the five highest scores. At the next session's open, move from current weights toward the target only as far as the largest pro-rata step that keeps total strategy traded notional (buys plus sells, divided by NAV) at or below 0.50 for that rebalance; maintain fully invested long-only weights. Hold until the next scheduled formation, with no event-driven trades. Report actual average and maximum total-notional turnover per rebalance. If the cap binds, report the resulting actual weights and active share; portfolio advancement also requires mean active share ≥50% versus point-in-time EW30.

Charge 0.05% per dollar of total traded notional, buys plus sells counted once as total notional. Use actual executed share-level trades at the registered fill prices. Compare daily-mark net returns with the equal-weight portfolio of point-in-time available Dow names. Require positive net excess return, higher net Sharpe than EW30, average turnover ≤0.50 NAV per rebalance, active share ≥50%, and gross active return at least four times realized costs. For the net excess-return endpoint, use the one-sided circular block bootstrap with 5,000 draws, 10-session blocks and seed `20261111`; require raw and full-family Holm-adjusted p < 0.05. Report gross and net excess returns, Sharpe, drawdown, active share, turnover, costs, cost/gross-edge ratio, bootstrap p-values, and DSR.

## Evidence basis and limitations

The proposal is motivated by the interpretation of short-term reversal as liquidity-provision compensation and by evidence that high-volume price changes can reverse. CLV is a price-location proxy, not signed order flow, and this test does not identify who demanded liquidity. Mega-cap competition and transaction costs may remove the effect. The following published results motivate the mechanism but do not establish an A1 edge:

- Campbell, Grossman and Wang (1993), “Trading Volume and Serial Correlation in Stock Returns,” *Quarterly Journal of Economics*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118454)
- Nagel (2012), “Evaporating Liquidity,” *Review of Financial Studies*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs066)
- Cox and Peterson (1994), “Stock Returns following Large One-Day Declines: Evidence on Short-Term Reversals and Longer-Term Performance,” *Journal of Finance*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04428.x)
