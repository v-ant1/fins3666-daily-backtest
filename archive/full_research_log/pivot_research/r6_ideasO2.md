# Round O2: cross-asset and macro pressure screen

**Decision: no credible candidate currently meets the brief.** I screened two adjacent mechanisms against decisions 72–76, focusing first on the required six-year Dow excess Sharpe of about 0.8 after costs with no more than 20% turnover per rebalance. Neither paper family documents that outcome for a post-2010, low-turnover Dow tilt, and both have material input or transfer gaps. These are literature screens only: no project returns, signals, portfolios, or sealed data were loaded or calculated.

The p-values above are feasibility estimates under an explicit null-centered transfer assumption, not simulated project results. With only about 72 monthly observations, a real effect would also need to survive sampling uncertainty, costs, Holm adjustment and the frozen comparison against EW30. The published ETF daily-return Sharpes should not be read as Dow excess Sharpe: they concern a different universe, frequency, signal construction and benchmark.

## Candidate 1 — broad ETF creation-flow timing

**(a) Evidence gate.** Xu, Yin and Zhao find that the unexpected component of daily ETF creations predicts next-day ETF returns. Their investable ETF portfolios report daily-return Sharpe ratios around 0.064–0.087, but use ETF share-outstanding histories from Bloomberg and a flow decomposition involving secondary-market imbalance and ETF mispricing. The sample begins in 2001/2002 and ends in 2016, so the result is not a post-2010-only estimate. [Paper, DOI 10.1111/fima.12396](https://doi.org/10.1111/fima.12396).

**(b) Signal and portfolio gate.** The only potentially distinct adaptation would aggregate unexpected creation pressure in broad US equity ETFs into a month-end signal for total Dow exposure, with exposure stepped toward cash when flow pressure is negative and toward the fixed Dow basket when positive. It would be a market-timing overlay, not R5 D2/Cycle-3 holdings-based flow transmission, R6 H sector-to-stock transmission, or C2 month-end cash-demand pressure. The paper’s daily signal cannot simply be slowed to monthly and retain its reported return effect; no sign or monthly decay is documented for this adaptation.

**(c) Effect/power gate.** The paper gives daily ETF strategy Sharpe, not a monthly cross-sectional IC or Dow excess Sharpe. The reported daily figures are not portable to a monthly Dow timer, and the six-year project’s criterion-4 requirement is much higher than any defensible transfer estimate. The estimate above assumes no robust transfer and assigns only 0.0–0.1 expected annual excess Sharpe. **Gate fails before mechanism testing.**

**(d) Input and turnover gate.** Daily ETF creations/shares outstanding and flow-decomposition inputs must be timestamped and available without paid data. R5’s verified coverage check found only SPY and DIA with daily share histories; that did not satisfy its fixed ETF-flow holdings-coverage gate. The source paper itself uses Bloomberg shares outstanding. The already-staged cross-asset data do not, on the evidence available for this screen, verify a broad ETF share-history panel plus the demand/mispricing components. A monthly turnover cap cannot repair this input gap. **Gate fails unless a free, broad, survivorship-aware panel is verified input-only.**

**(e) Novelty and kill gate.** The signal source is aggregate primary-market creation pressure used to time overall Dow beta. That is distinct in target and construction from the excluded R6 H sector-to-constituent transmission test and from R5 D2’s flow-through-holdings proposal, though it remains adjacent to the old ETF-flow family. Kill if the documented daily effect cannot be converted to a preregistered monthly estimand without re-optimizing, if the free data gate fails, or if anyone tries to cite the ETF strategy Sharpe as Dow excess Sharpe.

## Candidate 2 — aggregate mutual-fund allocation pressure

**(a) Evidence gate.** Chalmers, Kaul and Phillips report that aggregate US mutual-fund allocations move away from risky equity funds toward money-market funds when investors anticipate deterioration. Their eight dynamic strategies had lower raw returns than a buy-and-hold equity benchmark, but average Sharpe ratios 16% higher and systematic risk 48% lower. The underlying CRSP fund sample runs from February 1991 through March 2008, so it supplies no documented post-2010 performance estimate. [Paper, DOI 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2013.05.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2013.05.004).

**(b) Signal and portfolio gate.** A distinct implementation would use the latest publicly released aggregate equity-versus-money-market fund allocation or net-flow measure, available at the formation date, to make one monthly gross-exposure adjustment to a fixed Dow basket. The mechanism is slow capital reallocation across risky and safe fund classes, not a turn-of-month cash-flow shock (C2), a Treasury trend (D6), or sector ETF pressure transmission (H). No post-2010 timing coefficient or delayed-flow persistence is documented in the cited study.

**(c) Effect/power gate.** The cited 16% is a relative increase in Sharpe versus that paper’s own benchmark and includes risk reduction; it is not a 0.8 Dow excess Sharpe and cannot be turned into one without the baseline Sharpe, covariance, dates and signal returns. The conservative expected Dow excess Sharpe is therefore **0.0**, with null-centered six-year bootstrap p about **0.50**. **Gate fails the criterion-4 feasibility screen.**

**(d) Input and turnover gate.** The paper’s CRSP mutual-fund share-class panel is not a free input. Free quarterly Financial Accounts aggregates are not the same signal and are too coarse to establish the paper’s monthly decision rule; the staged R6 data inventory does not establish a free, real-time monthly allocation series. Before any outcome work, an input-only check would need to verify public release dates, revised-vintage handling, a consistent monthly series and at least 72 usable formations. Without those, the design is infeasible under the brief’s data rule.

**(e) Novelty and kill gate.** This would be a broad investor-allocation pressure signal controlling Dow beta, rather than the already-tested turn-of-month liquidity/cash-demand pressure in C2. It is still only a timing overlay and does not establish pressure spreading across related assets, so thesis fit is weaker than candidate 1. Kill if a free, timestamp-safe monthly series cannot be established, if post-2010 literature cannot document a return or flow effect of the needed magnitude, or if the proposed monthly tilt is merely a new label for C2.

## Conclusion

Rank 1 is the ETF creation-flow timer, then the aggregate mutual-fund allocation timer, but **neither is credible enough to use a variant or start an outcome test**. The most favorable published results do not establish ≥0.8 post-2010 Dow excess Sharpe at ≤20% turnover, and the less speculative effect transfers are near zero. Under the current data and evidence limits, say plainly that no viable cross-asset or macro candidate remains for Round O2.
