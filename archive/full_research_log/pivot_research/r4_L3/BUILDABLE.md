# Buildable network-pressure directions: evidence and data gates

**Scope.** Research synthesis only; no prices, signals, returns, or backtests were computed for this report. I checked `r4_L4\DATA_INVENTORY.md`, `r4_L4\HOLDOUT_FEASIBILITY.md`, and `r4_L1\POSTMORTEM.md`. `r4_L4\NETWORK_DATA.md` was absent when checked. L4 confirms development Dow daily OHLCV from 2020-11-27 to 2026-10-30, SEC 13F and historical filing archives, and a current (not historical point-in-time) SIC snapshot. Its holdout feasibility note says Dow lineage/date coverage is incomplete (37 of 40 ticker/lineage windows; KFT, DWDP and WBA absent, and UTX→RTX is a proxy). Availability checks are not evidence that these price rows or historical networks have been built. Consequently **none of the three directions below is cleared for the complete 30-name 2010–20 holdout using only data L4 has confirmed**. This is a data-readiness conclusion, not a negative empirical result.

## Candidate 1 — quarterly common-owner peers carrying daily OHLCV close pressure

**Mechanism and evidence.** Shared institutional owners can connect firms without a direct supplier relationship. The verified evidence includes Antón & Polk (2014), Card 3, Gao, Moulton & Ng (2017), Card 19, and flow mechanisms in Coval & Stafford (2007), Card 17, and Lou (2012), Card 18. The later JFE 2024 paper “Gradual Information Diffusion Across Commonly Owned Firms” reports monthly CIO peer predictability on a 1980–2017 sample, but the direct publisher page was inaccessible in this audit; it is not used as a verified quantitative premise. Antón–Polk and the flow papers are not proof that quarterly 13F topology predicts daily Dow pressure in 2020–26. A 13F graph should represent ownership at the last public filing date, not quarter-end: use filed positions only after their actual SEC acceptance/public filing timestamp. Graph refresh is quarterly or filing-date based; no look-ahead interpolation.

**Exact proposed data and measure.** Use SEC EDGAR 13F holdings and filing timestamps, map CUSIP/issuer to the contemporaneous Dow security, and form an undirected, weighted common-owner overlap edge from filing-date-visible institutional positions (e.g. cosine overlap of reported ownership vectors, with the precise weighting frozen before tests). Use each stock’s OHLCV close-location value as the conservative daily pressure proxy, `CLV = (2C − H − L)/(H − L)`; set it to zero if `H=L`, then normalize using only a trailing/expanding past window. Do not label this signed order flow: daily OHLCV cannot distinguish buyer-initiated from seller-initiated volume. Daily relative volume may be a control/scale, but does not create a signed trade imbalance. The proposal is related-peer lagged CLV predicting a linked stock’s future CLV/return, conditional on its own lag and market/industry controls. This keeps the pressure-spread mechanism and avoids the weak five-day blended measure. Chordia, Roll and Subrahmanyam (2002), Card 21, show why this remains only a proxy: their signed imbalance requires transactions and quotes, and their conditional reversal result does not establish that OHLCV CLV is order flow. L1’s postmortem is diagnostic only and not a fresh validation.

**Predicted sign and horizon.** Positive lagged peer CLV should predict positive next-day to next-week follower CLV or residual return, consistent with gradual price/pressure transmission. Because flow papers document slower quarterly/annual dynamics and later reversals, also report predeclared weekly-to-quarterly behavior descriptively; do not assume one horizon from the other or choose a winning horizon after seeing results.

**Placebo and test.** Freeze the public-filing graph at each decision date; compare the linked-peer lag coefficient with (a) degree-preserving shuffled edges and (b) matched same-industry peers without common owners. Run a mechanism/predictive panel with follower own lags, broad market and sector returns, and calendar effects before any portfolio. Require the real graph’s out-of-sample coefficient to exceed the shuffled-link coefficient with the same sign; use a single predeclared horizon and cluster inference by date and firm. A later costed portfolio is outside this research-only task.

**Buildability by period (L4 cross-check).** Development, 2020-11 to 2026-10: **partially buildable / high data-engineering burden**. Daily OHLCV and SEC 13F sources are confirmed; however, L4’s inventory says structured SEC 13F starts in 2013, CUSIP-to-point-in-time issuer mapping is high effort, filings disclose quarter-end holdings only after a delay, and the raw holdings/edge files were not independently checked here. Holdout, 2010-01 to 2020-10: **not fully buildable from confirmed data**. Raw SEC filings archive exists, but the first 2010–12 ownership network would need to be parsed and mapped; L4 confirms no structured 13F before 2013, and its Dow price-lineage coverage is incomplete. The task’s 2010–20 price holdout is sealed; L3 did not load or analyze it. Until L4 confirms point-in-time ownership and price availability for every required security/date, this is not a both-period-cleared candidate.

**Strongest case against / falsifier.** The 30-name subset may have too few distinct common-owner edges after excluding passive/index diversification; 13F is delayed, quarterly and omits short positions and intraperiod trades, so it cannot identify a daily flow shock. Results in historical broad universes may reflect concentrated active managers, smaller stocks, marketwide flow shocks, or costs that do not survive in Dow megacaps. The weakest evidence is the extrapolation from monthly/weekly historical stock predictability to daily close-location pressure in 30 contemporary large firms. Falsify the direction if the real, lagged common-owner graph has no stable signed incremental prediction over own/market/industry lags, or does not outperform degree-preserving shuffled edges, at the single predeclared weekly horizon.

## Candidate 2 — directed within-industry leader-to-follower pressure, with dated SIC and size/attention asymmetry

**Mechanism and evidence.** Hou (2007), existing verified Card 6, finds same-industry return lead-lag consistent with gradual information diffusion from large to small firms; Lo & MacKinlay (1990) is a related size lead-lag anchor. A directed edge should point from a prior-period leader to a follower; it should not be symmetrized or estimated from same-window return correlation. Industry should be a dated classification, and the leader/follower rank must be known at the signal time. Hou’s old-sample result is not presumed to persist in the Dow, where constituents are all very large and close substitutes are few.

**Exact proposed data and measure.** SEC 10-K filing headers can potentially provide SIC at the public filing timestamp; however, L4 currently confirms only a latest/current SIC snapshot, not a historical PIT industry panel. Build dated SIC membership from filings only after a separate L4 inventory confirmation. Within each dated industry, orient one-way from the firm with higher trailing dollar volume (OHLCV close × volume, as an observable attention/liquidity proxy) to lower-volume names; freeze the rank using prior data only. Use daily CLV as defined in Candidate 1. Dollar volume is not a direct measure of investor attention or market capitalization, so describe it only as a proxy. A version using market cap/analyst attention is infeasible unless a dated source is confirmed; do not substitute current rankings retrospectively.

**Predicted sign and horizon.** Following positive leader CLV, lower-volume same-industry followers should exhibit positive lagged CLV and/or residual returns over the next day/week if information/pressure diffuses slowly. Require directionality: reverse edges (small-to-large), unrelated-sector links, and within-industry shuffled ranks should not show the same incremental effect. No claim that these effects survive large-cap concentration or after costs is supported by the cited old-period literature.

**Placebo and test.** Pre-register industry classification, one prior-volume ranking window, and next-week response. Compare leader→follower against reversed direction, degree/rank-preserving random links within matched industry, and cross-industry placebo links. Test the peer CLV coefficient after follower own lags, broad market, sector returns and common-owner exposure; split large/small within-industry ranks only if the defined Dow set produces enough observations. Mechanism/prediction first; no portfolio in this task.

**Buildability by period (L4 cross-check).** Development, 2020-11 to 2026-10: **not yet buildable using only confirmed PIT data**. Dow OHLCV is confirmed, but L4’s SIC file is a current snapshot; historical SEC filings are a plausible free source, not yet confirmed as a complete dated network. Holdout, 2010-01 to 2020-10: **not fully buildable**. Dated SIC can in principle be reconstructed from archived SEC filings, but L4 has not confirmed the required issuer/identifier lineage or generated the PIT SIC panel, and the holdout price coverage is incomplete. As with Candidate 1, this cannot be advertised as cleared for both periods until the missing PIT network and complete price panel are resolved by L4.

**Strongest case against / falsifier.** There may be very few within-industry Dow pairs and little meaningful size/attention asymmetry among the largest firms; industry membership may be too broad, SIC may be stale or mis-map changing issuers, and same-day common news can look like transmission. Hou’s historical effect may be attenuated by faster information processing, electronic trading, post-publication decay and costs. The weakest evidence is that a monthly/return lead-lag result becomes a daily CLV pressure channel in today’s Dow. Falsify if leader CLV does not predict follower CLV/returns at the single predeclared next-week horizon conditional on follower own and common factors, or if reversed/shuffled edges perform comparably.

## Candidate 3 — asset-specific time-series pressure for cross-asset ETFs (research benchmark; not holdout-ready)

**Mechanism and evidence.** Treat unlike assets according to their own economics instead of making one stock-like correlation graph. For bonds and equities, Pitkäjärvi, Suominen & Vaittinen (2020), verified Card 20, find monthly cross-asset trend associations between country bond and equity indexes. Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), Card 13, document own time-series momentum across equity, bond, commodity and currency futures. These support monthly own-asset or specifically equity-bond trend signals, not daily ETF-flow contagion across all bonds, commodities, FX and equity ETFs. Bond yield/duration, commodity futures curve/roll, and FX carry exposures require appropriate underlying series; ETF close returns alone do not expose all these state variables.

**Exact proposed data and measure.** If L4 confirms a common point-in-time history for all instruments, use each ETF’s own total-return series for a prespecified 12-minus-1-month trend/pressure signal, volatility scale from past returns only, and monthly rebalance. For the specific equity-bond cross-asset test, orient the paper’s documented direction: bond trend positively predicts equity trend; equity trend negatively predicts bond trend. Do not extend that direction to FX or commodities without separate evidence. If futures/curve data are not confirmed, label ETF trend as a return-only proxy, not carry/flow pressure. Current `oos3\xasset` corpus existence does not establish pre-2020 history or point-in-time adjusted price/holdings availability.

**Predicted sign and horizon.** Paper-grounded direction is monthly, 1–12 month lookback/holding: bond past returns positively predict equity returns and equity past returns negatively predict bond returns. For each instrument’s own trend, the MOP evidence is 1–12 months, with longer-horizon partial reversal. No daily sign or 1–5 day claim is supported.

**Placebo and test.** Compare the specified equity-bond cross-lag against reversed asset direction, time-shuffled month labels, and within-asset own-trend-only predictions. Control each target ETF for its own lagged trend and the broad market/rate factor where available; evaluate the cross-asset increment before any allocation simulation. Do not pool commodity/FX with bonds under a single graph. A placebo preserving serial structure but breaking cross-asset alignment should remove any alleged spillover.

**Buildability by period (L4 cross-check).** Development, 2020-11 to 2026-10: **data corpus exists, but full point-in-time/total-return and asset-specific inputs for all 30 instruments are not confirmed by L4**. Holdout, 2010-01 to 2020-10: **not buildable from confirmed data**. L4 has not confirmed the full ETF history or futures/curve data; the cited cross-asset corpus begins after the full holdout start and cannot fill 2010–2020. This is therefore a research benchmark, not a direction eligible for the current two-period requirement.

**Strongest case against / falsifier.** The 2020 paper studies monthly international indexes, not daily ETF flows; its asset set is bonds/equities, not heterogeneous US ETFs. The 30 instruments may not include the needed clean equity-bond pairs and ETF returns can be driven by duration, roll yield, currency basis or tracking error. Data history, distribution adjustments and transaction costs can overwhelm the signal. The weakest evidence is transfer from country indexes/futures to the project’s ETF panel. Falsify if the specified cross-asset lag adds no stable out-of-sample signal beyond each ETF’s own trend and common risk factors, or if reversed/shuffled month links perform similarly.

## Decision and gates

1. **Mechanism priority:** Candidate 1 has the clearest relation to the thesis and the strongest directly relevant network evidence, but the measurable mechanism in these papers is fund flow/ownership, which free quarterly 13F snapshots cannot observe daily. Treat the daily OHLCV CLV peer-spread prediction as a new, deliberately weak proxy test.
2. **Candidate 2:** best directed-network design, but currently conditional on historical PIT SIC construction and dense-enough Dow industries; do not treat the current SIC snapshot as historical truth.
3. **Candidate 3:** asset-appropriate evidence is real but it fails the currently confirmed 2010–20 data requirement; retain only if L4 separately confirms the missing history and asset inputs.

No direction is **fully cleared for both development and holdout** on the L4-confirmed complete universe. To make one eligible, L4 must first resolve complete point-in-time Dow price lineage and document the relevant date-stamped 13F graph or SIC network. That availability work is a gate; it does not authorize L3 to inspect sealed holdout outcomes. Any follow-up statistical work should use the existing fixed variant budget, record the test before looking at results, and report null/placebo/cost failures.
