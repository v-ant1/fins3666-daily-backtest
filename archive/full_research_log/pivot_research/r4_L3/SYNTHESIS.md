# Synthesis — candidate directions for networked pressure

## Answer to the research questions

### Which links carry pressure?

- **Customer–supplier ties:** strongest direct fundamental mechanism in the literature. Customer news forecasts supplier returns with a lag (Cohen & Frazzini; Menzly & Ozbas). Direction is known from real economic input/output, but observed link lists are sparse, disclosure-lagged and difficult to map to every Dow pair. It is the clearest “shock in one firm, linked firm responds later” design.
- **Shared active ownership:** common owners can make holdings covary because fund flow shocks and manager trades affect several stocks together (Antón & Polk; Greenwood & Thesmar). The more natural return prediction is *pressure then reversal* (excess comovement), not persistent same-sign alpha. Ownership is quarterly and delayed; 13F reports are not daily trade records.
- **Shared ETF membership:** a real arbitrage channel lets ETF-side liquidity shocks reach constituent stocks. Holdings weights, creations/redemptions and ETF premium/discount matter; membership alone is a static, overly dense link. Evidence shows higher volatility/negative autocorrelation and some 3–5-day reversals, while informative flows can continue (Ben-David et al.; Xu et al.). This is especially relevant for the broad Dow ETF holdings, but requires historical holdings and ETF flow/NAV data.
- **Shared analyst coverage:** a strong information-processing network, with the most striking reported connected-stock return result in this literature (Ali & Hirshleifer). Monthly connected-firm returns predict next-month returns; effect weakens in large caps/strongly attended firms in some specifications. Historical IBES/FactSet analyst-to-firm links are likely needed.
- **Industry/technology links:** industry ties can capture shared news and big-to-small slow diffusion (Hou). Technology similarity has evidence in larger samples, but the target paper was not directly verified in this run and is not used to justify a direction. Both can reduce to industry/characteristic momentum if their direct link adds no incremental predictive content.
- **Large-to-small / attention lead-lag:** Lo–MacKinlay show cross-autocovariance can create contrarian profits, and Hou finds large firms lead small same-industry firms, particularly after negative information. The observed effect is expected to be weaker inside a roster of mega-cap Dow names.
- **Return-correlation networks:** correlation is symmetric and contemporaneous, blends shared market/sector factors with actual pressure and may be driven by the same-window returns one hopes to predict. Re-estimating it from a short rolling window creates unstable edges and look-ahead risk; diffusion then smooths noisy signals among assets that merely co-move. It cannot answer who led, who followed, or whether a shared demand channel transmitted a shock. Use correlations as a control or placebo benchmark, not as causal edges.

### Propagation math

1. **Directed lagged VAR / Granger graph.** Estimate (p_{t+1}=A p_t + B p_{t-1} + \epsilon_{t+1}) where (p) is a pre-defined pressure measure, with own lags and common market/sector controls. A directed edge (i\to j) exists only if lagged pressure at i adds predictive information for j beyond j’s own history. Use sparse/shrinkage estimation, rolling training-only windows, and block or circular-shift nulls; 30 nodes and roughly six years of daily observations still make edge-selection multiplicity serious. Granger means incremental prediction, not structural causation.
2. **Diebold–Yilmaz connectedness.** Generalized VAR forecast-error variance decompositions yield order-invariant directional spillover measures. Useful for quantifying “to/from” volatility across assets; not itself a signed expected-return predictor. Treat pressure and volatility as separate dependent variables.
3. **Transfer entropy.** (TE_{i\to j}=I(p_{j,t+1};p_{i,t}\mid p_{j,t},\ldots)) can detect nonlinear directed information beyond linear VAR. It needs bias-corrected estimation and pairwise multiple-testing/shuffle controls. In short daily panels it can produce unstable, spuriously dense graphs; discovered edges should be frozen before testing future returns.
4. **Graph filters.** On a fixed undirected graph, (H_\tau=e^{-\tau L}p) is a low-pass heat-kernel smoother; it spreads/smooths node pressure according to the graph. (p-H_\tau) is a high-pass residual emphasizing local disagreement. The residual is mathematically meaningful as “unsmoothed/local pressure” but does not on its own mean unpriced pressure, buying, or an expected return. If the hypothesis is that pressure spreads, lagged neighbor pressure (with direction) better matches the mechanism than same-window smoothing.
5. **Compare with current symmetric exp(-L):** current heat diffusion is a zero-lag, undirected response; it neither distinguishes source and recipient nor imposes a time delay. It may show graph-local commonality but cannot test propagation. A better test observes a source shock at t and asks whether linked nodes’ pressure/returns respond at t+1…t+h, with edge direction defined ex ante or learned only in a training window.

### Cross-asset pressure

Do not rank bonds, commodities, FX and equity ETFs as if they were interchangeable stocks. Each ETF return combines distinct exposure, carry, roll, duration, currency and volatility; raw returns have different scales and economic meanings. For any later cross-asset test, work with asset-specific return/flow/pressure series, standardize by ex-ante volatility, and define links by economic transmission or measured flow, not generic correlation. Use ETF NAV/creation-redemption flows, holdings, Treasury yield/term factors and inflation/FX controls. Compare within-asset own history against cross-asset lagged pressure. Verified sources here support asset-specific trend across futures (Card 13), directional volatility connectedness (Card 10), and bond maturity-supply pressure within bond markets (Card 14); they do not by themselves establish bond-to-equity return transmission. The often-cited 2020 cross-asset momentum paper was found but its direct paper page could not be opened during this review, so it remains unverified and does not support a candidate direction.

## Candidate directions

### Candidate A — ETF flow shock transmission through weighted holdings (stocks; most directly pressure-based)
- **Mechanism and evidence:** Liquidity shocks can enter an ETF and pass through arbitrage/creation-redemption activity into constituent securities; ETF ownership increases volatility and negative autocorrelation (Card 8). Aggregate or decomposed ETF flows show 3–5-day reversals, but informative flows may drift rather than reverse (Card 9 / 17). This is evidence for a *temporary demand-pressure* channel, not a guaranteed excess return.
- **Network and data:** Directed event source is a US equity ETF’s unexpected, non-information flow shock at day t. Its outbound edge to stock j is contemporaneous ETF j-holdings weight (w_{ETF,j,t}), preferably aggregated across overlapping ETFs with flow scaled by ETF assets and constituent liquidity. Data: CRSP/yfinance daily prices, ETF holdings snapshots, shares outstanding/NAV, flow estimates, and the Dow roster; ideally FactSet/CRSP ETF data. Freeze only disclosed/available holdings as of t-1.
- **Predicted sign/horizon:** Negative ETF redemption shock yields negative same-day constituent pressure, followed by positive constituent residual return over days +1 to +5 for non-information-driven flows. For creation shocks, sign reverses. The opposite continuation response for unexpected informative flows is a distinct competing mechanism; pre-specify how flow components are identified or use only externally classified forced/liquidity events.
- **Placebo:** Reassign each ETF’s holdings weights to randomly selected Dow names within size/liquidity bins while keeping daily ETF flow series and graph degree/weight distribution intact. Also test future flows as a timing placebo.
- **Test design:** First event-study constituent price/pressure response by event-time, with stock and date effects, market/industry controls, overlapping ETF shocks, standard errors clustered by event/date. Next test whether holdings-weighted source pressure predicts follower response after own pressure and market movement. Only after a registered signed event effect and failed shuffled-link placebo consider a long-short/market-neutral formation. Benchmark costs and EW30 later; not part of this literature phase.
- **Known failure mode:** Dow stocks are heavily represented in broad ETFs, so flow shocks may be common-market shocks; identifying non-information redemptions is hard, and the 3–5-day effect may be too small after 5 bp/side costs.

### Candidate B — Customer-to-supplier pressure/news transmission (stocks; cleanest directed economic graph)
- **Mechanism and evidence:** Public, material customer-supplier links transmit cash-flow information; limited attention delays supplier adjustment (Cards 1–2). The test keeps the thesis while replacing correlation with a directional operating link. Literature results are mainly monthly and historical, so a daily Dow signal is a new, narrow test rather than presumed replication.
- **Network and data:** Directed edge customer (i\to) supplier (j), weighted by disclosed customer share of supplier sales. Data: historical SEC 10-K/10-Q segment disclosures or FactSet Revere/Compustat customer-segment relationships; market prices and earnings/analyst news. Use point-in-time reported relationships only; link availability may be low across 30 Dow firms.
- **Predicted sign/horizon:** Positive pressure/news at a customer predicts positive supplier residual return as its cash-flow implications diffuse, primarily over the next 1–20 trading days; compare to monthly paper horizon. Separate earnings/news events from candle-only pressure to avoid calling fundamental information “price pressure.”
- **Placebo:** Shuffle supplier endpoints within industry/size bins, preserving out-degree and sales-weight distributions; alternatively reverse edge direction (supplier→customer) where the economic hypothesis predicts customer-led effects.
- **Test design:** Predefine customer shock dates or pressure threshold from a training-only calibration; event-study supplier pressure/returns at t+1…t+20, controlling supplier own lagged returns/pressure, market, industry and overlapping customers. Report all horizons with Newey-West/HAC and multiplicity adjustment. Check whether source-to-target event response exists before considering a neutral portfolio.
- **Known failure mode:** Sparse links leave too few independent shocks; large Dow customers may already be priced immediately, and data may only update quarterly/annually. Analyst attention may erase the effect, consistent with Menzly–Ozbas.

## Recommendation and decision relevance

The L1 postmortem has now appeared and supports the diagnosis: five-day blend IC was 0.0037 (t=0.38), heat IC was −0.0017 (t=−0.14), and weekly turnover made the original top-five heat portfolio trail EW30 after costs. That makes a direct source and a directionally defined network the priority; daily candle pressure should not be presumed predictive.

Start with A if holdings and ETF flow/NAV data are available, because it directly tests transitory buying/selling pressure moving through an observable holdings network and has a clean lagged response; it must distinguish non-information demand/redemption pressure from informative flows. Candidate B is the strongest economic-link alternative if FactSet/Compustat relationship data are available, but a 30-name universe may not yield enough links. No cross-asset return direction is proposed from this review: the available verified evidence is not enough to justify a direct cross-asset sign for the Dow ETF sample. The existing correlation heat kernel remains a useful baseline/control, not the proposed mechanism.

No backtests, portfolio simulations, holdout access, or outcome-based selection were performed. These candidates are research designs only and require coordinator/user selection and separate pre-registration before any results are calculated.
