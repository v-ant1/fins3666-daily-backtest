# Red-team review — network pressure directions

Reviewed 10 November 2026. This is a research-only challenge to the two candidate directions in `SYNTHESIS.md`; it uses no returns, signals, backtests or holdout outcome data. It cross-checks `r4_L4/DATA_INVENTORY.md`. `r4_L4/NETWORK_DATA.md` was not present at review time, so no additional network extract or link counts could be checked. L4’s documented holdout inspection was limited to availability metadata; this review also used only its written inventory.

## Direct assessment

Neither direction is ready for a full daily test on the stated data. Customer-supplier links are the cleaner causal graph but likely too sparse among just 30 Dow firms, and their annual material-customer disclosures do not guarantee a daily, point-in-time edge history. ETF holdings make the denser graph, but overlapping index/sector funds can link nearly every Dow constituent, while free historical holdings and daily flow data are incomplete or lagged. The papers do not establish that either signal survives modern Dow trading costs.

## Candidate A — ETF flow shocks through holdings weights

### Strongest case against it

- **Dense, ambiguous links in this universe.** Dow constituents are widely present in broad-market and sector ETFs. Aggregating overlapping ETFs may give many stock pairs some connection; degree then mostly measures index/sector membership and market-cap weight. If weights are close to static or broad, the graph cannot identify a particular source-to-recipient path. A common redemption can be a market-wide shock rather than transmission from one stock to another. Shared membership is an exposure relation, not evidence that pressure travels in a direction.
- **Pressure sign is not uniform.** Ben-David, Franzoni and Moussawi’s accessible 2014 working-paper abstract supports higher daily volatility associated with ETF ownership, not a signed flow alpha. Liao, Yin and Zhao distinguish informative from non-informative flows: unexpected flow can predict continuation, while some demand components reverse. Thus an ETF creation/redemption does not by itself identify non-information pressure. The proposed “negative redemption today, rebound over days +1 to +5” is a hypothesis, not the literature’s general prediction.
- **Flow data are not a free, universal point-in-time daily series.** L4 says SEC N-PORT is monthly holdings, published quarterly with a lag, from October 2019 through September 2026. Current issuer holdings are often downloadable daily but are not a guaranteed historical archive. Some issuer share-count/NAV histories can support approximate daily creation/redemption reconstruction, but L4 did not find a standardized free daily flow archive across ETFs. Fund-by-fund availability, corrections, publication timestamps and UIT coverage (including SPY/DIA) remain unresolved. A current holdings download cannot be backfilled as if it were known in the past.
- **Large-cap and after-cost evidence is thin.** The ownership/volatility study does not demonstrate tradable net returns for Dow stocks. A gross 3–5-day reversal, if present, could be swallowed by spread, market impact, overlapping flow events and weekly portfolio turnover. Prior card statistics from the 2014 working paper are not a modern-flow strategy test.

### Weakest evidence and falsifying result

The weakest link is the mapping from ETF-level flow shocks to **signed, temporary constituent pressure**. Existing evidence cited here mostly concerns volatility, autocorrelation or ETF returns; it does not show that daily unanticipated ETF outflows create a delayed and profitable reversal in Dow members.

Reject this direction if, after defining an externally observable flow shock and using only holdings available by t−1, (a) holdings-weighted source shocks do not predict the sign and event-time pattern in constituent pressure/returns on days +1…+5 after own-pressure, date, market and sector controls, or (b) shuffled holdings links reproduce the effect. Reject the directional transmission interpretation if recipients move only contemporaneously with the market, if effects appear before the disclosed flow, or if informative and non-information flows cannot be separated. A surviving gross effect would still need separate after-cost evidence; no such result is presumed here.

## Candidate B — customer-to-supplier pressure/news

### Strongest case against it

- **Sparse edges among 30 large firms.** SEC 10-K customer disclosures generally identify major customers above a materiality threshold; customers may be anonymized as “Customer A,” and disclosure is inconsistent. A Dow supplier may disclose a large non-Dow customer, while the named supplier/customer pair may not both be constituents. Therefore a complete 30-node graph is unlikely; the edge count must be measured from dated filings before any outcome test. L4 independently rates free SEC disclosures “too sparse for a complete 30-name network.” No verified L4 network file or edge count was present.
- **The strong historical result does not map cleanly to Dow daily returns.** Cohen and Frazzini’s evidence is mostly monthly and from 1980–2004. Their customer-news strategy’s one-year value-weighted/larger-stock result is about 70 bp/month and not significant; prices converge faster for large firms. Menzly and Ozbas find cross-predictability weakens with analyst coverage and institutional ownership, conditions common among Dow companies. The proposed 1–20 day daily window is shorter than the central monthly literature horizon and should not be chosen after looking at outcomes.
- **Disclosure and information timing.** The link is usually revealed in annual/quarterly financial statements, while the real relationship may start, end or change before the filing. Using fiscal-period links before publication leaks information. A customer shock also contains direct fundamental news, not necessarily order-flow pressure; if supplier returns respond to earnings/cash-flow news, that supports economic information diffusion but does not validate the candle-derived buying-pressure mechanism.
- **Free and commercial link histories are imperfect.** SEC filing archives are free and historical, but parsing, entity resolution and publication-date controls are high effort. L4 says FactSet Revere entitlement is unconfirmed; Compustat segments do not automatically supply complete customer links. Vendor coverage and historical link start/end dates would need verification before being treated as point-in-time.
- **Costs and independence.** With few links, a handful of earnings days may dominate the result. Event overlap, gaps, wider spreads around news and turnover can make an apparent gross response untradeable. Standard errors based on 30 names would overstate precision if the independent shocks are far fewer.

### Weakest evidence and falsifying result

The weakest evidence is the extrapolation from old monthly customer-news predictability to daily **buying-pressure propagation** among current Dow members. The literature’s large-cap/attention caveats point in the adverse direction, and the graph may be too small to estimate rather than merely noisy.

Reject a daily Dow direction if a filing-date-correct, pre-outcome graph contains too few independent customer→supplier events for a meaningful test; or, if testable, customer pressure/news does not predict supplier residual pressure/returns at the predeclared horizon after supplier own lags, market/industry controls and event controls. Reject the link interpretation if degree-preserving within-industry shuffled links perform as well, reversing edges performs equally well, or the apparent effect is confined to common industry moves. Do not replace a failed daily test with a different horizon or newly inferred link rule.

## Decision rule for the next research phase

Before a candidate consumes any of the project’s 30 variants, require (1) an as-of dated link table with source, filing/publication date, effective period and stable identifiers; (2) the number of eligible Dow nodes, edges, independent shocks and event dates reported without using outcomes; (3) a predeclared horizon and sign tied to mechanism; and (4) a degree- and sector-preserving shuffled-link null. If the data audit shows too few independent edges/events, record “not testable on this universe” rather than broadening the graph after seeing results. Only after a successful predictive mechanism test should portfolio costs be considered.
