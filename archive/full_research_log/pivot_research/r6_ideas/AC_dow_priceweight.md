# AC — Dow price-weighted ETF flow pressure

**Status: SCREEN ONLY.** No signal values, returns, ICs, event outcomes, or portfolio results were calculated. No row dated before 2020-11-27 was read from a data file; the only empirical feasibility check below used a predicate-filtered development panel. This direction is new and has not been authorized for outcome testing.

## Identity and immutable-path preflight

- **Mechanism:** a net primary-market creation in DIA requires the fund to acquire its Dow basket. Because the DJIA is price-weighted, the dollar value of an equal-share basket allocated to a constituent rises with that constituent's nominal price. A positive unexpected DIA creation should therefore impose more immediate dollar demand on high-price-weight constituents; a redemption reverses the sign.
- **Exact new output path:** `r6_ideas/AC_dow_priceweight.md`. The path did not exist at preflight, and this name is not used by another idea file.
- **Development boundary:** 2020-11-27 through 2026-10-30 only. The minimum permissible observation date is 2020-11-27. No holdout path is required or read for this screen.
- **Source/overlap scan:** searched `r6_ideas/*.md`, `R5_FINAL.md`, and `specs/r6_decisions.md` for DIA pressure, primary creation/redemption, and price-weighting. The closest prior direction is Round H, Direction 2; the distinction and remaining overlap are detailed below.

## 1. Overlap with prior work

Round H's DIA proposal formed a daily secondary-market OHLCV score (`CLV × standardized volume surprise`) and asked whether the Dow basket as a whole reversed over 21 sessions. It failed because one DIA series gives all 30 names the same sign, so it did not identify a top-five stock ranking; the 30 constituent rows were not 30 independent source shocks; and neither daily DIA flows, historical holdings weights, nor NAV basis was staged. Round H's pass probability was below 1%. The R5 ETF creation/redemption proposal also failed its data-coverage gate, with its available daily share history limited to SPY and DIA and only quarterly N-PORT holdings for broader holdings maps (`R5_FINAL.md`, Cycle 3).

AC changes the proposed source from DIA secondary-market OHLCV to primary-market creations/redemptions and maps each common flow into different constituent dollar exposures using the price-weighted Dow allocation. It asks for a **cross-sectional weekly rank** of next-week returns, not a DIA-pressure prediction or a 21-session equal-basket reversal. This is a real signal-definition change, not a re-run of H: H never measured primary flow or tested a price-weight exposure. However, the entire daily source remains one DIA shock per date, and the proposed cross-section may simply rank a mechanical price/liquidity characteristic. It cannot claim independent stock-level flow shocks.

**Verdict: PASS narrowly on non-duplication; FAIL as a ready direction until its exposure series and identifying variation are established.**

## 2. Exact pressure measure and dated data source

The S&P DJI methodology defines a price-weighted index as one with uniform constituent share counts and weights determined by constituent prices ([Index Mathematics Methodology, “Price Weighted Indices,” pp. 14–15](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/tc/documents/methodologies/methodology-index-math.pdf)). DIA's prospectus describes in-kind creation/redemption in 50,000-unit aggregations, and says the Trust holds component stocks in substantially the same weighting as the DJIA ([SEC 497 prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1041130/000119312526067870/d35140d497.htm); [State Street DIA product page](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/individual/etfs/state-street-spdr-dow-jones-industrial-average-etf-trust-dia)). Thus, for a fixed subscription amount, a price-weight component receives a dollar allocation proportional to its index price weight, `w[i,t] = raw_close[i,t] / sum(raw_close[j,t] for active Dow members j)`.

If a free, timestamp-valid DIA flow history is obtained, the single proposed signal would be:

`pressure[i,t] = sum(flow_dollars[s] * w[i,s] for s in the last 5 complete sessions) / mean(dollar_ADV20[i,s] over those sessions)`

Here `flow_dollars[s] = change_in_DIA_shares_outstanding[s] * previous_day_DIA_NAV_per_share`, with inflows positive and redemptions negative. The weight uses the **unadjusted nominal closing price** and point-in-time Dow roster, not retrospectively split-adjusted closes; a split changes the nominal price weighting that the index actually applies. ADV20 is a trailing-only stock dollar-volume scale. The signal is a signed fraction of recent stock trading capacity. It uses no later data than the formation close.

**Timing:** the formation is each fifth development trading session. To avoid assuming that same-day issuer share counts are published before the close, the five-day flow sum ends at the prior session; price weights and ADV20 also end at that session. Form at close `t` and, if separately authorized, enter at the next session's open. Predict the next five-session close-to-close abnormal return beginning with that next open. The 2020-11-27 first formation is unavailable because there is no five-session history within the permitted window; the expected ceiling is about 297 complete weekly formations through 2026-10-30. No signal/outcome series was built in this screen.

For the requested flow inputs, ETFIQ documents a free, attribution-required daily flow estimate based on the change in issuer-published shares outstanding times the prior day's NAV; it explicitly notes that this is a derived flow, not an issuer-published flow, and that it does not say which investor traded ([ETFIQ flow methodology](https://etfiq.com/methodology/flows)). ETFIQ also documents monthly SEC Form N-PORT flow fields and N-CEN creation-unit size. Those sources make an input path conceivable, but do not themselves verify a point-in-time DIA flow file covering our exact development dates or when each historical value became available.

**(a) Literature — PARTIAL.** Harris and Gurel's S&P 500 addition/removal event study finds an immediate price increase over 3% after addition announcements that was nearly fully reversed within two weeks; it is a classic price-pressure result, but it is about index inclusion shocks in an older sample, not recurring DIA creations or modern Dow mega-cap rank IC ([*Journal of Finance* 41 (1986), 815–829, DOI: 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1986.tb04550.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1986.tb04550.x)). Xu's 366-ETF study finds unexpected ETF flows predict next-day ETF returns and that flow predictability varies with ETF premium/discount; sample is U.S. passive equity ETFs, February 2001–December 2016, and the target is ETF returns rather than constituent cross-sectional returns ([*Financial Management* (2022), DOI: 10.1111/fima.12396](https://doi.org/10.1111/fima.12396)). Comerton-Forde and Marta directly separate primary-market activity from secondary trading and report increased volatility and illiquidity among overweighted constituents when primary-market creations/redemptions are large; they also report that creation/redemption is infrequent and limited. That supports a transmission channel but does not document a positive next-week mega-cap return premium or a rank IC ([*Journal of Financial Markets* 75 (2025), 100983, DOI: 10.1016/j.finmar.2025.100983](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2025.100983)). None provides the proposed direction's effect size.

This confirms daily DIA price/volume coverage only. The staged file has no flow, shares-outstanding, NAV, or holdings columns.
- Existing Dow development input QA supports **30** PIT Dow members at a formation when listed, **298** weekly formation dates in the J1 calendar (the first has no trailing Dow warm-up), and a 2020-11-27 through 2026-10-30 price window. AC requires five complete flow days, so its first eligible formation would be later than the calendar's first date. The screen does not assume that all 30 names have a valid source flow at every formation.
- The Dow PIT SIC2 cross-section has at most **18** industry groups (the J1 input audit's Dow roster summary). These are not independent flow shocks: every stock receives its pressure from the same one DIA flow series per week. The effective source breadth is one per formation date; cross-stock return dependence can further reduce inference power.
- Dow membership must be joined on the next-open date. The score needs historical raw price and volume for all active members, plus split and constituent-change handling. No current roster or current DIA holdings may be backfilled into earlier dates.

**(b) Mega-cap / post-2020 case — FAIL as evidence, plausible as mechanism only.** The Dow names and DIA are large and liquid; this could make the economic transmission tradable, but also makes persistent impact less likely. The evidence above is either 1980s index inclusion or ETF-level flow forecasting ending in 2016, with no post-2020 Dow-size cross-sectional estimate. The 2025 primary-market paper supports short-run volatility/illiquidity effects, not a return premium. It would be unjustified to transfer its effect to mega-cap excess returns.

## 4. Effective breadth and power

No paper located reports a rank IC for `DIA primary flow × Dow price weight / stock ADV`, so there is **no sourced plausible IC** with a matched universe and horizon. The Harris–Gurel event response is not an IC and cannot be converted without the event sample, return dispersion, and a mapping to this recurring flow signal. I therefore ran only clearly labeled planning scenarios, not a claimed literature-power result.

`r4_harness/power_sim.py` was run with design assumptions `assets=18` (a conservative SIC2-group proxy for cross-sectional IC noise), `dates=297`, `horizon=5` (NW lag 4), `signal_autocorrelation=0.2`, and `ic_sd=1/sqrt(18−1)=0.242535625`, 5,000 draws. At the generic R6 planning reference IC of **0.020** (not a sourced AC effect), simulated `P(NW t ≥ 2)` was **23.54%**; the pre-Holm 80%-power MDE was **0.04689**. At the more generous illustrative IC **0.030**, power was **43.14%**. The 18-cluster approximation and AR(1) assumption are optimistic: one flow source drives the whole cross-section and the simulation has no two-way date/SIC2 cluster correction. The mechanism-power gate cannot pass on the current evidence.

**(c) Power verdict: FAIL.** The power calculation has no independently documented signal-specific effect, and its generic 0.020 / 0.030 scenarios are well below the full-stack portfolio hurdle. More simulated power at a hypothetical effect cannot substitute for a literature effect estimate.

## 5. Criterion-4 portfolio feasibility first

Use the cheapest qualifying shape from `report_drafts/CRITERION4_BOUND_BY_SHAPE.md`: a **weekly rank-weighted top half** of the Dow, top 15 names with weights proportional to ranks 1–15, versus EW30. It has **55% active share**. That design study's 80%-power rank-IC hurdle is **0.074 weekly** after the modeled Holm/bootstrap requirement. Under its stated persistence scenario `rho=0.2`, simulated total-notional turnover is **116% per rebalance** and cost is **24.1% of gross edge at the 0.074 hurdle**; so it clears the criterion-4 cost branch only at or above that strong edge (the alternative turnover branch is not met). At IC 0.020 or 0.030, linear cost share under the same assumptions would be roughly 89% or 59% of gross edge, respectively, and turnover remains above 50%; neither satisfies the goal's cost test. These are shape-study assumptions, not observed AC turnover or returns.

The best comparable cited facts do not approach 0.074: Xu reports ETF-return predictability, not stock rank IC; Harris–Gurel's >3% is an index-event price response and not a weekly cross-sectional flow effect. Even the latter cannot be translated to this hurdle without unsupported assumptions. A live portfolio would need a turnover cap or qualifying cost ratio, 0.05% per side actual-trade costs, positive net excess and higher Sharpe than EW30, block bootstrap with the fixed Holm family, and DSR at the cumulative trial count. This screen establishes none of these.

**(d) Criterion-4 verdict: STOP — FAIL, infeasible on current evidence.** The best documented studies do not provide a matching IC; the illustrative effects are below the 0.074 weekly bound and fail the cost branch at the portfolio-shape study's turnover.

**(e) Novelty — PARTIAL.** The price-weight interaction is a Dow-specific, mechanically interpretable mapping from a common creation/redemption shock into different dollar exposures. It differs from Round H's undifferentiated DIA OHLCV pressure. But price weighting is a known index construction rule, and applying it to ETF flows is a narrow implementation rather than a new economic channel; the literature already studies constituent price pressure from index demand. The same one-source-shock identification weakness remains.

## Final screen decision

**Overall: STOP — FAIL. Do not advance to an outcome runner or spend a variant.** The idea has a defensible mechanical premise and is distinct enough from H to count as a new question, but fails the sourced-effect/power, criterion-4 feasibility, and exact-flow coverage gates. The greatest chance of changing this decision would be a no-outcome, development-only verification that free DIA primary-flow history and contemporaneous DIA holdings weights are available daily from 2020-11-27 onward, plus independent post-2020 mega-cap evidence with a rank-IC or equivalent pressure effect near 0.074 weekly. Without that, this is not a credible path through the fixed pass criteria.

**Subjective probability of passing the full R6 criteria if later tested:** **<1%**. This is a judgment, not a fitted probability. The screen's remaining data gap and effect-size mismatch make the true probability lower than the earlier Round H market-timing proposal's already-sub-1% assessment.
