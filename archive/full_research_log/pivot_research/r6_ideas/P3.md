# R6 Idea Gate P3 — attention/lottery buying pressure (MAX)

**Status: FAIL-GATE; do not spend a variant or open outcome data. Rank: 1 of 1 in this request. Subjective chance of passing the complete R6 stack: 1%.** The mechanism is plausible and its input is free, but there is no convincing post-2010 mega-cap survival case, the SIC2-cluster power is weak, and the likely Dow excess Sharpe is far below the decision-76 requirement. No project returns, signals, portfolio outcomes, or holdout data were loaded or calculated.

## Proposed direction

At each calendar month-end, define a stock's **MAX** as its highest adjusted daily total return during that just-completed calendar month, requiring at least 15 valid sessions. The signal is `−MAX`: a large positive daily return attracts attention and lottery-seeking purchases, creates temporary demand pressure, and predicts lower returns over the following month. Estimate the date-wise cross-sectional Spearman IC on the point-in-time S&P 500 by relating `−MAX` to the stock's next 21-session return, measured from the next session's open to the 21st session's close. Rebalance only monthly. The conditional Dow implementation is a long-only top-five portfolio sorted on `−MAX`, with 20% maximum total-notional turnover per monthly rebalance, at 0.05% cost per traded dollar, against EW30.

This is an own-stock pressure-and-reversal hypothesis, not peer transmission. The development mechanism would use the fixed SIC2 groups only for cluster-aware inference and its registered placebo; it would not diffuse signals across SIC2 links.

## Gates

### (a) Literature — PASS for the general mechanism

Bali, Cakici, and Whitelaw, [“Maxing out: Stocks as lotteries and the cross-section of expected returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 99 (2011), DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2010.08.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2010.08.014), find that the prior month's maximum daily return is negatively associated with next-month returns. Their full NYSE/Amex/Nasdaq sample reports a value-weighted high-minus-low MAX return spread of −1.03% per month and a four-factor alpha of −1.18%, for July 1962–December 2005. They interpret the result as investors overpaying for extreme upside / lottery-like payoffs; they also note high-MAX stocks are, on average, small and illiquid.

Barber and Odean, [“All That Glitters: The Effect of Attention and News on the Buying Behavior of Individual and Institutional Investors,” *Review of Financial Studies* 21 (2008), DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhm079](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhm079), find individual investors are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks, including stocks with extreme one-day returns. Barber, Huang, Odean, and Schwarz, [“Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users,” *Journal of Finance* 77 (2022), DOI 10.1111/jofi.13183](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13183), provide more recent evidence: intense Robinhood-user buying predicts negative subsequent returns, with the paper reporting average 20-day abnormal returns of −4.7% for the most purchased stocks. These papers support attention-driven buying and reversal, but the latter is a retail-order signal rather than MAX itself.

### (b) Mega-cap and post-2010 survival — FAIL / weak evidence

There is a reasonable mechanism case: spectacular positive daily returns remain attention-grabbing, and short-sale limits can make it difficult to arbitrage overpricing. The original MAX result survives controls for size and also reports value-weighted portfolios, so it is not purely an equal-weighted microcap result. That is not evidence that the effect remains material among today's largest companies.

The qualification is substantial. Bali et al. describe high-MAX stocks as small and illiquid and their sample ends in 2005. Fong and Toh, [“Investor sentiment and the MAX effect,” *Journal of Banking & Finance* 46 (2014), DOI 10.1016/j.jbankfin.2014.05.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2014.05.006), find the effect varies with investor sentiment; their CRSP sample ends in 2007. Tao, Brooks, and Bell, [“When is a MAX not the MAX? How news resolves information uncertainty,” *Journal of Empirical Finance* 57 (2020), DOI 10.1016/j.jempfin.2020.03.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2020.03.002), use news data through 2016 and report that MAX without news is negatively related to returns, whereas news-covered MAX stocks exhibit momentum. This warns that MAX may mix transient attention pressure with fundamental news and can have different signs across event types.

The 2022 Robinhood study establishes that modern retail-attention trading can predict reversals, but it does not establish a MAX effect in Dow mega-caps or a result that survives after 2020. The evidence supports testing the idea in large caps only weakly; it does not justify assuming a persistent large-cap edge. Gate (b) therefore fails.

### (c) SIC2-clustered S&P power and criterion-4 feasibility — FAIL

**Mechanism power (synthetic only).** I used `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with 72 monthly observations (the maximum in the development calendar), horizon 21 (Newey–West lag 20), autocorrelation 0.20, and 20,000 synthetic draws. The cluster stress uses 54 SIC2 groups, about 499 firms or 9.24 names per group, and within-group IC correlation 0.15, giving the pre-existing cluster-adjusted planning SD of 0.20538. At a planted mean rank IC of 0.02, simulated `P(NW t ≥ 2)` is **20.485%** (Monte Carlo SE 0.00285); effective N is about 48.97, and the simulator's 80%-power MDE is **0.08340**. IC 0.02 is a sensitivity benchmark, not an effect converted from the MAX papers. Their reported decile return spreads cannot be converted honestly to an S&P SIC2 date-wise rank IC without the underlying cross-section. The mechanism is therefore substantially underpowered at the modest benchmark, and the literature does not supply an effect estimate that justifies the 0.083 MDE.

**Cost hurdle.** For a monthly 20% total-notional turnover cap, a stated 6% monthly cross-sectional return-dispersion assumption, a top fraction of 5/30, and 0.05% per traded dollar, `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic(0.20, 0.06, 0.0005, 5/30)` gives a cost-only rank-IC floor of **0.001155**. That corresponds to about 1 bp NAV per rebalance and 12 bp per year at twelve monthly rebalances. The stricter diagnostic that trading costs remain below 25% of gross edge would require gross IC of about **0.004619** (four times the cost-only floor). The R6 turnover-or-cost gate itself is satisfied by an actual turnover cap below 50%; the 20% is the design cap, not a measured turnover result. The 6% dispersion is a planning assumption and was not estimated from returns.

**Criterion-4 feasibility estimate.** Decision 76 requires starting from expected Dow excess Sharpe around **0.8 or higher after costs** at no more than 20% monthly turnover, because the six-year net-excess bootstrap must also survive the R6 Holm family. No cited paper reports a post-2010, Dow-like top-five versus EW30 active Sharpe for MAX. As an explicitly subjective planning center—not an outcome or a paper statistic—I would expect only **0.15 annualized net excess Sharpe** for this top-five large-cap adaptation, with a broad 0.0–0.30 range. The central six-year iid-normal approximation is `t ≈ 0.15 × sqrt(6) = 0.37`, one-sided **p ≈ 0.36**; even treating it as one Holm endpoint among nine gives an approximate adjusted p of **1.00**. These are optimistic planning approximations, not the required block-bootstrap result. Neither the effect evidence nor this feasibility estimate approaches criterion 4; gate (c) fails.

### (d) Free data feasibility — PASS for construction, subject to input QA

MAX can be computed from daily adjusted close prices using the existing free daily-price inputs; the project does not need paid trades, analyst links, news, or short-sale data to construct it. The intended study also needs a dated point-in-time S&P 500 roster, trading calendar, corporate-action-adjusted daily prices, and dated SIC2 classifications for cluster inference. The S&P roster/SIC panel already staged for R6 can support those joins. Before any outcomes, an input-only report would still need to verify valid-session counts, split/dividend adjustment continuity, and PIT membership coverage for every formation date; names with missing prices remain excluded by the fixed date-wise rule rather than backfilled from a current roster. No price values or return outcomes were opened for this gate review.

### (e) Novelty versus R5/R6 — PARTIAL, low incremental novelty

MAX is a distinct, precisely defined own-stock signal: it uses the single largest daily return in a completed month, rather than R6-G's trailing abnormal-volume visibility score, D1's daily Reg SHO short-volume flow, E1's monthly same-industry CLV peer pressure, or J1's five-session SIC2-relative return reversal. Relative to R5, it is separate from retail order flow, ETF creation or fund flows, 13F common-owner CLV, SIC leader–follower pressure, Treasury-to-SPY momentum, supplier–customer earnings links, and index-change pressure. Relative to the rest of R6, it is also distinct from analyst/news networks, overnight/intraday industry pressure, OTC flow, industry momentum and peer-high anchors, insider purchases, seasonality, earnings events, short interest, cybersecurity incidents, activist campaigns, and broad ETF/fund allocation. However, MAX is conceptually adjacent to short-term reversal and R6-G's attention/visibility search, and it does not test pressure spreading through related assets. The distinction from J1 is the lottery/attention shock in the maximum daily return, not reversal of a peer-relative multi-day return; it is real but narrow. Treat MAX as a fresh observable, not evidence for a new network mechanism. Novelty is not strong enough to offset the power and criterion-4 failures.

## Recommendation

**Do not advance P3 to a preregistration or variant.** The broad literature supports negative next-month returns after high MAX in historical all-stock samples and supports retail attention buying in newer data, but evidence for an after-2010 mega-cap MAX premium is not established. The S&P SIC2 planning power is only about 20% at IC 0.02, the estimated Dow excess Sharpe is far below the 0.8 feasibility threshold, and the strategy is an own-stock reversal with limited novelty relative to R5/R6. The one-percent full-stack probability is a subjective judgment, not a statistical estimate.

*No project outcomes or holdout data were computed or inspected. The power figures are from synthetic `power_sim.py` assumptions; the cost floor uses the stated planning dispersion.*
