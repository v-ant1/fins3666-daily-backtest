# R6 Idea Gate — G: dense daily volume shocks

**Scope.** This is a literature, design, and input-feasibility review only. No return values, forward outcomes, ICs, or holdout data were loaded or calculated. The power and cost figures below are design calculations using the existing synthetic harness and input metadata.

## Ranking and overall decision

| Rank | Direction | Gate verdict now | Judgmental probability of passing all R6 criteria |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Own-stock high-volume visibility shock | **FAIL-GATE pending a credible mega-cap effect and power** | 7% |
| 2 | SIC3 bellwether volume shock propagating to peers | **FAIL-GATE pending a Dow-relevant peer effect and power** | 3% |

The probabilities are judgment calls for passing the complete, fixed R6 stack: frozen specification, correct positive mechanism result, valid placebos, net Dow performance above EW30, multiplicity correction, trial-count DSR, turnover/cost gate, and coordinator-only holdout. They are not Monte Carlo powers. Direction 1 ranks higher because the high-volume premium has direct U.S. evidence and a recent value-weighted result; direction 2 adds a more specific network test but has weaker evidence that the channel reaches already-visible Dow firms.

## Common fixed design proposal

Use the existing point-in-time adjusted daily OHLCV panel. A single daily abnormal-volume score can be fixed as the trailing-only standardized log-volume shock, where the log-volume mean and standard deviation use the preceding 50 sessions and exclude the current session from the normalizer. This produces a score each day for every name with a valid bar. No future data enter the normalizer. For the own-stock hypothesis, use the positive volume shock itself as the visibility signal; acknowledge that volume is unsigned and cannot distinguish buying from selling. For the peer hypothesis, use the previous day's shock of the point-in-time SIC3 industry bellwether, defined by the largest lagged market capitalization in the target's SIC3 group, and leave the target itself out. A peer exposure must be lagged one session, and any target with no other SIC3 peer is ineligible for the peer test.

Trade only at a 21-session cadence, holding through the next 21-session outcome window, and apply a hard 50% total-notional turnover cap per monthly rebalance (buys plus sells / NAV). Long-only top five of the PIT Dow 30 versus EW30 has approximately 83.3% active share at equal weights. If all five are replaced, turnover is roughly 200%, so the turnover cap must be enforced by retaining positions or the design fails the turnover criterion; no performance-based claim can be made about whether that is feasible.

### Cost hurdle

I called `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` with total-notional turnover 0.50, 21-session cross-sectional return dispersion **assumed** to be 6%, cost 0.05% per side, and top fraction 5/30. The result is **rank IC 0.002887** to make gross top-five active return just equal the stipulated trading cost. The helper's mapping is `cost × turnover / (dispersion × sqrt(12) × (0.5 − top_fraction/2))`; total turnover already includes buys and sells, so it must not be doubled again. At the 50% cap, cost is 2.5 bp per monthly rebalance, or about 30 bp/year at 12 rebalances. R6 also requires costs to be less than 25% of gross edge, so the stricter hurdle is four times break-even: **IC > 0.01155** under the same assumptions (gross active return >10 bp/month, or about 120 bp/year). The 6% dispersion is a planning assumption, not estimated from project returns.

## Power analysis (synthetic and SIC3-cluster proxy)

The PIT SIC3 membership input has **131 distinct SIC3 groups** on 2026-10-30. To avoid treating 500 correlated names as 500 independent observations, I ran `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with `universe_size=131` (one effective unit per SIC3 group), 71 non-overlapping 21-session formation periods over the 1,488-session development span, horizon 21 (Bartlett Newey–West lag 20), signal autocorrelation 0.20, and 5,000 simulations. The simulator's null-rank approximation gives IC SD `1/sqrt(131−1) = 0.0877`; this is a conservative cluster proxy, not a full within-industry cluster bootstrap. Date-level IC inputs are synthetic: no project IC or return data entered the analysis.

| Synthetic planted rank IC | Simulated P(NW t ≥ 2) | 80% MDE | Effective N |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.01155 (the cost / 25%-of-gross boundary) | 27.1% | 0.03586 | 48.3 |
| 0.0183 (R6 design benchmark used for prior power discussions) | 44.6% | 0.03586 | 48.3 |
| 0.0600 (generous sensitivity, not an expected effect) | 99.5% | 0.03586 | 48.3 |

The S&P mechanism therefore reaches 80% power only for a rank IC of about **0.036** or larger under this model. A 0.06 IC implies about 52 bp/month top-five gross active return under the 6% dispersion mapping, close in magnitude to the published 53 bp/month value-weighted high-minus-low volume-decile estimate; that is a deliberately generous sensitivity, not an apples-to-apples conversion because the portfolio, weighting, dates, and signal are different. At a Dow-only 30-name cross-section, the same 71-date design gives 20.4% power at IC 0.0183 and an 80% MDE of 0.07593. R6 permits the S&P 500 for the mechanism gate, but the Dow portfolio must independently clear its net-performance gate. For Direction 2, reducing the effective breadth to SIC3 clusters is especially important; this simulation still understates common within-industry residual dependence and should be followed by a cluster bootstrap in any later frozen runner.

## 1. Own-stock high-volume visibility shock

**Question.** Does an unusually large positive volume shock, relative to that firm's own trailing 50-session volume, predict positive returns over the next 21 sessions because the shock increases visibility and demand?

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS.** Gervais, Kaniel and Mingelgrin, [“The High-Volume Return Premium,” *Journal of Finance* (2001), 56(3), 877–919, DOI 10.1111/0022-1082.00349](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00349), report that stocks with unusually high volume over a day or week appreciate over the following month and motivate visibility-driven demand. Kaniel, Ozoguz and Starks, [“The High Volume Return Premium: Cross-Country Evidence,” *Journal of Financial Economics* (2012), 103(2), 255–279, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.08.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.08.012), find the effect across 41 markets, but also find it is weaker for relatively large firms and FTSE All-World constituents. Wang, [“The High Volume Return Premium and Economic Fundamentals,” *Journal of Financial Economics* (2021), 140(1), 325–345, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.10.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.10.006), reports a size-adjusted, value-weighted U.S. high-minus-low-volume-decile premium of 0.53% per month using 1963–2016 data. This extends the evidence into 2010–2016 and value weighting, but not into 2020–2026.
- **(b) Large-cap and post-2020 case — FAIL / weak.** The visibility story could survive in mega-caps if volume shocks reveal attention shifts or bring new marginal buyers even when a firm is already well followed. But Kaniel et al.'s direct size result cuts against it, and the U.S. 2021 study stops in 2016. A large-cap, liquid universe has less scope for new investor recognition, and high volume can also be selling, news incorporation, index rebalancing, or liquidity provision. No verified result establishes that this premium survived in mega-caps after 2020.
- **(c) Power — FAIL at the cost-compatible effect; conditional only for a very large effect.** The SIC3-cluster S&P simulation has 44.6% power at IC 0.0183 and 27.1% at the 0.01155 cost/gross boundary, versus an 80% MDE of 0.03586. Power rises to 99.5% at IC 0.06, but the old high-minus-low decile portfolio result cannot be directly translated into that rank IC. Gate (c) is not passed without a defensible large-cap effect at or above the MDE.
- **(d) Cheap data feasibility — PASS for availability, not yet for quality.** `r6_C1/dow_components_dev.parquet` and `r6_C1/sp500_components_dev.parquet` contain daily development OHLCV inputs; the accompanying QA reports 1,488 sessions, 43,944 Dow asset-date rows across 33 ticker aliases, and 716,987 S&P asset-date rows across 564 names. The point-in-time S&P/Dow rosters and SIC inputs are available in `r6_data` and `r6_C1`. This supports an inexpensive input build without an external market-data purchase. Before freezing, check all PIT name-date bar coverage, splits and volume adjustment, stale bars, and the initial 2020Q4 gap disclosed in the input QA; do not treat current constituents as the historical universe.
- **(e) Novelty — limited.** The volume shock and next-month premium are already the published result. The modest contribution would be a trailing-only abnormal-volume score, tested at 21-session cadence on PIT large caps and Dow constituents with cluster-aware inference, explicit turnover control, and a direct EW30 portfolio hurdle. That is a clean modern transport test, not a new discovery claim.

## 2. SIC3 bellwether volume shock propagating to peers

**Question.** Does an extreme volume shock at a prominent industry firm predict subsequent returns of its other SIC3 peers that did not themselves have an extreme-volume signal?

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for the components; no paper establishes this exact mechanism.** Gervais, Kaniel and Mingelgrin (2001, cited above) support an own-firm attention shock. Hameed, Morck, Shen and Yeung, [“Information, Analysts, and Stock Return Comovement,” *Review of Financial Studies* (2015), 28(11), 3153–3187, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhv042](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv042), find that information from heavily followed industry bellwethers transfers to less-followed peers, but not vice versa. Drake, Jennings, Roulstone and Thornock, [“The Comovement of Investor Attention,” *Management Science* (2017), 63(9), 2847–2867, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2016.2477](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2477), document industry-level attention comovement and attention transfer from a firm's earnings announcement to peer firms. Neither study tests whether volume shocks themselves propagate into returns, so the proposed link remains an unverified synthesis.
- **(b) Large-cap and post-2020 case — FAIL / weaker than Direction 1.** A bellwether's volume may reveal attention to common industry fundamentals, and published U.S. evidence supports one-way information transfer from prominent firms. But the transfer is strongest toward neglected peers; Dow constituents are generally prominent and highly followed. A broad S&P cross-section gives more less-followed targets for mechanism estimation than the Dow portfolio does, so the portfolio setting may remove the very condition that supports the mechanism. The attention paper supports peer transfer generally, not post-2020 volume-induced return continuation.
- **(c) Power — FAIL at ordinary effects; same SIC3-cluster results are optimistic.** The one-observation-per-industry S&P calculation gives 44.6% power at IC 0.0183, 27.1% at the cost/gross boundary, and requires IC about 0.03586 for 80% power. For peer propagation, this is an upper bound: one bellwether shock can expose many targets in a SIC3 group, but those targets do not create independent shock events. A future test must cluster on SIC3 and formation date, remove the target's own shock, and require that the sign survive the predeclared random-link and time-shift placebos.
- **(d) Cheap data feasibility — PARTIAL.** Daily OHLCV and point-in-time SIC3 links are already present for the S&P/Dow development universe (`r6_C1`), and the latest S&P PIT slice has 131 SIC3 groups. The needed lagged market capitalization to define the industry bellwether must be assembled point-in-time from development price bars and historical shares outstanding (or a disclosed free SEC/XBRL source). Some SIC3 cells will contain only one name, and the 30-stock Dow will have few usable peer sets. These are measurable input constraints, not a reason to substitute a different peer definition after results.
- **(e) Novelty — moderate but bounded.** The new element is to treat a bellwether's *abnormal volume* as the source shock, then predict same-industry targets with no own shock, using point-in-time SIC3 links and cluster-aware placebos. Existing work already documents bellwether information transfer and attention transfer, so the general peer mechanism is not novel. The specific open question is whether volume adds a directional, investable signal beyond the target's own abnormal volume and standard industry information.

## Recommendation

Keep Direction 1 as the stronger idea for an input-only, preregistered mechanism design, but do not freeze either candidate yet. Both fail the current evidence bar because large-cap survival after 2020 is unknown and the cluster-aware power simulation needs an effect around 0.036 IC for 80% power; the literature's monthly portfolio spreads are not directly comparable to that IC. Direction 2 is more novel but less likely to survive when the eventual targets are Dow mega-caps and the networks are sparse. Before either advances, complete the PIT coverage audit, define adjusted volume precisely, justify a large-cap effect size without looking at outcomes, and ensure the 21-session schedule and enforced 50% turnover cap are fixed before any test.
