# R6 idea gate proposals — B

**Scope.** Four proposal-stage mechanisms for the thesis that buying pressure persists or spreads through economically or informationally related assets. No signal/return outcomes or sealed-holdout data were examined. This is not a preregistration and authorizes no strategy build. I ran a cheap source-availability preflight: searched the workspace for relevant exports and checked UNSW’s public database catalogue. That found no local I/B/E/S analyst-detail, Revere, or historical news-link extracts; the catalogue identifies I/B/E/S, FactSet Lionshares, and LSEG WorkSpace, but does not document a Revere subscription or analyst-level coverage entitlement. Thus source discovery is complete, while row-count/coverage pulls remain open gates for data-dependent ideas.

## Ranking by probability of clearing the full R6 stack

Probabilities are judgmental chances of passing **all** locked gates (development mechanism, portfolio after costs, Holm-adjusted inference, and one-shot holdout), conditional on obtaining valid point-in-time inputs. They are deliberately low because R5 found no passing strategy, the traded universe has only 30 large caps, and effects in the literature are commonly stronger in smaller/less-followed firms. These are not estimated probabilities from outcomes.

| Rank | Direction | Chance of full pass | Main reason |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | I/B/E/S co-covered peer pressure and revisions | 10% | Direct literature on shared-analyst connected-firm momentum, with an interpretable information-broker channel; Dow coverage saturation and data entitlement are serious risks. |
| 2 | Revere supply-chain pressure transmission | 8% | Economic links are stronger than return correlation and Revere appears broader than 10-K major-customer disclosures; large-firm attenuation and subscription uncertainty lower the odds. |
| 3 | Co-covered earnings-announcement spillover | 5% | Event timing sharpens identification and should give more power than unconditional daily tests; overlap with the first direction and only 180 issuer-year clusters constrain power. |

These are four hypotheses, not four approved variants. If one survives its input gate, the owner must select at most three pre-registered variants under the goal-wide 30-variant budget and must record the idea-gate decision before outcomes.

## Common numeric power convention

For planning, use `minimum standardized effect to have 80% probability of observing t ≥ 2 ≈ 2.84 × σ / sqrt(N_eff)` (normal approximation, before Holm correction). `N_eff` is the conservative number of independent date or issuer-year clusters after overlap/serial dependence, not the raw stock-day or edge count; `σ` is the standard deviation of the test statistic's cluster-level observations. These checks ask whether a plausible effect could produce the required t-statistic; they do not assert that a given effect exists. The eventual preregistration should replace these planning assumptions with a simulation using the frozen Newey–West/event statistic and actual row counts, before outcomes.

## 1. Shared analyst coverage: connected-firm pressure and forecast revisions

**Mechanism and candidate test.** Build a point-in-time graph from analysts jointly covering two firms. When a source firm has a signed price-pressure or forecast-revision shock, test whether the weighted pressure among its co-covered neighbors predicts the target’s next 2–10 trading-day market-adjusted return. Use analyst overlap as the information link, not a rolling return-correlation graph. Keep the source’s pressure and the target’s own pressure separate. The primary direction is same-sign continuation; no sign flip is allowed after registration.

**Gate (a), literature.** Ali and Hirshleifer find connected-firm momentum based on shared analyst coverage and report that the signal captures spillover effects: [Journal of Financial Economics (2020), DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.10.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.10.007). Muslu, Rebello, and Xu document return comovement and analyst forecast expectations among firms sharing coverage: [Journal of Accounting Research (2014), DOI 10.1111/1475-679X.12057](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.12057). Hameed, Morck, Shen, and Yeung find directional information spillovers from highly followed firms to neglected peers: [NBER paper 15833 / DOI 10.3386/w15833](https://doi.org/10.3386/w15833). These papers support the link and information mechanism; they do not prove post-2020 Dow alpha.

**Gate (b), post-2020 mega-cap case.** Shared analysts process information across firms, and complex/indirect relationships can be harder for investors to price than a sector label alone. That channel could remain when a co-covered peer makes a firm-specific revision or pressure shock. The counter-case is strong: Dow constituents receive abundant coverage, so shared coverage may be nearly complete and any lag may have been arbitraged away. Require the graph not to be saturated and compare against degree-preserving/shuffled analyst links; otherwise kill it before outcomes.

**Gate (c), power.** Development has about 1,479 trading dates. For a daily cross-sectional rank-IC series with assumed SD 0.20 and conservative `N_eff=500` date clusters, the planning effect needed for an 80% chance to observe t ≥ 2 is about `0.20 × 2.84/√500 = 0.025` mean rank IC, before Holm correction. The wider point-in-time S&P 100/500 can support mechanism estimation, but the Dow result remains mandatory.

**Gate (d), cheap data feasibility.** [UNSW’s database catalogue](https://www.unsw.edu.au/business/our-research/research-environment/databases) lists I/B/E/S Detail through WRDS and LSEG WorkSpace broker estimates, but states WRDS individual accounts are not available to ordinary students and that WorkSpace accounts are limited; it does not confirm the user’s entitlement to analyst-level identity/coverage records. Workspace inventory found no relevant export. **Status: NOT PASSED.** Before preregistration, a data owner must obtain a permitted sample extract and report analyst IDs, covered-issuer counts, point-in-time history, date coverage, duplicate handling, and the share of Dow firms with at least one non-self peer. Do not infer shared coverage from consensus estimates alone.

**Gate (e), novelty.** The new test is a direct daily signed-pressure/revision transmission test on a predeclared Dow portfolio, with wider-universe power estimation and explicit network placebo, rather than another broad monthly momentum portfolio. Novelty is measurement and test design, not the claim that analyst networks are new.

## 2. FactSet Revere supply-chain pressure transmission

**Mechanism and candidate test.** Use dated supplier/customer links to transmit a firm’s signed five-session pressure shock along the direction of business exposure. Test whether customer pressure predicts supplier returns (or the reverse direction only if separately justified before data access; do not choose direction from results). Weight by relationship confidence/recency only if those fields are available and frozen. Exclude stale or post-dated links.

**Gate (a), literature.** Cohen and Frazzini document return predictability across principal customer–supplier links: [Journal of Finance (2008), DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01379.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01379.x). Agarwal, Leung, Konana, and Kumar find that co-search attention conditions supply-chain return predictability: [Information Systems Research (2017), DOI 10.1287/isre.2016.0656](https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2016.0656). Yu, Hu, and Zhong find customer returns predict Australian firms’ returns, but report weaker predictive power among larger, more-attended firms: [Pacific-Basin Finance Journal (2023), DOI 10.1016/j.pacfin.2023.101975](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pacfin.2023.101975). The latter is an explicit caution for this universe.

**Gate (b), post-2020 mega-cap case.** Revere can include relationships not visible in the 10-K major-customer threshold, potentially repairing the thin disclosed-link problem behind R5 D7. Proprietary, detailed, dated links may still take time for investors to map. However, the broader link count can add weak or irrelevant edges, and information on large firms diffuses quickly. Predefine a minimum direct-link count and use only clear supplier/customer relationships; reject the idea if most Dow names have no usable in-universe or wider-universe links.

**Gate (c), power.** On 1,479 development dates, set an input gate of at least 500 independent date clusters with one or more valid pressure shocks and linked target observations across the point-in-time S&P 500 mechanism universe. At `N_eff=500`, the standardized mean predictive effect must be about `2.84/√500 = 0.127` for an 80% chance to observe t ≥ 2. Separately require at least 20 Dow names with dated usable links for a Dow mechanism test; 500 broad-universe clusters do not substitute for that test.

**Gate (d), cheap data feasibility.** The [UNSW catalogue](https://www.unsw.edu.au/business/our-research/research-environment/databases) lists FactSet **Lionshares** ownership data, not Revere. [FactSet documentation](https://go.factset.com/hubfs/Website_Downloads/Statistical%20Package%20Integration/Docs%203.0/ExtractVectorFormula.pdf) says a Revere subscription is required; public descriptions indicate broader relationship coverage than the SEC 10-K threshold, but this does not establish UNSW entitlement or a usable historical export. Workspace inventory found no Revere file. **Status: NOT PASSED.** A data owner must obtain a small Revere sample and provide counts of point-in-time active links for Dow and S&P 500 identifiers, relationship start/end dates, reverse-disclosure provenance, and timestamp/version history before registration. Do not substitute later/current edges for historical ones.

**Gate (e), novelty.** The proposed addition is daily signed pressure transmission over dated, broader business-relationship records, with link-direction and edge-quality diagnostics. It differs from D7’s narrow 10-K disclosed customer list. It must not be described as novel merely because the vendor or source changed.

## 3. Earnings-announcement pressure spillover among co-covered firms

**Mechanism and candidate test.** Treat a co-covered firm’s earnings announcement as a timed information shock. Measure the source’s announcement-window abnormal return and/or forecast surprise, then test whether the signed response among its pre-event analyst co-covered peers predicts those peers’ subsequent 2–10-session abnormal returns. Freeze the announcement timestamp convention and avoid overlapping event windows. This is a distinct event-study direction from unconditional peer-pressure forecasting, though it shares the analyst-link input gate in idea 1.

**Gate (a), literature.** Baker, Ni, Saadi, and Zhu find peer earnings announcements relate to analyst forecast-based drift and discuss intra-industry information transfer: [International Review of Financial Analysis (2017), DOI 10.1016/j.irfa.2017.02.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2017.02.002). Madsen finds customer returns predict supplier returns shortly before supplier earnings announcements, with the predictability attenuating after the announcement: [Journal of Accounting Research (2017), DOI 10.1111/1475-679X.12158](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.12158). Ali and Hirshleifer’s shared-analyst links support use of analyst coverage as the peer relation (citation in idea 1). These are adjacent mechanisms, not a paper proving this exact Dow specification.

**Gate (b), post-2020 mega-cap case.** Earnings news can convey information about common products, customers, costs, or demand, while a co-covering analyst is a plausible information conduit. The event timing should reduce dilution from unrelated daily pressure. But large Dow issuers attract immediate attention, so any delayed peer response may be small. The hypothesis should be strongest on crowded announcement days or for less central peers only if that interaction is prespecified and within the three-variant cap.

**Gate (c), power.** Approximate raw events as `30 issuers × 4 quarterly reports × 6 years ≈ 720`. Issuer-year dependence leaves at most `30 × 6 = 180` conservative independent clusters. At `N_eff=180`, the standardized event response must be about `2.84/√180 = 0.212 SD` for an 80% chance to observe t ≥ 2. Overlapping events or incomplete coverage raise the MDE. A wider S&P 100/500 event panel can test the mechanism with better power but cannot replace the Dow event result.

**Gate (d), cheap data feasibility.** UNSW’s catalogue confirms I/B/E/S forecast data and LSEG WorkSpace company-event/news features through limited accounts, but no earnings calendar or analyst-detail extract exists in the workspace. **Status: NOT PASSED.** Before outcomes, pull only an event/coverage manifest (issuer, release timestamp, forecast availability, analyst identifiers, linked-peer counts) for the development window; do not compute post-event returns in this feasibility step. Set a minimum of 500 timestamped events and 10 Dow issuers with at least one valid linked peer, or kill the proposal as underpowered/infeasible.

**Gate (e), novelty.** This isolates the event-time path of buying-pressure response along a shared-analyst graph and tests fade over a fixed short horizon, rather than treating all peer returns as a rolling momentum factor. The novelty claim is the network-by-event measurement and Dow feasibility test, not earnings drift itself.

## 4. News co-mention / attention network pressure

**Mechanism and candidate test.** Form dated links from independent news articles that mention two companies together, using only articles published before the target return window. A source firm’s signed announcement/news-window price pressure is transmitted to linked Dow firms over 1–10 sessions; compare it with same-day unlinked firms and degree-preserving link permutations. Co-mention frequency is a candidate economic-information link, not by itself proof of causation.

**Gate (a), literature.** Scherbina and Schlusche identify links through news-story co-mentions and report future return cross-predictability that survives excluding customer–supplier pairs; this is an AEI/SSRN working paper, not a journal result: [DOI 10.2139/ssrn.2363436](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2363436). Chen, Guo, and Tu find co-mentioned firms’ media-connection strength forecasts return comovement, not necessarily alpha: [Journal of Economic Dynamics and Control (2021), DOI 10.1016/j.jedc.2021.104191](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jedc.2021.104191). Hirshleifer, Peng, and Wang show social connectedness changes market reactions and post-announcement dynamics: [Review of Financial Studies (2025), DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhae025](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhae025). The evidence supports attention and linkage mechanisms, but effects are not interchangeable.

**Gate (b), post-2020 mega-cap case.** News links can reflect strategic, competitive, financing, or product ties missing from standard industry codes; cross-industry links may therefore add information beyond the existing SIC test. Against this, Dow firms are heavily covered, mentions are often market-wide, and fast professional processing likely removes much of any lag. Restrict candidate links to recurring or event-specific co-mentions with a clear published-before-target chronology; do not rely on generic broad-market articles.

**Gate (c), power.** Require at least 250 independent development date clusters containing a resolved Dow source, a valid pre-existing link, and at least one target. At `N_eff=250`, the standardized effect needed for an 80% chance to observe t ≥ 2 is `2.84/√250 = 0.180 SD`. If the manifest yields only 100 usable event-day clusters, it rises to `0.28 SD`; treat that as a likely no-go rather than lowering the effect threshold.

**Gate (d), cheap data feasibility.** [Public GDELT data](https://www.gdeltproject.org/data.html) provides a plausible no-license route, and the workspace inventory contains no prebuilt news-link panel. This is a source-level check only; no GDELT article/mention sample was pulled or resolved against historical Dow identifiers. **Status: NOT PASSED.** The data owner must pull a small, outcome-free manifest: article counts by month and company, exact publication times, entity-resolution precision on a hand-audited sample, multi-company article counts, and coverage for at least 20 Dow firms. Any historical entity mappings must be available point-in-time. If identity precision is inadequate or date coverage is sparse, kill the idea.

**Gate (e), novelty.** The proposed test uses news links only when formed before the target window, measures signed price pressure rather than just return comovement, requires a peer transmission direction, and carries a link-shuffle placebo. This is a stricter trading test than co-mention/comovement evidence; it is not a claim that news networks are new.

## Omitted candidate notes

- **52-week-high anchoring with peers:** George and Hwang (2004) and Hong, Jordan, and Liu (2015) show the 52-week-high effect and its industry-information component ([George & Hwang DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2004.00695.x); [Hong et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pacfin.2015.02.011)). I did not rank it among the four because the core published signal is an individual/industry momentum characteristic, while a defensible point-in-time peer-link source and incremental pressure mechanism are still unspecified. It risks becoming a generic factor tilt. Reconsider only with a genuinely ex-ante network definition and distinct evidence.
- **Options-implied pressure:** omitted because this preflight found no options-implied positioning or dealer-flow history in the allowed workspace/catalog sources. Option prices alone do not identify signed customer pressure or dealer inventory. A data source and identification design would be needed before it could clear (d).

## Disposition

**No direction is cleared to build.** Gate (a) is supported by verified DOI/working-paper records above, and (b), (c), and (e) are proposal-level assessments. Gate (d) remains explicitly unpassed for all four: only catalogue/workspace source checks were possible here, not a row-level feasibility pull. Next legitimate step is an outcome-free, point-in-time availability manifest by a data owner with valid access; only an idea that passes that input gate should go to independent red-team review and preregistration. No holdout access or testing is authorized by this note.
