# Round U — Mutual-fund flow pressure into Dow holdings

**Screen verdict: no direction clears all gates.** The strongest paper mechanisms are real, but they require a timely mutual-fund holdings-and-flows panel. The local N-PORT extract is selected ETF series, and the candidate designs substantially repeat the already rejected common-owner / ETF-flow families. This is a pre-outcome idea screen only: no signal was constructed, no project return or outcome was calculated, and no holdout data was accessed.

## Shared source, timing, and power assumptions

SEC Form N-PORT is free and covers registered management funds, but current public data are not a timely monthly trading tape. Funds report quarterly, public data sets update quarterly, and the current form provides only the third month of the quarter publicly; filings generally arrive up to 60 days after quarter-end. SEC amendments that would make more monthly holdings public have compliance dates delayed to 2027/2028, and the February 2026 SEC proposal to return to quarterly public disclosure remained at the proposed-rule stage in the SEC rulemaking record as checked 2026-11-11 ([SEC docket](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/public-comments/s7-2026-05)). Thus a causal design may use only holdings at the public filing date, never the underlying report date as if it were known then. Public lag makes the historical “predict forced trading before it happens” mechanism especially difficult to trade.

For all three gates (c), I ran the project’s `r4_harness/power_sim.py`, with assumptions only and no project outcome inputs: 503 S&P names, 53 SIC2 clusters, 23 usable non-overlapping quarterly observations (roughly six development years after the filing lag), `signal_autocorrelation=0.20`, 5,000 simulations, seed 20261115. I adjusted the null IC standard deviation for SIC2 dependence using an assumed within-cluster correlation of 0.15: `sqrt((1 + (503/53 - 1)*0.15)/(503 - 1)) = 0.06730`. Since reports are quarterly, I used horizon 1 on the quarterly observation series rather than pretending there are 23 independent monthly or daily filing updates. A separate measured post-2020 effect does not exist to calibrate the planted effects below; these are optimistic sensitivity assumptions, not observed or paper-estimated ICs.

| Candidate | Fixed hypothesized signed mean IC | Simulated probability of NW t ≥ 2 | 80% MDE | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Extreme-outflow fire-sale pressure / rebound | +0.020 | 0.356 | 0.0399 | Underpowered even if the flow-pressure proxy really has a 0.02 rank IC. |
| Predictable flow-induced demand / continuation | +0.010 | 0.176 | 0.0399 | Very underpowered at a smaller, cautious effect. |
| Flow-chasing sentiment / reversal | +0.020 after reversing the raw flow sign | 0.356 | 0.0399 | Same power as the positive 0.02 case; magnitude is unsupported for Dow mega-caps. |

`power_sim` models date-level IC noise, not fund-level correlated errors or the missing-holdings problem; the stated power should therefore be read as optimistic. Monthly overlapped 1–3 month returns would require HAC adjustment and would not create additional independent N-PORT updates.

## 1. Extreme outflow fire-sales, then buy the transiently pressured holdings

**Candidate definition if it were eligible.** At each actual public fund filing, estimate fund flow as change in fund net assets minus the reported portfolio return, then combine the previously disclosed stock weights with the fund’s extreme negative flow to estimate sell pressure per stock, scaled by lagged dollar volume or shares outstanding. Buy the Dow holdings with the largest *prior-disclosed* extreme-outflow pressure, expecting a positive 1–3 month rebound. Use only report rows filed before formation and exclude any amended filing until its amendment availability date. This differs in trading direction from front-running expected outflows: it is a post-pressure liquidity-provision test.

- **(a) Literature — PASS for mechanism, mixed for the exact trade.** Coval and Stafford, [“Asset Fire Sales (and Purchases) in Equity Markets,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 86 (2007), 479–512](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2006.09.007), use mutual-fund flows over 1980–2004 and show extreme-flow funds trade existing positions, creating price pressure in overlapping holdings. Dyakov and Verbeek, [“Front-running of Mutual Fund Fire-sales,” *Journal of Banking & Finance* 37 (2013), 4931–4942](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbankfin.2013.08.013), report about 0.5% monthly alpha for anticipating distressed funds’ forced sales over 1990–2010, but find the effect is primarily below the NYSE mean size; for the largest stocks pressure reverted before their strategy could detect it. Wardlaw, [“Measuring Mutual Fund Flow Pressure as Shock to Stock Returns,” *Journal of Finance* 75 (2020), 3221–3243](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12962), shows common flow-pressure measures can embed the stock’s realized return mechanically; with cleaner measures the quarterly outflow effect is negligible and there is no subsequent reversal. This is a significant caution against assuming a rebound.
- **(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 survival — FAIL as supporting evidence.** The economic channel can still operate in liquid mega-caps when many funds face a common redemption shock, but liquidity makes the price impact less durable. Dyakov–Verbeek explicitly find the large-stock price effect is gone before their public-data strategy acts. I found no verified study establishing a post-2020 1–3 month rebound in mega-cap holdings. Wardlaw’s correction weakens the classic estimate rather than validating persistence.
- **(c) Power — FAIL.** Under the shared optimistic setup, a planted +0.020 IC gives 35.6% probability of NW t ≥ 2 and 80% MDE 0.0399. No paper reports a matching SIC2-adjusted rank IC for the Dow-sized post-filing rebound; the published effect’s large-cap timing caveat makes a 0.02 monthly persistence assumption generous.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — FAIL for active mutual funds; ETF-only subset is feasible to inspect.** I performed a coverage-only filter of `r4_L4/data/etf_nport_filing_coverage.csv` and `etf_dow_comembership.csv`, retaining rows only where both the report date and filing-availability date are within 2020-11-27–2026-10-30. The resulting selected SPY/DIA/QQQ/sector-SPDR extract has 366 filing records over 60 filing dates and 46 distinct report dates; 2,531 mapped Dow holding rows cover 36 unique symbols across 12 ETF series. These are filing/position counts only. They show the staged extract is not an active mutual-fund panel and has no active-fund subscriptions/redemptions field. A full free SEC N-PORT pull and CUSIP lineage build is technically possible, but current public holdings are delayed and the local data do not contain the required fund-level flow history. SEC’s current [N-PORT data page](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-n-port-data-sets) describes quarterly public data; its [2025 rule update](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/04/s7-26-22) delayed the 2024 monthly-publication changes to 2027/2028.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL by exclusion.** This is the same mutual-fund flow × existing portfolio holdings / forced trading mechanism as the already screened R5 D3 common-owner flow direction and the current Round U launch. Its opposite trade timing (after instead of before pressure) does not make it a genuinely new information source. It also cannot be substituted for the rejected R5 ETF-flow idea using ETF holding data.

## 2. Predictable flow-induced purchases in existing holdings

**Candidate definition if it were eligible.** Predict investor subscriptions from lagged fund performance and flows, multiply those predicted net flows by the latest holdings that were publicly available at formation, aggregate the expected signed buying by Dow name, and predict positive 1–3 month returns for names with anticipated flow-induced buying. Fix the positive sign; do not switch to reversal after seeing a result.

- **(a) Literature — PASS for a distinct timing decomposition, not for a fresh project family.** Lou, [“A Flow-Based Explanation for Return Predictability,” *Review of Financial Studies* 25 (2012), 3457–3489](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs103), shows expected flow-induced trading positively forecasts stock and fund returns in the following year and subsequently reverses; it partially explains stock momentum. Coval and Stafford (2007, above) establish that flows expand or contract existing positions and induce pressure. Frazzini and Lamont, [“Dumb Money: Mutual Fund Flows and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 88 (2008), 299–322](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2007.07.001), find that over 1980–2003 investors direct money to funds holding stocks with lower future returns, providing a contrary horizon/sign caution.
- **(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 survival — FAIL for demonstrated effect.** Predictable subscriptions could cause repeated demand even in highly liquid names, but the edge must survive public disclosure delays and fund flow prediction error. Lou’s result is on a following-year horizon, not evidence of a post-2020 Dow-scale 1–3 month return premium. Public, stale holdings are a weak basis for knowing what positions a manager will use future subscriptions to buy.
- **(c) Power — FAIL.** At a cautiously planted +0.010 IC, simulated power is 17.6%; the MDE is 0.0399. There is no comparable paper IC for the specified monthly or quarterly public-availability test, so a larger assumed effect would be unjustified.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — FAIL for the staged inputs.** The same development-only SEC panel count is above. The SEC Form N-PORT data schema provides portfolio data, but the staged builder explicitly extracts only SPY, DIA, QQQ and Select Sector SPDR registrants, not open-end active mutual-fund complexes. Aggregate free industry flow series would not identify the specific fund’s holdings to multiply by its subscriptions; inferred TNA changes would also need contemporaneous fund returns, share-class continuity, and distributions handled correctly. Therefore it is not a ready stock-level pressure panel.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL by exclusion.** Lou’s expected flow-induced trading applied to each stock through its fund owners is precisely the common-owner flow network / flow-to-holdings class already screened in R5 D3 and the existing Round U fund-flow proposal. Recasting it as predicted rather than realized flows is an estimator change, not a new source of pressure.

## 3. Flow-chasing sentiment and subsequent reversal

**Candidate definition if it were eligible.** For each stock, aggregate disclosed mutual-fund ownership weights times public fund subscriptions over the latest available period; classify high positive flow-weighted exposure as retail-demand crowding. The fixed prediction is *negative* excess return over the next 1–3 months for the most flow-crowded Dow holdings. Form the signal with a Wardlaw-style return-clean flow measure where possible; do not use the stock’s contemporaneous return to construct the pressure proxy.

- **(a) Literature — PASS for flow-based sentiment, weak for the short horizon.** Frazzini and Lamont (2008, cited above) document that investors direct money toward funds holding stocks with low future returns over 1980–2003, consistent with a negative longer-run sentiment sign. Lou (2012, cited above) finds a positive expected flow-induced return component over the following year that later reverses, so the 1–3 month negative prediction is not directly licensed by his result. Wardlaw (2020, cited above) demonstrates that measurement choices can mechanically contaminate pressure and finds a negligible quarterly decline/no reversal using cleaner flow instruments.
- **(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 survival — FAIL as documented.** Retail fund allocation can reach large companies through broad active funds, but those issuers’ valuation and order flow are closely followed. The literature cited here uses older samples and does not establish that flow-chasing creates a tradable negative 1–3 month Dow premium after 2020. The strongest post-2000 critique is measurement sensitivity, not persistence.
- **(c) Power — FAIL.** After fixing the contrarian direction as a positive IC on the sign-reversed signal, a planted +0.020 gives power 35.6%, with MDE 0.0399 under the 23-update design. This is optimistic and does not compensate for no post-2020 mega-cap effect estimate.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — FAIL for the staged panel.** The SEC counts above are ETF-only and include no categorized retail/active-fund flow record. N-PORT ownership cannot distinguish retail-motivated demand from institutional flows; public industry totals lack fund-to-stock attribution. Free data therefore do not supply the required stock-level sentiment × holding measure in the current workspace.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL by exclusion.** This retains the same flow-weighted fund ownership mechanism rejected in R5 D3/Round U. Reversing the sign and timing relative to Lou’s flow-induced continuation does not create a new pressure source, and it risks reintroducing the flow-induced return measurement problem Wardlaw identifies.

## Final decision

All three are literature-motivated, but none is eligible to advance: power is low at realistic quarterly public-data cadence; the cheap local coverage check found only 12 ETF series and no active mutual-fund flows; and all three preserve the already-tested flow × common-holdings architecture. I recommend **no freeze and no return test**. Reconsider only if a new, timely, free active-fund flow/holdings source becomes available and a coordinator decision explicitly clears the novelty conflict; until then, stop this family rather than relabeling another R5/R6 flow test.
