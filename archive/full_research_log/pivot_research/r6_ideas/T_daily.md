# Round T: short-horizon daily pressure ideas

**Scope.** This is a literature and input-feasibility screen only. I used no returns, signals, predictive results, or portfolio outcomes, and did not access any holdout data. No variants were used. I rank the mechanisms by the *potential documented edge*, not by how easy the data would be to obtain; the full R6 pass probabilities are low because costs, statistical power, and the fixed criteria bind.

## Ranking

| Rank | Candidate | Expected edge case | Gate verdict | Judgmental chance of passing all R6 criteria |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Same-time intraday return continuation at a one-day lag | Published half-hour continuation spans many trading days, but the paper itself says a simple arbitrage strategy loses after bid–ask spread. | **FAIL-GATE: no full-window free intraday data and no daily-cost case** | 1% |
| 2 | Signed closing-auction imbalance as temporary price pressure | Direct order-pressure evidence and a modern auction mechanism; the imbalance series needed for the test is not freely staged across development dates. | **FAIL-GATE: required free-data coverage** | <1% |
| 3 | Finance-forum net bullishness as retail buying pressure | Published sentiment results support next-day continuation in a broad sample, but the source panel is subscription-based and this is too close to the already rejected retail-attention family. | **FAIL-GATE: excluded family and no free/staged data** | 0% |

These are not predictions of measured Dow performance. The reported probabilities are subjective estimates of clearing the entire R6 stack, including a valid mechanism test, net Dow result versus EW30, multiplicity correction, turnover/cost rules, and the later holdout.

## Common design-only power and turnover calculation

The point-in-time S&P SIC2 panel has 53 SIC2 groups and 503 price-covered names on 2026-10-30. For a conservative common planning comparison, I treated one observation every five sessions as the unit, giving 297 dates over the development span, horizon 5 (Newey–West lag 4), and AR(1) dependence 0.20. To allow dependence inside SIC2, I set the simulated date-level IC standard deviation to `sqrt((1 + (503/53 − 1) × 0.15)/(53 − 1)) = 0.2089`, using 0.15 as an explicit within-group correlation stress assumption. This is not estimated from returns. With 5,000 synthetic draws, planted IC 0.0183, and seed 20261114, `r4_harness/power_sim.py` reports power **26.38%** (Monte Carlo SE 0.00623), effective N 216, and 80%-power MDE **0.04043**. The planted IC is an R6 comparison assumption, not a literature estimate for any of the three signals. An event-only signal would have fewer effective dates and lower power.

For trading-cost scale only, I used `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` with top fraction 5/30 and 0.05% per side. At a 50% **total traded notional** cap, an assumed daily cross-sectional return dispersion of 2% gives cost break-even IC 0.00866; meeting the goal's costs-below-one-quarter-of-gross-edge condition requires about 0.03464. For a five-session holding-period dispersion assumed at 4.5%, these values are 0.00385 and 0.01540. These dispersions are planning assumptions, not measured from the project. The same cap costs 2.5 bp of NAV per rebalance: daily trading at the cap would cost about 6.3% per year at 252 sessions; five-session rebalancing would cost about 1.26% per year at roughly 50 rebalances. A strategy that cannot operate at meaningfully lower turnover or produce gross edge well above these amounts is not credible under the fixed costs.

## T1 — Same-time intraday return continuation, daily lag

**Proposed question.** Does a stock that earns a high return in a particular 30-minute interval tend to earn a high return in that same interval on the next trading day, because institutional demand recurs at a predictable time? The signal is the preceding session's point-in-time relative return in a fixed 30-minute slot; the primary outcome would be the next session's return in the same slot. The original paper uses the half-hour interval, not daily close-to-close momentum. A portfolio implementation would need to rebalance at that intraday time, so it cannot honestly be represented as a once-daily signal traded at the next open.

- **(a) Verified literature — partial pass.** Heston, Korajczyk and Sadka, [“Intraday Patterns in the Cross-section of Stock Returns,” *Journal of Finance* 65 (2010), 1369–1407](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x), document cross-sectional continuation at same half-hour intervals separated by whole trading days, lasting up to 40 trading days. Their evidence includes recurring volume and imbalance patterns, but those do not explain the returns. Their earlier public paper reports a top-minus-bottom decile return difference of about 3.11 bp in the matching interval on the next day, which is small at a single interval. Heston, Korajczyk, Sadka and Thorson, [“Are You Trading Predictably?” *Financial Analysts Journal* 67 (2011), 36–44](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v67.n2.6), reproduce the daily same-slot continuation over the post-decimalization period and emphasize execution timing. The 2011 paper says its practical implication is improved execution timing; the 2010 study's trading implementation loses money after bid–ask spreads. These are verified, related papers, not independent proof of a net alpha for a daily long-only Dow portfolio.
- **(b) Mega-cap and post-2020 case — fail/weak.** The periodicity appears across stocks, and larger, more liquid names make interval timing operationally easier. But the main data are from 2001–2005 and the follow-on evidence is pre-2020. Neither paper shows persistence in Dow mega-caps after 2020. More critically, more liquidity makes a 3.11 bp interval effect easier to trade but also leaves little margin over spread, commissions, slippage, and market impact; the source paper's own cost conclusion is adverse.
- **(c) Power — fail at the planning benchmark.** The common SIC2-cluster simulation above is deliberately an upper-bound approximation for an intraday design: 26.38% power at IC 0.0183, MDE 0.04043. The literature reports interval portfolio spreads, not the cross-sectional rank IC needed to calibrate power. Its 3.11 bp next-day interval spread is not convertible to an IC without interval return dispersion, exact portfolio mapping, and trade costs. The effect could be smaller than the benchmark. At most, the simulation says a materially larger effect could be detected; it does not imply adequate power at the published scale.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — fail.** I checked staged panel schemas and development-only coverage: `r6_data/sp500_ohlcv_dev.parquet` has 774,939 daily ticker-date rows with only date, ticker, open, high, low, close, adjusted close, and volume; the companion `r6_C1/sp500_components_dev.parquet` has 716,987 rows and only daily open/close-derived fields. Neither contains half-hour observations. The free Yahoo/yfinance interface allows intraday intervals but caps requests at the most recent 60 days; its official downloader documentation says “Intraday data cannot extend last 60 days” ([yfinance source](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/blob/main/yfinance/multi.py)). That cannot reconstruct 2020–2026. No intraday values were analyzed or retained. A six-year, point-in-time S&P and Dow intraday panel would require a licensed source or prospective collection, not a free data path already staged.
- **(e) Novelty — signal is distinct; execution claim fails.** This is not P1's overnight return signal or the project's CLV/signed-volume score: it is a repeated same-time 30-minute return pattern. The genuine open question is whether a predeclared daily-lag signal persists in the post-2020 mega-cap sample *after slot-level costs*. It must not be diluted into ordinary daily momentum or rescued by trying many slots and holding periods. Under the available free-data rule, this distinct question cannot be tested.

**Gate result: FAIL-GATE.** The best documented pattern among these candidates is not a viable free-data daily portfolio: data are unavailable for the full window, the effect is measured in basis points per half-hour interval, and published cost evidence is negative.

## T2 — Signed closing-auction imbalance as daily pressure reversal

**Proposed question.** Does a signed buy imbalance that remains in the closing auction predict a negative next-session return, as liquidity pressure temporarily pushes the closing price away from value and subsequently reverses? Use the final observable closing-imbalance snapshot or a predeclared signed imbalance summary, not an unsigned daily-volume proxy. Form the signal after the close and enter no earlier than the next open; measure next-open-to-close return so the overnight movement is not incorrectly credited to a strategy that could not trade at the prior close. Exclude month-end and option-expiration dates from the primary signal so this does not retest the already screened C2/C3 cash-demand and expiration events.

- **(a) Verified literature — pass for mechanism, not current investability.** Cushing and Madhavan, [“Stock Returns and Trading at the Close,” *Journal of Financial Markets* 3 (2000), 45–67](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1386-4181(99)00012-9), analyze NYSE market-on-close imbalance indications and find return reversals after published imbalances, including next-day reversal, consistent with transient liquidity pressure. Bogousslavsky and Muravyev, [“Who Trades at the Close? Implications for Price Discovery and Liquidity,” working-paper version](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3485840), study the new high-volume closing-auction regime. They report that auction price deviations revert quickly and almost completely on average, and link auction turnover to indexing and passive ownership. Their study also cautions that closing-auction deviations are typically small; their result does not establish a reliably signed, cross-sectional Dow premium. The evidence concerns direct auction information, not a pressure value inferable from daily candles.
- **(b) Mega-cap and post-2020 case — plausible mechanism, unproven return edge.** The auction has become a large share of U.S. trading, and benchmarked institutions concentrate orders near the close. NYSE's 2023 description notes its imbalance feed reports buy/sell imbalance, paired quantity, and indicative clearing prices minute by minute near the close. This can make transient imbalance plausible even in mega-caps. Yet the recent evidence mainly supports more efficient matching and fast correction; it does not show a durable post-2020 next-session return anomaly for the Dow. The opportunity would depend on the imbalance still being predictive after the market digests the published feed.
- **(c) Power — fail/unknown at plausible edge.** The common S&P SIC2 simulation above has 26.38% power at planted IC 0.0183 and 80%-MDE 0.04043 for 297 nonoverlapping five-session observations. The papers do not give a Dow-relevant rank IC or return dispersion for the entry point proposed here. Auction distortions are described as small and quickly reverting, so no credible mapping supports a planted IC near the MDE. Actual daily event samples and SIC2 dependence would make power worse. A full 1-day rolling simulation with horizon 1 would have NW lag zero in the supplied harness and would be a poor basis for claiming power under serial dependence; hence I use the conservative nonoverlap calculation rather than that misleading daily count.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — fail for full development history.** The staged S&P OHLCV has 774,939 ticker-date rows including daily total volume, not an auction-only volume split or signed imbalance. NYSE's free public tool exposes closing-auction history for the 1,000 largest daily auctions over a **trailing three-month** window, with minute imbalance and paired-quantity charts ([NYSE free tool description](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/nyse-introduces-closing-auction-imbalance-analysis-tool)). NYSE identifies historical TAQ products as the source for full historical auction and imbalance records, not a free six-year archive ([NYSE historical data catalog](https://www.nyse.com/market-data/historical)). The development window spans nearly six years; the free viewer does not cover it. A daily close-volume proxy from Yahoo is not signed imbalance and cannot be substituted after seeing results. I checked availability descriptions and staged schemas only; no auction signal values or returns were downloaded or calculated.
- **(e) Novelty — partial pass, but not a rescue.** A signed pre-close exchange imbalance is different from J1's SIC2-relative weekly reversal and from C2/C3's calendar-specific flow shocks, provided month-end, option-expiration, and index-reconstitution event dates are excluded. The test would ask about a direct order-pressure signal with a next-session reversal sign, not generic reversal. However, an unsigned auction-volume ratio is not the same signal and would overlap the rejected volume family; it cannot stand in for the unavailable imbalance feed.

**Gate result: FAIL-GATE.** Mechanism is well motivated, but the signal needed for a clean post-2020 Dow test is not available free across the required dates, and the cited price deviations may be too small to survive costs.

## T3 — Daily net-bullish retail message pressure

**Proposed question.** Does a point-in-time increase in net bullish finance-forum messages indicate retail buying demand that carries into the next session? A single frozen daily signal would be the number of bullish minus bearish ticker-tagged messages, divided by all non-neutral messages, with the same-day message cut-off fixed before the next open. It would not use message volume alone as an attention proxy.

- **(a) Verified literature — mixed.** Barber and Odean, [“All That Glitters: The Effect of Attention and News on the Buying Behavior of Individual and Institutional Investors,” *Review of Financial Studies* 21 (2008), 785–818](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhm079), show that individual investors are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks, but their results are about brokerage trades and attention, not a free message polarity series. Boehmer, Jones, Zhang and Zhang, [“Tracking Retail Investor Activity,” *Journal of Finance* 76 (2021), 2249–2305](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13033), find that marketable retail buy imbalance predicts about 10 bp of next-week spread, but they infer retail trades from transaction-level data; message sentiment is not the same measure. Cookson, Lu, Mullins and Niessner, [“The Social Signal,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 158 (2024), 103870](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2024.103870), use daily data across StockTwits, Twitter and Seeking Alpha and find sentiment predicts positive next-day returns while attention predicts negative returns. They also find net retail buying is associated with same-day social sentiment/attention, with the link lasting only one to two days. This is the strongest current mechanism precedent, but the authors say their platform and financial data were collected through agreements and institutional subscriptions, not a public free archive ([replication package description](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/xffyybvw4j/1)).
- **(b) Mega-cap and post-2020 case — partial but weak.** The Social Signal sample spans 2012–2021 and includes large U.S. firms, covering the start of the retail-trading surge. It provides evidence that a finance-platform sentiment measure can predict next-day returns after standard controls. It does not report a separate post-2020 Dow mega-cap effect, and the period includes the exceptional GameStop episode. Its results also show platform-specific sentiment is not interchangeable, so a generic Reddit/Google proxy cannot inherit the StockTwits result.
- **(c) Power — fail at the planning benchmark.** The same S&P SIC2-cluster power calculation gives 26.38% at IC 0.0183 and an 80%-MDE of 0.04043. A message-event design would have fewer than 297 useful five-day blocks once zero-message days, low-count names, and event clustering are accounted for. The Social Signal paper's day-ahead coefficient is not provided as a comparable rank IC in the sources reviewed, and the 10 bp retail-trade spread from Boehmer et al. cannot be converted to rank IC without universe dispersion and portfolio construction. The effect therefore cannot be shown to exceed the power threshold or daily cost hurdle ex ante.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — fail.** I inventoried staged `r6_data` directories and the free/staged panels: no historical StockTwits, Twitter/X, or Seeking Alpha message-level or ticker-day sentiment file is present. The 2024 replication package expressly says the underlying StockTwits, Context Analytics and Ravenpack data require agreements/subscriptions. Free web pages or live feeds cannot reconstruct complete point-in-time 2020–2026 message counts and labels without a stable archive. No message values were downloaded.
- **(e) Novelty — signal-level distinction, but family excluded.** Net bullish-minus-bearish content is different as a measure from P3's MAX return proxy and the S4–S6 Wikipedia/pageview attention count. Nevertheless it is still the rejected retail-attention family and the project has already screened the same general hypothesis of social/attention pressure. The explicit “no earlier rejections” rule means I cannot re-label it as an eligible new direction. It would also be an information/sentiment predictor as much as a pressure measure unless buy-side demand is independently validated.

**Gate result: FAIL-GATE / excluded.** The mechanism has modern broad-market support, but it requires paid/staged data and is not eligible as a fresh R6 idea under the exclusion rule.

## Overall conclusion

No candidate passes all gates. The intraday periodicity effect is potentially the strongest documented daily continuation pattern, but its published return increment is only a few basis points per slot and the source itself warns it does not cover spreads; no full-window free intraday panel is staged. Signed auction imbalances have a clean short-horizon reversal rationale, but NYSE's free history is only a trailing three-month display, while the longer historical feed is proprietary. Direct retail sentiment is an arguably distinct observable, but it overlaps the previously rejected retail-attention direction and its principal data are not free. Under the fixed-cost and data constraints, do not freeze any of these or spend a variant.
