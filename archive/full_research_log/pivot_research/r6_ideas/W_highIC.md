# Round W — high documented price-pressure effects

**Verdict: no new, eligible candidate clears the requested screen.** I checked recent journal evidence for effects large enough to matter against the independent criterion-4 audit, then removed any direction already represented in R5/R6. The nearest recent papers are promising mechanism studies, but their high effects either belong to already-screened ownership/index families or are not stock-return rank ICs that can be mapped to a low-turnover Dow portfolio. No signal, forward return, portfolio return, or holdout value was read or calculated.

## Criterion-4 scale and ranking

The independent audit estimates an optimistic top-five Dow versus EW30 requirement of about **0.083–0.089 monthly rank IC**, before realistic dependence penalties. The requested 0.05 search target is therefore a useful minimum literature screen, not a sufficient portfolio hurdle. Percent price impact, annual long-short factor returns, risk premia, return variance explained, and rank IC are different quantities; I do not convert one into another without a reported mapping.

| Literature lead rank (conditional edge, before novelty gate) | Direction | Documented magnitude relevant to pressure | Full gate verdict | Chance of full-goal pass |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Dynamic short- versus long-run investor demand elasticity | JFE 2026 reports price impacts up to three times larger at quarterly than long-run horizons; **no comparable portfolio spread or rank IC** is reported in the abstract. | **FAIL-GATE**: close to P4/U ownership-demand work; no timely free PIT investor-trade panel. | <1% |
| 2 | Mutual-fund fire-sale exposure / pressure risk | JFE 2023 reports a high-minus-low long-short exposure return of 3–7% annually; this is a risk premium, not a monthly IC, and is already in U_fundflow. | **FAIL-GATE**: exact common-owner/fund-flow family already screened. | 0% eligible under novelty rule |
| 3 | Benchmark-driven correlated institutional demand shocks | RFS 2026 identifies a risk-premium/beta mechanism using index-membership quasi-experiments; no eligible pressure IC is reported, and index-change pressure is excluded. | **FAIL-GATE**: index-membership/passive-demand overlap; no new signal. | 0% eligible under novelty rule |

These probabilities are judgment calls conditional on all fixed criteria, not fitted probabilities. The ordering reflects the papers' mechanism evidence, not data convenience.

## Synthetic power screen

I used `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with S&P-sized (N=503), assumed IC SD (1/\sqrt{502}=0.04464) for monthly observations, or 0.06730 for the quarterly sensitivity (a planning SD adjusted for SIC2 clustering using an assumed within-group correlation of 0.15). Dates are 72 monthly observations, horizon 21 / Newey–West lag 20, for a six-year monthly design; and 23 non-overlapping quarterly observations, horizon 1 / lag 0, for delayed holdings data. Signal autocorrelation is set to 0.20. These are synthetic Gaussian date-level experiments; neither the SD adjustment nor the planted effect is measured from this project.

| Synthetic case | Planted mean IC | Probability NW t ≥ 2 | Simulated 80% MDE | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Monthly, 72 observations | 0.020 | 0.9054 | 0.0181 | The mechanism test is powered under the simulator's assumptions. |
| Monthly, 72 observations | 0.050 | 1.0000 | 0.0181 | High detection power does **not** clear the 0.083–0.089 portfolio bound. |
| Quarterly, 23 observations | 0.020 | 0.3560 | 0.0399 | Weak for a cautious, unsupported effect. |
| Quarterly, 23 observations | 0.050 | 0.9074 | 0.0399 | Powered only after assuming a large IC not established by the cited papers. |
| Monthly, 72 observations | 0.083 | 1.0000 | 0.0181 | Artificially planting the criterion-4 bound makes the mechanism test nearly certain by construction. |
| Quarterly, 23 observations | 0.083 | 0.9996 | 0.0399 | Same conditional point; this is not evidence that such an effect exists. |

Command pattern: `python r4_harness/power_sim.py --assets 503 --dates 72 --horizon 21 --signal-autocorrelation 0.2 --mean-ic 0.02 --simulations 5000 --seed 20261114` (quarterly runs replace dates/horizon with `23`/`1` and pass `--ic-sd 0.0673`). Seeds for the runs were 20261111–20261117. The simulator does not check Holm-adjusted portfolio power, transactions costs, daily signal sparsity, or whether the assumed IC can be obtained from these mechanisms.

## 1. Dynamic investor demand elasticity — fail gate

**Candidate considered, not advanced.** The possible pressure concept would estimate an issuer's near-term price response to disclosed institutional share changes and distinguish temporary impact from later accommodation. It would test whether a large short-horizon demand impact is followed by reversal, not treat static institutional ownership as a buy signal.

- **(a) Literature — strong mechanism, insufficient effect-size evidence.** Van der Beck, [“Short versus long-run demand elasticities in asset pricing,” *Journal of Financial Economics* (2026), DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2026.104337](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2026.104337), estimates a dynamic demand system using investor trades and reports price impacts as much as three times larger at quarterly horizons than in long-run equilibrium; it proposes a stock-level long-term-reversal measure. Koijen and Yogo, [“A Demand System Approach to Asset Pricing,” *Journal of Political Economy* 127 (2019), 1475–1515](https://doi.org/10.1086/701683), provide the holdings-based demand-system foundation and show how heterogeneous institutional/household demand relates to asset movements and predictability. Neither abstract supplies a comparable monthly rank IC at or above 0.05 for a free-data Dow book. The published magnitude is elasticity/price-impact scale, not return alpha.
- **(b) Large caps and post-2010 — plausible, not demonstrated at the needed scale.** The mechanism is not size-specific: portfolio adjustment by institutions can affect mega-cap prices. Large stocks also have deep liquidity and more arbitrage, and the JFE abstract's dynamic elasticity estimate is not a post-2020 mega-cap return signal. The literature does not establish a Dow monthly rank IC of 0.083–0.089.
- **(c) Power — conditional only.** For a hypothetical monthly signal, the synthetic power cases above give 90.5% power at IC 0.02 and 100% at 0.05 for 72 S&P dates. With quarterly public holdings timing, 23 non-overlapping observations yield 35.6% power at 0.02 and 90.7% at 0.05. These calculations show sampling precision conditional on a planted IC, not evidence that the price-impact ratio implies either IC.
- **(d) Free-data feasibility — partial availability, fail for the required signal.** The project has development-window daily OHLCV and a selected 13F PIT position panel. An input-only scan of `r4_L4/data/13f_development_pit_positions.csv`, keeping both report date and filing availability date within 2020-11-27–2026-10-30, found 223,932 position rows, 142 managers, 33 tickers, 23 report dates and 274 availability dates. This selected panel is not a comprehensive investor-trade history and updates quarterly; the need to avoid using a holding before its filing date further reduces timeliness. No transaction-level investor trade or dynamic-demand-system dataset is staged. The free inputs cannot reproduce the paper's design as written.
- **(e) Novelty — fail.** This is a re-estimation of holdings-based investor demand/flow pressure already screened in P4, U_fundflow and related fund-flow/common-owner proposals. Calling the estimator “short-run elasticity” would not supply a new source of signed buying pressure. Do not start another variant.

## 2. Fire-sale exposure — fail gate

- **(a) Literature — documented return spread, but not the target statistic.** Aragon and Kim, [“Fire Sale Risk and Expected Stock Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 149 (2023), 578–609](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2023.06.006), measure stocks' links to funds anticipating systematic outflows and report a 3–7% annual high-minus-low long-short return. Coval and Stafford, [“Asset Fire Sales (and Purchases) in Equity Markets,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 86 (2007), 479–512](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2006.09.007), document flow-induced trades and price pressure in overlapping fund holdings. Wardlaw, [“Measuring Mutual Fund Flow Pressure as Shock to Stock Returns,” *Journal of Finance* 75 (2020), 3221–3243](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12962), shows that common flow-pressure constructions mechanically incorporate realized stock returns unless corrected, and finds a negligible quarterly decline without subsequent reversal under a cleaner measure. The 3–7% annual factor spread is not a rank IC or a positive buying-pressure continuation estimate, so it cannot be claimed to reach the criterion-4 bound.
- **(b) Large caps/post-2010 — mechanism can affect large names, magnitude not isolated.** A systematic mutual-fund outflow can include large firms, but the article's stated estimate is not a post-2020 Dow-only effect or an identified low-turnover long-only result. The main channel prices bearing fire-sale risk; it is not necessarily a temporary pressure shock that persists in the predicted direction.
- **(c) Power — quarterly dependence is a limitation.** For the 23-quarter synthetic case the tool finds 35.6% power at IC 0.02 and 90.7% at 0.05 (NW lag 0). Both effects are planning inputs, not measured conversions from the paper's long-short premium. The portfolio criterion is stricter than an NW mechanism t-test.
- **(d) Free-data feasibility — fail as a new signal.** The same input-only 13F position panel has 142 managers but only 33 distinct tickers after the development-only date filter. It is a selected extract, not the broad manager/fund flow universe needed to estimate systematic outflows and security-level exposure. Filings become usable only on availability dates; no trade timestamps are present. Free daily OHLCV does not repair the missing pressure/flow panel.
- **(e) Novelty — fail.** The mechanism is already explicitly covered by `r6_ideas/U_fundflow.md` and the related R5 common-ownership/fund-flow work. It is not eligible for a new R6 direction even if a better data panel were acquired.

## 3. Correlated benchmark-driven demand — fail gate

- **(a) Literature — recent, causal channel; no eligible rank-IC magnitude.** Kim, [“Correlated Demand Shocks and Asset Pricing,” *Review of Financial Studies* (published 28 October 2026), DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhag094](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhag094), argues that benchmark-relative institutional mandates induce procyclical risk-taking and correlated demand, increasing exposed-stock betas and risk premia; the paper uses index-membership quasi-experiments. Aragon and Kim (2023, above) independently show that constrained owners' correlated outflows can affect stock exposure and expected returns. These are risk-premium and ownership mechanisms, not documentation of a 0.05 or larger monthly stock-level buying-pressure rank IC. Kim's paper supports the broad possibility of demand spreading, but it does not specify an eligible Dow price-pressure trade.
- **(b) Large caps/post-2010 — likely relevant but not a tradable post-2020 effect estimate.** Benchmark mandates clearly include large firms, so this channel plausibly applies in mega-caps. The causal result uses membership changes and concerns conditional betas/risk premia. It does not establish a post-2020 monthly signal with sufficient net returns; those membership events are also disallowed in this search.
- **(c) Power — same monthly synthetic case, not empirical validation.** At a hypothetical monthly IC 0.02 the simulator reports 90.5% chance of NW t ≥ 2 and MDE 0.0181; at 0.05 it reports 100%. Even the latter sits below criterion 4's approximate 0.083–0.089 IC scale, and power cannot overcome the excluded event family.
- **(d) Free-data feasibility — fail for a distinct direct measure.** The development panel includes PIT S&P membership and daily prices, but membership changes are explicitly off-limits and do not identify order quantities. The selected 13F panel counts are above; it cannot measure all mandate-driven trades. A non-index event alternative would need a new pressure series not found in the staged files.
- **(e) Novelty — fail by exclusion.** This is the index-membership/passive and institutional benchmark-demand family already screened in `r6_ideas/P4.md`, `r6_ideas/U_fundflow.md`, and R5's index-change direction. Omitting the event date but retaining exposure to benchmark-driven flow would still be the same core mechanism; it is not a genuinely new variant.

## Cheap feasibility check and disposition

The file-only/input-only check confirms daily S&P/Dow development OHLCV and the limited 13F panel summarized above. The local development OHLCV is daily, not intraday TAQ; no historical 30-minute bars or intermediary inventories are staged. Thus a tempting very-high-effect alternative, residual reversal from 30-minute price-pressure data, is infeasible from the allowed free inputs and in any event overlaps the already screened short-horizon reversal family. I did not fetch or examine stock price values for this check.

**Final rank among eligible new ideas: none.** All three closest recent literatures fail novelty; the first two also fail the staged free-data requirement, and none documents a comparable large-cap rank IC at the fixed criterion-4 level. The correct outcome is not to add a portfolio wrapper or re-label a previously screened source. No new R6 variant is recommended.

### Reproducibility and scope

- Power simulations use only synthetic assumptions; no project prices, returns, signals, portfolio outcomes or holdout information entered them.
- The 13F counts used only identifiers and report/availability dates inside the development window; holdings quantities and value fields were not used in analysis.
- Input sources checked: `r6_data/QA.md`, `r4_L4/data/13f_development_pit_positions.csv`, R5 final, and the existing R6 idea inventory. No sealed files were opened.
