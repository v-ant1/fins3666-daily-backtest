# R6 literature screen: large-cap pressure effects after 2010

**Screen date:** 11 November 2026  
**Decision:** **No eligible candidate clears the requested screen.** The nearest quantified effects are well below the project’s monthly SIC-cluster MDE of about 0.02 in rank IC, rely on paid TAQ data, fail the large-cap persistence test, or overlap a direction already tried in R5/R6. The ranking below is a diagnostic ordering of near-misses by the best comparable effect-to-MDE approximation; it is not a shortlist or approval to test.

## Screen and conversion rule

The comparison target is the existing synthetic planning value in [`r4_harness/POWER_SIM.md`](../r4_harness/POWER_SIM.md): approximate monthly SIC-cluster MDE = 0.02 rank IC. This is a scale comparison, not a statistical power estimate for any paper’s original sample. Where a paper reports an IQR return spread rather than a rank correlation, I use

`approximate rank IC = reported IQR return spread / (1.349 × cross-sectional return SD)`

under a linear relation to normal-score ranks. For weekly results I scale Hou, Xue and Zhang’s 8.84% monthly cross-sectional SD for NYSE-big stocks by `sqrt(4.33)`, giving 4.25% weekly. The mapping is rough: “big” in that paper means above the NYSE median, not the top NYSE quintile, and return distributions are not normal. Ratios are shown as `|approximate IC| / 0.02`; they must not be read as model power. Hou et al. report the big-stock dispersion and define “big” as above NYSE median in [*Replicating Anomalies*](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhy131).

## Ranked untried near-misses

### Reading the ranking

The order-flow-volatility and repurchase estimates do not document positive large-cap continuation. OFI cross-impact has the best network fit among the untried directions, but its published effect cannot be put on the monthly IC/MDE scale and its data are paid. None is a defensible R6 proposal.

### Previously tried family excluded from the ranking

Marketable retail order imbalance is a useful quantitative comparator but is not ranked as an untried candidate. In Boehmer, Jones, Zhang & Zhang (2021), the largest market-cap tercile’s IQR return spread is just 2.6 bp/week (t=3.68), versus 21.9 bp/week among small firms. Using weekly SD 4.25%, a rough rank-IC interval is about 0.0035–0.0045, or 0.18–0.23× the 0.02 MDE; this is not a monthly rank IC, and an unchanged four-week extrapolation is unsupported for large stocks. The implementation requires licensed TAQ data, and the mechanism overlaps R6’s already-screened retail-flow (D2/OTC proxy) family. Sources: [paper PDF, Table IV and text](https://www.pbcsf.tsinghua.edu.cn/__local/4/54/C2/14F447AB4BFAB3483114AC602DA_F576AF2B_107C91.pdf?e=.pdf), [DOI 10.1111/jofi.13033](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13033).

## Other close matches screened out

- **Marketable retail order flow:** excluded from the ranking because it belongs to the already-screened R6 retail-flow family; the large-stock estimate is only 2.6 bp/week, and the TAQ data are not free. Detail and source links are above.
- **Cross-asset limit-order-book OFI:** Cont, Cucuringu & Zhang (2023) study lagged cross-impact with Nasdaq ITCH/LOBSTER data for the top 100 S&P 500 firms in 2017–2019 and report short-horizon forecast improvement that decays rapidly. This is the closest *network* match, but the paper does not provide a weekly/monthly rank IC or a monthly long-short Sharpe that can be converted reliably to the 0.02 MDE; ITCH/LOBSTER historical order-book data are not free. It fails the requested horizon, magnitude and free-data screens. [Paper and DOI 10.1080/14697688.2023.2236159](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2023.2236159); [full-text data description](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2023.2236159).
- **Closing-auction imbalance / passive-investor price pressure:** reported effects can be sizable, but the documented strategy is predominantly a temporary imbalance reversal around index/passive close trading. That overlaps the project’s excluded ETF-flow/index-pressure and reversal families; current imbalance feeds are exchange proprietary and a free historical S&P panel was not verified. It is not a new candidate here. See [Wu & Jegadeesh, *Closing Auctions: Information Content and Timeliness of Price Reaction*](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3732955) and the NYSE’s [auction impact analysis](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/closing-auction-immediate-market-impact-price-drift-and-transaction-cost-of-trading).
- **Retail public trade-signing result:** excluded above because R6 D2 already covered the retail-flow family. The paper’s own Table IV makes the main limit plain: the IQR return spread falls to 2.6 bp/week in the largest market-cap tercile from 21.9 bp/week among small firms.

## Bottom line

No researched, untried direction satisfies all three requirements simultaneously: (1) a published post-2010 effect in large caps at weekly-to-monthly horizon with a comparable effect size at or above roughly 0.02 monthly rank IC; (2) free, usable inputs; and (3) a thesis-consistent mechanism not already tested in rounds A–L or the named R5 families. This search does not establish that no such effect exists; it establishes that the verified nearest matches do not justify spending another R6 variant under the specified screen. No project outcomes, development return data, or holdout data were accessed for this report.
