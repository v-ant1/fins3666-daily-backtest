# R6-V microstructure scan: SEC fails-to-deliver balances

**Verdict: FAIL-GATE; no direction is ready to advance.** I found one measure that is not separately named in the existing R6 idea files or `R5_FINAL.md`: the SEC’s semi-monthly fails-to-deliver (FTD) balance. It is the nearest untested free-data microstructure measure, but it is not a clean signed buying-pressure series, has mixed literature, and does not currently satisfy the thesis/network requirement. All other apparent candidates in this scan were already tested or screened in the R5/R6 documents; I do not relabel them as new ideas.

## Candidate V1 — FTD settlement balance pressure

**One fixed design if it were ever revisited:** at each SEC posting, measure the latest public FTD balance as a fraction of the stock’s trailing 20-session share volume, keeping absent rows as missing rather than treating them as zero. Test whether unusually high FTD pressure predicts a negative next-five-session return, consistent with informed short-sale pressure continuing; fix this sign and horizon before outcomes. A secondary market-neutral form could short high-pressure stocks against low-pressure stocks, but it would be a different portfolio construction and is not proposed here. The current SEC file cannot tell how long any fail has remained outstanding, so it cannot identify the 13-settlement-day threshold close-out cohort. No network diffusion claim is warranted from the current proposal; using SIC2 neighbors would simply graft an already-tested industry-link design onto an unverified FTD signal.

### Gates (a)–(e)

- **(a) Literature — PARTIAL / mixed.** Autore, Boulton and Braga-Alves, [“Failures to Deliver, Short Sale Constraints, and Stock Overvaluation,” *Financial Review* 50 (2015), 143–172](https://doi.org/10.1111/fire.12063), use publicly available FTDs and report overvaluation among stocks reaching threshold failure levels. Stratmann and Welborn, [“Informed Short Selling, Fails-to-Deliver, and Abnormal Returns,” *Journal of Empirical Finance* 38 (2016), 81–102](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2016.05.006), report negative abnormal returns proportional to FTDs and interpret the evidence as informed short selling continuing despite nonbinding constraints. But Fotak, Raman and Yadav, [“Fails-to-Deliver, Short Selling, and Market Quality,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 114 (2014), 493–516](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2014.07.012), find higher FTDs improve liquidity and pricing efficiency in 1,492 NYSE stocks over 2005–2008 and report no evidence FTDs caused price distortions in the financial crisis. These studies do not establish a stable sign or a post-2020 buying-pressure effect.
- **(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 case — FAIL.** FTDs can occur in large and liquid stocks because delivery failures can arise from both short and long sales, lending/recall timing, processing issues, and oversubscribed issuance. That keeps an operational-friction mechanism possible. But none of the cited papers establishes a post-2020 Dow mega-cap return effect, and deep liquidity plus centralized clearing can absorb or resolve transient failures rapidly. The SEC itself cautions that FTDs “may have little or no relationship” to the prior/next day’s balance and do not reveal fail age or cause. The historical findings cannot be assumed to survive in this universe.
- **(c) Power — CONDITIONAL PASS at an optimistic planning effect; practical inference remains weak.** I ran `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with the point-in-time S&P 500 cross-section (`N=503`), 53 SIC2 groups, 144 semi-monthly update dates over six years, a five-session horizon represented as one non-overlapping outcome per update (`horizon=1`), date-series signal autocorrelation 0.20, 5,000 draws, and seed `20261112`. I set the IC noise SD to 0.06730 using `sqrt((1 + (503/53 − 1)×0.15)/(503 − 1))`, which assumes within-SIC2 correlation 0.15; that correlation and noise SD are planning assumptions, not estimated from returns. At a planted absolute IC of 0.0183, `P(NW t ≥ 2)=0.8508` and the 80%-power MDE is 0.01594; at 0.010, power is 0.4360. This is conditional simulation only. The literature gives no matching post-2020 SIC2 rank IC to justify 0.0183, and because FTD data are sparse and the signal is an outstanding balance rather than a flow, this specification may materially overstate actual effective breadth. The power tool does not correct for security-level signal sparsity, tickers without valid CUSIP lineage, or repeated mechanical zeros.
- **(d) Free-data feasibility — PASS for availability, FAIL for a ready point-in-time signal.** The SEC’s official [Fails-to-Deliver Data page](https://www.sec.gov/data/foiadocsfailsdatahtm) provides files twice monthly: first-half data at month-end, second-half data about the 15th of the following month. I fetched only the development-period October 2026 first-half file (`cnsfails202610a.zip`) in memory and counted dates/tickers without reading quantity or price values. Its 9 settlement dates contained 51,410 rows and 12,750 unique symbols; matching symbol/date keys to the staged point-in-time S&P roster gave 461 S&P tickers with at least one row in this short sample. The SEC documentation says the quantity is a cumulative net balance (not new daily fails), zero balances are omitted, and the cause may be long or short sales. It also says it cannot guarantee accuracy or posting dates. This verifies a free input source, not full-window PIT coverage, a daily flow series, or Dow candidate density. Historical exact publication timestamps would need a conservative as-of rule; the assumed half-month release timing should not be mistaken for observed posting times.
- **(e) Novelty and thesis fit — novelty PARTIAL; thesis FAIL.** FTD balances are not the same field as FINRA twice-monthly short interest or daily short-sale volume, both of which are already present in R6; they record unsettled delivery obligations, not a reported short position or a daily trade flow. This gives a narrow data-definition novelty. But FTD is not intrinsically buying pressure, the SEC says it is not necessarily due to short selling, and a missing/zero row is not an observed zero-pressure reading. The five-session own-stock prediction is not a network spillover, and a SIC2 extension would reuse an already-tested peer architecture without a literature-backed transmission mechanism. The design therefore fails the thesis-fit part of this gate.

## Stop decision

**Do not freeze a variant or spend a test slot.** The FTD source is free and has enough broad ticker/date availability to justify an input audit, but available observations do not isolate signed buyer or seller pressure, persistent fails, or a linked-asset response. A subsequent project would need actual posting-date history, a verified zero/missing convention, a point-in-time persistent-threshold indicator, and an independently motivated peer transmission mechanism before its literature-calibrated power could be considered. Those additions are not demonstrated by this screen; no substitute was pulled and no outcome work is authorized here.

No signal values, stock prices, forward returns, rank ICs, strategy statistics, or holdout data were inspected or computed for this note.
