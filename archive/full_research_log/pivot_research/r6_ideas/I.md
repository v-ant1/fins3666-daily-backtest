# R6 Idea Gate — I: recurring calendar-month pressure

**Scope.** This is a literature and design review only. I did not read price bars, calculate signals, forward returns, project ICs, or outcomes, and I did not access holdout data. The power figures use only synthetic draws from `r4_harness/power_sim.py`; the cost numbers are formula-based planning assumptions.

## Ranking and decision

| Rank | Direction | Gate verdict now | Judgmental chance of passing the full R6 stack |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Own-stock same-calendar-month return pressure | **FAIL-GATE for outcome testing now** | 3% |
| 2 | Same-calendar-month SIC2 peer pressure | **FAIL-GATE for outcome testing now** | 1% |

These probabilities are subjective chances of clearing the complete fixed R6 sequence, including mechanism, placebo, net Dow performance, costs, multiplicity, and holdout—not Monte Carlo power. The own-stock direction ranks first because the literature directly documents its return pattern; the peer version fits the pressure-spreads-through-links thesis more closely but has weaker industry-specific evidence and lower effective breadth. Both fail the current power gate, and neither has evidence that its seasonal return signal is investable in Dow mega-caps after 2020.

## What history is allowed

**Pre-2020-06 Dow prices or signals are not allowed, even when they would be used only to form a lagged signal.** They belong to the sealed holdout. For a clean common S&P mechanism / Dow portfolio design, I would use only the registered development history beginning 2020-11-27, which is available for the Dow, and discard the incomplete November 2020 month. A fixed 12-month same-calendar-month lag first supports a complete forecast for **December 2021**: at the end of November 2021, the complete December 2020 return is known. The proposed monthly test therefore has **59 formation/outcome months, December 2021 through October 2026**. No pre-2020-06 observation is needed or permitted.

The papers use much longer histories—often five to twenty years. With the allowed panel, one can pre-register the single annual lag (12 months), but not honestly replicate a 5- or 20-year seasonal history. A full five-year same-month average would first be available for December 2025 and leaves only **11 monthly observations** through October 2026, too few to support the intended test. The shortened 12-month version is a transparent transport test, not a reproduction of the published long-history strategy.

## Shared monthly design and cost hurdle

For each forecast month, rank point-in-time S&P 500 or Dow members on a score fixed before that month's first trade. Use the adjusted total return for the **same calendar month one year earlier**, known at the prior month-end. The outcome window is the next full calendar month's adjusted return. Re-form the portfolio monthly. The Dow portfolio is long the top five scores against point-in-time EW30; once fully established, equal-weight top five has 83.3% active share versus EW30. Enforce a **20% cap on total traded notional / NAV per rebalance** (buys plus sells), with partial transitions if a target change would breach the cap. Actual turnover and active share would have to be disclosed in any preregistration; the cap can dilute the target score and does not guarantee the 50% active-share condition during transitions.

Using `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` with a **20% monthly total-turnover cap**, **5 bp per traded dollar**, an assumed **6% monthly cross-sectional return dispersion**, and top fraction 5/30 gives:

| Hurdle | Rank IC | Monthly active edge / costs |
|---|---:|---:|
| Costs break even | 0.001155 | 1 bp cost per month |
| Costs at most 25% of gross edge | **>0.004619** | >4 bp gross edge; 1 bp cost per month |

At 12 rebalance months, the cap bounds modeled transaction cost at 12 bp/year before any unmodeled spread or impact. The 6% dispersion is an assumption, not a statistic estimated from project outcomes. The cost-compatible IC is not a forecast of either paper's portfolio return: published long-short return spreads cannot be converted to this design's rank IC without additional data and assumptions.

### SIC2-aware synthetic power

The planned mechanism uses a date-wise rank IC with **59 non-overlapping monthly forecast months**. The stress gives the S&P effective cross-section one independent unit per minimum point-in-time SIC2 group (**53 groups**, IC SD `1/sqrt(53−1)=0.138675`) and the Dow one per minimum group (**18 groups**, IC SD `1/sqrt(18−1)=0.242536`). For the peer version, a SIC2 group is the natural unit because peer scores share a source; this makes the S&P group-level result an optimistic upper bound if multiple industries remain correlated.

I ran `r4_harness/power_sim.py` for 5,000 synthetic trials per row, a one-sided `NW t ≥ 2` rule, `horizon=1` monthly forecasts, and planted ICs at the 25%-of-gross cost boundary and the existing 0.0183 R6 comparison benchmark. Seeds are 20261111–20261114. Because `power_sim.py` maps `horizon=1` to NW lag 0, these results assume independent monthly test statistics; they do **not** penalize serial correlation between adjacent monthly ICs and should be read as optimistic. No project returns or ICs enter the simulation.

| Design / universe | SIC2 effective groups | IC SD stress | Power at IC 0.00462 | Power at IC 0.0183 | 80% MDE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Own-stock mechanism, S&P 500 | 53 | 0.138675 | 16.4% | 29.6% | 0.05130 |
| Own-stock portfolio-use stress, Dow 30 | 18 | 0.242536 | 14.7% | 21.8% | 0.08973 |
| Peer-pressure mechanism, S&P SIC2 groups | 53 | 0.138675 | 16.4% | 29.6% | 0.05130 |
| Peer-pressure portfolio-use stress, Dow SIC2 groups | 18 | 0.242536 | 14.7% | 21.8% | 0.08973 |

The peer rows reuse the same conservative one-unit-per-SIC2 calculation; the simulation does not create 500 independent peer observations. The own-stock rows use 500/30 nominal assets with the corresponding cluster IC-SD supplied explicitly. The common 80%-power MDE is more than 11 times the cost-compatible IC in the S&P design. Even the reference IC 0.0183 has only 29.6% S&P power and 21.8% Dow power. Power is therefore a **FAIL** for both designs at a plausible cost-compatible edge. The 13% annual strategy return reported by Keloharju et al. is not an IC estimate and cannot justify a larger planted IC here.

## 1. Own-stock same-calendar-month return pressure

**Hypothesis.** A firm's relative performance in a given calendar month recurs because recurring attention, mood, or trading demand leaves predictable one-month seasonal pressure. At the end of month `m−1`, score stock `i` by its market-relative adjusted return in calendar month `m` one year earlier; predict a positive cross-sectional return during calendar month `m`. The score is a return-based pressure proxy, not measured signed order flow.

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for recurring return predictability; PARTIAL for a flow mechanism.** Heston and Sadka, [“Seasonality in the Cross-Section of Stock Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 87 (2008), 418–445](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2007.02.003), report same-calendar-month winner continuation at annual lags up to 20 years, averaging more than 50 bp per month for their long-short portfolios, with robustness to size and industry controls. Keloharju, Linnainmaa, and Nyberg, [“Return Seasonalities,” *Journal of Finance* 71 (2016), 1557–1590](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12398), report 13% per year for a broad historical same-month strategy and document seasonalities in stocks, portfolios, anomalies, commodities, international indices, and daily returns. Their result is not proof of buying pressure: they argue seasonalities are intertwined with common factors. Keloharju et al., [“Are Return Seasonalities Due to Risk or Mispricing?” *Journal of Financial Economics* 139 (2021), 138–161](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.07.009), find same-month continuation offset by other-month seasonal reversals, consistent with temporary mispricing. Hirshleifer, Jiang, and Meng, [“Mood Beta and Seasonalities in Stock Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 137 (2020), 272–295](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.02.003), offer investor mood as one mechanism and discuss recurring trading/inflows as plausible explanations; neither paper directly measures fund flows for this proposed 12-month signal.
- **(b) Large-cap / post-2010 persistence — PARTIAL.** The seasonal pattern is not confined to microcaps: the 2021 JFE study covers U.S. returns through 2016 and reports results for all-but-microcap stocks and value-weighted industry portfolios; the 2020 mood study covers 1963–2016. These samples include 2010–2016 and support historical persistence outside the smallest firms. They do not establish survival in the 2020s, and neither isolates Dow mega-caps as the target universe. The one-month annual recurrence can also be offset by non-congruent months, making it unsafe to interpret a positive monthly signal as a persistent buy-and-hold premium.
- **(c) Power and cost — FAIL.** At the formula-based 0.00462 IC needed for costs to be at most 25% of gross edge, SIC2-aware S&P power is 16.4% and Dow power is 14.7%; the S&P 80%-power MDE is 0.05130. At the 0.0183 comparison benchmark, power is only 29.6% and 21.8%. The one-month cost calculation assumes 6% return dispersion and caps turnover at 20%; signal turnover and achieved active share remain unobserved. The main literature uses long histories and its reported portfolio returns cannot be substituted for the required IC hurdle.
- **(d) Free data — PASS for basic availability; limited history.** The development daily OHLCV and S&P point-in-time roster, Dow point-in-time roster, and dated SEC SIC metadata are available locally; daily adjusted prices can produce complete monthly returns without paid flow or holdings data. The Dow portfolio starts 2020-11-27, and a complete 12-month lag restricts the common test to December 2021–October 2026. Use the point-in-time roster, aliases, delisted firms, and corporate-action-adjusted prices; do not backfill signals with pre-2020-06 Dow history or use current constituents as historical membership.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL / low.** Same-calendar-month return continuation is the published result. A single one-year-lag, post-2020-only Dow/S&P transport test is clean and relevant to the project but is a short-sample replication, not a new pressure measure. Calling the return itself “buying pressure” would overstate what it measures.

## 2. Same-calendar-month SIC2 peer pressure

**Hypothesis.** A target stock's other SIC2 peers' relative returns during calendar month `m` one year ago predict the target's return during month `m` this year. At the end of month `m−1`, compute a leave-one-out mean of the peers' market-relative returns in the previous year's same calendar month; score target stocks on that peer signal and test a positive next-month rank IC. The network is point-in-time SIC2, and the target's own prior same-month return is excluded from its peer score. This asks whether a recurring pressure pattern transfers through linked firms, rather than merely replicating own-stock seasonality.

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PARTIAL.** Heston and Sadka (2008, above) show the own-stock return pattern remains after controlling for industry, so the effect is not reducible to a simple industry sort. Keloharju et al. (2016, above) document return seasonalities in well-diversified industry portfolios as well as individual stocks, which motivates—but does not test—a peer link. In their [NBER working paper, “Common Factors in Return Seasonalities” (No. 20815)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20815), the decomposition of one same-month stock strategy attributes only 0.12 percentage point of its 1.15% monthly return to the industry component, versus 1.03 points to the non-industry component. That is a warning that industry is a small part of the published effect. No cited paper establishes that a peer's prior-year same-month performance predicts a target firm's return one year later.
- **(b) Large-cap / post-2010 persistence — PARTIAL to weak.** The broad U.S. same-month pattern appears in samples extending through 2016 and in non-microcap and industry portfolios, but large-cap persistence does not establish a peer-to-peer mechanism. The industry component in the NBER decomposition is modest, and same-industry names share news and common risk exposure; either can create correlation without pressure spillover. No verified study shows this particular peer signal survives in Dow mega-caps after 2020.
- **(c) Power and cost — FAIL, likely more optimistic than reality.** If one treats the minimum 53 S&P SIC2 groups as independent sources and uses 59 monthly dates, `power_sim.py` gives 16.4% power at IC 0.00462, 29.6% at IC 0.0183, and an 80%-power MDE of 0.05130. The Dow has only 18 minimum SIC2 groups and corresponding power of 14.7% / 21.8%, with MDE 0.08973. These assume independent monthly ICs and therefore overstate power if peer signals or target returns co-move across SIC2 groups. The same 20%-turnover cost hurdle applies; reducing trading costs does not solve the lack of independent monthly observations.
- **(d) Free data — PASS for a narrow SIC2 construction; PARTIAL for point-in-time integrity.** The locally staged daily OHLCV and PIT membership panels plus dated SEC-derived SIC intervals are sufficient to calculate a lagged leave-one-out peer signal without paid data. SIC assignments must be known as of the formation date, ticker histories must remain point in time, and groups with too few independent peers must be excluded by a fixed eligibility rule. Some Dow SIC2 groups may be singletons or small, reducing the effective number of peer signals further. No price, signal, or outcome coverage has been measured for this memo.
- **(e) Novelty — PARTIAL.** A leave-one-out same-calendar-month SIC2 peer score is a distinct network extension, and explicitly excluding the target separates it from own-stock seasonality. The literature already shows seasonal returns in industry portfolios, so the novelty is only the cross-firm forecast direction and target-level test. It remains weak as a buying-pressure claim until a future pre-registered test distinguishes pressure transfer from common industry exposures.

## Recommendation

Do not advance either direction to outcome testing yet. If the user wants one candidate retained, keep the own-stock 12-month same-month signal as a tightly bounded replication; keep the peer extension as a secondary hypothesis only if its SIC2 eligibility and clustered power are fixed in advance. The compliant history rule is workable, but the resulting 59 monthly observations are far below the design's 80%-power MDE. Any future preregistration must keep the single one-year lag, monthly schedule, 20% total-turnover cap, fixed placebos, and one-month forecast horizon; it must not add older data, longer lag sets, or calendar-specific variants after seeing results.
