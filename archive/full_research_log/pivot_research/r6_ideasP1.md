# Round P1 — own-stock overnight return pressure

## Recommendation

**FAIL-GATE for a new outcome test under the current R6 criterion-4 screen.** The mechanism is genuinely distinct from J1's within-industry close-to-close reversal and has a strong historical large-stock result, but the published signal return is mostly an overnight/intraday tug-of-war, the evidence is not a post-2010-only mega-cap estimate, and a transparent transfer to a Dow top-five portfolio implies an excess Sharpe far below the required 0.8. This is a literature and synthetic-planning assessment only; I read no project outcome data and ran no real-data tests.

## Proposed single direction

At each month-end, calculate each point-in-time constituent's cumulative one-month (21 completed-session) overnight return, using only completed sessions:

`ON21_i,t = product_{d=t-20..t}(adjusted_open_i,d / adjusted_close_i,d-1) - 1`.

The score is the raw cross-sectional rank of `ON21`. At the next trading open, form an equal-weight long-only basket of the five highest-scoring Dow constituents; hold for 21 trading sessions and rebalance monthly. The primary mechanism would rank the point-in-time S&P 500 on the same score and test the next 21-session close-to-close return from the post-formation execution point, with SIC2-clustered uncertainty and a date-level time-series test. Given the signal timing, exclude the overnight move used to calculate the final signal from the traded return. Freeze one execution delay, one horizon, one portfolio and one endpoint before outcomes.

The 21-session lookback is fixed to the prior month to match the primary Lou et al. cross-sectional sort, not selected from alternatives. With the permitted daily data beginning June 2020, there are at most roughly 76 monthly formations through October 2026. The gap between signal end and execution is explicit; no pre-June-2020 data are needed.

## Gates (a)–(e)

**(a) Verified literature — PASS for mechanism, partial for the proposed full-period return.** Lou, Polk and Skouras sort stocks monthly on the preceding month's overnight return and report that the winner-minus-loser portfolio earns a 3.47% monthly three-factor alpha overnight (`t=16.83`) followed by a −3.02% monthly intraday alpha (`t=−9.74`). The two components imply only about +0.45% monthly combined close-to-close alpha before implementation costs; the paper explicitly cautions that costs reduce profitability. Their sample is 1993–2013. They exclude the bottom NYSE size quintile in this test, say results are robust to excluding small caps, and thus provide large-stock evidence, but not a clean post-2010-only estimate. The paper's documented **0.77 Sharpe is for the overnight leg of the conventional 12–2-month price-momentum factor**, not the long-short strategy sorted on past overnight return; I do not transfer it as the Sharpe of this proposed signal. The overnight-sort portfolio itself is not given a total-return Sharpe suitable for criterion 4. [Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019), *Journal of Financial Economics*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.03.011) ([full paper](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)).

Bogousslavsky's 2021 JFE article uses U.S. common stocks from 1986–2015 and documents that momentum returns accrue overnight, while several other anomaly returns reverse overnight; it supports a distinct day/night clientele and arbitrage mechanism, not a post-2010 standalone Dow Sharpe for this exact ON12 sort. His opening-price sensitivity is also a practical warning: overnight estimates depend on how the opening price is measured. [Bogousslavsky (2021), *Journal of Financial Economics*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.07.020) ([paper PDF](https://finance.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/files/media/attachments/The%2520Cross-Section%2520of%2520Intraday%2520and%2520Overnight%2520Returns_21Dec1620170112151545.pdf)).

**(b) Large-cap and post-2010 transfer — PARTIAL / FAIL for the required modern evidence.** The 2019 result is not only a microcap phenomenon because of the size exclusion and robustness statement, but its sample ends in 2013 and the main sort covers a broad universe above the bottom NYSE size quintile, not the current Dow or S&P mega-caps alone. Bogousslavsky extends the intraday decomposition through 2015 and studies value-weighted portfolios, but does not establish that the one-month overnight-sort return spread remains at its historical magnitude among the largest stocks after 2010. The mechanism is plausible for large stocks; the qualifying effect-size transfer is unverified.

**(c) Mechanism power — FAIL under an explicitly conservative SIC2-cluster planning assumption.** I used the harness synthetic simulator, not prices or returns: 500 names, 76 monthly formation dates, one monthly holding period, and monthly IC-series autocorrelation 0.20. As the simulator's horizon parameter is in observations, I set it to 2 to obtain a one-lag Bartlett Newey–West adjustment (`NW lag=1`) on the monthly IC series. The null rank-IC SD `1/sqrt(499)=0.0448` is inflated by `sqrt(5)` to 0.1002 for an assumed SIC2 cluster design effect of five. The planted mean IC is 0.015, an optimistic planning translation of the paper's +0.45% monthly close-to-close long-short alpha using a 10% monthly cross-sectional return-dispersion assumption and a decile long-short rank mapping; it is not a measured project statistic. With 5,000 simulations and seed 20261111, estimated `P(NW t >= 2)=0.255` (Monte Carlo SE 0.0062), effective N=63.3, and the 80%-power MDE is 0.0358. The effect transfer is below the MDE and detection power is weak. The harness does not natively estimate SIC clusters; the stated design effect is an ex-ante sensitivity assumption applied to the synthetic IC SD, not estimated from outcomes.

**(d) Cost and criterion-4 feasibility — FAIL.** At the required 0.05% per traded dollar, 20% monthly total turnover costs 1 bp of NAV per rebalance; 50% turnover costs 2.5 bp. Calling the existing `break_even_rank_ic` helper with 10% monthly return dispersion and its top-1/6 mapping gives a cost-only rank-IC hurdle of 0.00069 at 20% turnover and 0.00173 at 50%. These hurdles cover explicit trading cost only and say nothing about achieving the portfolio Sharpe gate. The paper's value-weight winner-minus-loser three-factor close-to-close alpha is about 0.45% per month. As an intentionally favorable transfer, assume the Dow top-five long-only tilt captures half that spread (0.225% monthly gross active return), and assume 15% annualized active risk. After the 20% turnover cost (0.01% per month), this is about 0.172 annualized net excess Sharpe; at 50% turnover it is about 0.160. A broad 10–20% active-risk planning range implies roughly 0.12–0.26, still well short of the binding ≥0.8 expected Dow excess Sharpe at ≤20% turnover in decision 76. The Dow top-five monthly holdings may in practice exceed the 20% turnover cap; this transfer assumes the cap is met and is therefore generous.

For criterion 4, a central expected net excess Sharpe of about 0.17 over six years corresponds under an iid monthly normal approximation to `z = 0.17*sqrt(6)=0.42`, one-sided raw `p≈0.34`. Across the plausible 0.12–0.26 range, raw p is approximately 0.39–0.26; the Holm-adjusted p across the R6 family would be near 1.0. Those are feasibility approximations, not block-bootstrap calculations: a Sharpe scalar is not a monthly return path, so it cannot produce the mandated 10-session block-bootstrap p. The documented effect is insufficient for a credible claim that the required bootstrap/Holm gate can pass.

**(e) Novelty and data — PASS for distinctness and inputs; FAIL overall because earlier gates fail.** This is own-stock overnight clientele pressure and continuation at monthly frequency. J1 is industry-relative close-to-close reversal; C1 is peer intraday return. This proposal uses neither peers nor the intraday component, so it is a genuinely different signal family, though still adjacent to conventional price momentum. Adjusted opens and closes and point-in-time Dow/S&P membership already exist in staged `r6_data`; ordinary OHLCV-based overnight return construction needs no paid trade-level data. It can be built after the 21-session warmup, with no sealed-period access. Daily OHLC open prices are less precise than the VWAP/quote-based open definitions in the papers, so execution and opening-price sensitivity must be disclosed.

## Honest probability and disposition

**Estimated chance of passing criterion 4: about 2% (low).** This is a judgmental planning probability, not a fitted probability: the closest documented effect yields an optimistic transferred Dow Sharpe around 0.17, versus the required 0.8, and its modern large-cap persistence is not established. Keep this as a literature-backed mechanism candidate only; do not authorize an outcome run unless new, independent post-2010 large-cap evidence materially raises the expected after-cost Dow excess Sharpe at the fixed turnover cap.
