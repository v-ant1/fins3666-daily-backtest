# Round P2 — co-movement pairs with pressure-confirmed divergence

**Recommendation: do not open an outcome test.** Historical co-movement gives a genuinely different relative-value structure from J1, but the documented edge has decayed and does not support the required six-year Dow excess Sharpe of about 0.8 after costs. I estimate a low chance of full-stack success (about **2%**, subjective), before accounting for the difficulty of keeping a two-leg strategy inside the turnover cap. This note uses literature and synthetic design calculations only; no project outcomes, prices, returns, signals, or sealed data were read.

## Direction

Form pairs from historical co-movement, not SIC membership. Using only the allowed development window, select each pair from the trailing normalized-price paths; freeze the pair and hedge ratio for the trading window. A trade requires (i) a material pair-spread divergence from its training relation and (ii) one-sided pressure in the leg causing the divergence, proxied with signed volume/close-location data already in the daily OHLCV panel. If the pressure is temporary, the spread should converge: buy the relatively pressured-down leg and short the relatively pressured-up leg. Use an equal-risk, self-financing long-short pair portfolio, with a fixed maximum total-notional turnover cap of 50% per monthly rebalance (20% is the stricter sensitivity). Do not add parameter searches or optimize on the development outcomes.

This differs from J1 in three defining parts: J1 ranks weekly SIC2-relative stock returns, is long-only top five, and does not estimate a pair relationship; P2 learns a stock-to-stock co-movement partner and hedge ratio from trailing price paths, trades the pair spread in both directions, and requires a leg-specific pressure confirmation. The shared word “reversal” does not make this a J1 horizon or turnover rescue: no J1 signal, thresholds, or results are reused. If the implementation reduces to buying recent losers within industries or changes J1’s cadence to lower costs, it is a forbidden rescue and must be killed.

## Gates (a)–(e)

### (a) Literature and post-2010 evidence

Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst find up to 11% annualized excess return for self-financing distance-matched pairs over 1962–2002; they interpret the result as temporary mispricing of close substitutes. That result is pre-2003 and cannot serve as a current effect-size estimate. [Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst (2006), DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhj020](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhj020).

Do and Faff find a pronounced downward trend in simple pairs profitability. Their reported top-20-pair mean monthly excess return falls from 1.24% (1962–1988) to 0.56% (1989–2002) and 0.33% (2003–2009), with reported Sharpe ratios falling from 1.04 to 0.43 to 0.32. Their later cost analysis says the pairs and industry-relative contrarian approaches were largely unprofitable after 2002 after commissions, impact and borrow costs. This is the closest broad-market historical proxy, but it still ends in 2009 and does not establish a post-2010 Dow effect. [Do & Faff (2010), DOI 10.2469/faj.v66.n4.1](https://doi.org/10.2469/faj.v66.n4.1); [Do & Faff (2012), DOI 10.1111/j.1475-6803.2012.01317.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-6803.2012.01317.x).

There is a more optimistic but less comparable S&P 100 result: a copula method reports annualized Sharpe 1.52 for its top five mean-reversion pairs over a 1990–2014 study, after a 12-month formation, 48-month pseudo-trading selection, and pair-specific exit thresholds. This full-period, multi-stage selected result does not isolate 2010–2014, does not document a simple pressure-confirmed distance rule, and is not a clean low-turnover post-2010 effect. It cannot responsibly be used as the expected Dow effect. [Krauss & Stübinger (2015), working paper](https://ideas.repec.org/p/zbw/iwqwdp/152015.html); survey: [Krauss (2017), DOI 10.1111/joes.12153](https://doi.org/10.1111/joes.12153).

**Gate (a): fails the magnitude/recency standard.** The positive literature supports the mechanism historically; the more relevant large-cap evidence is either declining through 2009 or a highly selected full sample ending 2014, not an independently documented post-2010 effect.

### (b) Mechanism and prediction

The pair relation is fixed using only trailing normalized daily prices. A divergence is signed by which leg has the more pressure-heavy OHLCV move. Predict that the pressured leg’s relative move partially reverses and the matched-pair spread converges over the next month. Report the pair-level spread change and an S&P cross-sectional rank-IC/event statistic separately; a portfolio return alone is not mechanism evidence. Use a pair-label or partner shuffle that preserves each stock’s marginal history as the placebo.

This signal is a proposal, not a published conversion of CLV×volume into pair returns. If pressure confirmation does not have an independently motivated, frozen sign before outcomes, the idea fails its mechanism gate.

### (c) SIC2-cluster power and criterion-4 feasibility

**Synthetic mechanism power.** I used `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with 54 SIC2 clusters, 72 monthly dates (the six-year ceiling), `horizon=1`, signal autocorrelation 0.20, 20,000 simulations, and cluster-adjusted IC SD 0.20538. The SD is the explicit design approximation `sqrt((1 + (9.24 − 1) × 0.15)/(54 − 1))`, using about 499 S&P names / 54 SIC2 groups and an assumed within-SIC2 correlation of 0.15; it is not measured from project returns. At the sensitivity IC 0.020, synthetic probability of `NW t ≥ 2` is **17.4%**, and the 80%-power MDE is **0.0688**. The literature does not report a monthly pressure-confirmed S&P rank IC, so 0.020 is a benchmark sensitivity, not a published pair effect. The helper uses lag zero for `horizon=1`; despite generated AR(1) inputs, its test does not HAC-adjust that serial correlation. Thus these figures are optimistic, and SIC2-cluster count is only a proxy for pair-network dependence, not a pair-cluster bootstrap. **Gate (c): fail.**

**Criterion 4 estimate.** The closest broad large-cap pair estimate is Do and Faff’s reported 0.32 Sharpe for 2003–2009, with the paper warning that after-2002 returns are largely unprofitable after realistic costs. I use **0.16 annualized Dow excess Sharpe as a judgmental central gross-to-net transfer**, with a generous pre-cost ceiling of **0.32** and a plausible net range of **0.0–0.2** once the six-year/large-cap restriction and project costs are recognized. This is far below the required ~0.8. Under an IID normal approximation for 72 monthly observations, central SR 0.16 implies a one-sided null-tail probability near **0.35**; the net 0.0–0.2 range implies roughly **0.50–0.31**. The ceiling 0.32 implies about **0.22** before project costs. These are transparent six-year p-value proxies, not bootstrap results from portfolio returns; a block bootstrap and Holm adjustment would not make the case stronger. **Criterion-4 gate: fail.**

**Cost hurdle.** The approved `break_even_rank_ic` helper is a top-fraction long-tilt approximation, not an exact cost model for market-neutral pairs. For an explicit Dow illustration with 5 names out of 30, assumed one-month cross-sectional return dispersion 6%, and 5 bp/side costs, the helper gives cost-only IC hurdles of **0.001155 at 20% turnover** and **0.002887 at 50% turnover**. To meet the project's cost rule that costs stay under one-quarter of gross edge, the corresponding gross-signal hurdles are four times those values: **0.00462** and **0.01155**. These are planning conversions; actual paired legs, short-borrow fees, slippage and simultaneous pair overlap could make costs higher. The turnover cap controls trading volume but does not create alpha.

### (d) Data and implementation feasibility

The allowed daily OHLCV panel contains the raw ingredients for normalized prices, close location and a signed-volume proxy; SIC2 membership on the staged S&P roster supports the requested cluster design. A frozen run must use only data dated 2020-11-27 through 2026-10-30, with pairs and hedge ratios formed strictly from prior observations. No extra paid borrow, quote, or intraday inputs may be required. Before outcomes, an input-only check must show sufficient S&P pair coverage within SIC2 for the mechanism sample and identify how pair overlap is handled; the Dow has only 30 names and at most 15 disjoint pairs at one time. If shorting costs/availability cannot be bounded using the fixed project cost convention, disclose that as a failed implementation condition rather than treating 5 bp/side as all-in pair cost.

### (e) Novelty, ranking, and kill rule

The relation comes from learned historical co-movement, not an SIC2 sort or J1’s within-industry return ranks. The proposed pressure confirmation makes the test specifically about whether one-sided buying/selling pressure explains temporary pair divergence; a generic pairs backtest without that component would not test the stated thesis. This is a genuinely different signal and portfolio structure, but it is not a documented, post-2010 scalable edge.

**Rank: 1 of 1; subjective chance of clearing all R6 mechanism, cost, portfolio, turnover and inference gates: about 2%.** Kill before outcomes if: (i) the cited literature cannot justify a frozen pressure sign; (ii) pair-cluster dependence makes the S&P power below the already-low synthetic result; (iii) no realistic method can keep total-notional turnover at or below 50%; or (iv) someone uses the selected 1990–2014 S&P 100 Sharpe as a post-2010 Dow expectation. Do not spend a variant unless the coordinator explicitly accepts these failed feasibility gates.
