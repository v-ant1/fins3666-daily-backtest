# L3 fallback directions after D4–D6

**Status: research shortlist only; no outcome data were loaded or calculated.** These are candidate research designs, not approved variants or portfolio rules. D1–D6 already cover retail order pressure, ETF flow, mutual-fund expected flow, daily CLV on 13F ownership, SIC volume leaders, and monthly SPY–Treasury trend, respectively. The shortlist below changes the observable pressure source and timing, and requires coordinator approval and a preregistration before any outcome test.

## Decision

Advance three designs for a later, separately authorized cycle: (1) peer responses to SEC-filed earnings news, (2) 13F-reported institutional position changes as delayed demand shocks, and (3) the overnight/intraday split of network pressure. Treat index-reconstitution spillovers as a low-priority event study because the Dow-only event universe is likely too sparse; do not call it a funded primary direction until an event-count-only feasibility audit passes. Do not test generic month-end or quarter-end effects from daily 13F snapshots: those snapshots do not identify daily trades at the month/quarter end.

All three shortlisted mechanisms use a predeclared sign and horizon, and each requires a single fixed test, not a sweep. Their literature motivates the hypothesis but does not establish that a Dow-only sample will have power or net returns.

## Candidate 1 — Earnings-release pressure spilling through the dated common-owner graph

**Question.** Does the first market response to an issuer's earnings release predict same-sign short-horizon returns in its other Dow names connected by common institutional ownership?

**Inputs and eligibility.** Use only point-in-time Dow constituents and the dated `13f_cosine_overlap_edges.csv` graph / its documented point-in-time successor. A source event is a Form 8-K that actually contains Item 2.02, “Results of Operations and Financial Condition”; verify the item in the filing body or filing index, not from form type alone. The SEC says Item 2.02 covers a public release of material information about completed quarterly or annual results and requires the release date and text as an exhibit. Use the EDGAR acceptance timestamp, convert it to US Eastern time, and map the event to the first complete regular trading session whose open occurs after acceptance. If the issuer provides an earlier release timestamp in the exhibit, use the later of that time and EDGAR acceptance. (This conservative availability rule keeps the signal from using a filing not yet public.)

**Fixed signal and outcome.** For source k on its first complete session d, define `shock[k,d] = close-to-close return[k,d] − close-to-close return[SPY,d]`. At d close, calculate the target peer score `E[j,d] = Σk shock[k,d] × W[k,j,d]`, with zero for the source itself; W is the latest common-owner edge matrix publicly available by d, with row sums normalized to one and stale age reported. The sole primary predictive statistic is the date-pooled Spearman rank correlation between E[j,d] and peer j's cumulative next-session-open through fifth-session-close return. Exclude j if it has its own Item 2.02 event from d−5 through d+5 sessions, exclude the source, and keep one observation per target/date after aggregating simultaneous sources by the displayed sum. A distinct source event is one issuer-filing accession; amendments and duplicate accessions referring to the same release count once.

**Predicted sign and horizon.** Positive: positive first-response news shocks predict positive linked-peer returns over the next five sessions; negative shocks predict negative returns. This is an information-transfer and potential pressure-spread hypothesis, not proof that return shocks are uninformed demand. Foster (1981), Ramnath (2002), and Hameed et al. (2015) report intra-industry or prominent-to-neglected information transfers; Thomas and Zhang (2008) show that those transfers can overshoot and reverse when the later firm's own earnings arrive. See Cards 25–28.

**Placebo.** Keep each date's event sources, shock values, graph degrees, and target counts fixed, but randomly reassign the event-day common-owner edge weights to eligible non-source Dow recipients, without replacement within each event-source row, preserving each source total weight and number of recipients (one fixed 1,000-draw procedure, seed 20261110). Recompute the same rank IC. The observed IC must exceed the 95th percentile of this one-sided placebo distribution for the network-specific interpretation to pass. Also report the placebo distribution and original IC; do not infer “no effect” merely from a nonsignificant real test.

**Free-data path / both windows.** SEC EDGAR filing histories and archived 8-K documents are free; SEC documents the no-key submissions API and public filing archive. Daily Dow OHLCV, the SIC panel, and 13F position/edge panels have a free-data path in development and holdout. The workspace currently contains a 2010–2020 13F holdout-position panel and `sic_panel.csv`; the new per-quarter top-50 PIT roster is still a required L4 input and is not present in the directory snapshot checked for this task. Run the holdout only through the coordinator's one-shot route; disclose Dow roster/price gaps under R5 decisions. Never use a fixed current manager roster for earlier dates.

**Failure modes / gate.** Item 2.02 can omit oral-only announcements; acceptance timing can lag a press release; daily close-to-close source shock mixes news and demand; source and peer announcements can overlap; same 2-digit SIC does not imply an economic link; the Dow has few independent industry groups. Before outcomes, require at least 100 nonoverlapping source events in each period and at least 20 event dates with 3+ valid linked peers; if not met, label infeasible and stop. These counts are design gates, not results.

## Candidate 2 — Disclosed 13F position-change pressure, then pressure on connected holdings

**Question.** After disclosed quarter-end positions become public, does recent manager buying pressure predict continuation in commonly owned Dow names over five sessions?

**Fixed signal.** For each manager m in the point-in-time top-50 roster, issuer i, and report quarter q, define the split-adjusted share change `Δshares[m,i,q] = shares[m,i,q] − shares[m,i,q−1]`. Define manager's observable Dow-sleeve denominator `A[m,q] = Σi shares[m,i,q−1] × close[i,q]`, where the denominator marks prior-quarter shares at q's close, reducing price-change contamination. Set `f[i,q] = Σm Δshares[m,i,q] × close[i,q] / Σm A[m,q]`, where membership in m's sum is determined by the q-quarter PIT top-50 roster; if either report is missing, do not substitute zero. This is a signed reported-position-change proxy, not actual daily order flow: 13F omits short positions and intraperiod round trips. Define connected-stock signal `F[j,q] = Σi W[i,j,q] × f[i,q]`, using only the graph and filings available on the first trading day after the filing date; the signal is first usable on the next trading day, per binding R5 decision 6. The primary outcome is one date-wise Spearman rank IC between F[j,q] and target j's next-session-open to fifth-session-close return, pooled over eligible filing dates, with each report quarter's first public top-50 roster fixed as of its public availability.

**Predicted sign and horizon.** Positive: names connected to net-bought holdings should have positive returns over the next five sessions if managers' demand persists or is copied across holdings. Literature finds temporary price impact around mutual-fund fire sales/purchases and flow-induced return continuation over longer periods; this short five-session propagation claim is a new, narrower extrapolation, not directly established by Lou (2012) or Coval and Stafford (2007). Existing Cards 17–18 cover these sources.

**Placebo.** For each report date, degree-preserving permute the common-owner graph endpoints within the contemporaneous Dow roster (seed 20261110, 1,000 draws); preserve f, target availability, and date. Compare the real IC with the one-sided 95th percentile of the permuted-graph ICs and report the full distribution. As a second falsification table (not a second primary endpoint), report the sign of the same IC for the immediately preceding five-session return window; that window is not tradable after filing publication and should not be described as a prospective result.

**Free-data path / both windows.** SEC 13F filings and historical SEC archive are free; daily OHLCV and PIT SIC/ownership inputs have a free-data route for both periods. Existing workspace files show `13f_holdout_positions.csv` and `sic_panel.csv`; however, `13f_top50_pit_roster.csv` is not yet in the L4 data directory snapshot, so full PIT buildability is conditional on L4 completing it. Coordinator must execute the single holdout run; no worker reads or uses holdout prices. Missing securities, amendments, split adjustments, confidential-treatment omissions, and graph age must be disclosed.

**Failure modes / gate.** 13F records are quarter-end snapshots filed with a lag (up to the statutory 45-day deadline); position changes are not actual trades, and 30-stock sleeve weights can be noisy. Holdings signal can therefore describe stale historical activity rather than fresh buying pressure. The proposed mechanism fails if point-in-time top-50 membership or filing dates cannot be reconstructed for both periods, or if fewer than 30 report dates per period have at least 20 finite stock scores and 3+ usable connected peers. Check counts only before outcomes; below either minimum, declare infeasible and stop without expanding the roster, universe, or lookback.

## Candidate 3 — Overnight/intraday pressure split on the common-owner network

**Question.** Does pressure concentrated in the overnight session persist in connected names overnight, while the opposing intraday clientele absorbs part of it?

**Fixed signal.** For each stock i and session t, compute `ON[i,t] = ln(adjusted_open[i,t] / adjusted_close[i,t−1])` and `ID[i,t] = ln(adjusted_close[i,t] / adjusted_open[i,t])`; where adjusted open is `raw_open × adjusted_close / raw_close`, matching the existing daily-price adjustment convention. At close t, form `N_ON[j,t] = Σi W[i,j,t] × mean(ON[i,t−4:t])` and `N_ID[j,t] = Σi W[i,j,t] × mean(ID[i,t−4:t])`, with the latest point-in-time common-owner graph known by t. The primary mechanism endpoint is the date-pooled Spearman rank IC between N_ON[j,t] and j's next five overnight returns summed over sessions t+1…t+5. A single prespecified cross-period diagnostic is the Spearman IC between N_ON[j,t] and j's next-five intraday return sum. No portfolio is formed in this screening design.

**Predicted sign and horizon.** Same-sign overnight peer continuation over five sessions; opposite-sign intraday response as a secondary diagnostic. Lou, Polk and Skouras (2019) find own-stock monthly overnight and intraday components each continue in their own session and show offsetting cross-period returns. Applying that clientele mechanism to other holdings on a 13F graph is a new hypothesis. Do not claim their paper established network transmission. See Card 29.

**Placebo.** Use one degree-preserving randomization of W within the date's eligible graph (1,000 draws, seed 20261110), holding return components and dates fixed; compare the real N_ON IC with the one-sided 95th percentile of the placebo ICs. The own-stock signal's IC is a required diagnostic but not a separate candidate or parameter variant.

**Free-data path / both windows.** Daily Dow OHLCV supplies open, high, low, close, and adjustment fields for development; the coordinator's holdout OHLCV route and existing 2010–2020 ownership panel can supply holdout inputs after the one-shot approval. It does not need intraday TAQ. Top-50 PIT roster remains an L4 dependency. Do not touch country ETFs from the sealed basket.

**Failure modes / gate.** Open-to-close components combine overnight news, auction effects, corporate actions, and asymmetric liquidity; the paper's effect is monthly and uses a broad cross-section, so a 30-name daily network extension may not replicate it. Require at least 500 dates per period with 20+ finite signals before outcomes; otherwise stop as infeasible. Do not tune window length or try alternative component definitions after observing returns.

## Screened but not advanced

- **Index-addition/deletion pressure spilling to peers.** Public S&P index notices and free daily prices make the event-source price-pressure literature relevant. Define a future feasibility screen as all official S&P 500 or DJIA membership announcements involving an issuer in the fixed point-in-time Dow universe, with shock sign `+1` for addition and `−1` for deletion, network recipients among the other Dow names connected by pre-event 13F edges, and the same five-session peer-return target. Harris and Gurel (1986) report a >3% initial price rise after additions that nearly reverses within two weeks; Woolridge and Ghosh (1986) report a next-day addition/deletion response; Wurgler and Zhuravskaya (2002) show larger inclusion jumps without close substitutes. But these are event-firm, not peer-spillover estimates. The New York Fed reassessment finds no permanent value effect and argues pre-event momentum/earnings explain much of the apparent permanent effect and comovement. Count official Dow-linked event accessions only, without returns: require 30 distinct development events and 30 holdout events before even proposing a test. The network would otherwise have too few independent shocks. If under the gate, abandon; do not widen to non-Dow price histories.
- **Generic month-end/quarter-end institutional rebalancing among common owners.** Rejected as currently framed. Quarter-end 13F snapshots cannot reveal day-level flow at calendar month-end, and using quarter-end prices/holdings as if they were available then is look-ahead. The actual-activity literature uses daily proprietary institutional trades. A disclosure-timed quarterly 13F change signal is Candidate 2 and is not labeled a month-end trade test.
