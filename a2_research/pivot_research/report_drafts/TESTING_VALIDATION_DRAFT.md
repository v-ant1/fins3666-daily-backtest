# Testing and validation

We separated development from the holdout so that choices could be made using development data without repeatedly checking the final evaluation period. Development uses point-in-time Dow constituents (the membership list known on each date) and daily data from 27 November 2020 to 30 October 2026. No worker computed or used holdout prices, returns, or signals. During an availability check, L4 accidentally saw rendered historical DWDP OHLCV rows; no values were computed or used, and L4 was excluded from later hypothesis, preregistration, and implementation work (`specs/r4_trials.md`). The coordinator is responsible for one frozen holdout run on 1 January 2010 to 31 October 2020. The available roster has known gaps: KFT, DWDP and WBA are missing, and UTX is represented by the RTX lineage. The benchmark is equal-weighted across available names. Results will show the DWDP membership window both included and excluded so its effect is visible.

Before looking at outcome data for a direction, the team registered its hypothesis, signal, predicted direction and horizon, inputs, variants, placebos, and decision rules. After checking the inputs, the coordinator froze each eligible specification and recorded its content hash. A frozen test is run as written; a secondary variant cannot replace a failed primary. This makes it harder to change the method in response to an attractive result.

Testing proceeds from the proposed mechanism to a portfolio. First, the registered signal is compared with its specified future return using date-level Spearman rank correlations for D4/D5 (the correlation between signal ranks and later-return ranks), or the registered cross-lag coefficient for D6 (whether one series predicts a later value of another). Newey–West standard errors adjust the uncertainty estimate for dependence across nearby dates in these monthly or daily test series. D4 and D5 use lag four; D6 uses lag eleven. A mechanism must meet its pre-set direction and statistical thresholds before a portfolio result can be considered. Placebos are checks that deliberately break the proposed relationship: D4 and D5 use network rewiring or time shifts, D5 also uses within-industry leader permutations, and D6 compares the observed relation with circularly shifted bond-signal coefficients. Placebo results are reported alongside the observed estimate rather than treated as a substitute strategy.

The completed D4, D5 and D6 mechanism tests did not pass. D4’s primary mean rank IC was −0.0181 across 1,479 dates (Newey–West t = −2.42, lag 4), significantly in the direction opposite its registered positive prediction; the registered positive-direction raw p-value was 0.992. That one-sided p-value tests for a positive effect, so it is near one when the estimate is negative; the negative t-statistic points the other way. This is evidence of short-term reversal in the measured sample, not a proposed trading strategy. D4’s degree-preserving, random-link, fixed-shift and placebo-equivalence gates also failed. An independent calculation from the saved development bundle reproduced its primary IC and t-statistic exactly. D5’s primary mean rank IC was 0.00746 (Newey–West t = 0.56), below the registered IC hurdle of 0.0183 and t-statistic hurdle of 2; its permutation placebo also failed. D6’s primary coefficient was 0.0769 (Newey–West t = 0.77), and its observed coefficient did not exceed the registered 95th percentile of the circular-shift placebo. All three directions were recorded as failures, with no portfolio tests or rescue variants. D7 remains pending and not ready for freeze while gate chronology and endpoint count are unresolved; insert its status and any authorized tests only after those issues are settled.

If a mechanism passes, the portfolio stage applies the frozen holdings and rebalance rules, next-session-open execution and an equal-weight benchmark. Trading costs are 0.05% per side on traded value. The strategy must have positive net excess return and a higher net Sharpe ratio than its benchmark. Uncertainty in portfolio excess return is assessed with a 10-session circular block bootstrap using 5,000 draws. This resamples short blocks of adjacent observations, preserving some of the time clustering in returns. No portfolio result is available for D4, D5 or D6 because their mechanism gates failed.

We control false positives from testing several registered endpoints with the Holm correction, applied across the complete pre-registered family for the goal. Holm adjusts p-values so the chance of at least one false positive across the whole family is controlled. The coordinator maintains that family in the trial log; `report_drafts/HOLM_INTERIM.md` contains the current interim values. We also report the deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR) using the full 30-variant budget. This adjusts an apparent Sharpe for the chance of selecting a good result from many tried strategies. The 30-variant limit is fixed, and parameter sweeps are not allowed.

Several process limitations and deviations are disclosed. The holdout has the roster gaps listed above. A coordinator error initially put file-path hashes in the D4 and D6 freeze lines; the content hashes were corrected before either outcome was computed, with the frozen text and date unchanged. D6 was also run accidentally by L1 after freezing; the artifacts were preserved, and the official guarded run produced the same result. Both are disclosed, counted once in the Holm family, and no result was selected by rerunning. D4’s missing AAPL and TRV mappings were fixed and the inputs rebuilt before D4’s single mechanism run; the runner correction was made before its outcome was computed. A formatting issue after the saved endpoint results was finalized from those results without rerunning. The interim Holm calculation is in `report_drafts/HOLM_INTERIM.md`.

**Method references:** Newey and West (1987), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913610); Holm (1979), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2307/4615733); Bailey and López de Prado (2014), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2460551).

**Other terms in plain English:** **CLV** is close-location value, describing whether a stock closed near its daily high or low. **SIC** is the industry code used to group companies; **EW30** means equal weights across the available Dow names. A **placebo** breaks the proposed link or timing. **Circular shift** rotates one series in time to compare the observed association with deliberately misaligned versions. **Turnover** is the traded amount relative to portfolio value; **active share** measures how different the portfolio weights are from the benchmark. A **circular block bootstrap** resamples short consecutive blocks of observations to preserve some time clustering. **Holm-adjusted p-values** account for multiple registered tests, and the **DSR** is a Sharpe measure adjusted for strategy selection. The 10-session blocks and 5,000 draws are portfolio-inference design settings; no such portfolio inference was run for D4–D6.
