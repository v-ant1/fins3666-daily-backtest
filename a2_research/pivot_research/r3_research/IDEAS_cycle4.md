## Shared protocol to preregister before looking at selection results

- Use the two required selection windows only: Dow 2020-11-27–2022-12-30 and cross-asset 2020-11-27–2022-12-30. Signals use complete observations through the signal close and orders fill at the next eligible session's open. No lookback shortening, price imputation, or universe changes.
- At each 21-session rebalance on the validated engine's 2020-11-27 anchor, assign average ranks `q_i` from 0 (least desirable) to 1 (most desirable). Set `w_i = (1/30) * (1 + (2*q_i - 1))`. This is a long-only, fully invested `a=1.0` rank tilt, with no leverage or cash. The 1% whole-rebalance no-trade band, 0.05% per-side cost on actual traded notional, final liquidation and same-grid EW30 benchmark follow the validated engine.
- For a family with two definitions, discard a candidate if it has invalid signal dates or mean actual turnover above 50% per rebalance in either selection universe. Freeze the eligible definition with the highest unweighted mean of Dow and cross-asset annualized net Sharpe; ties go to lower mean turnover and then listing order. For a one-definition family, freeze it only if valid and within the same turnover cap. If no candidate qualifies, record EW30 for that family. Write all choices before opening the four fixed test periods; test each frozen family once, with no retuning.
- Report the required risk-adjusted and raw results, turnover, pooled block-bootstrap p-value, DSR at about 98 trials, and realized annualized tracking error versus EW30. The `a=1.0` rank rule creates substantial target active share mechanically; **do not use that as evidence of distinct performance**. These directions target realized TE above 3% a year, but only the measured result can establish that. A family that misses the actual TE target should be described as a miss even if its active share passes the formal alternative criterion.

## 1. Unusually high dollar-volume event premium

**Rationale.** A shock in trading activity can make an asset more visible and draw subsequent demand; this is an attention/participation mechanism, different from signed-volume pressure, low-volume reversal, or an illiquidity penalty. Gervais, Kaniel and Mingelgrin (2001) report that stocks with unusually high (low) daily or weekly volume tend to appreciate (depreciate) over the following month. That stock-market finding does not establish that a 21-session event return predicts cross-asset ETF returns, so the ETF test is a genuine portability check.

**Inputs and construction.** For each asset, use raw close times reported volume as daily dollar volume `V_d`, and adjusted close for returns. For each historical event date `d`, compute `shock_d = V_d / median(V_(d-20), ..., V_(d-1))`. The event qualifies when `shock_d >= k`. At signal close `t`, take event dates `d=t-83,...,t-21` (63 dates); each event's label is the already-observed forward 21-session adjusted-close return `adjclose_(d+21)/adjclose_d - 1`. Score the asset by the arithmetic mean of the labels for qualifying events. Require at least five qualifying events; otherwise mark that date invalid and fail the candidate rather than substituting a different signal. Higher average post-event return ranks higher. The score uses no return or volume after `t`.

**Fixed definitions.** `HVOL21_K15` uses `k=1.5`; `HVOL21_K20` uses `k=2.0`. All other inputs and rules above are identical. The effective history is 104 daily rows including the signal date (103 sessions before it): 20 for the first shock baseline, 63 event dates, and 21 sessions to observe the final forward label. The selection window has this history in both approved universes. This sequential warm-up is disclosed rather than silently shortening the event sample.

**Risk, turnover and failure mode.** A slow 63-date event sample and 21-session rebalance should yield roughly 25–45% actual turnover per rebalance; either candidate must pass the 50% cap. The full-strength rank tilt aims for 3–8% realized annualized TE. Dollar-volume normalization is within-asset, so it does not compare raw share counts across securities and can be applied to stocks and ETFs. Failure modes include only a few independent volume shocks, overlapping 21-session outcome labels, provider volume inconsistencies, and attention shocks that reflect bad news rather than favorable visibility. If either universe frequently has fewer than five events, mark that variant invalid.

**Citation.** Gervais, S., Kaniel, R. and Mingelgrin, D. H. (2001), “The High-Volume Return Premium,” *The Journal of Finance*, 56(3), 877–919. https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00349.

## 2. Weekday-specific relative-return seasonality

**Rationale.** Calendar-time and trading-time models imply different weekday patterns, and French (1980) documents a historical weekday/weekend effect in US stock returns. This is a cross-sectional portability test: it asks whether some assets have a more favorable weekday profile than their same-day universe benchmark, not whether the entire market has a weekday premium. The 63-session window gives about 12 observations per weekday, so estimates are intentionally shrunk.

**Exact score.** Using the 63 most recent completed daily adjusted-close returns, compute each date's cross-sectional equal-weight return `m_d`. For each asset `i` and weekday `w` (Monday through Friday), calculate `mu_iw = mean(r_i,d - m_d)` over observations with weekday `w`, and count `n_iw`. Define `shrunk_iw = (n_iw/(n_iw+10))*mu_iw`; the desirability score is the equal-weight mean of the five `shrunk_iw` values. Higher scores rank higher. Require at least eight observations for every weekday for every asset; otherwise the candidate fails validation. This does not use the next holding period's weekday returns or any forward market information.

**Fixed definition.** `WEEKDAY63_SHR10`: 63 completed sessions and a fixed shrinkage prior of 10 observations; there is no parameter sweep. Rebalance every 21 sessions under the shared protocol.

**Risk, turnover and failure mode.** Shrinkage should moderate rank churn; forecast 20–45% actual turnover per rebalance, subject to the hard selection cap. The active rank tilt aims for 3–8% realized annualized TE because it reallocates across all 30 assets based on different weekday profiles; actual TE must be reported and may be lower if weekday alphas are mostly common. A shared schedule, same-day benchmark residual, and equal weighting over weekday estimates make the rule implementable in both universes. The effect may have disappeared, be common to all assets (no usable cross-section), or be calendar noise in a 63-session window; daily bars may also encode asynchronous underlying-market closes in cross-asset ETFs.

**Citation.** French, K. R. (1980), “Stock Returns and the Weekend Effect,” *Journal of Financial Economics*, 8(1), 55–69. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(80)90021-5.

## 3. First-half versus second-half-of-month relative-return profile

**Rationale.** Ariel (1987) documents a historical difference between returns early and late in the calendar month. Unlike a whole-market calendar trade, this portfolio seeks differences in how the 30 assets respond to that calendar profile, measured relative to the same-day equal-weight universe. It is separate from weekday seasonality, uses only 63 daily observations, and avoids a 12-month or multi-year seasonal lookback that the cross-asset history cannot support.

**Exact score.** On the 63 most recent completed adjusted-close returns, define phase `F` for calendar day-of-month 1–15 and `L` for day-of-month 16 through month-end. For each asset and phase `p`, calculate the mean same-day excess return `mu_ip = mean(r_i,d - m_d)` and count `n_ip`, where `m_d` is that date's 30-asset equal-weight return. Set `shrunk_ip = (n_ip/(n_ip+10))*mu_ip`. From the known session calendar, count how many of the next 21 eligible sessions fall in each phase, `N_F` and `N_L`; score `S_i = (N_F*shrunk_iF + N_L*shrunk_iL)/21`. Higher expected 21-session same-day-relative return ranks higher. Require at least 15 observations in each phase per asset; otherwise fail the candidate. Calendar dates are known in advance, while future prices are not used.

**Fixed definition.** `MONTHPHASE63_SHR10`: 63 completed sessions, fixed 10-observation shrinkage, and the shared 21-session rebalance. This is one preregistered definition, not a search across month cutoffs or horizons.

**Risk, turnover and failure mode.** The two conditional means move slowly and are shrunk, so expected actual turnover is 20–45% per rebalance; enforce the 50% cap. Full-strength rank weights aim for 3–8% realized annualized TE. Residualizing by each date's equal-weight return focuses the ranking on cross-sectional differences and makes it applicable to both stocks and multi-asset ETFs. The published market-level effect may not exist in this sample or may be uniform across assets; 63 sessions contain only about three months and can give unstable phase estimates. Month-end flows and local trading calendars may differ across ETFs, weakening a common interpretation.

**Citations.** Ariel, R. A. (1987), “A Monthly Effect in Stock Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics*, 18(1), 161–174. https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(87)90066-3. Jaffe, J. and Westerfield, R. (1989), “Is There a Monthly Effect in Stock Market Returns? Evidence from Foreign Countries,” *Journal of Banking & Finance*, 13(2), 237–244. https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-4266(89)90062-9. Their weak international evidence is a reason to treat this as a falsifiable hypothesis rather than assume it transfers.
