## 1. Overlap with prior work

- `r6_ideas/N.md` already screens activist Schedule 13D campaigns as directed sponsor-to-target links, where a source campaign predicts pressure at other targets associated with the same activist. This screen would score the **same disclosed activist campaign and target company itself**, not the activist’s other targets.
- That target-versus-peer distinction is real, but the source, event, and claimed mechanism still substantially overlap: both infer buying pressure from activist block accumulation and a public Schedule 13D campaign. The own-target version is also the most standard activist event study, while it abandons the central “pressure spreads through related assets” component of Advik’s thesis.
- Brav et al. and the subsequent activism literature already study target-stock reactions around 13D/campaign disclosures. Changing from a peer outcome to the campaign target’s own stock does not create a new pressure source.
- **Verdict: FAIL.** The event-level pressure source substantially duplicates N2, and own-stock activism is not a network-spillover test. A genuine distinction would require a new and independently measured channel, not simply changing which stock’s return is examined.

## 2. Exact pressure measure and dated data source

A reproducible candidate would retain only Schedule 13D and 13D/A filings for issuers on the point-in-time Dow roster. At each weekly formation, take the latest newly accepted filing for issuer i, keep Item 5(c) transactions by reporting persons whose Item 4 purpose is to influence management or policy, and calculate `P[i,t] = (Σ shares_bought − Σ shares_sold) / shares_outstanding_used_in_the_filing`. The transaction list covers the past 60 days or the time since the prior 13D, whichever is shorter; its reported share amounts, prices, and dates are filing inputs ([SEC Regulation 13D-G interpretation](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/corporation-finance-interpretations/exchange-act-sections-13d-13g-regulation-13d-g-beneficial-ownership-reporting)). Transactions disclosed jointly by multiple persons must be deduplicated. If an amendment has no new transactions, it does not reset the signal. The proposed sign is **positive** for the target’s next five-session return, on the hypothesis that disclosed activist accumulation and expected intervention draw buyers. The measure is a filed past-trade amount, not identified live exchange order flow.

The signal timestamp would be the SEC filing acceptance time, and the first permissible formation would be after public acceptance, with the trade placed at the next session’s open. Item 5 reports beneficial ownership and specified transactions, but transactions may have occurred before disclosure; the filing therefore reveals accumulated ownership with a reporting delay rather than timestamping each contemporaneous market buy. SEC rules distinguish control-intent 13D filers from passive or qualified institutional owners that use 13G. The SEC’s 2023 amendments shortened the initial 13D filing deadline from ten days to five business days and require 13D amendments within two business days; this improves reporting timeliness but does not remove the pre-filing accumulation window ([SEC release 2023-219](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-219)).

The free source is SEC EDGAR’s [filing search](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/) and date-filtered SEC filing-search index (`https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index`). A filing-level build would need a CIK-to-security and point-in-time Dow roster crosswalk, duplicate/amendment handling, a definition of qualifying activist intent, and secure parsing of filing acceptance times. SEC filings and company identifiers are public; issuer-level activist classification from Item 4 may require manual review. The actual coverage test here is only 2024, not the entire 2020-11-27 to 2026-10-30 development window.

- **Input-only feasibility pull:** every SEC search request included `dateRange=custom`, `startdt=2024-01-01`, `enddt=2024-12-31`, and `forms=SC 13D`; no earlier filings were fetched. A broad form/text query returned 8,611 2024 SC 13D-root search records across all issuers. This includes multiple filings/amendments and is not a count of campaigns or Dow events.
- **Five-name target check:** with the same date filters, I queried five known 2024 Dow constituents by CIK (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, JPM, CAT) and read SEC search metadata fields only (`file_date`, `form`, `display_names`, `ciks`). The metadata display showed **zero records with one of these five names as the first displayed entity**. JPM’s CIK appeared in 49 records, but its role cannot be determined reliably from the search metadata alone. These counts establish zero *confirmed* target events in the metadata-only check, while leaving the 49 JPM-related records unresolved; no event signal values were constructed.
- **Outcome restriction:** no company filing text or exhibits were opened; no stock prices, forward returns, ICs, portfolio returns, or outcome statistics were retrieved or calculated.
- **Verdict: FAIL for full-window point-in-time feasibility.** The public source is accessible, but one year of five-name metadata does not establish complete point-in-time mapping or enough usable development events; no full-window filing-only roster manifest was built.

There is a large gap between 8,611 all-market filings and a usable Dow event count. The filing index includes initial filings, amendments, multiple reporting persons, passive 13G filings excluded from the signal, and records for targets outside the Dow; campaign identity cannot be equated with an accession number. Ticker and CIK lineages, mergers, name changes, and point-in-time membership must be resolved. The 2024 five-name check is explicitly not a coverage estimate for the full Dow universe.

Published sources also warn against assuming that the filing sample resembles mega-cap opportunities. Brav, Jiang, Partnoy, and Thomas, [“Hedge Fund Activism, Corporate Governance, and Firm Performance,” *Journal of Finance* 63 (2008), 1729–1775, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01373.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01373.x), assemble 1,059 hedge-fund/target pairs for 2001–2006. They report about 7–8% abnormal return over the **−20 to +20 trading-day announcement window**, with price and abnormal turnover beginning to rise 1–10 days before filing; the positive announcement effect is not reversed over the following year. They note that relatively few target firms are large caps because accumulating a meaningful stake is costly, and that some activism at very large firms uses less than the 5% disclosure threshold. Their event-window abnormal return is not a post-filing weekly rank IC.

Becht, Franks, Grant, and Wagner, [“Returns to Hedge Fund Activism: An International Study,” *Review of Financial Studies* 30 (2017), 2933–2971, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhx048](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhx048), examine 1,740 publicly disclosed engagements in 23 countries from 2000–2010, including 1,125 U.S. engagements. Their U.S. announcement abnormal return is about 7% in a −20 to +20 day window, and performance differs with engagement outcomes. This provides a second documented campaign-announcement result, not evidence for a current-window, post-filing, five-session mega-cap buying-pressure rank signal. Von Lilienfeld-Toal and Schnitzler, [“The Anatomy of Block Accumulations by Activist Shareholders,” *Journal of Corporate Finance* 62 (2020), DOI 10.1016/j.jcorpfin.2020.101620](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcorpfin.2020.101620), analyze U.S. 13D filings from 2001–2016 and emphasize heterogeneous filer types; their results do not establish a contemporary Dow weekly effect either. These are later publications but their samples do not supply post-2015 development-period estimates.

- **Verdict: FAIL.** The metadata-only screen confirmed no target events among five selected large caps (with 49 JPM-related records unresolved by role), while the cited papers show that large caps are underrepresented and published effects are pre-filing campaign windows rather than post-filing pressure at a weekly horizon.

## 4. Effective breadth and power at a sourced plausible effect

The activism literature documents large announcement-window abnormal returns, but none of the cited estimates is a rank IC for a positive five-session return formed after the SEC filing is public. Brav et al. find that price pressure starts before filing, the immediate event window includes pre-filing sessions, and the subsequent-year effect is not reversed. Becht et al.’s 7% window estimate also combines the days before and after filing and depends on campaign outcomes. These effects cannot be converted to a defensible post-filing weekly rank IC without assumptions about event-time returns, cross-sectional dispersion, and the share of the effect already incorporated before filing. No sourced plausible effect matching the signal, horizon, and modern mega-cap setting is available; therefore a formal power pass is not possible.

For a transparent sensitivity analysis only, I ran `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with 30 names, 308 weekly dates (six-year design approximation), a five-session horizon/NW lag 4, date-level signal autocorrelation 0.2, 5,000 simulations, and **0.10 qualifying events per week** with event-cluster shape 0.5. The 0.10 event rate and planted IC 0.02 are assumptions, not estimates from the small 2024 sample or published literature. At this scenario, the harness’s one-sided `t ≥ 2` power was 14.08%; mean active dates were 26.75, effective dates 19.45, and the harness’s unadjusted 80%-power minimum detectable effect was 0.1117.

For the multiple-testing sensitivity, I used the conservative first Holm step for a 24-test family (one-sided α = 0.05/24; the exact current/growing R6 family was not inspected). Reusing the same simulated clustered-event design and lag-4 Newey–West statistic at the corresponding z cutoff of 2.865 gave **5.12% power** at IC 0.02 and an approximate Holm-adjusted 80%-power MDE of **0.1457**. This is a synthetic sensitivity, not an empirical statistic. A real 13D event sample may be much sparser than the assumed 0.10 events/week; the 2024 sample found no target events among its five selected names. Event overlap, SIC2 dependence, amendments, and parallel activists would further reduce effective breadth.

- **Verdict: FAIL.** There is no sourced post-filing, five-session mega-cap rank-IC effect to power, and the illustrative clustered-event sensitivity is poorly powered even before a full point-in-time roster test.

## 5. Criterion-4 portfolio feasibility first

- **Intended portfolio if the signal were eligible:** at weekly formations, rank all point-in-time Dow names by the most recent eligible 13D/13D-A accumulation disclosure and hold a long-only rank-weighted top half (15 names; ranks 1–15 weighted linearly; 55% active share) against EW30. This cheapest qualifying shape is only a planning reference; zero-event names would tie at zero, leaving the selected book driven by arbitrary tie-breaking unless a separate rule were specified.
- **Costs and turnover:** apply 0.05% per side to trades. Neither turnover nor strategy weights were computed. The synthetic Criterion-4 shape report estimates 116% weekly total-notional turnover at signal autocorrelation 0.2 for RankTopHalf, so its alternative feasibility route is cost below 25% of gross edge; that route passed only when the assumed mean rank IC was at the report’s **0.074** threshold. No effect or event coverage evidence here supports that threshold. Sudden filing-driven score changes and zero-signal ties could make realized turnover substantially different.
- **Effect-to-portfolio translation:** the published 7–8% abnormal campaign-window returns are not post-filing returns and cannot be used as gross edge for this portfolio. The literature reports positive announcement reactions and pre-filing accumulation; it does not supply the post-filing IC or return sequence needed to infer excess Sharpe, net costs, or the block-bootstrap distribution. I do not equate a campaign event-study CAR with strategy performance.
- **Fixed criterion:** the synthetic [Criterion-4 shape analysis](../report_drafts/CRITERION4_BOUND_BY_SHAPE.md) gives a 0.074 rank-IC hurdle for the weekly RankTopHalf at ρ=0.2, 55% active share, and 0.05% cost per dollar of traded notional. The fixed test still requires positive net excess return and Sharpe versus EW30 plus one-sided block-bootstrap evidence with Holm adjustment for the current/growing family; this screen does not relax those terms.
- **Verdict: FAIL — criterion-4 infeasible on available evidence.** The post-filing effect is not identified, the known event sample is too sparse in the five-name check, and no defensible evidence shows a gross edge at or above the synthetic hurdle after trading costs.

## Final screen decision

- **Overall: STOP — FAIL.** The signal substantially overlaps N2’s activist-13D campaign mechanism, removes the network-spillover element, lacks validated full-window point-in-time coverage, and has no published post-filing weekly mega-cap rank-IC effect. The small date-filtered 2024 input check confirmed no target events from metadata alone in five selected Dow names (with 49 JPM-related records unresolved by role), and synthetic power under an illustrative event-rate scenario was low. Do not register a variant, draft a preregistration, or run a portfolio.
- **What evidence could change this decision?** A complete filing-only panel with validated point-in-time target mapping and materially more large-cap campaigns; independent post-2015 sample evidence reporting tradable post-filing weekly pressure rather than pre-filing announcement CAR; and a new mechanism that is genuinely distinct from the existing activist-campaign network while clearing the fixed cluster-adjusted power and Criterion-4 bounds. None is shown here.
