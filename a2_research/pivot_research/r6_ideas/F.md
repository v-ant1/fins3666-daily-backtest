# Round F — pressure through dated business and information links

**Gate decision: none of these is eligible to advance as a new R6 direction.** This is an evidence and input-feasibility screen, not a pre-registration. It covers the three requested families, but the customer/supplier and shared-owner/analyst mechanisms repeat directions already rejected in R5/R6, while SIC4 leader-to-follower pressure is too close to the rejected SIC leader/follower design. Calling a different field, network source, or horizon “new” would not cure that overlap. I accessed no holdout data and calculated no signal returns, forward returns, ICs, or strategy outcomes.

The rankings concern the prior literature’s **gross mechanism signal**, not the odds of satisfying the project’s costs and portfolio tests. All three face the fixed criterion-4 feasibility constraint: a roughly six-year Dow top-five design should be expected to deliver excess Sharpe of about 0.8 or better after costs, with the frozen turnover/cost rule and full-family Holm correction. None has a verified Dow-sized post-cost result near that bar. These probabilities are subjective, not outputs of `power_sim.py`.

## Shared power calculation (design-only)

I used `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with a 500-name S&P cross-section, 1,470 daily formation dates as a conservative development-window approximation, 21-session forward horizon (NW lag 20), serial correlation 0.80, and cross-sectional IC standard deviation 0.08. The 0.08 is an explicit stress assumption (about 1.8 times the independent-null `1/sqrt(499)` SD) to allow for industry/network dependence; it is not estimated from project outcomes. At a **hypothetical** mean rank IC of 0.020, 1,000 seeded simulations gave power 0.925 and an 80%-power MDE of 0.0158 (effective time observations about 207). That says a 0.020 daily rank-IC effect could be detected in a broad S&P mechanism panel under those assumptions; it does not say any direction has that effect.

For the quarterly 13F network, the local graph evidence spans 23 development-quarter snapshots (2020-12 through 2026-06). As a conservative separate sensitivity, I used 500 names, 23 independent update dates, one statistic per update, the same 0.08 IC SD, rho 0.20 and hypothetical IC 0.020. Simulated power was 0.266 and the 80%-power MDE was 0.0474. Counting every daily date between quarterly network updates would create pseudo-replication unless a model justified the dependence; it is therefore not the primary owner-network power statement. The simulation has no price, signal, or return inputs.

For the annual 10-K and daily SIC4 cases, the daily 1,470-date result is only a conditional upper bound: a static/slowly updated graph does not create 1,470 independent link shocks. A credible design would need a link-cluster-aware model and a pre-set evidence-based effect size. The classic papers report portfolio spreads or return predictability, not the rank IC for the proposed CLV-pressure signals, so 0.020 is a planning sensitivity and not a conversion of their estimates.

## 1. Customer-to-supplier pressure through disclosed 10-K links

**One fixed candidate, if it were eligible:** use customer-company five-session signed CLV-dollar-volume pressure, lagged until the 10-K naming the relationship is public, as the source. Predict a positive next-21-session response among its dated suppliers, with the leave-one-out source pressure aggregated by disclosed customer revenue share. Do not add a customer-to-supplier reverse direction, Revere edges, alternative text thresholds, or another horizon after seeing data. The candidate's 21-session S&P power sensitivity is the shared daily calculation above; its practical binding gate is link coverage, not the parametric power result.

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for the link mechanism.** Cohen and Frazzini’s [“Economic Links and Predictable Returns” (*Journal of Finance*, 2008)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01379.x) use principal-customer links and report delayed cross-firm return adjustment; their broad-sample long-short alpha is not an estimate for a Dow pressure signal. Menzly and Ozbas’ [“Market Segmentation and Cross-predictability of Returns” (*Journal of Finance*, 2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01578.x) find customer/supplier industry cross-predictability and explicitly report weaker effects when analyst coverage and institutional ownership are higher. As later adjacent evidence, Agarwal, Qian, and Zou’s [“Disaggregated Sales and Stock Returns” (*Management Science*, 2021)](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2020.3813) find customer-spending information predicts 60-day returns and extends along production chains; it uses private-card spending, not public 10-K links or buying pressure.
- **(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 survival — FAIL / evidence against.** The fundamental demand-information mechanism can survive for important customer relationships, and the later customer-spending paper shows a production-chain response in a sample that includes large firms. But Menzly–Ozbas find attenuation with analyst/institutional attention, Cohen–Frazzini report faster price convergence in larger firms, and the 2021 paper says delays are more pronounced for small firms. No verified study establishes a post-2020 Dow-sized continuation effect from disclosed 10-K links. The pressure-to-peer step is an additional untested extrapolation.
- **(c) Power — conditional pass for a hypothetical mechanism size; practical gate fails.** `power_sim.py`: S&P 500 cross-section, 1,470 daily dates, 21-session horizon/NW lag 20, `ic_sd=0.08`, rho 0.80, planted IC 0.020: power 0.925; 80% MDE 0.0158. This is conditional on 1,470 usable cross-sections. It cannot compensate for absent links or prove the hypothetical effect plausible. The literature does not provide a same-statistic pressure IC to plant. The current Dow implementation would also need a disclosed-link cohort and a turnover cap; the network is too sparse to assume a concentrated top-five can be formed.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — FAIL.** The local input-only `r4_L4/data/customer_supplier_status.csv` reports `rows_built=0` and says Dow 10-K major-customer relationships from the development window were not systematically coded. SEC EDGAR and company filing-history APIs are free ([SEC developer resources](https://www.sec.gov/about/developer-resources)), but the link source is not a ready panel: only material dependencies are discussed, identities can be withheld or described generically, and ticker/CIK resolution must be dated. The 2020 SEC modernization of Item 101 also makes historical disclosure conventions nonuniform ([SEC rule guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/modernization-regulation-s-k-items-101-103-105-small-entity-compliance-guide)). No filings were scraped for outcome work; the cheap pull was the local status/coverage check only. UNSW FactSet/Revere would be a different, licensed link dataset and cannot be substituted after the gate.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL by exclusion.** The disclosed customer/supplier link and return-transfer family overlaps R5 D7 and the previously reviewed B supplier/customer direction. Changing the shock to CLV pressure may be a different measured source variable, but it does not make the rejected business-link family an eligible new R6 idea. This cannot be advanced under the explicit exclusion.

## 2. Shared 13F-owner pressure network

**One fixed candidate, if it were eligible:** update the graph only when filings are public. Give target *i* the leave-one-out mean of other holdings’ five-session signed CLV-dollar-volume pressure, weighted by the shared active 13F-manager overlap measured from the latest available reports. Positive peer buying pressure predicts positive 21-session target return. A quarter’s position date is not its availability date: use each manager’s actual filed date, respect late filings/amendments, and never use a quarter’s positions before the filing. No passive-manager label or extra flow-based source is assumed.

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for common-holder flow pressure.** Antón and Polk’s [“Connected Stocks” (*Journal of Finance*, 2014)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12149) connect stocks through common active mutual-fund owners and find shared ownership forecasts return comovement; their evidence also links common ownership to cross-stock reversal, making a continuation claim uncertain. Coval and Stafford’s [“Asset Fire Sales (and Purchases) in Equity Markets” (*Journal of Financial Economics*, 2007)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2006.09.007) show fund flows create pressure in common holdings. Greenwood and Thesmar’s [“Stock Price Fragility” (*Journal of Financial Economics*, 2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.06.003) show correlated owner liquidity shocks make assets susceptible to common demand shifts.
- **(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 survival — plausible structure, no qualifying evidence.** Large liquid firms still share active institutions, and correlated fund redemptions or deployment can move several co-held positions together; a network may therefore spread flow pressure beyond a source stock. But 13F is a stale quarterly snapshot, not a trade tape; mega-cap ownership and news are highly monitored, and Antón–Polk document comovement/reversal rather than post-2020 continuation from source buying pressure. No verified post-2020 evidence reaches Dow-sized net alpha.
- **(c) Power — FAIL on graph-update cadence; daily version is only optimistic.** The hypothetical daily S&P case is power 0.925 at IC 0.020 under the stressed inputs above. However, the locally verified topology has 23 usable quarterly snapshots. One statistic per quarterly graph update gives only 0.266 simulated power at 0.020 (80% MDE 0.0474). Treating daily observations under a fixed quarterly graph as independent would inflate power; the simulation does not model shared-fund/industry cluster dependence. Since 13F flow/ownership studies do not report a matching pressure-rank IC, there is no defensible effect size that clears the conservative calculation.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — PARTIAL, not S&P-ready.** The SEC publishes free structured [Form 13F data sets](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/form-13f-data-sets); the SEC readme says its XML-derived holdings begin in May 2013 ([readme](https://www.sec.gov/files/form_13f_readme.pdf)), and filings may be submitted up to 45 days after each quarter ([SEC FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f)). In the local development-only `r4_L4/data/13f_development_coverage.csv`, I filtered to report dates 2020-12-31 through 2026-06-30: **23 quarters, 50 selected manager rows each, 153 distinct managers**, with 6,401–14,418 mapped Dow position rows per quarter. This is an input-coverage count, not a signal or outcome. The staged file is selected top managers and Dow-only; it does not establish the full S&P 500 network, all filers, complete CUSIP lineage, or actual contemporaneous trading. A full SEC bulk pull is feasible but the filing lag and manager selection still bind.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL by exclusion.** R5 D3/D4 and R6’s prior common-owner directions already tested fund-flow/common-owner pressure and CLV diffusion. P4 separately screened passive ownership and found manager-level 13F cannot identify passive mandates. This candidate retains the same common-owner mechanism and is not a fresh direction; it is not revived here.

## 3. SIC4 large-firm pressure leading smaller same-industry stocks

**One fixed candidate, if it were eligible:** on each development day, compute a value-weighted five-session signed CLV-dollar-volume pressure shock among the largest market-cap tercile of S&P 500 firms in each dated SIC4 group. Predict positive next-21-session returns among the smaller SIC4 peers. Exclude a leader from its own follower bucket. Use point-in-time SIC4, membership, market capitalization and trading availability. The fixed sign is positive continuation; Hou’s result on lagged returns, especially negative information, does not itself prove that a positive buying-pressure shock has that sign.

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for large-to-small return lead-lags, not for this signal.** Hou’s [“Industry Information Diffusion and the Lead-lag Effect in Stock Returns” (*Review of Financial Studies*, 2007)](https://doi.org/10.1093/revfin/hhm003) reports large firms lead small firms mainly within industries, with the effect stronger in small, neglected industries and driven by sluggish response to negative information. Chordia and Swaminathan’s [“Trading Volume and Cross-Autocorrelations in Stock Returns” (*Journal of Finance*, 2000)](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00231) connect volume to lead-lag cross-autocorrelations. Lo and MacKinlay’s [“When Are Contrarian Profits Due to Stock Market Overreaction?” (*Review of Financial Studies*, 1990)](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/3.2.175) establish that cross-autocovariances can generate apparent contrarian profits even when individual returns are not negatively autocorrelated. These papers motivate a directional network but they do not estimate CLV/volume buying pressure transfer.
- **(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 survival — FAIL / adverse extrapolation.** Information about a major firm can still affect a smaller peer through shared customers, input costs or industry demand. The counter-evidence is stronger for this exact target: Hou’s mechanism is more pronounced in small/neglected industries and concerns a historical return lead-lag; Menzly–Ozbas’ newer customer/supplier evidence similarly attenuates with informed-investor coverage. The Dow’s mega-cap firms have dense coverage and may mostly be leaders, leaving very few smaller same-industry followers. I found no verified post-2020 Dow-scale pressure-spillover effect.
- **(c) Power — conditional pass at a hypothetical daily IC, but no supported effect size.** `power_sim.py` at S&P N=500, n=1,470 daily dates, 21-session horizon/NW lag 20, `ic_sd=0.08`, rho 0.80 and planted IC 0.020 gives power 0.925 and 80% MDE 0.0158. This assumes valid SIC4 source/follower pairs exist on every date. It does not account for clustered sources or scarce Dow followers, and the underlying papers do not report a comparable signed-pressure IC. The numbers are a synthetic sensitivity only.
- **(d) Cheap free-data feasibility — PARTIAL.** The local `r6_data/sp500_pit_roster.csv` plus dated `r6_data/sp500_sic_panel.csv` permit an input-only development check. I used quarter-end dates from 2020-12-31 through 2026-09-30 and only SIC effective dates on or after 2020-11-27. Active S&P roster size was 503–505; valid dated SIC covered 417 names at 2021-03-31 and generally 497–502 thereafter. There were about 180 SIC4 groups per later snapshot, with 82–95 singleton groups and median group sizes only 1–2. These data identify that broad estimation is technically buildable; they do not establish a tradable Dow follower cohort. Current local free inputs contain no verified daily shares-outstanding market-cap panel aligned to these SIC4 dates; SEC XBRL company facts or FactSet would be needed for point-in-time size ranks. No price values or returns were opened for this coverage check.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL by exclusion.** R5 D5 already tested large-to-small SIC leader/follower pressure. This proposal would change SIC resolution to SIC4 and fix a 21-session horizon, but it preserves the same directed industry leader-to-follower architecture. It therefore falls under the “anything already rejected” exclusion and cannot be called new.

## Decision

All requested families have 2+ verified literature references and a numeric S&P planning check, but none clears all gates. Supplier/customer links fail actual local link coverage and repeat D7; shared 13F links are only partially covered and repeat common-owner tests; SIC4 pressure lead-lag has small-group/input issues and repeats D5. The full-goal likelihood is effectively negligible under the fixed criterion-4 bar, even where the hypothetical mechanism simulation has power. Do not freeze a variant or run a return test from this memo; a genuinely new family or user-approved change to the fixed criteria is required.
