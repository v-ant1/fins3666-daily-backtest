## (a) Literature verification and what it actually supports

- Lou, Polk and Skouras, [“A Tug of War: Overnight versus Intraday Expected Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 134 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.03.011), is real and its DOI resolves. It studies US stocks over 1993–2013, excludes microcaps, and reports one-month continuation in each component plus cross-period reversal. Its large-stock momentum result has 1.24% monthly close-to-close profit, with 0.80% overnight (t=2.50) and 0.44% intraday (t=1.24); this is a market-wide momentum sort, not CLV pressure diffusing over industry peers. The paper itself warns that transaction costs make exploiting the patterns less attractive.
- Heston, Korajczyk and Sadka, [“Intraday Patterns in the Cross-section of Stock Returns,” *Journal of Finance* 65 (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x), is real and its DOI resolves. It documents same-time-of-day half-hour return continuation that lasts at least 40 trading days, while noting that volume, order imbalance, volatility and spreads do not explain that return pattern. It supports intraday periodicity, not daily OHLCV as signed order flow or linked-firm spillover.
- The more directly relevant study is Huaixin Wang, [“Decoding Momentum Spillover Effects,” *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 61(5), 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/S002210902510238X), published online 11 November 2025 and in the August 2026 issue. It uses monthly peer intraday and overnight returns, linked firms, future focal-firm day/night returns, own-stock component controls, and reports industry links among alternative link definitions. Its core result is asymmetric: peer intraday returns predict focal intraday returns, while peer overnight returns are followed by overnight continuation and intraday reversal that largely cancel in close-to-close returns. This is directly on the same overnight/intraday linked-firm question and includes a close analogue of the proposed industry network.

Thus the minimum “two verified papers” count is met, but the paper pair originally cited is weaker than C implies for the actual network mechanism. The direct 2026 predecessor is an essential citation and makes a generic industry-peer extension non-novel.

## (b) Post-2020 mega-cap case

C's argument has one real supporting point: Lou et al. report that the overnight/intraday spread in momentum is stronger for large stocks, and their sample ends in 2013. That does not establish that an OHLCV proxy has a post-2020 expected-return edge, and a large-stock gross alpha is not proof of a tradable net strategy.

Wang's linked-stock sample ends December 2021 and includes economically linked-firm tests, with industry among alternative connections; it therefore provides some post-2010 and early post-2020 linked-stock evidence. But it is not evidence for this project's daily CLV construction, late-2021 through 2026 persistence, five-day horizon, Dow top-five portfolio, or the specific assertion that an industry graph adds information beyond own overnight/intraday return components. The current case should be “untested and possibly weakened by faster arbitrage,” not “likely survives.”

## (c) Recomputed power with the harness

C1 does not freeze a primary horizon. I use **five sessions** as the explicit conditional case because its cost discussion is weekly and its data description defines daily overnight/intraday inputs. This is a sensitivity calculation, not authorization to choose a horizon after seeing outcomes. I used 1,500 date observations, horizon 5 (Newey–West Bartlett lag 4), AR(1) date-series dependence `rho=0.40` borrowed from C's stated assumption, positive mean IC of 0.0183 and 0.026 (the dossier's proposed range), 2,000 Monte Carlo replications, fixed seeds `20263440` (S&P .0183), `20264210` (S&P .026), `20262970` (Dow .0183), and `20263740` (Dow .026), and the simulator's null-rank IC SD approximation `1/sqrt(N-1)`. No return or project IC was supplied to the simulator.

| Universe for mechanism estimate / trading check | IC SD assumption | Mean effective N | 80% MDE | P(t ≥ 2), IC=.0183 | P(t ≥ 2), IC=.026 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S&P 500-sized (500 names) | 0.0448 | 792.2 | 0.00452 | 1.000 (SE 0) | 1.000 (SE 0) |
| Dow 30-sized (30 names) | 0.1857 | 792.2 | 0.01875 | 0.769 (SE 0.0094) | 0.963 (SE 0.0042) |

The S&P row is only the harness's optimistic baseline: it treats the null cross-sectional rank-IC SD as `1/sqrt(N-1)` and does not cluster shocks by date, industry, or linked neighborhood. A reported probability of 1.000 means all 2,000 draws detected; it is not certainty (the approximate one-sided 95% lower bound is 0.998). Since C itself says those clusters matter, the S&P number cannot be presented as a passing cluster-adjusted power analysis. The Dow row misses 80% power at the lower IC target and clears it at 0.026 in this simplified daily-series model; at that lower target, the portfolio claim would need much stronger evidence than a noisy Dow-only mechanism estimate. As a check on the dossier's stated **weekly** setup, its own 307 weekly observations and `rho=.40` give `N_eff = 307*(1-.4)/(1+.4) = 131.6`; with IC SD `.20`, its stated approximate 80%-power threshold is `2.8*.20/sqrt(131.6) = .0488`, far above `.0183–.026`. These are different sampling assumptions, so they should not be blended into one power claim.

Reproduction from project root (synthetic assumptions only):

```powershell
python r4_harness/power_sim.py --assets 500 --dates 1500 --horizon 5 --signal-autocorrelation 0.4 --mean-ic 0.0183 --simulations 2000 --seed 20263440
python r4_harness/power_sim.py --assets 500 --dates 1500 --horizon 5 --signal-autocorrelation 0.4 --mean-ic 0.026 --simulations 2000 --seed 20264210
python r4_harness/power_sim.py --assets 30 --dates 1500 --horizon 5 --signal-autocorrelation 0.4 --mean-ic 0.0183 --simulations 2000 --seed 20262970
python r4_harness/power_sim.py --assets 30 --dates 1500 --horizon 5 --signal-autocorrelation 0.4 --mean-ic 0.026 --simulations 2000 --seed 20263740
```

For the `.026` column, change `--mean-ic` to `0.026` and use the seeds shown in the review run if exact Monte Carlo replication is needed: S&P 500 seed `20264210`, Dow seed `20263740`. The model is suitable for a first sensitivity calculation, not the industry/date block simulation required by C.

## Cost and turnover check

Under C's **assumptions only** (weekly single-stock SD 4%, top-five-vs-universe selection coefficient 1.4, Spearman IC .0183), the near-zero conversion is `rho_P = 2 sin(pi*rho_S/6) = 0.01916`. Implied gross top-five active return is `0.01916 * 4% * 1.4 = 0.1073%`, or **10.73 bp per week**. At 5 bp per side and 50% one-way turnover, buys equal 50% of NAV and sells equal 50%, hence 100% total traded notional and **5 bp cost**; net is 5.73 bp and cost/gross is 46.6%. If the top five are entirely replaced, one-way turnover is 100%, total buys plus sells are 200% NAV, cost is **10 bp**, and net is only 0.73 bp. At Spearman IC .026, the same mapping yields roughly 15.25 bp gross; full-replacement costs leave only about 5.25 bp.

A five-stock equal-weight portfolio versus EW30 has 83.3% active share (`0.5 * [5*(20%-3.33%) + 25*3.33%]`), so it clears the R6 50% active-share rule. The arithmetic in C's shared cost convention is therefore internally correct, including the 50% turnover terminology if it means one-way turnover. But a 46.6% cost/gross ratio is not the R6 alternative threshold of costs below 25% of gross edge; the trade would have to pass via measured average turnover ≤50%. No turnover is measured here (nor can it be without outcomes). The 4% weekly volatility, top-five coefficient, actual weekly turnover, fills, and edge are assumptions or unknowns, not evidence. A generic description of an industry-peer ranking is not enough to infer top-five turnover or validate that gross IC-to-return mapping. Also define whether `.0183` is the actual primary IC hurdle or a cost-derived approximate threshold; the same value cannot simultaneously stand in for both a demonstrated predictive effect and an assumed portfolio conversion.

The SEC says its public submissions history and company filings are available through data APIs; its submission dataset documents SIC “as of the filing date” ([SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces), [SEC Financial Statement Data Sets](https://www.sec.gov/files/fsds.pdf)). So a dated SIC assignment appears feasible from filing headers, but C has not built or coverage-tested this join. Before the power result is treated as an S&P feasibility pass, the team needs the daily development OHLCV panel, survivorship/delisting and ticker-identity checks, and dated SIC coverage by active member. ## (e) Novelty and proposal-specific blockers

C describes the novelty as source-stock overnight/intraday “pressure” forecasting subsequent returns among same-industry peers beyond own-stock persistence. That is too close to Wang (2026), who decomposes peer returns into overnight/intraday, predicts focal returns by component, controls own components, and checks alternative links including industry. C offers a daily CLV framing and a proposed Dow portfolio, but no explicit formula or argument that CLV adds information beyond the return components that the published study already tests. Calling an industry label a network does not by itself create a new mechanism.

C1 also lacks a frozen direction, primary outcome frequency, and horizon. It alternates between “persistence and/or spreads,” a weekly IC calculation, and unspecified subsequent 1–10 day peer response, so there is no one pre-outcome sign/horizon against which to evaluate power or novelty. Daily OHLCV does not measure signed buyer-initiated trades, and industry similarity can simply capture shared news or industry momentum; the own-return controls and within-industry/random-link placebos must be defined in advance.

## Exact conditions to reconsider C1

Keep the current **FAIL-GATE** label unless all of the following are met before any outcome test:

1. Rewrite the novelty claim against Wang (2026) and specify a genuinely distinct empirical object, such as a pre-defined CLV buying-pressure shock that is not simply peer intraday/overnight return, with an explicit reason it should add information beyond both peer and focal components. If the proposed result is merely a daily-frequency / Dow rerun of the published spillover, stop treating it as a novel candidate.
2. Freeze one predicted sign, one horizon and sampling schedule (daily overlapping 5-session vs weekly non-overlapping) plus one primary IC/event statistic. State exactly how own-stock return components, market/industry returns, dated SIC, and placebo links are handled.
3. Complete a no-outcome S&P feasibility manifest: available daily bars by date and point-in-time member, historical ticker/issuer mapping, dated SIC coverage and sector/industry counts. The existing roster alone is not enough.
4. Replace the S&P IID-cross-section power check with a synthetic or literature-calibrated date-and-industry-block simulation at the locked effect, signal dependence, number of independent industries and actual roster sizes. It must reach 80% power for the pre-registered mechanism statistic; report Monte Carlo error. Separately state the lower-powered Dow trading design honestly.
5. Freeze the portfolio rule and its actual-turnover definition. The assumed top-five gross-edge map is only an order-of-magnitude hurdle. To use the R6 turnover branch, the realized (later) mean one-way turnover must be ≤50% per rebalance; otherwise show costs below 25% of gross edge, then still meet net-excess/Sharpe, Holm-adjusted bootstrap and DSR gates.
6. Preregister no more than three variants and ensure the fresh runner diff / execution guards are in place before any outcome access. Never use the holdout for idea repair.

## Brief confirmation: C2 and C3 remain FAIL-GATE

- **C2, month-end cash-demand / linked equities:** fail. [Etula et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhz054) supports settlement-cycle liquidity pressure and turn-of-month reversals, and [Lakonishok and Smidt (1988)](https://doi.org/10.1086/296445) documents historical Dow seasonal patterns, but approximately 72 monthly events are not enough at the dossier's few-basis-point plausible effect under its own cluster assumptions. The direct institutional cash-flow measure and a causal firm-to-peer network are not available in the proposal; a calendar/sector proxy would not establish the claimed pressure mechanism.
- **C3, deep-in-the-money call exercise at expiration:** fail. [Chen and Lu (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2014.03.003) report a narrowly conditional approximately 0.8% expiration-day decline among stocks with sufficiently high deep-ITM call open interest; [Roll, Schwartz and Subrahmanyam (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2004.08.005) find expiration-date strike clustering and an average underlying-return impact of at least 16.5 bp in their 1996?2002 sample; C?s description of that paper as showing only ?limited evidence? of underlying price impact is inaccurate. The study?s mechanism (hedge rebalancing/price clustering) is still different from Chen and Lu?s conditional deep-ITM call-exercise selling-pressure result, and neither establishes current post-2020 peer transmission. A free, validated point-in-time historical option OI panel for the target period is not staged, and neither cited result establishes post-2020 mega-cap/network persistence. It is therefore blocked by input feasibility and lacks a credible linked-peer novelty case.
