# R6 Idea Gate Z — closing-auction and end-of-day pressure

**Status: FAIL-GATE all three; do not freeze or spend a variant.** I ranked by the best plausible documented edge rather than ease of data access. The review uses literature plus the staged **2024 Alpaca sample files only** for feasibility; I did not calculate sample returns, signals, predictive statistics, or portfolio outcomes, and I did not access holdout data.

|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Last-half-hour stock-level return reversal / persistence | **FAIL-GATE** | <1% | Recent work finds a small late-day cross-sectional reversal, but the largest-stock result is weak and it is not an after-cost Dow result; the one-minute sample does not contain the final half-hour. |
| 2 | Signed closing-auction imbalance as pressure | **FAIL-GATE** | <1% | Direct pressure/reversion mechanism, but Alpaca's close-auction endpoint returned no close array in the 2024 sample, and closing-cross prints reveal size without buy/sell side. |
| 3 | Same-slot last-half-hour momentum from Gao et al., cross-sectionally adapted | **FAIL-GATE** | <1% | The cited result is market-level ETF intraday predictability, not stock-level pressure; the cross-sectional extension has no compatible effect estimate and the saved bars are not near the close. |

These are subjective full-stack probabilities, not statistical estimates. No candidate clears the data, novelty, timing, or criterion-4 feasibility screen.

## Criterion-4 and common power screen

The independent [criterion-4 bound audit](../report_drafts/CRITERION4_BOUND_AUDIT.md) estimates a required **0.083–0.089 rank IC** for a long-only top-five Dow book versus EW30 in its monthly 20%-turnover illustration, corresponding to annual active Sharpe about **1.00–1.05** before dependence penalties. That is a planning lower bound under that stated design, not a universal number for a 1-day signal. None of the auction or intraday papers reports a post-2020 Dow top-five rank IC or a net active Sharpe that can be compared directly with it.

The closest fresh cross-sectional scale evidence is a working paper discussed below: it reports a 3.05 bp/day reversal for a sample that excludes the heavily traded names, versus only 0.74 bp/day on the full index in 2020 onward. These are return spreads, not rank ICs, so I do **not** convert them to the audit's IC metric. They are nevertheless small in daily trading-cost terms: a 50% total-notional daily turnover cap costs up to 2.5 bp NAV each session, or 6.3% simple annualized, at 5 bp per traded dollar; a full daily entry and exit costs at least 10 bp before impact. The 3.05 bp subset is not a Dow estimate, and the 0.74 bp full-index scale is below the per-session cost at the turnover cap. Thus the source evidence does not make a Dow excess Sharpe near 1.0 credible. I cannot give a defensible numerical expected Dow Sharpe from these literature spreads and 2024 access probes alone.

For a daily cross-sectional mechanism test, I ran the harness's synthetic planning simulation with 500 nominal stocks, 54 effective SIC2 groups, within-group IC correlation 0.15 (assumption), `IC SD=0.20538`, 1,488 daily dates, AR(1)=0.20, and a five-lag-dependence setting (`horizon=5`, NW lag 4). It uses only synthetic Gaussian draws. At planted IC 0.0183, `P(NW t ≥ 2)` was **81.76%** (20,000 simulations; MC SE 0.00273), effective N **1,082.15**, and 80%-power MDE **0.01774**; at the criterion-4 audit's 0.083 and 0.089 IC levels, power was **100%** in 5,000 draws each. This is a sensitivity calculation, not observed power for the signals; event and ticker dependence beyond the SIC2 stress could reduce it. A detectable mechanism at 0.0183 would still be far below the audit's portfolio feasibility threshold.

The pre-2020 history limitation is material: Alpaca's documentation/audit lists equity history beginning in **2016**. That would leave the first six calendar years of the project's 2010–2020 sealed holdout uncovered, so an Alpaca-only test could not satisfy the complete holdout gate. This is a vendor-history note only; I did not inspect any holdout observations.

## 1. End-of-day cross-sectional reversal (highest expected edge of these three)

**Candidate definition.** At 3:30 p.m. ET on session `t`, rank Dow stocks by their point-in-time return over the preceding regular-session interval ending at 3:30 p.m.; predict that the intraday losers reverse relative to winners over the final 30 minutes and/or following session. The return to the prior close must not be credited to a trade formed at 3:30. A second, distinct auction-pressure variant would use the last pre-auction signed imbalance and forecast next-session reversal, but that is already separately screened as T2 and is not a rescue.

### Gates (a)–(e)

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for the phenomenon, partial for a tradable next-day signal.** Baltussen, Da, and Soebhag, [“End-of-Day Reversal” (2024 working paper, revised 2025)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/cf_dev/AbsByAuth.cfm?per_id=366576), describe a cross-sectional last-half-hour reversal through December 2019, concentrated in positive price pressure on intraday losers. Vidovich and Zerjal's [6 November 2026 preprint, “The End-of-Day Reversal After 2019”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09286), reports 3.05 bp/day for 2023–2026 in S&P constituents excluding about 80 heavily traded names, but only 0.74 bp/day for the full index from 2020 onward; the authors say the full-index weakness is concentrated in the very largest stocks and their pre-registered mechanism test does not pass. Cushing and Madhavan, [“Stock Returns and Trading at the Close,” *Journal of Financial Markets* 3 (2000), 45–67](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1386-4181(99)00012-9), find reversal after NYSE MOC imbalance indications, including next-day reversal. The first two papers support late-day cross-sectional price pressure; they do not establish a next-open-to-close tradable Dow edge.
- **(b) Mega-cap / post-2020 case — FAIL.** The updated preprint includes 2023–2026 evidence, but its main estimate intentionally drops heavily traded stocks and the full-index spread is much smaller; its descriptive comparison says the loss of strength is concentrated in the largest names. This is directly adverse to Dow transport. Cushing and Madhavan use 1997–1998 market structure and are not evidence of persistence in post-2020 mega-caps. The closing window is liquid and easy to execute, but that also compresses price deviations and raises the hurdle for an after-cost anomaly.
- **(c) Power — FAIL for portfolio feasibility.** The shared SIC2 `power_sim` run shows that 1,488 daily observations could detect a 0.0183 IC with about 82% synthetic power and that a 0.083 IC is easily detectable. The published full-index 0.74 bp/day return spread has no valid conversion to IC, however, and the effect is weakest in the target's largest names. The criterion-4 audit requires roughly 0.083–0.089 IC in its long-only design, for which no evidence is available. Power therefore does not fix the effect-size gap.
- **(d) 2024 sample feasibility — FAIL for the required window.** The saved `bars_1min_dow_2024.json` includes only a short 2024-06-03 slice (six one-minute rows each for AAPL, CAT, and JNJ); it is not a full session or final-half-hour archive. The 2024 closing-trade sample is a single AAPL close window, not a daily cross-section. These files demonstrate endpoint/schema access only and cannot establish six-year point-in-time coverage or estimate effects. The strategy also has to enter before the final interval to capture same-day reversal; buying at the next open would miss that part.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL.** `r6_ideas/T_daily.md` already screened the same intraday return-continuation and signed closing-auction reversal directions (T1/T2). The new end-of-day reversal paper improves the literature context but does not create a new signal family; a turn-of-close sort is a near duplicate of those screens. A separate late-day reversal test cannot be used to re-label or rescue the same outcome family.

**Verdict: KILL.** The newest cross-sectional evidence is specifically weaker in the most heavily traded names, does not clear the audited criterion-4 scale, and lacks saved full-window data.

## 2. Closing-auction imbalance / close volume pressure

**Candidate definition.** Use the last eligible pre-close SIP imbalance snapshot, signed buy-minus-sell quantity scaled by paired quantity or normal daily volume, to predict a same-direction close continuation or next-session reversal. Sign and timing must be frozen: a closing-cross print size alone is not imbalance and cannot determine the sign of pressure.

### Gates (a)–(e)

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for the auction mechanism, not for the exact free proxy.** Bogousslavsky and Muravyev, [“Who Trades at the Close? Implications for Price Discovery and Liquidity,” *Journal of Financial Markets* 66 (2023), 100852](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.finmar.2023.100852), report that U.S. closing auctions rose from 3.1% of daily volume in 2010 to 7.5% in 2018, usually match near the pre-close bid or ask with lower impact than continuous trading, and auction price deviations reverse quickly and almost completely on average. They also link auction-to-intraday volume growth to indexing and ETFs. Cushing and Madhavan (2000, linked above) document next-day reversals after published NYSE MOC imbalance indications. These results support real closing pressure, but they describe exchange imbalance feeds or market-wide auction behavior, not a freely reproducible six-year cross-sectional signal.
- **(b) Mega-cap / post-2020 case — PARTIAL mechanism, FAIL edge.** The 2023 paper studies the modern high-volume U.S. auction regime; large, index-heavy names participate in sizable auctions. But it concludes price impact is generally low and deviations revert quickly, which makes the opportunity short and competitive. There is no verified post-2020 Dow abnormal-return spread conditional on a free signed imbalance series. A pressure effect that completes in the auction cannot be captured by an order entered after observing the final imbalance.
- **(c) Power — FAIL for criterion 4.** The daily synthetic IC simulation has 81.76% power at planted IC 0.0183 and 100% at 0.083, but the auction papers do not supply a matching Dow cross-sectional rank IC. “Almost complete” deviation reversion is not evidence for 0.083 IC or a net Sharpe near 1.0. For a signal learned only after close, next-open entry additionally misses overnight reversal. The rate of event-day exposure in the Dow cannot be calibrated from the one-date sample without calculating outcomes or expanding data access.
- **(d) 2024 Alpaca feasibility — FAIL for signed closing imbalance.** In the three 2024 `auctions_*.json` probes, the dedicated auction endpoint returned one record per symbol but the close-auction array was null; the audit found opening-auction records instead. The 2024 SIP close-trade sample returned 684 prints in a three-second window for AAPL and retained three candidate closing-auction-condition records, but these prints report price/size/condition, not buy-versus-sell imbalance. The 2024 one-minute files do not provide repeated closing-auction observations. Alpaca's trade, auction, and bar references document the available fields ([trades](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stocktradesingle-1), [auctions](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockauctionsingle-1), [bars](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/stockbarsingle-1)); these small 2024 probes do not establish a usable historical imbalance panel.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL.** `r6_ideas/T_daily.md` T2 already specified a signed closing-auction imbalance with a next-session reversal sign, correctly distinguishing it from unsigned daily volume. `r6_ideas/M_SURVEY.md` also lists auction reversal/index pressure as previously considered and overlapping excluded ETF/index-flow families. The Alpaca data-access audit changes no part of that novelty ruling.

**Verdict: KILL.** The 2024 samples fail the essential sign field and the prior direction is already screened.

## 3. Cross-sectional same-slot intraday momentum

**Candidate definition.** At the end of a session, rank a stock on its prior-session final-half-hour return and predict continuation in that same half-hour on the next session, using point-in-time constituents. The target is the following session's final half-hour, so entry can occur before that interval; it is not the Gao market timing trade and it needs full close-adjacent minute bars.

### Gates (a)–(e)

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for intraday predictability, PARTIAL for stock-level buying pressure.** Gao, Han, Li, and Zhou, [“Market Intraday Momentum,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 129 (2018), 394–414](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.05.009), use high-frequency S&P 500 ETF data from 1993–2013 and find that the first half-hour return predicts the final half-hour return, with stronger predictability on high-volatility, high-volume, recession, and major-news days. That is a **market-level same-day** timing effect, not cross-sectional continuation among Dow stocks. Heston, Korajczyk, and Sadka, [“Intraday Patterns in the Cross-Section of Stock Returns,” *Journal of Finance* 65 (2010), 1369–1407](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x), find cross-sectional continuation in same half-hour intervals across trading days, but report that order imbalance, volume, volatility, and spreads do not explain the effect. It is adjacent evidence, not proof that the last half-hour is a buying-pressure signal.
- **(b) Mega-cap / post-2020 case — FAIL.** Gao et al. cover market ETF behavior through 2013; Heston et al. document historical cross-stock periodicity, but neither shows that this exact late-slot score survived among Dow mega-caps after 2020. The phenomenon could reflect routine intraday flow timing, but the published mechanism does not isolate buy pressure or provide a post-2020 cross-sectional Dow effect.
- **(c) Power — FAIL at the criterion-4 scale.** The synthetic daily-date test is not slot-specific and cannot infer the effect size of this adapted score. At 0.0183, the planning power is 81.76%, but the criterion-4 audit's 0.083–0.089 IC floor is 4.5–4.9 times larger; no literature result gives a compatible late-slot Dow IC. Repeated daily slot trading at 50% total turnover could cost up to 6.3% simple annualized, before auction spread or market impact. The papers do not establish an edge large enough to clear those hurdles.
- **(d) 2024 sample feasibility — FAIL for the signal window.** The saved 2024 SIP 1-minute bars contain only a five-minute interval on 2024-06-03: five rows for AAPL and MSFT in `bars_1min_2024.json`, and six rows each for AAPL, CAT, and JNJ in `bars_1min_dow_2024.json`. The requested interval is not the final half-hour before close. Separate daily bars cover only three 2024 sessions for three names. This confirms that the API can return intraday bars but does not verify the necessary full-session sample or daily point-in-time Dow cross-section. Feasibility was checked on these 2024 samples only; no bar returns were calculated.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL.** The exact recurring same-slot stock-return idea is already in `r6_ideas/T_daily.md` T1. Gao's market-ETF first-half-hour signal is distinct in its source feature, but adapting it cross-sectionally still lacks direct cross-sectional buying-pressure evidence and does not satisfy the requested novelty bar. No new R6 variant is justified.

**Verdict: KILL.** The cited papers study related but different objects, and the available 2024 bars do not contain the needed closing interval.
