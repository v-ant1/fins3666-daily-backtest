# Round 6 idea gate — N: two remaining event-pressure channels

**Status: no credible candidate clears all gates; do not spend a variant or open an outcome test.** This note proposes two mechanisms distinct from the tested and screened R5/R6 directions, but both fail the current S&P 500 SIC2-cluster power screen and have unresolved free-input coverage. They are ranked only against each other. Probabilities below are judgmental chances of passing the full R6 mechanism, portfolio, correction, turnover and sealed-holdout stack, not statistical power.

**Scope.** I used the R5/R6 reports and decision log, published sources, and synthetic calls to `r4_harness/power_sim.py`. I did not load price or return panels, form signals, count events in project data, or access holdout data. No outcomes were computed.

## Common design and cluster-power screen

Both ideas would use a single monthly formation and next-month return horizon, with no parameter sweep. If an idea ever cleared its input and power gates, its Dow portfolio would be long-only top 8, equal-weighted at target, and limited to **20% one-way turnover per monthly rebalance**. The turnover cap is enforced on submitted trades. No leverage or shorting. A later portfolio test would still need the goal's active-share, 0.05%/side cost, EW30, Sharpe, block-bootstrap/Holm and frozen-holdout gates; a low turnover cap does not imply those gates pass.

For the mechanism power screen, use the S&P 500 PIT cross-section as required, approximately 499 covered names, 54 SIC2 groups and 9.24 names per group. The synthetic cluster-adjusted per-date IC SD is `sqrt((1 + (9.24 − 1) × 0.15) / (54 − 1)) = 0.20538`, assuming within-SIC2 correlation 0.15. This is an explicit planning assumption, not estimated from project returns. A month is one observation; `horizon=1` avoids imposing a 20-month Bartlett lag on non-overlapping monthly outcomes, while `signal_autocorrelation=0.2` retains serial dependence. The 72-date development calendar is a ceiling; the incident event gate will reduce it. The minimum detectable effect is already too large under the optimistic full-calendar design.

If a future analysis uses non-event months as zero-signal months, it must register that estimand and its variance rather than presenting the event-only power as unconditional power.

## Rank 1 — N1: cybersecurity breach pressure spills to unaffected rivals

**Subjective full-stack pass chance: 1%.** Best of these two only because breach events plausibly recur more often than large-cap activist campaigns and the related-firm response has empirical support. Still not credible to advance: the literature shows heterogeneous immediate reactions, the peer sign is not settled, event coverage is unknown, and design-matched power is very low.

### Gates

**(a) Mechanism and evidence.** A material breach can create selling pressure in the breached firm and reveal common cyber or reputation risk to peers; a competing channel is customer substitution that benefits rivals. Kamiya et al. report negative industry effects following successful attacks involving personal financial data ([DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.05.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.05.019)); rival trading volume rises after a focal breach in another study ([DOI 10.1016/j.jaccpubpol.2021.106916](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaccpubpol.2021.106916)). Those results support a peer-response mechanism, but do not establish persistent or tradable monthly pressure in current Dow constituents. A recent review describes both contagion and substitution findings, so a fixed sign is a real risk ([DOI 10.1177/03128962241293658](https://doi.org/10.1177/03128962241293658)).

**(b) Signal, sign, horizon and portfolio.** Use public SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05 disclosures of material cybersecurity incidents (the [SEC final rule](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/33-11216.pdf) defines this item and its content). After a verified public timestamp, measure the source's one-session market-adjusted move; for each other active PIT same-SIC2 firm, form an exposure-weighted peer signal from the disclosed breach shock. The preregistered prediction is same-sign propagation: a negative source shock predicts negative one-month abnormal returns among its same-SIC2 rivals. The event-study mechanism is the slope of peer one-month return on source shock, with same-sign slope positive. For a long-only portfolio, rank the cross-section by predicted next-month return (more resilient, less exposed peers rank higher) and use the fixed Dow top-8/20%-turnover design. Keep directly breached firms out of the peer response test; hold the prior basket when no qualifying event creates a peer score.

**(c) Power.** The event-mode S&P/SIC2 simulation above assumes an optimistic 0.50 qualifying source-event dates per month and yields an 80% MDE near 0.097 rank IC; power is 13.42% even at planted IC 0.02. The cited papers do not report an equivalent monthly cross-sectional rank IC, so there is no defensible effect size known to clear this MDE. **Gate fails.** Before any outcome work, the input-only gate must identify at least five unaffected S&P PIT peers in at least 20 distinct SIC2-event months. The 0.50 event-date/month scenario has a mean of 21 active months; this is a proposed feasibility rule, not an observed coverage claim.

**(d) Data and implementation.** SEC filings are free, but Item 1.05 is not a ready-made historical event panel. Parse 8-K accession and acceptance timestamps, item text, named issuer, first public release time, and breach severity without opening price data; require at least 20 distinct qualifying SIC2-event months, five unaffected peer names per event, and 250 S&P names with valid PIT SIC2/price eligibility on at least 80% of formation months. If the filing-only event history fails, do not fill it with GDELT or paid breach databases after seeing the gate. The already-staged 8-K *earnings clock* is not a cybersecurity incident file, so its availability does not count as a pass.

**(e) Novelty and kill rule.** New source: a verified cyber incident and cyber-risk/reputation spillover, rather than earnings, general news co-mentions, order flow, volume shocks, or return reversal. Network: dated SIC2 exposure groups only, not the prior return-correlation, 13F, ETF, supplier-customer or leader/follower graphs. The topical use of public 8-K/news events is adjacent to K/B4, but the event class, causal channel and signal are different. Kill before outcomes if (i) the event/peer coverage threshold fails, (ii) literature-backed sign cannot be frozen because contagion and substitution cannot be separated, or (iii) the coordinator will not accept the stated low power. No post-result sign switching.

## Rank 2 — N2: serial activist campaigns transmit demand across campaign targets

**Subjective full-stack pass chance: 0.5%.** The underlying target demand is measurable and disclosure data are free, but available evidence is target-focused, not peer propagation; repeat-campaign coverage in 30 Dow names is likely sparse; and the event-mode power is worse than N1.

### Gates

**(a) Mechanism and evidence.** An activist crossing a disclosure threshold and stating a campaign can represent purposeful acquisition plus governance pressure. Brav et al. document target price and operating responses around hedge-fund activism ([DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01373.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01373.x)); Greenwood and Schor find that long-run target returns are concentrated among firms later acquired ([DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2008.05.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2008.05.005)). Neither establishes that price pressure spills to a serial activist's other targets, which is the distinct hypothesis here.

**(b) Signal, sign, horizon and portfolio.** Parse free SEC Schedule 13D/13D-A filings for activist CIK, issuer CIK, filing/acceptance date, reported ownership change, and an explicit activist-purpose flag. Build a directed sponsor-to-campaign graph: after a disclosed campaign at issuer A, score issuer B only if the same named sponsor has an earlier active campaign disclosure at B; exclude the event firm and stale or withdrawn campaigns. Predict that a new campaign produces positive next-month abnormal demand at the sponsor's other active targets, conditional on the source issuer's announcement reaction being positive. The mechanism statistic is the next-month peer return on disclosed source pressure, clustered by SIC2; report a sponsor-label shuffle and matched-random-target placebo. Long-only Dow top-8 follows the fixed cap of 20% one-way turnover per month; hold the prior basket when no new campaign creates a peer score.

**(c) Power.** The S&P/SIC2 event-mode simulation assumes 0.25 qualifying activist event dates per month; it gives a 0.1376 80%-power MDE and only 12.95% power at planted IC 0.02. This rate is a design sensitivity, not a measured Form 13D count. The activism literature's target effects cannot be converted honestly to peer rank IC. **Gate fails.** In addition to the general 250-name SIC2 rule, require at least 13 distinct campaign-source months and at least three active non-source target firms in at least 10 months, all established from filing metadata before prices or returns are joined. The scenario has a mean of 13.2 active months; these are proposed feasibility rules, not observed counts.

**(d) Data and implementation.** SEC EDGAR filings and submission metadata are free; the SEC describes Schedule 13D filings and provides access through [EDGAR](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/schedules-13d-and-13g). Resolving sponsor identity, amended/withdrawn status, ticker lineage, beneficial ownership and campaign purpose is manual/parsed work. Run a filing-only coverage manifest first. Reject passive Schedule 13G filings, stale campaigns, duplicate amendments and unverified sponsor identities. If a paid activist database is needed to reach the fixed coverage threshold, this direction fails the free-data constraint.

**(e) Novelty and kill rule.** This is a sponsor-campaign network and a newly disclosed activist acquisition/intent signal. It is not D4's quarterly common-ownership-weighted CLV graph: common activist identity and campaign timing, not overlap of 13F portfolio weights, define an edge; it is not the Form 4 insider transaction direction, because an activist's public campaign and strategic intervention are the source. The 13D filing can lag the actual stock accumulation, so stale timing and anticipation are serious limitations. Kill if filing-only coverage misses any threshold, if the sponsor-target graph is too dense or sparse for a meaningful placebo, or if return information enters before the graph freeze.

## Recommendation

Rank N1 ahead of N2, but **neither is credible enough to advance under the current gates**. Their synthetic power is below 14% even at a planted monthly IC of 0.02, while the 80% MDEs are about 0.10–0.14; neither literature base supplies a comparable post-2010 large-cap effect to justify those magnitudes. The correct decision is to stop proposing R6 variants unless new, pre-outcome evidence or a genuinely new data source changes the power or coverage case. Do not describe either as a pass or as a rescue for a failed direction.

No project returns, event returns, signal scores, portfolio results, holdout rows, or holdout availability were inspected for this note.
