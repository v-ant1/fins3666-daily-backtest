# Hostile red-team: R6 ideas B

**Disposition: no proposal clears the complete idea gate today.** Each proposal gets `FAIL-GATE` because its required point-in-time input coverage has not been demonstrated with an outcome-free manifest; several also have material thesis, power, or mega-cap weaknesses. “Buildable on free data” below means the required data can plausibly be assembled without a paid vendor, not that the current proposal has passed its coverage or quality gate. No return, signal, or portfolio outcome was computed for this review, and no holdout data was accessed.

The shared proposal formula, `2.84 × σ / √N_eff`, is a reasonable rough normal approximation for 80% power against a one-sided t-statistic threshold of 2. It is not a verified power estimate: B does not estimate `σ` or `N_eff` from an input-only sample, account for dependence and missing links, or translate the standardized effect into the locked economic/cost hurdle. Wider-universe power must be estimated on point-in-time S&P 100/500 data and must not be presented as proof that 30 Dow names have adequate power.

## Revised priority ranking

This ranks the *chance of reaching a valid test with currently identifiable inputs*, not the chance of profitable returns. This is a hostile feasibility ranking; expected edge remains very uncertain.

| 3 | Earnings-announcement spillover among co-covered firms | **FAIL-GATE** | **No, as specified** | SEC can supply filing/event timestamps, but cannot supply the missing analyst co-coverage graph; its power claim conflicts with its conservative issuer-year cluster count. |

The order puts the free, testable input path first, not because its alpha is most likely. If ranked by literature-based edge conditional on valid inputs, #1 (analyst links) and #2 (supply-chain links) have more direct established return-predictability evidence than news comovement, but the expected tradable edge in mega-cap Dow names remains low for all four.

## 1. Shared analyst coverage: connected-firm pressure and revisions

**Gate: FAIL-GATE. Free-data buildable: NO.**

- **(a) Literature — mostly passes.** Ali and Hirshleifer find momentum spillovers on a shared-analyst network, with stronger effects for complex/indirect links; that is direct return-predictability support, though it is monthly momentum rather than daily signed-pressure transmission ([JFE paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.10.007)). Muslu, Rebello, and Xu find coverage-specific return comovement and forecast information, not a trading-alpha result ([JAR paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.12057)). Hameed, Morck, Shen, and Yeung study information/return comovement across followed and neglected firms; it supports the broad mechanism, not the proposed Dow pressure sign ([NBER Working Paper 15833](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15833)). These citations do not validate the precise 2–10 day, post-2020 Dow hypothesis.
- **(b) Mega-cap case — weak.** The information-broker story is plausible, but the paper’s complex/indirect-link result does not establish that mega-cap analysts still process pressure slowly. The Dow’s heavy coverage could make its graph nearly complete; the proposal says to kill a saturated graph but supplies no pre-outcome saturation threshold or evidence that enough independent neighbor variation remains. “Shared analyst” should not be inferred from analyst counts, which do not reveal analyst identity or which firms each analyst covers.
- **(c) Power — arithmetic conditional, estimate unsupported.** Under B’s assumed `σ=0.20`, `N_eff=500`, the MDE is `2.84×0.20/√500 = 0.0254` mean rank IC. But the 500 independent date-cluster assumption and 0.20 daily IC SD are not measured; dates are serially dependent and a wide S&P sample does not repair low Dow graph breadth. The proposal also omits the conversion to the pre-registered IC/cost hurdle. Require an outcome-free S&P 100/500 analyst-link coverage manifest, then derive power with the frozen statistic and conservative dependence; otherwise this gate fails.
- **(d) Data / free substitute.** I/B/E/S Detail, LSEG/Workspace or equivalent analyst-level historical coverage is required, with analyst identifiers and dated firm coverage. UNSW catalogue discovery is not proof of user entitlement or historical PIT fields. Free analyst-count feeds cannot reconstruct analyst-to-firm pairs; sector/industry, GDELT co-mentions, or SEC filings define different networks and cannot be called substitutes. Do not register until the extract’s history, dates, issuer matching, Dow coverage, and network saturation are documented.
- **(e) Novelty — incremental.** Shared-analyst spillovers are established by Ali–Hirshleifer; this proposal’s novelty is daily signed-pressure measurement and its specific Dow test. That is a useful extension but not a new network mechanism. It should be described as an incremental measurement extension, not a new anomaly.

## 2. FactSet Revere supply-chain pressure transmission

**Gate: FAIL-GATE. Free-data buildable: NO, as specified.**

- **(a) Literature — passes support, not exact design.** Cohen and Frazzini document customer-to-supplier return predictability using principal customer disclosures ([JF paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01379.x)). Agarwal et al. find that investor co-search attention conditions cross-return predictability among supply-chain partners; this is not evidence that daily CLV pressure itself transmits ([ISR paper](https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2016.0656)). Yu, Hu, and Zhong report customer-return predictability in Australia, but also find it weakens for larger and more-attended firms ([PBFJ paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pacfin.2023.101975)).
- **(b) Mega-cap case — adverse evidence outweighs proposed survival story.** “More detailed Revere relationships than 10-K” may improve link count, but it says nothing about the lag surviving attention in mega-caps. The Australian paper’s explicit large-firm and attention attenuation directly cuts against the Dow case. The R5 D7 availability check found only three 10-K customer links, all with Amgen as supplier, zero Dow customers, and zero earnings events; this free SEC substitute demonstrably failed its prior input gate. Do not imply a free proxy fixes it.
- **(c) Power — threshold is not a coverage result.** With the proposed minimum `N_eff=500`, the standardized MDE is `2.84/√500 = 0.127` SD. But B has not established 500 independent source-shock/linked-target date clusters or 20 Dow companies with PIT links. Nor is “standardized cluster response” tied to the specified return statistic or a plausible economic effect. The count must come from an outcome-free, dated-link and eligible-pressure manifest on the wide universe and Dow separately; under the current proposal, power is unverified.
- **(d) Data / free substitute.** Revere requires a confirmed FactSet subscription and a history with relationship effective dates/versions. SEC 10-K major-customer identities/XBRL are free but, as the R5 D7 check shows, too sparse for this test. GDELT co-mentions are not supply-chain records; treating them as such would replace the mechanism with proposal #4 and require a new gate. FactSet Lionshares availability is not evidence of Revere access.
- **(e) Novelty — vendor swap is not novelty.** Directional, lagged supply-chain returns are established. Applying daily signed pressure to a richer relationship feed is incremental, and the broader feed alone cannot be claimed as a mechanism innovation. The date/versioning and transmission design would need to add evidence beyond the established customer-momentum result.

## 3. Earnings-announcement spillover among co-covered firms

**Gate: FAIL-GATE. Free-data buildable: NO, as specified.**

- **(a) Literature — adjacent, with a timing warning.** Baker, Ni, Saadi, and Zhu find same-industry peer announcement returns relate to industry-wide earnings news and analyst-forecast-based drift, but do not test a shared-analyst graph ([IRFA paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.irfa.2017.02.002)). Madsen finds customer returns predict suppliers shortly *before* the supplier’s own earnings announcement, but not after it ([JAR paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.12158)); this is a warning against B’s proposed post-event 2–10 session continuation. Ali and Hirshleifer supports shared-analyst links generally ([JFE paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.10.007)), but no cited study establishes this exact event-by-co-coverage pressure effect.
- **(b) Mega-cap case — plausible shock, weak delayed-return case.** Earnings announcements are timed information events, but mega-cap releases are prominent and heavily processed. B offers no reason why a peer’s pressure response should remain delayed after the event, and Madsen’s timing result points to resolution before supplier announcements. More importantly, its test uses source announcement return/surprise, not a defined buying-pressure measure; as drafted it drifts from the thesis. Require a signed volume/CLV pressure measure at the source or label a different question.
- **(c) Power — B’s own numbers contradict its pass threshold.** `30×4×6≈720` raw Dow events is only a rough upper bound. B then says issuer-year dependence leaves at most `N_eff=180`, giving `2.84/√180=0.212 SD`. Yet its data gate demands 500 timestamps and 10 Dow issuers. Five hundred raw events do not create 500 independent clusters; 180 Dow issuer-years do not support a 500-cluster Dow power claim. Wider S&P 100/500 events can estimate mechanism power, but no outcome-free event/coverage count is supplied, and Dow inference remains thin. Fix the sampling unit and calculate the MDE for both universes before gate passage.
- **(d) Data / free substitute.** SEC EDGAR 8-K item 2.02 submissions are free and timestamped; SEC says its submissions APIs need no API key and include public filer histories ([SEC EDGAR API documentation](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)). The existing `r4_L4\data\earnings_clock.csv` is an availability lead (64 CIKs), but its source roster is `S&P500_top50_market_cap_2026-11-06`, not evidence of PIT universe coverage. SEC fixes event timing only. It does not provide analyst identities or the co-coverage relation. Analyst counts, same-industry peers, GDELT co-mentions, or 8-K co-filing are not faithful substitutes for a co-covered network. Free buildability therefore fails as specified; a SEC-event plus news-network design would be a new idea, overlapping #4.
- **(e) Novelty — modest and currently thesis-misaligned.** Event-time conditioning plus analyst edges could be a narrow design extension. Peer announcement drift and analyst-network spillovers are already studied separately, and the proposed source return/surprise is not pressure. Novelty is not established by combining two known inputs; require a clearly defined, signed pressure transmission mechanism.

## 4. News co-mention / attention network pressure

**Gate: FAIL-GATE. Free-data buildable: YES, conditionally.**

- **(a) Literature — passes for links/comovement; return-alpha support is mixed.** Scherbina and Schlusche report future cross-predictability among co-mentioned firms, but the cited version is an AEI/SSRN working paper, not a peer-reviewed journal result ([working-paper record and DOI](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2363436)). Chen, Guo, and Tu show news co-mention strength predicts return comovement and correlated fundamentals, not necessarily a tradable alpha ([JEDC paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jedc.2021.104191)). Hirshleifer, Peng, and Wang find social connectedness changes announcement reactions and post-event dynamics ([RFS paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhae025)); social ties are not news co-mentions, so this is supporting context rather than direct evidence for B’s link measure. The cited base supports a plausible network, but not the specific signed-pressure continuation claim.
- **(b) Mega-cap case — two-sided, unresolved.** News can reveal indirect cross-industry relationships and strategic/competitive links. But Dow firms receive high media and investor attention, generic market stories can co-mention many names, and a news-defined relation may simply proxy same-day common news. B does not establish that a lag survives since 2020. Require recurring/event-specific links, chronology before the target window, broad-market-story filtering fixed before outcomes, and measurable Dow coverage.
- **(c) Power — optimistic until manifest.** If all 250 required clusters were independent, the proposed standardized MDE is `2.84/√250 = 0.180 SD`; with 100 usable clusters it is `0.284 SD`. Neither 250 nor the assumed cluster SD is evidenced by a sample pull. News/article counts are not independent signal dates, and highly covered firms may create correlated clusters. No cost/IC hurdle or economically plausible standardized effect is shown. The gate therefore fails until an outcome-free manifest provides counts, cross-date dependence diagnostics, and pre-registered statistic-specific power for the S&P mechanism universe and Dow trading universe.
- **(d) Data / free substitute.** GDELT publishes free, open raw data, including GKG archives ([official download page](https://www.gdeltproject.org/data.html); [GKG archive index](https://data.gdeltproject.org/gkg/index.html)). It is the only proposal with a credible no-license route. Still missing are a validated sample of historical issuer resolution, article timestamps, duplicate/syndicated-story handling, company-name collision rates, point-in-time ticker/CIK mappings, and counts of independent usable clusters. SEC 8-K filings can anchor event dates but do not replace a broad media network; SEC press releases or mentions are narrower and may be event-selected. Free-data buildable is **yes in principle**, but the idea is not yet build-ready and fails this gate.
- **(e) Novelty — low to moderate.** News co-mention links and cross-prediction predate this proposal, and Chen et al. already use co-mention strength for comovement. Using GDELT rather than licensed news, adding signed pressure, a strict pre-target chronology, and network placebos is a measurement/design extension. GDELT availability itself is not novelty; the incremental pressure-transmission claim remains to be shown.

## Required action before any proposal is promoted

No direction is ready for preregistration or outcome testing. For the leading free-data candidate (#4), run only an outcome-free input manifest: archive period and row counts; article-time and deduplication coverage; historical issuer-link precision on a predeclared hand-audit sample; per-month Dow/S&P coverage; and independent date-cluster counts. For #1 and #3, obtain verified analyst-level PIT coverage or explicitly retire the “co-covered” mechanism; SEC dates alone do not supply it. For #2, require confirmed Revere entitlement and a link manifest; the prior 10-K substitute failure is a stop signal, not a reason to relax thresholds. Then redo power against the locked R6 statistic and economic/cost hurdle, and ask for independent gate review before any return outcomes are opened.
