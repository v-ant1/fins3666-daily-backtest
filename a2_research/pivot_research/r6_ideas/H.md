# Round 6 idea gate — H: ETF pressure transmission

**Scope and status.** This is a pre-outcome idea review. I used literature sources, development-input inventory, and synthetic runs of `r4_harness/power_sim.py`; I did not compute any project returns, ICs, event outcomes, or portfolio results, and did not use holdout data. The R6 decisions log already labels the earlier ETF-flow direction dead, so these are new, narrower, outcome-free proposals and should not be read as reviving the old flow strategy by assertion.

## Direction 1 — Sector ETF pressure spills into constituents with a short lag

**Fixed question.** At each close, measure the 5-session pressure of each of the 11 Select Sector SPDR ETFs as a trailing-only sum of daily close-location value multiplied by a trailing 63-session standardized log-volume surprise. Give every point-in-time Dow constituent in that ETF's sector the same signed score. Predict a positive constituent return over the next 21 sessions, with inference clustered by date and sector. This is a pressure-transmission hypothesis, not a claim that ETF volume is itself a primary-market flow.

- **(a) Literature.** Ben-David, Franzoni and Moussawi, [“Do ETFs Increase Volatility?” *Journal of Finance* 73 (2018), 2471–2535](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12727), use index-membership variation and find ETF ownership raises underlying-stock volatility and negative return autocorrelation; they report a return premium of up to 56 bp per month for high ETF ownership. This supports potential transmission and reversal, but is an ownership result, not a daily flow or CLV signal. Da and Shive, [“Exchange traded funds and asset return correlations,” *European Financial Management* (2018), DOI 10.1111/eufm.12137](https://doi.org/10.1111/eufm.12137), report that ETF turnover is associated with more stock comovement and discuss subsequent reversal as a signature of temporary pressure. Crucial counter-evidence: Box, Davis, Evans and Lynch, [“Intraday arbitrage between ETFs and their underlying portfolios,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 141 (2021), 1078–1095](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.04.023), find little evidence ETF trading leads constituent returns, including at daily frequency. Brown, Davies and Ringgenberg, [“ETF Arbitrage, Non-Fundamental Demand, and Return Predictability,” *Review of Finance* 25 (2021), 937–972](https://doi.org/10.1093/rof/rfaa027), use primary-market creations/redemptions and find a high-flow-minus-low-flow ETF strategy earns 1.1–2.0% monthly; that is evidence on ETF return predictability, not proof of stock-level transmission from daily ETF price/volume.
- **(b) Large-cap and post-2020 case.** All 11 sector ETFs and Dow constituents are liquid and widely held, so ETF basket arbitrage can reach large firms. But this weakens any slow-diffusion story: Box et al. find little ETF-to-constituent predictability, and both Ben-David et al. and Da–Shive report stronger effects among smaller or less liquid stocks. The cited samples do not establish a post-2020 Dow pressure premium. The surviving case is limited to a short, fixed 21-session horizon and measurable pressure in the ETF itself; it is a hypothesis, not a post-2020 fact.
- **(c) Cluster-aware power.** I ran 5,000 synthetic draws at 1,488 development dates (2020-11-27 to 2026-10-30), horizon 21 (NW Bartlett lag 20), AR(1) date dependence `rho=.20`, negative-binomial event/date clustering shape `.5`, and planted IC `.0183`. The IC SD is the harness's null-rank approximation `1/sqrt(K−1)`, where `K` is the number of effective ETF shock clusters per date; no project outcomes or signal autocorrelation were used. For all 11 ETFs treated as independent, the result is **P(t≥2)=90.1%**, harness date-level effective N **801**, and 80%-power MDE **.00852**. But sector ETF shocks are correlated; sensitivity makes that assumption decisive:

  | Assumed independent sector shock clusters/date | Synthetic power at IC .0183 | 80%-power MDE | Effective date N |
  |---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 11 (optimistic upper bound) | 90.1% | .00852 | 801 |
  | 5 | 37.7% | .01997 | 707 |
  | 3 | 16.7% | .03646 | 630 |

  The 11-fund design has at most **16,368 nominal ETF-day clusters** (11 × 1,488); the harness's 801 is the HAC effective date count, not 801 independent constituent observations. Its event generator clusters event counts by date but does **not** implement two-way sector-and-date clustered errors, and sector shocks share market news. Therefore 90.1% is an optimistic planning bound; with five effective sector clusters, the design misses 80% power at .0183. The harness cannot certify the actual gate until a valid joint-cluster simulation is implemented or justified. No literature result supplies a comparable cross-sectional Spearman IC for this daily CLV-volume score, so .0183 is a scenario benchmark, not a literature effect estimate.
- **(d) Free data and inputs.** `r6_data/etf/etf_ohlcv_dev.parquet` has all 19 requested ETFs, **1,614 dates each** from 2020-06-01 to 2026-10-30; 11 are sector SPDR ETFs. Restricting to the project window leaves **1,488 sessions** and 16,368 sector-ETF bars. The file contains OHLC, adjusted close and volume, but no daily shares outstanding, creation/redemption flow, NAV discount/premium, or historical ETF holdings weights. `stock_sector_sic_mapping.csv` joins point-in-time S&P/Dow roster intervals to the *latest* 2026 GICS-sector labels: those labels are not historical; the latest-sector join matches 503 S&P tickers and 32 Dow tickers, leaving historical identities unmapped. Thus bar coverage passes, while a defensible point-in-time sector-to-ETF link does not. A point-in-time sector/holdings panel and audited symbol lineage are prerequisites.
- **(e) Novelty.** Prior ETF literature studies ownership, primary-market flows, ETF turnover/comovement, or ETF/portfolio arbitrage; the proposed new test uses a daily OHLCV-based, trailing CLV-volume shock and asks whether it predicts next-month returns specifically among point-in-time Dow sector members. That is a modest application-level distinction, not a claim of a new transmission channel. The 2021 direct null finding is a serious replication challenge.

**Portfolio and cost hurdle.** Conditional only: long-only top five of the Dow, EW30 benchmark, next-open entry, monthly decision, 0.05% per side actual-trade cost. A plain monthly top-five rebalance has no guaranteed turnover bound. To target the R6 ≤50% total-notional turnover rule, freeze a one-substitution-per-month maximum: replace at most the lowest-scored held name with the highest-scored eligible nonholding when its score ranks above the incumbent; otherwise retain. At equal target weights this is 40% total turnover (one 20% sale plus one 20% purchase), before weight drift; the actual-trade runner must report it and may not assume the cap passes. At 6% assumed 21-session cross-sectional return dispersion and top fraction `5/30`, `break_even_rank_ic(turnover=.40, dispersion=.06, cost_per_side=.0005)` is **.00231**. At the hard 50% cap it is **.00289** (2.5 bp per rebalance, about 30 bp/year at 12 monthly trades). These are cost-only linear rank-return hurdles, not alpha estimates; the 6% dispersion is a stated planning assumption, not measured from outcomes. The design passes the turnover branch only if realized total-notional turnover stays ≤50%.

**Gate call:** FAIL-GATE as currently supported. Reconsider only after a dated point-in-time holdings/sector panel, an inference design that respects both sector and date dependence, and a fixed score/portfolio spec are in hand.

## Direction 2 — DIA pressure precedes reversal in its Dow constituents

**Fixed question.** Compute a daily DIA pressure score from close-location value times trailing-only standardized log-volume surprise. A positive score is hypothesized to be non-fundamental demand that propagates into the Dow basket and reverses over the following 21 sessions; pre-register the signed statistic as negative pressure times next-21-session Dow-basket residual return. The intended claim is constituent pressure reversal, not that DIA's own subsequent return is predictable.

- **(a) Literature.** Ben-David et al. find that ETF ownership increases negative stock return autocorrelation, and Da–Shive find ETF activity associated with constituent comovement and evidence consistent with reversal. Brown et al. provide supporting evidence that primary-market flows contain non-fundamental demand information, but their 1.1–2.0% monthly long-short result is across ETFs and uses creation/redemption measures. Box et al. directly challenge using secondary-market ETF trading as the source of transmission: ETF returns do not lead portfolio prices in their tests, and the daily ETF-trading/constituent-price relation is small. Taken together, the evidence motivates a tightly scoped sign-reversal test but does not validate DIA OHLCV as a flow proxy.
- **(b) Large-cap and post-2020 case.** DIA is directly tied to large Dow names and is continuously traded. That link is also highly arbitraged, and the cited stronger results among small/illiquid names work against the mega-cap case. None of these papers establishes survival of the proposed price/volume proxy after 2020.
- **(c) Cluster-aware power.** Unlike the sector design, each date has only **one DIA shock cluster**, however many Dow names respond. There are 1,488 nominal DIA-day clusters; with the synthetic `rho=.20`, NW lag 20, the harness's effective date N is **1,012**. Since `power_sim.py` is cross-sectional and requires at least two assets, there is no honest way to treat the 30 Dow constituents as independent shocks or claim a valid cluster-adjusted cross-sectional IC test from its default mode. As a separate, explicitly synthetic event-mean sensitivity (not a rank IC), I set the SD of the one-cluster date statistic to 2% and the signed 21-session effect to 10 bp; 5,000 draws give **35.6% power** and an 80%-power MDE of **17.9 bp**. These noise/effect values are scenario assumptions, not literature estimates or project measurements. The effective N is dates, not constituent rows; the 10 bp scenario misses 80% power. This fails the power gate unless an input-only, valid one-way time-cluster design demonstrates otherwise at a defensible effect size.
- **(d) Free data and inputs.** DIA OHLCV is complete in L4's development bundle (1,488 usable project-window dates). The point-in-time Dow roster exists, but historical DIA weights, daily shares outstanding/primary flows and ETF NAV basis are not staged. A mean basket event study can use the 30 names as one date-level outcome, but it cannot turn the same DIA score into differentiated constituent ranks. A top-five Dow portfolio therefore has no signal for which five names to choose; using current DIA weights or a current Dow list would introduce look-ahead/survivorship. A separate market-timing overlay would require a frozen exposure and benchmark design and would be a different, more macro-sensitive strategy.
- **(e) Novelty.** The narrow potential contribution would be a post-2020, point-in-time Dow test of whether a daily secondary-market DIA pressure proxy precedes 21-session constituent-basket reversal, with the entire date treated as one ETF shock cluster. This differs from ownership/primary-flow studies. It is not compelling novelty if the data cannot distinguish non-fundamental pressure from market news or if it is not tradable under R6 portfolio rules.

**Portfolio and cost hurdle.** A single DIA signal gives every Dow constituent the same sign, so it cannot generate a top-five cross-sectional book. One could test a long/short Dow basket conditional on DIA pressure, but that needs a separately frozen exposure and benchmark; the current top-five long-only design is not implementable from this signal. For scale only, applying the shared top-five formula at 50% total turnover and 6% monthly dispersion gives an IC hurdle of **.00289**, but that hurdle is inapplicable until a differentiated portfolio signal exists. Even assuming a portfolio with one replacement per month would produce a nominal 40% total turnover and .00231 hurdle, the missing ranking signal remains the blocker.

**Gate call:** FAIL-GATE. Do not proceed to outcomes without an identifiable constituent-level exposure/ranking rule, valid event-level power and a staged point-in-time holdings/weights source.

## Why these are not yet ready

The local L4 bundle solves the ETF-bar availability question but not the flow/holdings question. Free OHLCV permits CLV and trailing-volume proxies, which should be labelled proxies rather than creations/redemptions; the current GICS join is a latest-sector snapshot, not a point-in-time history. The literature is mixed, including a direct 2021 daily-frequency null, and the power results depend sharply on how many ETF shock clusters are truly independent. R6 decisions also state that ETF-flow was previously dead; any new attempt must be justified by its pre-registered signal and data design, not by relabeling the same flow premise.

No variants were run or consumed, and no project outcomes were accessed.
