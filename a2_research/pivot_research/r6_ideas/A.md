# R6 Idea Gate — reversal and overshoot family

**Purpose and scope.** Four research directions for the idea gate, ranked by expected *net* edge. These are hypotheses, not pre-registrations or test results. No returns, signals, correlations, event counts, or outcomes were computed from project data for this note; no holdout data were opened. The local availability check was limited to file inventory and development-only data headers/coverage metadata: Dow daily OHLCV files are present under `repo/prices-daily-lookback` and `w1_engine/prices-daily-lookback`, and R4 has a development-only point-in-time 13F panel. These checks establish that inputs exist for some directions, not that coverage passes a future fixed gate.

**Common power convention (illustrative, not an outcome estimate).** The development window has about 1,500 sessions. For the rank-IC mechanism test, use 1,500 daily cross-sections and conservatively assume only 500 effective dates after serial dependence/HAC adjustment. If daily rank-IC standard deviation is 0.29, a mean IC of 0.026 gives (t\approx0.026/(0.29/\sqrt{500})=2.00). The 0.29 is a conservative planning assumption anchored to the scale of the already reported R5 D4 summary, not recalculated here. A point-in-time S&P 100 panel would contain about 150,000 stock-days (100 × 1,500); S&P 500 about 750,000. Cross-sectional breadth helps estimate each date's rank statistic and event counts, but the effective time-series sample remains roughly 500 dates; it does not turn 750,000 observations into independent trials. A test requiring materially less than 0.026 mean rank IC is underpowered under this convention. For event designs, event counts and a transparent minimum detectable effect are stated separately.

## 1. Pressure-conditioned liquidity-provision reversal — estimated full-pass probability: 8%

**Core candidate.** A large, high-volume signed candle can be temporary liquidity demand rather than information. Form daily pressure as close-location value × dollar volume, standardised within stock by trailing history; predict the opposite-sign, market- and sector-residualised return over the next five sessions, with the sign fixed before testing. The eventual portfolio would be long the most negative pressure names and short the most positive, with a 30-name Dow implementation assessed against the fixed active-share requirement. A single pre-registered primary is preferable; at most two declared diagnostics can distinguish volume-conditioned from unconditional pressure.

**(a) Literature.** Campbell, Grossman and Wang's market-maker model and evidence link high volume to lower return autocorrelation and predict more positive expected returns after high-volume declines ([QJE DOI 10.2307/2118454](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118454)). Nagel interprets short-term reversal returns as liquidity-provision compensation and finds it is state-dependent, rising in turmoil ([RFS DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhs066](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs066)). Cox and Peterson find that bid–ask bounce and liquidity explain reversals after large one-day declines ([JF DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04428.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04428.x)). These papers motivate a liquidity mechanism; they do not establish this CLV proxy or a net Dow edge.

**(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 case.** Mega-caps have tight spreads and deep continuous trading, which should limit residual price pressure; that is a serious headwind. The case for persistence is episodic constrained dealer/intermediary capacity and one-sided demand, not routine daily autocorrelation. The literature's stress-state dependence gives a reason to expect the mechanism to remain possible after 2020, but competition, ETFs and faster execution may have reduced its magnitude. Keep only if a pre-registered post-2020 mechanism test clears the fixed gate after realistic costs.

**(c) Power.** Use the common 1,500-session S&P 100/500 rank-IC calculation above: approximately 150,000/750,000 stock-days; with 500 effective dates, mean rank IC 0.026 is the illustrative (t=2) threshold. This is plausible but demanding for highly liquid stocks. For a five-session event test, a 5% daily extreme-pressure rate would imply about 7,500 raw S&P 100 events or 37,500 S&P 500 events over 1,500 sessions before overlap and date clustering; the event gate must report actual counts and a date-clustered minimum detectable effect before outcomes. These are planning counts from an assumed rate, not observed event counts.

**(d) Data and cheap feasibility.** Daily adjusted OHLCV and historical point-in-time S&P 100/500 membership are needed for the broad mechanism panel; daily adjusted OHLCV for the Dow is already present. Dow CLV and dollar-volume fields are derivable from the local OHLCV files. Free Yahoo/Stooq histories may support a rough availability check but not reliable point-in-time index membership; use UNSW FactSet for survivorship-controlled broad-universe prices and membership if available. No intraday feed is required. Existing local Dow OHLCV means the base signal is constructible; broad point-in-time roster and price access remains unconfirmed and must be pulled before freezing.

**(e) Novelty.** Short-term reversal itself is old. The new test is a fixed signed-volume/CLV proxy, an explicit next-five-session forecast and an active long–short Dow implementation, with an S&P-wide mechanism test and placebos. Do not claim a new anomaly until it survives costs and the existing literature's liquidity and microstructure controls.

## 2. Common-owner pressure followed by peer reversal — new D4 pre-registration candidate; estimated full-pass probability: 5%

**Core candidate and D4 reset.** This is a genuinely new direction, not a sign flip justified by the R5 result. Freeze the forecast as follows: for each Dow stock, form a five-session source-stock signed CLV-dollar-volume shock; map it to other Dow stocks using lagged, filing-date-available institutional holdings overlap, with the source excluded from its own peer measure. Predict that the connected peers' market- and sector-residualised returns over the subsequent five sessions reverse the *induced same-direction* peer move. Primary signal is continuous common-owner-weighted source pressure; no tuning of horizons, managers, cutoffs or graph weights after outcomes. A dated and hashed preregistration must define filing lag, eligible managers, holdings units, market/sector residualisation, the exact placebo-link test, and the portfolio before any return outcome is inspected. The old D4's negative IC is context only, not the reason to choose this sign.

**(a) Literature.** Antón and Polk link stocks through common active mutual-fund owners, document excess return comovement, and explicitly motivate a cross-stock-reversal strategy ([JF DOI 10.1111/jofi.12149](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.12149)). Coval and Stafford show that fund flows create price pressure among common holdings and that investors trading against constrained funds earn liquidity-provision returns ([JFE DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2006.09.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2006.09.007)). These results provide an ex-ante mechanism for temporary peer comovement and reversal; they do not prove a daily OHLCV proxy can identify it.

**(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 case.** Broad ownership makes the old network nearly complete, so a simple peer average mostly becomes a market average (R5 documents this limitation). A source-specific shock, leave-one-out peer response and market/sector residuals ask a narrower question: do ownership-linked stocks overshoot together when the source receives a measured pressure shock, then unwind? Passive ownership growth could strengthen common demand, while arbitrage, crowded mega-cap coverage and dense links could erase or obscure the effect. The thesis is plausible but the density problem makes this a low-probability direction; it must not advance unless the pre-outcome link coverage and placebo design show identifying variation.

**(c) Power.** The S&P 100/500 panel provides 150,000/750,000 stock-days and about 1,500 daily cross-sections; under the common assumptions, an average residual rank IC near 0.026 is needed for (t\approx2) with 500 effective dates. For an event-study alternative, an assumed 2% source-shock rate yields 3,000/15,000 raw source-days in S&P 100/500. With a conservative 50% reduction for overlapping five-day windows and date clustering, (N_{eff}\approx1,500/7,500); for a five-day residual-CAR standard deviation of 4%, the approximate two-sided 5%/80%-power MDE is (2.8×0.04/\sqrt{N_{eff}}\), or about 29/13 bp. These are assumption-based planning numbers, not observed coverage or effects. If the actual qualifying common-owner source events fall below 1,500 effective events, do not claim adequate S&P 100 power.

**(d) Data and cheap feasibility.** Needs point-in-time quarterly 13F holdings with filing dates, manager identifiers, security/CUSIP-to-ticker history, daily adjusted OHLCV, point-in-time S&P membership and GICS classifications. SEC EDGAR 13F filings and company facts are free, but bulk parsing, CUSIP lineage and historical rosters are labor-intensive; UNSW FactSet ownership/holdings plus prices and classifications is the practical route if licensed. Existing R4 development-only 13F files prove Dow-level holdings inputs were assembled and corrected, but they do not establish S&P 100/500 coverage or event power. The cheap gate is an input-only coverage pull (distinct managers, filing lag, mapped-name count, nonzero edge distribution); no returns or signal outcomes.

**(e) Novelty.** Prior common-owner work studies shared ownership and return comovement / reversal on broad samples. The proposed increment is a directional, source-shock event: measured daily pressure at one holding predicts a subsequent residual reversal in its filing-date-known co-owned Dow peers. That distinction is only novel if the link placebo is rejected and the exposure is not a disguised market/sector basket.

## 3. Intraday order-imbalance exhaustion and next-session reversal — estimated full-pass probability: 4%

**Core candidate.** Measure signed buyer-initiated minus seller-initiated dollar flow in the last 30 minutes (or a pre-specified fixed 30-minute window), scaled by total dollar volume. Test whether extreme imbalance accompanied by a same-direction price move predicts a partial reversal over the next session. This is distinct from daily CLV: it uses trade-level order direction and a narrow execution window. Freeze one window and one next-session horizon; no intraday parameter sweep. The Dow portfolio must account for opening execution, spread and short-sale costs.

**(a) Literature.** Heston, Korajczyk and Sadka find short-term reversal driven by temporary liquidity imbalances lasting less than an hour and bid–ask bounce ([JF DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x)). Chordia, Roll and Subrahmanyam find that market returns reverse after large negative-return/high-negative-order-imbalance days ([JFE DOI 10.1016/S0304-405X(02)00136-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-405X(02)00136-8)). Rif and Utz report next-minute reversal after extreme one-minute declines in Nasdaq 100 stocks, including large/liquid names, in 2014–2019 data ([QRFE DOI 10.1016/j.qref.2021.05.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.qref.2021.05.004)).

**(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 case.** Large stocks and electronic trading make signed-flow and execution data more meaningful and keep implementation capacity high. Yet the most liquid names also have the strongest algorithmic competition; the cited Nasdaq sample ends in 2019, and a 2021 paper does not by itself establish persistence through the post-2020 period. Any remaining opportunity likely requires real order imbalance and careful executable prices, not a candle approximation. Treat post-2020 survival as uncertain until a modern S&P 100 mechanism test passes.

**(c) Power.** S&P 100 over 1,500 sessions has roughly 58.5 million five-minute bars if there are 78 regular-session bars per name-day; S&P 500 has about 292.5 million. Even at only one qualifying extreme event per 1,000 bars, that is approximately 58,500/292,500 raw events. Dependence within stock-days and market-wide events is substantial; conservatively, if only 1% remain effective (585/2,925) and the next-session abnormal-return SD is 2%, the 80%-power MDE is (2.8×0.02/\sqrt{N_{eff}}\), about 23/10 bp. A one-minute prediction would be much more exposed to spread/bounce; the candidate specifically tests next-session returns. These bar and event counts are arithmetic planning assumptions, not actual data counts.

**(d) Data and cheap feasibility.** Requires timestamped trades and quotes or sufficiently granular bars with a validated trade-signing method, corporate actions, exchange calendar, and point-in-time S&P membership. No equivalent intraday file was found in the local development price folders. The economically credible source is UNSW FactSet intraday trades/quotes or another licensed feed; free daily OHLCV cannot construct signed order imbalance. Before registration, request a small *coverage-only* FactSet sample for S&P 100 and Dow names (dates, bar/trade counts, field availability, missingness); do not compute returns or imbalances. If FactSet does not provide the required history, kill this direction rather than approximate it with OHLCV.

**(e) Novelty.** The literature documents intraday temporary liquidity imbalance; the new test would ask whether a precisely fixed late-session shock predicts an executable next-session reversal in the post-2020 Dow. This is a new time window and implementation target, not a claim of discovering reversal.

## 4. Funding-stress liquidity spillover and reversal in related stocks — estimated full-pass probability: 3%

**Core candidate.** During pre-defined broad-market stress days, a large negative market move can constrain liquidity suppliers across stocks. Test if related Dow stocks with unusually negative signed CLV-dollar-volume pressure subsequently recover more over five sessions, with peer exposure defined by a fixed GICS group and residualised to remove the market move. This is a conditional event design, not a general buy-the-dip rule. Register one stress threshold using an external, pre-specified market variable (e.g. prior-close VIX above its trailing 80th percentile) and one horizon; do not search thresholds.

**(a) Literature.** Hameed, Kang and Viswanathan show that negative market returns reduce stock liquidity, more under tight funding conditions, and report returns to supplying liquidity after large market-value drops; they also document interindustry liquidity spillovers ([JF DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01529.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01529.x)). Nagel finds that liquidity-provision returns and conditional Sharpe ratios rise during market turmoil ([RFS DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhs066](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs066)). These support a conditional liquidity-supply channel, not a guaranteed sector-peer return forecast.

**(b) Mega-cap/post-2020 case.** Mega-cap depth should absorb ordinary shocks; the edge could survive only when intermediary funding is scarce and simultaneous demand spans related names. Such episodes occurred in the post-2020 sample, but will be few, market-wide, and quickly arbitraged. Sector classifications and broad market controls may leave little independent Dow variation. A stress-conditioned effect is thus plausible, yet likely underpowered and sensitive to the chosen event definition.

**(c) Power.** Planning on S&P 500: 1,500 dates × 500 stocks = 750,000 stock-days. If a fixed external stress rule selects 5% of dates and 10% of stocks on those dates meet the pressure event rule, this yields 3,750 raw events; after 50% overlap/date-cluster discount, (N_{eff}\approx1,875). With five-day abnormal-return SD 4%, the 80%-power MDE is about (2.8×0.04/\sqrt{1,875}=26\) bp. An S&P 100 panel would produce one-fifth those events and an MDE near 57 bp under the same assumptions. This direction needs the S&P 500 mechanism panel; the Dow portfolio alone is unlikely to support the stress interaction. Counts are assumed, not measured.

**(d) Data and cheap feasibility.** Needs daily adjusted OHLCV, point-in-time GICS/industry membership and S&P 500 constituents, daily VIX (free from Cboe/FRED-compatible sources) and market returns, plus historical universe prices. Dow OHLCV exists locally. For a point-in-time S&P 500 panel, UNSW FactSet prices/classifications and constituent histories are preferred; free daily price archives plus public historical constituent lists may be used for an availability check but need careful survivor/ticker audit. Cheap gate: count stress dates from the external VIX series and check broad-universe OHLCV and GICS coverage only, with no return or signal computation. Few stress dates would kill this idea before outcomes.

**(e) Novelty.** Prior work studies market-wide funding/liquidity shocks and aggregate liquidity provision. The increment is a fixed pressure-based, within-stress cross-sectional test across related mega-cap stocks followed by a Dow long–short implementation. The novelty is modest; do not treat a known stress-reversal result as a new edge unless the peer-specific part adds predictive information.

## Decision summary

The highest expected net edge is #1 because it uses inexpensive daily inputs and the deepest Dow names for implementation, despite a well-known anomaly and substantial competition. #2 best preserves the network component and has the clearest direct paper trail for cross-stock reversal, but D4's dense-graph failure and the need for broad point-in-time ownership data sharply reduce its probability. #3 has the most direct microstructure measure but depends on licensed intraday coverage and unusually clean execution accounting. #4 is a coherent stress mechanism but likely has too few independent episodes.

| Rank | Direction | Probability of passing all fixed R6 gates (subjective) |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Pressure-conditioned liquidity-provision reversal | 8% |
| 2 | Common-owner source pressure → peer reversal (new D4) | 5% |
| 3 | Intraday order-imbalance exhaustion | 4% |
| 4 | Funding-stress liquidity spillover and reversal | 3% |

These probabilities are deliberately low and refer to passing the full development, portfolio, correction and holdout criteria, not merely finding a significant mechanism. Each direction still needs an independent red-team of gates (a)–(e), a cheap input-only feasibility pass, and then a frozen preregistration before any outcome test. No direction in this note is an R6 pass.
