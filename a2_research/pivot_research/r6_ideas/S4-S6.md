## S4 — Expiration and month-end institutional pressure (screen only; not a new candidate)

### (a) Literature

Chen and Lu, [“Stock returns on option expiration dates: Price impact of liquidity trading,” *Journal of Empirical Finance* 28 (2014), 273–290](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2014.03.003), report an approximately 0.8% expiration-day fall for stocks with large deeply in-the-money call open interest relative to underlying volume, followed by short-term reversal. Roll, Schwartz, and Subrahmanyam, [“Stock Price Clustering on Option Expiration Dates,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 78 (2005), 331–361](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2004.08.005), find expiration-date price clustering, but do not establish a broad predictable return premium. For month-end pressure, Etula, Rinne, Suominen, and Vaittinen, [“Dash for Cash: Monthly Market Impact of Institutional Liquidity Needs,” *Review of Financial Studies* 33 (2020), 75–111](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhz054), link temporary month-end selling pressure and reversal to institutional liquidity needs; they report that month-end reversals are more pronounced in large, liquid stocks.

### (b) Mega-cap and post-2020 case

There is a plausible economic channel: assignment/exercise and dealer hedge unwinds can force stock trading, and pension cash needs need not disappear in mega-caps. But the 80 bp result is conditional on unusual option exposure, and the expiration papers do not show that this effect persists in Dow names after 2020. “Dash for Cash” is closer to the large-cap case, but it does not turn a common calendar event into a stock-specific next-month ranking. Index and pension rebalancing is also predictable and likely front-run. No cited estimate establishes a post-2020 Dow excess-Sharpe ≥0.8.

### (c) Synthetic power check

I ran `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with 53 SIC2 clusters, 71 monthly observations, one non-overlapping monthly outcome per observation (`horizon=1`), AR(1) IC dependence 0.20, 5,000 simulations, and planted IC 0.0183. To account for within-SIC2 dependence, the IC noise SD is 0.20855: `sqrt((1 + (499/53 - 1) × 0.15)/(53 - 1))`, using 499 S&P names, 53 independent SIC2 units, and assumed within-group correlation 0.15. This gives `P(NW t ≥ 2)=15.84%` (Monte Carlo SE 0.00516) and an 80%-power MDE of 0.07033. The 0.0183 input is a common R6 planning comparison, **not** an effect size from either paper. The 80 bp one-day event spread cannot be converted to a monthly rank IC without the exposed-stock fraction and cross-sectional return dispersion. Power therefore fails at the benchmark; the literature gives no comparable effect estimate to justify the MDE.

### (d) Cheap free-data feasibility

The staged Dow OHLCV is adequate to identify the underlying stock calendar, but there is no staged historical stock-option open-interest panel. The open `yfinance` implementation exposes option chains by listed expiration through `Ticker.option_chain(date=...)`; its documented interface does not provide an archived historical chain/open-interest-by-date panel ([yfinance source](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/blob/main/yfinance/ticker.py)). The SEC's N-PORT data are fund holdings filings, not daily option exercise or pension cash flows; filings are delayed and do not supply the missing historical option exposure ([SEC N-PORT dataset documentation](https://www.sec.gov/files/nport_readme.pdf)). This is a data fail before any outcomes. No post-window or holdout chain was fetched.

### (e) Novelty and disposition

This is already the C3 deep-ITM call-exercise idea, and the month-end cash-demand branch is already C2; the earlier dossier and red-team marked both FAIL-GATE. Reframing the pair as “index and pension flows” does not make it new. **Do not reopen C2/C3 or spend an R6 variant.**

## S5 — Active-manager 13F herding changes

### (a) Literature

Sias, [“Institutional Herding,” *Review of Financial Studies* 17 (2004), 165–206](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhg035), finds that institutional demand this quarter is positively related to demand last quarter, with a herding component beyond return-chasing; the study's quarterly sample is 1983–1997. Its relation with returns is weakly positive over the next quarter or six months and is close to zero over the following year in the broadest sample. Coval and Stafford, [“Asset Fire Sales (and Purchases) in Equity Markets,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 86 (2007), 479–512](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2006.09.007), show that extreme mutual-fund flows induce forced trades and stock-level price pressure, with subsequent price reversals. Together these support crowded institutional demand as a pressure mechanism, not a known large-cap post-2020 alpha.

### (b) Mega-cap and post-2020 case

Institutional ownership is large in mega-caps, so correlated manager decisions can generate material demand; the broad price-impact mechanism is plausible. Counterevidence matters: disclosure is stale, the original Sias results predate the modern passive/ETF era, and liquid large-cap prices should absorb flow more quickly. The cited papers do not establish a 2020–2026 large-cap return effect. I assign no positive post-2020 effect size from those papers.

### (c) Synthetic power check

For a quarterly filing signal there are 23 development-window filing-availability quarters. Using 53 SIC2 units and the within-group-correlation stress `ic_sd=0.20855`, 23 dates, `horizon=1`, AR(1)=0.20, 5,000 simulations, and planted mean rank IC 0.0183, `r4_harness/power_sim.py` gives `P(NW t ≥ 2)=11.62%` (Monte Carlo SE 0.00453) and an 80%-power MDE of 0.12357. The IC is a planning benchmark only: neither paper supplies a comparable monthly/quarterly rank IC for today's Dow. The synthetic design is underpowered; no outcome-tested estimate can repair that in advance.

### (d) Cheap free-data feasibility

I counted only rows whose 13F availability date falls inside the development window in the staged `r4_L4/data/13f_development_pit_positions.csv` panel. That yielded 223,932 holdings rows, 23 report quarters (2020-12-31 to 2026-06-30), 142 managers, and 33 distinct tickers, with at most 30 names per report quarter; filing availability runs 2021-01-12 to 2026-08-14. This is useful Dow input coverage but is not an S&P 500 holdings panel, so it cannot furnish the requested S&P SIC2 mechanism cross-section as staged. SEC 13F filings are free, but managers may report up to 45 days after quarter-end, so each signal must be timestamped at public filing availability rather than report date ([SEC Form 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f)). The input gate fails until a development-only S&P panel and dated security links exist.

### (e) Novelty and disposition

This would rank the stock-specific breadth of **active managers' quarterly position increases**, not passive ownership levels (P4), common-owner adjacency carrying daily CLV (R5 D4), or ETF creations/redemptions. It is genuinely different at the signal level, but 13F reporting is quarterly and this staged panel is only Dow-wide. Novelty passes narrowly; power and input gates do not. **FAIL-GATE; do not build outcomes or a portfolio.** Subjective full-stack pass probability: **<1%**.

## S6 — Direct public attention shock from firm-page views (screen only; not a new candidate)

### (a) Literature

Da, Engelberg, and Gao, [“In Search of Attention,” *Journal of Finance* 66 (2011), 1461–1499](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2011.01679.x), use weekly Google ticker-search data for Russell 3000 stocks in 2004–2008. Abnormal search predicts higher prices over the next two weeks and eventual reversal within a year; their reported short-window return is about 34 bp over two weeks. Moat et al., [“Quantifying Wikipedia Usage Patterns Before Stock Market Moves,” *Scientific Reports* 3 (2013), 1801](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep01801), relate Wikipedia usage changes to subsequent stock-market movements. Pyun, [“The Wikipedia effect: Analyzing investor attention for strategic investment decisions,” *Economics Letters* 241 (2024), 111836](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econlet.2024.111836), report that firms with rising pageviews have higher returns on average and that pageview-based long-short portfolios outperform market benchmarks; that study's data extend through December 2023 but do not report a separate post-2020 Dow mega-cap effect size.

### (b) Mega-cap and post-2020 case

Retail attention can still create one-sided buying in large recognizable firms; the 2024 Wikipedia paper's sample is recent enough to show that the channel has not disappeared from all U.S. equities. The evidence does not isolate mega-caps or a post-2020 return effect, and Wikipedia views can react to fundamentals, product news, scandals, or stock moves. I would not assume the historical 34 bp short-window premium is available in the Dow after 2020.

### (c) Synthetic power check

I used the same S&P SIC2 stress as S4: 53 independent clusters, 71 monthly observations, `horizon=1`, AR(1)=0.20, IC noise SD=0.20855 (the explicit 499-name / 53-cluster / 0.15 within-cluster-correlation assumption above), 5,000 simulations, and planted IC=0.0183. Power is `15.84%` (Monte Carlo SE 0.00516), with an 80%-power MDE of `0.07033`. This planning IC is not an effect converted from the 34 bp two-week return or the 2024 portfolio; the necessary cross-sectional volatility and sample-specific portfolio spread are not reported here. Power fails at the benchmark, and there is no literature-calibrated IC that establishes an 80%-power pass.

### (d) Cheap free-data feasibility

I queried the public Wikimedia Pageviews API for the development interval 2026-10-01 through 2026-10-30. `Apple_Inc.`, `Microsoft`, and `The_Walt_Disney_Company` each returned 30 daily records through 2026-10-30, confirming the endpoint is reachable for three unambiguous Dow examples, not that all 30 Dow or 500 S&P companies have usable coverage ([Wikimedia Pageviews API](https://wikimedia.org/api/rest_v1/#/Pageviews%20data/getMetricsPageviewsPerArticleProjectAccessAgentPagegranularityStartEnd)). **Disclosure:** an initial feasibility request was bounded through 2026-11-01; I discarded its aggregate and report only the restricted October 1–30 response. No pageview value was used to form a signal, and no stock returns were queried. A full gate would still need a frozen company-article crosswalk, nonzero pageview coverage for the point-in-time S&P roster, stable time-series baselines, and consistent page-title handling.

### (e) Novelty and disposition

Pageviews are a direct attention observation, unlike P3's maximum daily return used as an attention/lottery proxy, so the observable is different. The pressure story—attention induces retail buying, transient price impact, and reversal—is nevertheless the same family already screened in P3, and this idea adds no related-asset transmission mechanism. Under the explicit exclusion of prior rejected ideas, this is not eligible for a fresh R6 slot. **FAIL-GATE; do not relabel P3 as a new attention test.**

## Overall conclusion

The highest reported event effect is in the option-expiration paper, but its sample is narrow and old, its free historical exposure data are missing, and it duplicates C3; month-end cash pressure duplicates C2. The direct-attention measure is accessible for a few development-period article pages, but it overlaps P3, has low synthetic power at the standard planning IC, and lacks a post-2020 Dow effect estimate. Active 13F herding is the only direction here not already rejected as the same experiment, but the quarterly sample, 14.8% power, and Dow-only staged holdings panel fail before outcomes. **No credible eligible S4–S6 candidate remains; no variants were used.**
