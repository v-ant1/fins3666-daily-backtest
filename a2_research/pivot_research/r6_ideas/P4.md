# P4 — passive-ownership demand pressure

**Status: FAIL-GATE; do not open an outcome test.** Changes in passive ownership are a plausible source of persistent, price-insensitive demand, but the cited papers document price-impact and market-structure mechanisms rather than a low-turnover Dow return strategy. The staged 13F panel is useful, yet its reported positions are manager-level and do not identify which shares are in index mandates. My subjective chance of passing the full mechanism, data, cost, portfolio, and inference gates is **about 1%**.

## Proposed direction

For each stock, construct the share of outstanding equity held by a pre-defined set of passive index portfolios, then measure its change from one disclosed holdings snapshot to the next. The formation signal is the change in passive shares as a fraction of lagged shares outstanding, using shares rather than reported dollar values so price changes do not masquerade as purchases. After the holdings filing is public, test whether a larger positive change predicts a positive next-month stock return. The economic claim is that benchmark-following demand is relatively price-inelastic and may continue to affect prices after its reported holdings change.

A monthly Dow implementation would hold the top five stocks by the latest available ownership-change signal against EW30, carry positions between filings, and enforce a **20% total-notional turnover cap per monthly rebalance**. Filings are quarterly and lagged, so a monthly formation uses only the newest publicly available filing and will often repeat the prior signal. Any estimation must timestamp the filing, not the report quarter, as the first date the signal is tradable.

## Gates (a)–(e)

### (a) Literature and effect-size gate — FAIL

The literature supports the mechanism but does not establish the needed strategy effect:

- Koijen and Yogo develop a holdings-based institutional demand system and show how heterogeneous demand can explain asset-price movements and predictability. It is an asset-pricing framework, not a passive-ownership-change portfolio result. [Koijen & Yogo (2019), *A Demand System Approach to Asset Pricing*, DOI 10.1086/701683](https://doi.org/10.1086/701683).
- Haddad, Huebner and Loualiche estimate that the rise in passive investing made demand for individual stocks about **11% more inelastic**. This is an elasticity result, not an expected monthly return or Sharpe for a Dow long-only tilt. [Working paper, DOI 10.2139/ssrn.3821263](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3821263).
- Gabaix and Koijen estimate that an aggregate dollar invested in the stock market increases aggregate market value by about five dollars under their granular-IV design. That is a broad-market price-impact estimate; it cannot be treated as a cross-sectional return forecast from the quarterly change in one stock’s passive holdings. [NBER working paper, DOI 10.3386/w28967](https://doi.org/10.3386/w28967).
- The counterevidence matters: Coles, Heath and Ringgenberg find that index investing changes information production but does **not** measurably worsen price efficiency in their Russell-index design. A 2% market-cap shift in passive ownership around reconstitution is not itself evidence of persistent next-month alpha. [Coles, Heath & Ringgenberg (2022), *On Index Investing*, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2022.05.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2022.05.007).

None of these documented effects maps to a post-2010 Dow excess Sharpe of at least 0.8. **Gate (a) fails the portfolio-magnitude requirement**, even though the demand mechanism is theoretically motivated.

### (b) Signal, sign, timing, and test

Freeze a roster of genuinely passive index portfolios before any returns are joined. For stock *i* at the first public filing date after quarter *q*, define passive ownership as reported shares held by those index portfolios divided by lagged shares outstanding. The signal is the change in that ownership fraction since the previous public filing. The prediction is positive: a positive increase in passive shares predicts positive one-month excess returns after disclosure. Do not use the market value change, because that mechanically embeds the stock’s price return.

Test the mechanism on the point-in-time S&P 500 using a monthly one-month forward-return rank IC, with standard errors accounting for SIC2 clusters and repeated quarterly signal values. Use a placebo that permutes the passive-manager labels within each quarter while preserving the total shares reported per stock. Report raw signal coverage, filing lags, number of distinct quarterly updates, and placebo results before considering the Dow portfolio. The direction is killed if the positive sign is not frozen from evidence before outcomes or if its measured signal is simply a scale proxy for firm size.

### (c) SIC2-cluster power and criterion-4 feasibility — FAIL

**Synthetic power.** I ran `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with 54 effective SIC2 clusters, 72 monthly dates, `horizon=3` (NW lag 2), signal autocorrelation 0.80 to represent repeated quarterly filings, 20,000 simulations, and an assumed cluster-adjusted IC SD of **0.20538**. The SD is `sqrt((1 + (9.24 − 1) × 0.15)/(54 − 1))`, using about 499 S&P names / 54 SIC2 groups and assumed within-SIC2 correlation 0.15; neither the correlation nor SD is estimated from returns. At a hypothetical planted IC of 0.020, the simulation gives **24.7%** power to reach NW *t* ≥ 2 and an 80%-power MDE of **0.1086**. The 0.020 effect is a sensitivity benchmark, not a literature-to-IC conversion. The tool’s 72 monthly observations overstate independent information if filings only refresh quarterly; its SIC2 clustering is also a proxy, not a true two-way cluster bootstrap. This is an optimistic design screen, and power remains low. `power_sim.py` reads design assumptions only; no project returns or signals entered the run.

**Criterion 4.** The cited literature supplies no six-year Dow-versus-EW30 return series or strategy Sharpe for this signal, so an evidence-based positive Sharpe estimate is unavailable. The honest evidence-only planning estimate is **0.0 expected annualized Dow excess Sharpe**, with a one-sided six-year block-bootstrap *p* proxy of **about 0.50** under the null. As an optimistic sensitivity only, an annualized excess Sharpe of 0.20 over six years implies a normal-approximation one-sided *p* around **0.31**, still well short of the coordinator’s roughly 0.8 Sharpe / *p* < 0.05 requirement before Holm. These are not observed results or an actual bootstrap; an empirical block-bootstrap p-value cannot be produced without portfolio outcomes, which are out of scope here. **Criterion-4 gate fails.**

### (d) Free-data, turnover, and costs — FAIL unless the input proxy is repaired

The staged `r4_L4/data/13f_development_pit_positions.csv` exists and includes report dates, manager IDs, filing-availability dates, tickers, reported shares, and reported values. But the staged panel is selected at the **manager** level (top managers by portfolio value); it does not say which reported shares are held in index-tracking mandates. Vanguard, BlackRock, State Street, or another manager can report both passive and active accounts. Their total manager holdings cannot validly be labelled “passive ownership.” The report-date positions also need a free, point-in-time shares-outstanding denominator. Do not substitute total 13F ownership for passive ownership or use current manager classifications retrospectively. The public SEC 13F process is quarterly and gives managers up to 45 days to file, so every formation must wait for actual filing availability. [SEC Form 13F FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/frequently-asked-questions-about-form-13f).

A data gate could pass only if a free, mandate-level N-PORT/index-fund file or a documented passive-only holdings series can be joined to the staged panel and shares outstanding with reliable point-in-time dates. If that cannot be verified input-only, stop before outcomes. Quarterly holding updates also limit the number of distinct pressure shocks and can produce clustered orders at disclosure/rebalance dates.

For the fixed top-five Dow illustration, `break_even_rank_ic(turnover=0.20, return_dispersion=0.06, cost_per_side=0.0005, top_fraction=5/30)` gives a **0.001155** cost-only IC hurdle. The assumed 6% one-month cross-sectional dispersion is a planning assumption, not project data. At 20% total traded notional, direct transaction cost is 1 bp of NAV per monthly rebalance, or about 12 bp per year; to keep costs below one-quarter of gross edge, gross active return must exceed about **48 bp/year**, equivalent to IC above **0.00462** under the helper’s linear rank mapping. This excludes any extra impact and does not address signal validity. The portfolio may change only within the 20% turnover cap; if the desired top-five book would breach it, carry the unfilled target forward. **Low costs do not cure the absent documented effect or passive-data classification gap.**

### (e) Novelty and comparison with R5 — distinct signal, weak feasibility

This is distinct from **R5 D4**: D4 used daily close-location pressure diffused through a graph of common 13F owners and predicted five-session returns; P4 would measure the quarterly change in passive index ownership itself and predict a next-month return. It uses no common-owner adjacency, CLV/NCP pressure score, or network diffusion. It is also distinct from **R5 ETF-flow**: that proposal sought ETF creations/redemptions and their constituent holdings, but was stopped because verified daily share-history coverage was limited to SPY and DIA and N-PORT holdings were quarterly. P4 is an issuer-level ownership-stock change from a named passive portfolio universe, not an ETF daily flow signal passed through holdings. The current staged manager-level panel still cannot identify the needed passive portfolio universe, which is a real input limitation rather than a reason to relabel all 13F ownership as passive.

**Final ranking: one direction, not recommended. Subjective chance of passing all gates: about 1%.** Kill before any outcome access if mandate-level holdings and the shares-outstanding denominator are not verified from free inputs, if the filing-date series cannot be formed point-in-time, or if the mechanism effect cannot be distinguished from size and index membership. No portfolio, return, or outcome test is authorized by this proposal.
