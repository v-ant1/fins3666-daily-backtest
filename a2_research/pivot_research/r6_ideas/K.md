## Data boundary and common design constraints

The event-count planning estimate is approximately **500 firms × 4 quarterly announcements × 6 years = 12,000 S&P issuer-quarter events**. The Dow portfolio has only about **30 × 4 × 6 = 720** gross issuer-quarter events before PIT membership changes, duplicate filings, partial sample endpoints, and missing events. These are gross planning counts, not observed counts; deduplicate amended or duplicate 8-Ks to one first qualifying filing per issuer/reporting quarter, use point-in-time constituent and issuer histories, and report gaps rather than filling them with current constituents.

The existing `r4_L4/data/earnings_clock.csv` is only a seed: it contains **1,649 Item 2.02 8-K/8-K/A filings across 63 tickers**, accepted from 2020-06-02 through 2026-10-30. The source set is point-in-time Dow plus a fixed 2026 top-50 S&P market-cap list, not the full S&P 500. Its existing QA says 897 events were classified before 09:30 ET, 732 at/after 16:00, and 20 during market hours. The classifications are based on **SEC acceptance time**, not verified issuer press-release time, so they are only a clock proxy.

Extending event metadata to the historical S&P roster is feasible at low monetary cost: `r6_data/sp500_sic_panel.csv` already maps PIT tickers to CIKs, and SEC EDGAR submissions provide public, unauthenticated filing histories and accepted-filing timestamps. Search/filter Form 8-K and 8-K/A with Item 2.02, using the CIK and ticker-lineage panel; fetch prior submission-history JSON files for any CIK whose relevant window falls outside its recent filing block. Add 6-K earnings releases for foreign private issuers only under a pre-set rule, because 6-K items are not tagged like 8-K Item 2.02. SEC documents acceptance timestamps and supports bulk submission-history downloads ([SEC EDGAR API documentation](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces), [SEC accessing EDGAR data](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data)). An S&P extension is therefore technically straightforward, but still needs point-in-time CIK lineage, deduplication, event-date validation, and a price-coverage report before it is decision-grade.

For daily-price alignment, use acceptance time only as the conservative time at which the SEC filing is available. A pre-open acceptance can map to that session's close; an after-close acceptance maps to the next session's close; intraday acceptances should be excluded from the primary event window or assigned to the next full session. In all cases the event-pressure score is known only after that close and any trade occurs at the next eligible rebalance. This avoids look-ahead but does not establish that EDGAR acceptance equals the first public news timestamp. If release-time uncertainty cannot be bounded, fail the event-timing gate rather than selecting a convenient day.

## Cluster-aware power and common portfolio cost assumptions

The raw 12,000-event count is not the inferential sample size. Announcements bunch into four quarterly earnings seasons per year. Treat the six-year window as **24 season clusters**, with one cross-sectional estimate per season; within each season, firms in the same 2-digit SIC are one effective industry cluster. Use 54 S&P SIC2 groups and 18 Dow SIC2 groups as conservative breadth assumptions, giving cross-sectional IC-SD stress values `1/sqrt(54−1)=0.13736` and `1/sqrt(18−1)=0.24254`. Apply an assumed AR(1) correlation of 0.50 across season estimates and Newey–West lag 4; the simulator's effective temporal N is about **10.79**, not 12,000. These are planning assumptions, not sample estimates.

I ran `r4_harness/power_sim.py` for 20,000 synthetic draws per row. The effective test unit is the season cluster; the event count is deliberately **not** used to shrink standard errors as if all same-season filings were independent. `horizon=5` here sets the simulator's NW lag to 4; it does not mean the event outcome window is five quarters. Results:

| Design / universe | Event/season clusters | Effective SIC2 groups | IC-SD stress | Planted mean IC | Power for NW t ≥ 2 | 80%-power MDE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Own-event pressure, S&P | 24 | 54 | 0.13736 | 0.00554 cost hurdle | 11.36% | 0.11885 |
| Own-event pressure, Dow | 24 | 18 | 0.24254 | 0.00554 cost hurdle | 10.74% | 0.20985 |
| Peer transfer, S&P | 24 | 54 | 0.13736 | 0.01386 cost hurdle | 14.72% | 0.11885 |
| Peer transfer, Dow | 24 | 18 | 0.24254 | 0.01386 cost hurdle | 12.53% | 0.20985 |

At the **0.0183** R6 reference IC (a benchmark, not an effect estimated by the papers), simulated power is only 17.77% for S&P SIC2 and 13.95% for Dow SIC2. The 80%-power MDEs are so large that neither mechanism currently has an honest small-effect power case. Event-count clustering may be worse than this model if SIC2 groups or calendar-week shocks remain correlated after season aggregation.

Both Dow portfolio illustrations use a 30-name PIT universe and top-five weights when fully established (83.3% active share versus EW30). One full name replacement sells 20% NAV and buys 20%, so total traded notional is **40%**, below the 50% cap. At 0.05% per dollar of total notional, each replacement costs `0.40 × 0.0005 = 0.0002`, or **2 bp**. Caps constrain turnover but can defer replacements and dilute a signal; they do not establish an edge.

## Direction 1 — own-firm announcement-return pressure persists

**Fixed question.** Does a firm's first complete post-acceptance session's market-adjusted return (the event pressure score) predict same-sign future market-adjusted returns after the event? Form the event return only after the timing rule above, measure pressure as the first full-session abnormal return, and test the next quarter's cumulative market-adjusted return. A positive event response predicts positive subsequent drift. Use one fixed horizon and no surprise-score or event-window sweeps.

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PASS for event-return drift; PARTIAL for the proposed pressure proxy.** Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara and Venkatachalam's 2008 paper, [“Earnings Announcements are Full of Surprises”](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/santa_clara_surprises.pdf), is a **working paper**, not a verified peer-reviewed 2008 JFE publication. Its three-day EAR-sorted portfolios show continuation, including reduced-but-positive results among the top 1,000 CRSP firms; the result supports the hypothesis but is not the same as an SEC-timestamped daily score. Chan, Jegadeesh and Lakonishok's [“Momentum Strategies,” *Journal of Finance* 51 (1996), DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb05222.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1996.tb05222.x), find that past earnings surprise predicts future return drift after controlling for past return; this is supportive but uses earnings surprise, not the proposed realized announcement-return score. Neither study establishes signed buying flow: an abnormal return mixes fundamental news repricing with order flow.
- **(b) Large-cap / after-2010 persistence — PARTIAL historically; FAIL for the required modern Dow claim.** Brandt et al. (working paper sample 1987–2004) repeat their EAR analysis on the top 1,000 CRSP firms and find the effect is reduced but remains positive and significant for several long holding horizons. That is real large-cap support, but it is not top-30 mega-cap evidence and ends before 2005. Chan et al. control for size but do not isolate post-2010 large caps. Chordia, Subrahmanyam and Tong, [“Have Capital Market Anomalies Attenuated in the Recent Era of High Liquidity and Trading Activity?” *Journal of Accounting and Economics* 58 (2014), DOI 10.1016/j.jacceco.2014.06.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacceco.2014.06.001), report broad anomaly attenuation associated with liquidity/arbitrage proxies. Josef Fink's [2021 review of PEAD](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbef.2020.100446) summarizes evidence that the classic effect may be small or absent in large, liquid U.S. stocks. No verified source establishes the proposed score in current Dow mega-caps after 2010 or after 2020.
- **(c) Clustered power and cost — FAIL.** For an explicit low-turnover design, refresh the Dow top-five portfolio once after each quarterly reporting season, using the latest available event score, hold for the next quarter, and permit at most one replacement per rebalance. This gives 40% turnover, 2 bp per rebalance, and at most 8 bp annual trading cost at four rebalances. Costs must be under 25% of gross active return, so gross excess must be at least **32 bp/year** (8 bp per quarter). For an illustrative, unmeasured 10% quarterly cross-sectional return dispersion, `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic(turnover=0.40, return_dispersion=0.10, cost_per_side=0.0005, top_fraction=5/30)` gives an IC cost-only hurdle of 0.001386 and a strict 25%-of-gross cost hurdle of **0.005543**. The season-cluster power at that hurdle is only 11.36% for S&P SIC2 and 10.74% for Dow SIC2; the corresponding 80%-power MDEs are 0.11885 and 0.20985. The quarterly refresh keeps costs low but may miss score changes during earnings season; quarterly portfolio turnover does not make the mechanism test more powerful.
- **(d) Free-data feasibility — PARTIAL.** EDGAR acceptance metadata is public, and the existing L4 clock demonstrates extraction for 63 tickers, but it has only 1,649 filings and acceptance time is not the true press-release timestamp. A PIT S&P extension through the existing CIK/SIC lineage is practical; 8-K-only capture misses some foreign issuers and some issuers may not use Item 2.02 consistently. The daily adjusted-price panel also has residual PIT names without bars, so event-price coverage must be measured on the complete event window before any outcomes are opened. No event return or outcome was calculated here.
- **(e) Novelty — FAIL / low.** Post-earnings-announcement drift and the event-return strategy are established replications. A timestamp-clean, PIT Dow/S&P transport with turnover control may be useful, but it is not a new pressure measure and may simply repackage PEAD.

## Direction 2 — early-announcer pressure transfers to non-announcing same-SIC2 peers

**Fixed question.** When the first firm in a PIT SIC2 group releases earnings, does its first full-session abnormal announcement return predict same-sign five-session abnormal returns of same-SIC2 firms that have not yet announced? Exclude a target from an event's forecast set once it has filed its own earnings report; aggregate overlapping source filings to one predetermined earliest announcement per SIC2/season so one industry shock does not masquerade as many independent events. The primary sign is **positive short-horizon spillover**; Thomas–Zhang's later correction result is a separately specified falsification, not evidence for indefinite continuation.

### Gates

- **(a) Verified literature — PARTIAL, with materially mixed signs.** Ramnath, [“Investor and Analyst Reactions to Earnings Announcements of Related Firms: An Empirical Analysis,” *Journal of Accounting Research* 40 (2002), DOI 10.1111/1475-679X.t01-1-00057](https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.t01-1-00057), reports that early announcers' forecast errors are informative about later announcers and that investors/analysts incompletely incorporate that news, with predictable peer returns in following days. Thomas and Zhang, [“Overreaction to Intra-industry Information Transfers?” *Journal of Accounting Research* 46 (2008), DOI 10.1111/j.1475-679X.2008.00294.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-679X.2008.00294.x), find that late announcers' price response to an early peer is **negatively related** to their response at their own later earnings release, consistent with overreaction and correction. These studies verify an information-transfer channel but do not show that a peer's abnormal return itself is a persistent buying-pressure signal. The evidence supports a tightly timed short-horizon test, not a generic positive drift claim.
- **(b) Large-cap / after-2010 persistence — FAIL-GATE.** Both cited studies predate the proposed post-2020 Dow test and do not show this signal survives among highly followed mega-cap firms. SIC2 peers share sector news and common shocks; a positive correlation can be information repricing rather than capital-flow transmission, while the Thomas–Zhang result warns that the initial peer move may reverse around the target's own report. There is no verified post-2010 evidence here for positive same-SIC2 Dow peer drift from the announcing firm's event return.
- **(c) Clustered power and cost — FAIL.** A candidate Dow implementation would rank non-announcing peers from the latest first-announcer shock, rebalance only in earnings-season weeks, hold for five sessions or until the target's own report, and allow at most one top-five replacement per active week. Turnover is capped at 40% per such rebalance (2 bp cost), but four seasons with about six active weeks each imply up to **24 rebalances/year**, 48 bp annual cost, and a gross-return requirement of at least **192 bp/year** for costs to remain ≤25% of gross. At an illustrative, unmeasured 4% five-session cross-sectional dispersion, the rank-IC cost-only hurdle is 0.003464 and the strict cost hurdle is **0.013856**. Under the one-observation-per-season, 54/18 SIC2 cluster simulation, power at that hurdle is only 14.72% for S&P SIC2 and 12.53% for Dow SIC2; 80%-power MDEs are 0.11885 and 0.20985. Seasonal bunching means event rows cannot be counted as independent observations, and the 50% cap may force missed or delayed peer trades.
- **(d) Free-data feasibility — PARTIAL.** The same EDGAR and daily-price inputs can support a free event build, but target-side “not yet announced” status requires complete issuer-level filings and de-duplication by fiscal quarter. Item2.02 timestamps provide a public filing clock, not necessarily the first public-release time; intraday timing errors can reverse which firm appears early. The SEC does not provide the industry-peer trade signal directly; it must be joined to PIT SIC2 assignments and point-in-time rosters. All inputs look obtainable, but complete usable events and synchronized price coverage have not been measured.
- **(e) Novelty — PARTIAL.** Intra-industry earnings information transfer is established. The narrow addition would be a realized abnormal-return shock, explicit target non-announcement filter, PIT SIC2 links, and a short investable window in a mega-cap portfolio. That is a useful adaptation, but likely a replication/measurement variant rather than a new network theory.

## Recommendation

Do not advance either direction to portfolio outcome testing on these gates alone. Direction 1 is the better bounded replication if the project wants an earnings-event design, but large-cap persistence, first-release-time accuracy, and the power shortfall remain unresolved. Direction 2 is more faithful to the network thesis but has contradictory transfer signs, a costly short-lived window, and extremely small effective event breadth; retain it only as a mechanism question with a pre-specified reversal placebo.
