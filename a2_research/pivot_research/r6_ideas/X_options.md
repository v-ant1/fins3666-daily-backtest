# R6 option-related pressure: three screened directions

**Status: pre-outcome research screen only.** This is not a preregistration and authorizes no strategy or variant. I used no return outcomes, option-signal outcome calculations, or holdout data. The only data pull was a free OCC volume-query feasibility check on development-window dates, recording response/row coverage and schema only.

## Exclusions and ranking

The existing C3 idea already covers deep-in-the-money call exercise and expiration pressure; S4–S6 explicitly reject that expiration family, and T_daily excludes expiration dates. B.md already flags options-implied pressure/dealer flow as infeasible because the workspace lacks historical positioning data. I therefore do not recommend reviving those proposals as variants. The three rows below are narrower candidate measurement designs, assessed for overlap and eligibility; a high literature effect does not cure a failed novelty or data gate.

|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Buyer-initiated opening option flow as signed pressure | **FAIL: input and novelty gates** | <1% | Pan–Poteshman find a large next-week spread, but their crucial buyer-initiated/opening classification is proprietary and absent from free aggregates. Probabilities are judgmental screens, not statistical estimates. Ranking is by reported edge scale before costs and not by likelihood of satisfying the full R6 goal.

## 1. Buyer-initiated opening option flow

**Proposed mechanism.** A signed call-versus-put measure constructed only from option trades initiated by buyers to open positions would be a direct pressure/information signal. The predicted sign would be positive for net call buying and negative for net put buying, over the following 5–21 sessions. That exact signal is not constructible from the free aggregate queried here; it is recorded to explain why the prominent literature result cannot be transferred to the proposed data.

**(a) Literature.** Pan and Poteshman, [“The Information in Option Volume for Future Stock Prices,” *Review of Financial Studies* 19 (2006), DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhj024](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhj024), use a unique dataset that identifies buyer-initiated volume opening new positions. Their low put-call-ratio portfolio outperforms the high-ratio portfolio by over 40 basis points the next day and over 1% the next week; those are the paper's historical results, not expected Dow returns. Johnson and So, [“The Option to Stock Volume Ratio and Future Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 106(2) (2012), 262–286, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.05.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.05.008), show that even unsigned option-to-stock trading volume predicts returns, reporting a 0.34% weekly spread between low and high ratio portfolios. The latter is a different, unsigned mechanism.

**(b) Large-cap / post-2020 case.** Equity options remain liquid for large firms and can offer leveraged information expression. But the cited effects use earlier periods and do not establish a post-2020 Dow effect; large-cap stocks have deep cash liquidity, extensive analyst coverage, and fast arbitrage. The strongest Pan–Poteshman evidence is specifically tied to trade direction and opening status, so its effect should not be assumed for an aggregate call-put ratio.

**(c) Power.** I ran `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with 500 assets, 72 monthly observations (six years), 21-session horizon, AR(1) 0.20, null-rank IC SD `1/sqrt(499)=0.0448`, 5,000 simulations, and fixed seeds 20261111/20261112. At an illustrative planted monthly IC of 0.05, `P(NW t≥2)=1.000`; at 0.02 it is 0.904. The simulated 80% MDE is 0.01818 (effective N about 48.97; Newey–West lag 20). These are assumption-only mechanism power figures, not calibrations from the papers or data, and omit SIC2 dependence and Holm correction. More importantly, even a real mechanism IC of 0.05 is below the optimistic 0.083–0.089 long-only Dow top-five-vs-EW30 criterion-4 lower bound in [the independent hurdle audit](../report_drafts/CRITERION4_BOUND_AUDIT.md).

**(d) Free-data feasibility.** The official [OCC Volume Query](https://www.theocc.com/market-data/market-data-reports/volume-and-open-interest/volume-query) exposes daily/weekly/monthly volume, customer/firm/market-maker account categories, and call/put filters, but states its query covers only the most recent 24 months. I queried the public CSV endpoint on 17 Oct 2025 for ten Dow names (AAPL, MSFT, JPM, XOM, CAT, WMT, UNH, HD, GS, DIS): all ten returned HTTP 200 with 18–21 CSV records; the header includes quantity, underlying, symbol, account type, call/put, exchange, activity date, and contract date. The same ten-symbol query for 15 Oct 2021 returned HTTP 200 with zero records for all ten. I did not inspect or use quantities. This confirms a recent sample exists but fails full-window coverage. Most critically, the fields do not identify aggressor side or whether a customer trade opened or closed a position. **Gate status: FAIL.**

**(e) Novelty.** B.md already rejects the general options-implied/dealer-flow direction for lack of signed, historical positioning data. This exact Pan–Poteshman measure also depends on an information field not present in OCC's free aggregates. Replacing it with unsigned call-put volume would change the construct and would not test the paper's proposed mechanism. No eligible variant should be registered from this screen.

## 2. Expiration-day hedge pressure and pinning

**Proposed mechanism.** Dated option inventory around strikes can make dealer delta hedging and exercise-related share trades affect the underlying near expiry. A candidate design would rank stocks by pre-expiry signed hedge/exercise exposure and predict a temporary close-to-close move toward strikes on expiry, with a predeclared reversal test after expiry. It would require contract-level open interest, strike, expiry, option type, and a defensible dealer-position sign; total contract volume alone is insufficient. The prior papers focus on individual-stock or index-futures price paths, so a separate peer-spillover hypothesis would need new evidence and cannot be smuggled in as a network effect.

**(a) Literature.** Ni, Pearson, and Poteshman, [“Stock Price Clustering on Option Expiration Dates,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 78 (2005), DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2004.08.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2004.08.005), document strike-price clustering in optionable stocks using 1996–2002 data and discuss hedge rebalancing and proprietary trading as contributors. Golez and Jackwerth, [“Pinning in the S&P 500 Futures,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 106 (2012), DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.06.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.06.010), find futures pinning on serial-futures-option expiries and anti-cross-pinning before S&P options expire, attributing the path to market-maker hedge rebalancing and investor exercise/reselling. These establish an expiry mechanism in historical samples; the futures-index result is not a Dow single-stock estimate.

**(b) Large-cap / post-2020 case.** Large-cap option markets are active, so the exposure exists in principle. Against that, the cited stock sample is old, deep cash liquidity should absorb stock flows, and growth in weekly and same-day expiries makes a single monthly event definition less representative. Neither source documents a persistent post-2020 Dow long-only premium or peer propagation. The conditional exposure may also be concentrated in a small subset of names, reducing breadth.

**(c) Power.** For a transparent comparison, the same 72-date, 500-name, 21-session synthetic design above gives 80% mechanism MDE 0.01818 under independent cross-sectional rank noise and AR(1)=0.20. This is optimistic for expiry events: it treats all 72 monthly observations as usable and does not model event clustering, cross-name industry dependence, selection into high-exposure names, or gaps in options histories. The actual published stock result in Ni et al. is strike clustering and a 16.5 bp average return alteration on expiry dates, not a monthly rank IC; it cannot be mapped to the 0.083–0.089 portfolio bound without exposure prevalence, conditional return dispersion, and a tradable rule. Thus the power calculation does not establish viability.

**(d) Free-data feasibility.** OCC's current query provides aggregate volume and a latest-24-month window, not a historical dated chain of open interest by strike and expiry with dealer sign. Cboe's [free historical-options page](https://www.cboe.com/us/options/market_statistics/historical_data/) describes historical options volume across Cboe exchanges, while detailed Open-Close data are a separate [DataShop product](https://datashop.cboe.com/cboe-options-open-close-volume-summary); Cboe-only volume is not consolidated total-market dealer inventory. The OCC sample above further shows no older 2021 rows through the queried endpoint. **Gate status: FAIL** for a 2020–2026 exposure reconstruction.

**(e) Novelty.** C3 already tested and rejected deep-in-the-money call exercise at expiration; S4–S6 also record the option-expiry family as a prior fail. A dealer-gamma label or a calendar-only expiry rule would be a relabeling unless it introduced a genuinely new, measurable signal and transmission channel. Since the required free exposure panel is unavailable, no distinct preregistration is justified.

## 3. Unsigned option activity relative to stock volume

**Proposed mechanism.** Measure monthly or weekly total listed option contracts traded in a stock relative to its share volume, without trying to infer buyer/seller side. Johnson and So's documented sign is negative: unusually high option-to-stock volume predicts lower subsequent returns. This is an activity/short-sale-constraint proxy, not literal buying pressure; it is included only as a separate measurement candidate, not as a proposed rescue of signed option flow.

**(a) Literature.** Johnson and So (2012, cited above) report a 0.34% weekly low-minus-high O/S spread. Roll, Schwartz, and Subrahmanyam, [“O/S: The Relative Trading Activity in Options and Stock,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 96 (2010), DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2009.11.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2009.11.004), define the option-to-stock volume ratio and show that it varies with firm characteristics and predicts higher absolute post-announcement returns; they caution that volume can reflect informed activity, hedging, or disagreement. Both papers support an association between relative options activity and future returns, but neither turns unsigned volume into signed purchases or validates a post-2020 Dow trade after costs.

**(b) Large-cap / post-2020 case.** Option usage is widespread among large firms and retail/professional participation may keep option volumes informative. Yet the proposed ratio can reflect volatility trading, hedging, multi-leg trades, closing positions, or the denominator's share-volume changes. Mega-cap stock volumes are large, and the cited studies do not provide the required post-2020 Dow effect magnitude. The sign is negative in the cited O/S studies, which is compatible with a pressure-reversal story only indirectly.

**(c) Power.** The 72-month, 500-name simulation provides an optimistic mechanism benchmark: an assumed +0.02 IC in the preregistered direction (after direction adjustment) yields 0.904 probability of `NW t≥2`; the 80% MDE is 0.01818. These assumptions ignore SIC2 and market-wide dependence, and should be treated as a floor on the required effect. A mechanism effect around 0.02 would still be much smaller than the criterion-4 IC lower bound of 0.083–0.089. The paper's weekly 0.34% spread is not a monthly rank IC and cannot be equated to that bound.

**(d) Free-data feasibility.** OCC's volume query can supply account-category and call/put aggregate volumes for recent dates; the sample check found all 10 tested Dow names returned records on 17 Oct 2025, but none on 15 Oct 2021 through the query endpoint. The official Cboe archive advertises historical volume across its exchanges from September 2019, but this is a subset of the options market and is not the consolidated total volume used by the papers; confirm exact file coverage and ticker mapping before any design. Stock volume exists in the staged development prices, but the option-volume coverage is incomplete through the directly checked free OCC interface. **Gate status: FAIL pending a documented full-window Cboe data manifest.**

**(e) Novelty.** This is not the signed option buying-pressure direction in Pan–Poteshman, and it is not the dealer inventory/expiration design already in C3. It is closer to Johnson–So's published unsigned activity ratio. The specific ratio was not found as a registered R6 test, but it is weakly connected to the core thesis: it measures options-market attention/activity and relies on a negative-return association, not identified stock-buying flow or a pressure network. On that basis it does not pass the thesis/substance gate, even if a complete free volume archive is later confirmed.

## Gate outcome and disposition

| Direction | (a) papers | (b) mega-cap/post-2020 case | (c) power | (d) free-data pull | (e) novelty/thesis | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer-initiated opening flow | Pass for historical mechanism | Not established post-2020 | Synthetic 500-name power is high only at assumed effects | Fail: free aggregates lack side/open-close; OCC older sample empty | Overlaps B's screened options-flow gap | Reject before variant |
| Expiry hedge/pinning | Pass for historical event mechanism | Not established for current Dow | Synthetic MDE optimistic; event effect not comparable to IC | Fail: no free historical chain/inventory | Already rejected C3/S4–S6 | Reject; do not reopen |
| Unsigned O/S activity | Pass for historical association | Not established post-2020 | Synthetic 0.02 IC power 90.4%; portfolio bound much higher | Partial: recent OCC coverage; Cboe subset needs manifest | Distinct ratio, but not signed buying pressure/network | Reject as outside core thesis |

**Conclusion:** no options direction passes all gates. The only sizeable published effect with the right direct buying-flow interpretation depends on proprietary signed/opening classification; free aggregates do not reproduce that field. Expiry pinning duplicates a rejected idea and lacks required exposure data, while the unsigned O/S ratio is not a measure of buying pressure and does not have a documented post-2020 Dow edge. No variant was used or authorized.
