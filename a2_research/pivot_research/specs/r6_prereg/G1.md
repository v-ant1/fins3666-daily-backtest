# G1 - Own-stock high-volume visibility premium

**Status: FROZEN - runner build authorized; outcome run coordinator-only.** This is one pre-registration for development testing only. It uses development-only daily OHLCV dated 2020-11-27 through 2026-10-30; workers must not inspect, download, load, plot, or calculate on sealed Dow holdout data.

## Hypothesis and fixed design

**Mechanism.** An unusually high trading-volume week can increase a stock's visibility and attract investor attention and demand, creating a short-lived positive price effect. The prediction is that stocks with greater abnormal volume in the completed formation week earn higher returns over the next 21 trading sessions. This is a visibility-demand proxy for buying pressure, not signed order flow: volume is unsigned and cannot distinguish buying from selling. The mechanism is therefore a testable hypothesis, not a claim that the volume shock itself identifies net buying.

**Primary sign and horizon.** Positive: the formation-week abnormal-volume score predicts positive next-21-session open-to-close returns. The confirmatory statistic is the mean of formation-date cross-sectional Spearman rank ICs in the point-in-time S&P 500. Use one-sided Newey-West inference with Bartlett weights and **lag 1 formation observation**; each 21-session outcome window is non-overlapping, while lag 1 allows adjacent formation-date dependence. The mechanism passes only if the mean IC is positive and the NW t-statistic is >=2.00. The cost-only rank-IC hurdle below is diagnostic only, not a pass condition. There is one signal, sign, horizon, portfolio, and strategy variant.

## Signal, timing, and development boundary

Use the staged development OHLCV panels only: S&P 500 OHLCV in `r6_data` and Dow 30 daily prices in `w1_engine/prices-daily*` with the already-staged Dow development gap-fill, plus the point-in-time rosters. Do not read any row before **2020-11-27** for either universe. No price, volume, or corporate-action data outside 2020-11-27 through 2026-10-30 may enter this test.

For ticker `i` and session `d`, define split-basis-consistent share volume `V[i,d]`. Use the staged share-volume field; if the source reports raw share counts, restate each earlier observation in the trailing window into the share units effective on `d` using only split factors effective by `d`. Do not apply dividend adjustments to volume. Before any outcomes, the input QA must document the source field, split-adjustment treatment, corporate-action coverage, ticker lineage, boundary, missing rows, and hashes; if split handling or volume coverage cannot be established, stop at input QA and report the variant not run rather than substitute a different proxy.

Let `x[i,d]=log(V[i,d])` for positive volume. For each day `d`, calculate the mean and sample standard deviation of `x` over the **50 preceding valid sessions** `d-50,...,d-1`, excluding day `d`; define `zvol[i,d]=(x[i,d]-mean50[i,d])/sd50[i,d]`. Require all 50 baseline observations, positive current volume, and nonzero baseline SD. For formation session `t`, define the one signal score as the arithmetic mean of the five completed daily shocks over `t-4,...,t`:

```text
HV5[i,t] = mean(zvol[i,d] for d = t-4,...,t)
```

The formation week ends at the formation close; its final day's reported volume is known then. This is the only signal transform. Missing bars are not filled, and no current-survivor substitute is allowed.

The staged development calendar has **1,488 sessions** from 2020-11-27 through 2026-10-30. Index sessions from zero at 2020-11-27. To ensure the first five-day formation week has 50 prior observations for each daily z-score without any earlier warm-up, start at session index **54** and form every **21 sessions** thereafter. The last eligible formation index is **1,461**, leaving all 21 forward sessions through index 1,482 within the development window; this fixes **68 formations**. There are no parameter-selected or date-selected formations. The S&P mechanism uses the same formation indices and calendar as the Dow strategy.

For a formation at session `t`, the next 21-session outcome begins at the adjusted open of session `t+1` and ends at the adjusted close of session `t+21`:

```text
fwd21[i,t] = adjusted_close[i,t+21] / adjusted_open[i,t+1] - 1
adjusted_open = raw_open * adjusted_close / raw_close
```

At outcome measurement, use the point-in-time S&P 500 roster at formation for the mechanism; use dated SIC2 as of formation. Do not use current constituents for past formations. A mechanism observation requires a valid `HV5`, known SIC2, and both endpoint bars. Report the eligible-name and SIC2 counts for every formation and all missing-data gaps.

**Input-only coverage gate first.** Before opening or dividing any forward-price values, make and hash the calendar, formation eligibility, PIT roster, SIC2, signal-bar and endpoint-bar availability masks using availability only. Require at least **250 eligible S&P names on each of the 68 formations**. If any formation fails, write the failed counts and stop all H19-H21 outcome calculations; do not drop dates, change the threshold, impute bars, or shorten the sample to force passage. Only after that gate passes may the runner compute `fwd21` and outcomes.

## Mechanism, cost diagnostic, and SIC2-preserving placebo

For each of the 68 eligible S&P formations, calculate the cross-sectional Spearman correlation between `HV5` and `fwd21`; the primary series is the sequence of date-wise ICs, not a pooled stock-session panel. Use a one-sided positive-mean Newey-West test with Bartlett lag 1 formation observation. Report mean IC, NW t-statistic, raw one-sided p-value, formation dates, eligible names, SIC2 coverage, and the coverage-gate counts. H19 passes only if mean IC > 0 and NW t >= 2.00. Report raw and R6-Holm-adjusted p-values separately; Holm adjustment is not an additional H19 or H20 mechanism gate.

For a cost-only planning diagnostic, the G direction note used `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` with turnover 0.50, an **assumed** 6% 21-session cross-sectional return dispersion, cost 0.05% per traded dollar, and reference top fraction 5/30, yielding IC 0.002887. This is not measured from project outcomes and is not a mechanism or portfolio gate. The mapping assumes a linear rank-return relation and a fixed top fraction; it does not reproduce the varying available-name K rule, turnover-cap path, or realized Dow costs. Preserve it only as a labeled diagnostic and do not use it to alter H19 progression.

Run one within-formation-date, within-SIC2 signal-label shuffle. Within each date and SIC2 group, permute the eligible names' `HV5` scores, preserving each group's score distribution and eligible-name set; leave singleton groups unchanged. Recompute the date-wise Spearman ICs and their mean for each draw. Use exactly **500 draws**, seed **20261111**, and save all draws and the fixed masks. The one-sided p-value is:

```text
p = (1 + count(shuffled_mean_IC >= observed_mean_IC)) / 501
```

H20 passes only if the observed mean IC is positive, exceeds the null's 95th percentile, and p < 0.05. Do not change the seed, SIC2 groups, eligible mask, draw count, or statistic after outcomes. H21 is not run unless the raw H19 and H20 gates both pass.

## Honest power statement

The available G design calculation treats the **131 SIC3 groups** as effective cross-sectional units, not all S&P names as independent. For the revised 68 non-overlapping formation observations, I used `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with `assets=131`, `dates=68`, `horizon=2` (one lag in formation-observation units), signal autocorrelation 0.20, assumed IC SD 0.0877 (`1/sqrt(130)`), planted mean IC 0.0183, 5,000 simulations, and seed 20261111. Synthetic power for NW t >= 2 was **0.3754** (Monte Carlo SE 0.00685); mean effective date count was 56.67 and the 80%-power MDE was **0.0331**. These are design assumptions, not measured signal autocorrelation or project effects, and the one-unit-per-SIC3 approximation may understate residual dependence.

The 0.0183 planted IC is a generic R6 planning benchmark, not a documented G1 or Dow mega-cap effect. The literature's reported monthly high-minus-low-volume portfolio spreads use a different signal, portfolio construction and sample and cannot be converted directly into this rank IC. The power result therefore indicates low power at the benchmark, and the actual large-cap effect is uncertain; keep the registered sign/t-statistic test unchanged and report failures honestly.

## Dow long-only portfolio, missing prices, and inference

Only after the S&P mechanism and placebo gates both pass, trade HV5 monthly on point-in-time Dow constituents at the 21-session cadence:

- At formation close, freeze the names with a valid `HV5` signal. At the next session's open, intersect those names with the PIT Dow roster effective at that open and with names whose execution open is available. Let `n` be the number of names in that same open-available PIT roster; it is also the same-date available-name EW30 benchmark cohort. Set `K=min(5,floor(n/2))`; hold the top K eligible names by HV5 equally weighted, or all qualifying names if fewer than K are available. If none qualify, hold cash. Break ties by ticker ascending. The same-session close is not used to decide the open-time roster, price availability, or buys.
- Start from point-in-time EW30 and move pro rata toward the target so buys plus sells divided by NAV are capped at **50% per rebalance**, including the initial transition. Rebalance every 21 sessions and hold between fills. Charge **0.05% per dollar of actual traded notional** (each buy and sell dollar once; do not double turnover again). Report average and maximum turnover, cap-bind count, costs, cost/gross-edge ratio, average realized active share, gross and net excess return, annualized net Sharpe versus EW30, and drawdown. Require average realized active share >=50% versus the same-date available-name EW30 benchmark.
- Apply coordinator decision 63 identically to strategy and EW30. If a held name lacks a close on a day, carry its last valid close for marking and record zero return until a valid close resumes; when prices resume, mark from the last valid close. Carry a stale holding at that last valid close until the next scheduled 21-session rebalance; sell it at that rebalance's next valid open. If no later valid open/price occurs (for example, after delisting), liquidate at its last valid close. Mark the position at the carried last valid close between the gap and the sale/liquidation, and include any return realized at the actual sale price. Do not create an unscheduled rebalance, silently drop a held name, or reconstitute either book with a successor.
- Compare daily net portfolio returns with point-in-time EW30 using the same available-name and missing-price rules. Apply a circular block bootstrap to daily net excess returns with **10-session blocks, 5,000 draws, and seed 20261111**. Report the raw and full-R6 Holm-adjusted p-values and DSR at the cumulative trial count.

Criterion 4 requires positive net excess return after costs, net Sharpe above EW30, average active share >=50%, and one-sided block-bootstrap p < 0.05 after full-family Holm correction. The 50% hard turnover cap meets the goal's turnover condition. Do not use an alternative turnover cap, cost condition, portfolio or signal to rescue a failure.

## Evidence basis and limitations

- Gervais, Kaniel and Mingelgrin (2001), [“The High-Volume Return Premium,” *Journal of Finance* 56(3), 877-919](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00349), document that unusually high daily or weekly volume predicts appreciation over the subsequent month and interpret the result through visibility and investor demand. Their result motivates the positive sign; it does not establish that an unsigned standardized volume score produces net buying pressure in Dow mega-caps under this turnover/cost design.
- Kaniel, Ozoguz and Starks (2012), [“The High Volume Return Premium: Cross-Country Evidence,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 103(2), 255-279](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2011.08.012), report broad international evidence but a weaker premium for relatively large firms, a relevant limitation for the Dow.
- Wang (2021), [“The High Volume Return Premium and Economic Fundamentals,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 140(1), 325-345](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.10.006), report a 0.53% monthly size-adjusted, value-weighted U.S. high-minus-low volume-decile premium over 1963-2016. This extends through 2016, not through the current development period; a decile portfolio spread is not a rank IC or the expected Dow excess return.
