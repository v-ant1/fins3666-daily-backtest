## Hypothesis and distinction from E1

**Mechanism.** Same-industry peers' previous-week **intraday buying-pressure proxy**, measured by dollar-volume-weighted close-location value (CLV), is followed by positive intraday returns in the focal firm during the next week. The proposed path is that professional, intraday demand and delayed price correction persist across tightly classified industry peers. The pressure input is the peer's high/low/close location weighted by its regular-session trading value; it is not simply the peer's open-to-close return.

**Why the primary sign is positive.** Lou, Polk and Skouras find continuation in the intraday return component and cross-period reversals, so an intraday pressure signal predicts continuation only in the later intraday component, not an assumed positive close-to-close effect. Wang (2026) finds that peer intraday returns positively predict focal-firm intraday returns in the following month, with only weak overnight reaction. The registered hypothesis applies that same-sign intraday continuation to a separate CLV pressure proxy and a five-session horizon. The expected **partial rank IC is positive**. No close-to-close, overnight, or negative-sign result can substitute for this primary claim.

This is deliberately different from E1: E1 uses monthly SIC2 peer CLV pressure to predict next-21-session total return. C1 uses weekly SIC4 links, a previous-week intraday CLV signal, a next-week **intraday-only** focal return, and tests incremental signal after removing own and peer overnight/intraday return components. The narrower group, shorter timing, outcome component, and residualized pressure measure test a different clienteles/slow-correction mechanism. C1 must not collapse into a generic peer intraday return sort.

## Signal, controls, outcome and primary statistic

### Intraday CLV pressure

For firm `j` and trading session `d`, calculate the regular-session close-location value from the same day's high, low and close:

```text
CLV[j,d] = 0,                                      if high[j,d] = low[j,d]
           (2*close[j,d] - high[j,d] - low[j,d])
             / (high[j,d] - low[j,d]),              otherwise
```

Daily dollar value is `raw_close[j,d] * volume[j,d]`. The previous-week firm pressure is the dollar-volume-weighted mean of its five daily CLVs:

```text
IP[j,t] = sum_d(CLV[j,d] * raw_close[j,d] * volume[j,d])
          / sum_d(raw_close[j,d] * volume[j,d]),
          d = the five sessions ending at formation close t.
```

The pressure lies in `[-1, 1]`; larger values mean more trading volume occurred on sessions closing toward their highs. The focal firm's **leave-one-out** peer pressure is the equal-weight mean across all other current SIC4 peers:

```text
PeerIP[i,t] = mean(IP[j,t] for j != i with active SIC4[j,t] = SIC4[i,t]).
```

This is a daily-OHLCV pressure proxy, not signed buyer-initiated order flow. Do not use the close-to-open overnight move in this score.

### Return-component controls and partial rank IC

To establish that CLV pressure adds information beyond the return components already studied in Wang (2026), form the following point-in-time controls over the **same five sessions ending at `t`**:

1. Focal firm's compounded intraday open-to-close return.
2. Focal firm's compounded overnight close-to-next-open return.
3. Equal-weight mean of leave-one-out SIC4 peers' compounded intraday returns.
4. Equal-weight mean of leave-one-out SIC4 peers' compounded overnight returns.

Use adjusted open/close from the approved development component input for returns. Each component is compounded as `product(1 + daily component return) - 1`. Controls must be computed from dates ending at `t`; no forward component or future industry membership enters.

For each weekly formation date, use the valid S&P500 cross-section. Convert `PeerIP`, the four controls, and the next-period outcome to within-date midrank percentiles. Regress the ranked `PeerIP` on an intercept and the four ranked return-component controls by ordinary least squares; separately regress ranked future intraday returns on the same controls. The **primary date-wise partial rank IC** is the Pearson correlation between the two residual vectors. The primary series is the sequence of these date-wise ICs.

The next-period outcome for focal firm `i` is:

```text
FUTURE_INTRADAY[i,t] = product_d(1 + adjusted_close[i,d] / adjusted_open[i,d] - 1) - 1,
                       d = the next five trading sessions after t.
```

The signal is formed after close `t`, and portfolio orders execute no earlier than the next session's open. Publish the scheduled-date and input-eligibility manifest before outcome access.

**Primary sign:** positive mean partial rank IC between leave-one-out peer CLV pressure and focal next-week intraday return. This is the only sign and horizon.

Calculate the cost-only rank-IC hurdle with `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` for the Dow top-five design. Use total traded notional (buys plus sells / NAV), not one-way purchases, for turnover:

```text
break_even_rank_ic(
    turnover=0.50,                 # 50% total buy-plus-sell NAV per rebalance
    return_dispersion=0.04,         # assumed weekly cross-sectional scale
    cost_per_side=0.0005,
    top_fraction=5/30,
) = 0.0043301270
```

The 4% weekly cross-sectional return dispersion is the planning assumption from C's cost discussion, not a project estimate. The helper output is a cost floor for the Dow design, not expected alpha and not evidence that turnover will fit the cap. ## Evidence and limitations

- Lou, Polk and Skouras, [“A Tug of War: Overnight versus Intraday Expected Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 134 (2019), 192–213](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.03.011), document firm-level continuation in overnight and intraday return components and offsetting cross-period reversal. Their sample is 1993–2013; it gives some large-stock and post-2010 support but does not establish a current peer-CLV edge.
- Wang, [“Decoding Momentum Spillover Effects,” *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 61(5) (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S002210902510238X), reports peer intraday-return continuation into focal firms' next-period intraday returns and weak overnight reaction; peer overnight returns show an overnight continuation followed by intraday reversal. The study's sample ends in 2021 and includes industry among alternative peer definitions, so the proposed CLV pressure variable, SIC4 graph, five-session horizon, incremental partial-rank test and Dow implementation are not established by it.

Daily OHLCV cannot identify buyer-initiated transactions, and close location may reflect news or common industry movement. The point of the residualized peer CLV signal and fixed shuffled/time-shift placebos is to test whether it contributes beyond return components and group size. A failure of coverage, cluster-aware power, mechanism, or placebo gates kills C1; do not relabel a conventional intraday-return sort as pressure diffusion.
