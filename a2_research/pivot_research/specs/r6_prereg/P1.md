# P1 — Own-stock overnight-return momentum

**Status: FROZEN 2026-11-11 (see footer).**

## Hypothesis and fixed design

**Mechanism.** Buying pressure from investors who prefer to trade at the open can persist across nights, so a stock with stronger overnight returns over the prior month may continue to outperform over the next month. This is an own-stock timing hypothesis. It does not use peer pressure, an industry network, or J1's reversal signal.

**Primary sign and horizon.** Positive: the trailing overnight-return signal predicts a positive next-calendar-month total return. The confirmatory statistic is the mean of monthly point-in-time S&P 500 date-wise Spearman rank ICs. Use one-sided Newey–West inference with Bartlett weights and **lag 1 monthly observation**. Monthly formations and next-calendar-month outcome windows do not overlap; lag 1 is the predeclared horizon-matched HAC lag. The mechanism passes only if the mean IC is positive and its NW t-statistic is ≥2.00. The cost-only IC floor below is a labelled diagnostic, not an additional gate. There is one signal, sign, horizon, portfolio, and strategy variant.

## Signal, timing, and data boundary

Use adjusted daily open and close prices. For security `i` and formation session `t`, define the overnight return for session `d` as:

```text
on[i,d] = adjusted_open[i,d] / adjusted_close[i,d-1] - 1
ON21[i,t] = product(on[i,d] for the 21 completed sessions d=t-20,...,t) - 1
```

`adjusted_open` is derived using the same-day adjustment factor, `raw_open × adjusted_close / raw_close`. `ON21` is a compounded return, not a sum. The signal score is the raw `ON21`; Spearman IC supplies the rank transformation. Use only price observations on or after **2020-06-01**. A stock with any missing required open or prior close in its 21-session window has no signal on that formation; do not fill a signal bar or use a current-survivor substitute.

The permitted Dow development price panel begins on **2020-11-27**. Form at the close of the final U.S. trading session of each calendar month, beginning on the first month-end for which 21 complete overnight observations can be calculated entirely from that panel: **2020-12-31**. Continue through **2026-09-30**, yielding at most 70 monthly formation dates. The S&P 500 mechanism uses exactly this same formation-date set. The signal month is the 21 sessions ending at the formation close. The next-month outcome starts at the adjusted open of the first U.S. trading session of the following calendar month and ends at the adjusted close of its last U.S. trading session:

```text
fwd_month[i,t] = adjusted_close[i,last_session(M+1)] /
                 adjusted_open[i,first_session(M+1)] - 1
```

This next-month open-to-close return excludes the overnight move already used at the formation date. The first forecast month is January 2021; no signal bar or outcome window before the allowed Dow development start of 2020-11-27 is used. The last formation is September 2026 because the staged development price window ends 2026-10-30. Freeze the formation and eligibility mask from the exchange calendar, point-in-time membership, SIC2 availability, and required-bar availability before opening outcome values.

Use the point-in-time S&P 500 roster for mechanism estimation and the point-in-time Dow roster for the portfolio. Use dated SIC2 as of formation for the SIC2 placebo and mechanism eligibility. A name needs a complete ON21 signal, known SIC2, and complete next-month endpoint bars for the mechanism cross-section. Report each monthly eligible-name count, SIC2 coverage, and data gaps; do not change dates or fill missing values to improve results. Apply the R6 date-wise price-gap rule: exclude a name only on dates its required inputs are unavailable.

## Mechanism, cost floor, and SIC2-preserving placebo

For each eligible S&P formation, calculate the cross-sectional Spearman correlation between `ON21` and `fwd_month`. The primary time series is the sequence of monthly date-wise ICs; do not pool all stock-months into one observation. Use a one-sided positive-mean NW test with Bartlett lag 1 monthly observation. Report mean IC, NW t, raw one-sided p, dates, and eligible names. H13 passes on the registered positive sign and NW t≥2.00; the cost-only IC floor below is a labelled planning diagnostic only and is not an additional pass or progression gate. Report R6-Holm adjusted p-values separately for every endpoint.

For a transparent cost-only planning diagnostic (not a gate), use the existing `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` helper with a 50% total-traded-notional turnover cap per monthly rebalance, 10% assumed monthly cross-sectional return dispersion, 0.05% cost per traded dollar, and the fixed reference top fraction 5/30:

```text
break_even_rank_ic(turnover=0.50, return_dispersion=0.10,
                   cost_per_side=0.0005, top_fraction=5/30)
    = 0.0017320508
```

The 10% return dispersion is a planning assumption, not estimated from project prices or outcomes. The helper maps a Spearman rank IC to top-quantile active return under a linear rank-return assumption; the result covers explicit costs only and does not guarantee a positive Sharpe or a criterion-4 pass. The 5/30 input is a fixed reference approximation; the portfolio's actual selection count is `K=min(5,floor(n/2))`, where `n` is the open-time available Dow roster count. Turnover is total buys plus sells divided by NAV and is not doubled again. The diagnostic never changes the pre-registered H13 sign-and-t-statistic gate.

Run a within-date, within-SIC2 signal-label shuffle. For each formation, permute the eligible names' `ON21` values within SIC2, preserving each group's score distribution and the eligible-name set; leave singleton groups unchanged. Recompute the monthly date-wise rank IC series and its mean for each draw. Use exactly **500 draws**, seed **20261111**, and retain the full null distribution and masks. The one-sided p-value is:

```text
p = (1 + count(shuffled_mean_IC >= observed_mean_IC)) / 501
```

The placebo passes only if the observed mean IC exceeds the 95th percentile of this positive-direction null and p<0.05. Do not change the seed, SIC2 groups, eligible mask, draw count, or statistic after seeing outcomes. H15 is not run unless the raw H13 and H14 gates both pass.

## Honest power statement

The proposed monthly IC test is underpowered at a modest effect. As an ex-ante planning run only, `r4_harness/power_sim.py` was used with 500 names, 71 monthly dates, monthly NW lag 1 (`--horizon 2`), signal autocorrelation 0.20, rank-IC SD 0.1002, planted mean IC 0.015, 5,000 simulations, and seed 20261111. The registered calendar now allows at most 70 dates, so this 71-date planning estimate is slightly optimistic and is retained only as a transparent approximate power diagnostic, not a pass gate. The IC SD is the conservative cluster-adjusted planning value carried from the P1 idea note (the null rank-IC SD `1/sqrt(499)` inflated by `sqrt(5)` for an assumed SIC2 design effect); it is not fitted from outcomes. Simulated power for NW t≥2 was **0.241** (Monte Carlo SE 0.00605), mean effective date count was 59.17, and the 80%-power MDE was **0.0370**. The planted IC of 0.015 is an optimistic translation of the published overnight/intraday evidence, not a measured P1 or project effect. Apply only the registered sign and NW t≥2 mechanism gate, and report failures honestly; a low-power result is not a pass.

The literature does not establish that this own-stock overnight-return sort produces a post-2010 Dow mega-cap excess Sharpe near the R6 feasibility benchmark. The prior P1 idea note used a generous transfer of the paper's roughly 0.45% monthly combined long-short alpha and estimated only about 0.17 net annualized excess Sharpe for a Dow top-five long-only adaptation under its assumptions. That is a planning judgment, not a forecast based on project outcomes. The development portfolio test, not this estimate, determines the registered result.

## Dow long-only portfolio, missing prices, and inference

Only after the S&P mechanism and placebo gates pass, trade the same `ON21` signal monthly on point-in-time Dow constituents:

- At formation close, form the signal-valid Dow set. At the first session of the following month, intersect that set with names in the point-in-time Dow roster effective at that open and with names whose execution open is available; only this intersection is eligible for the strategy. Let `n` be the count of point-in-time roster names with an available execution open at that same open, which is also the same-date available-name EW30 benchmark cohort. Set `K=min(5,floor(n/2))`; hold the top K eligible signal names equally weighted, or all eligible signal names if fewer than K qualify. If none qualify, hold cash. Ties are broken by ticker ascending. Never use the same session's close to decide roster membership or buy availability at its open.
- Start from point-in-time EW30 and move toward the target pro rata so total buys plus sells divided by NAV is at most **50% per rebalance**, including the initial transition. Rebalance monthly and hold between formation dates. Charge **0.05% per dollar of actual traded notional**, counting buys and sells once in turnover. Report average and maximum turnover, cap-bind count, costs, cost/gross-edge ratio, average realized active share, gross and net excess return, annualized net Sharpe versus EW30, and drawdown. The portfolio must maintain average active share ≥50% versus the same-date available-name EW30 benchmark.
- Apply coordinator decision 63 identically to strategy and EW30. If a held name lacks a close on a day, carry its last valid close for marking and record zero return until a valid close resumes; when prices resume, mark from the last valid close. Carry a stale holding at that last valid close until the next scheduled monthly rebalance; do not create an unscheduled sale. At that rebalance, sell at the name's next valid open. If no later valid open/price occurs (for example, after delisting), liquidate at its last valid close. Mark the position at the carried last valid close between the missing close and liquidation/sale, and include any return realized at the actual sale price. Apply the identical marking and sale rule to EW30. Do not silently drop a held name or reconstitute either book with a successor.
- Compare daily net portfolio returns against point-in-time EW30 using the same available-name and missing-price rules. Apply a circular block bootstrap to daily net excess returns with **10-session blocks, 5,000 draws, and seed 20261111**. Report raw and full-R6 Holm-adjusted p-values and DSR at the cumulative trial count.

Criterion 4 requires positive net excess return, net Sharpe above EW30, average active share ≥50%, and a one-sided block-bootstrap p<0.05 after full-family Holm correction. The registered turnover cap of 50% satisfies the goal's turnover-or-cost condition; the cost/gross-edge ratio is diagnostic and is not an extra pass gate. Do not use a different turnover cap or cost condition to rescue a failure.

## Evidence basis and limitations

- Lou, Polk, and Skouras (2019), [“A Tug of War: Overnight versus Intraday Expected Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 134(1), 192–213](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.03.011), document persistent overnight and intraday return components and opposite-sign patterns for several momentum/reversal strategies. Their sample covers 1993–2013; it supports separating night and day returns but does not establish the exact monthly `ON21` predictor's post-2010 mega-cap performance.
- Bogousslavsky (2021), [“The Cross-Section of Intraday and Overnight Returns,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 141(1), 172–194](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2020.07.020), studies the cross-section of intraday and overnight returns through 2015 and discusses how overnight risk and institutional constraints affect arbitrage. It motivates a distinct timing channel, but does not show that this P1 long-only Dow implementation passes the R6 cost and Sharpe gates.
- The papers report different return decompositions and portfolio sorts; their statistics are not treated as measured project effects. A daily adjusted-open price is also a proxy for the opening price used in the literature, and opening-price execution/measurement noise must be disclosed.
