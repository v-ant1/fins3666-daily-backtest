## Hypothesis and fixed design

**Mechanism.** A stock's unusually high return relative to its same-industry peers over the preceding week partly reflects temporary, non-fundamental price pressure. It predicts a lower relative return over the following week. The mechanism is tested on point-in-time S&P 500 membership; only after the mechanism and placebo gates pass is the same signal traded in a point-in-time Dow 30 top-five portfolio.

**Primary direction.** The raw signal is the stock's trailing five-session close-to-close return minus the mean trailing five-session return of its leave-one-out same-SIC2 peers. The primary date-wise Spearman IC is **negative**, measured against the stock's next-five-session return relative to those peers. High relative-return stocks are predicted to underperform peers; the portfolio therefore buys the five lowest relative-return scores. This is the only sign, score, horizon, and variant. No alternative lookback, horizon, classification, signal, or sign may rescue a failure.

### Point-in-time membership and SIC2 peers

1. Use `r6_data/sp500_pit_roster.csv` for S&P point-in-time membership and the corresponding PIT Dow membership input for portfolio formation. On each formation date, use only members effective on that date.
2. Use the dated SIC records in `r6_data/sp500_sic_panel.csv` and the corresponding Dow SIC panel. SIC2 is the first two numeric digits of the SEC SIC code. A SIC filing is usable on the first U.S. trading session strictly after its SEC acceptance date; it is not usable at that session's prior close. Carry the last known SIC2 forward only from its documented usable-from date and within the corresponding historical security/CIK lineage. Do not apply current SIC or a current ticker retroactively.
3. The eligible peer group for a target at date `t` consists of other PIT members with the same known SIC2 and complete required signal inputs at `t`. A target requires at least one other eligible peer. No SIC broadening, singleton imputation, or peer carry-forward is permitted.

### Signal, outcome, and timing

Use adjusted close for all five daily close-to-close returns. For ticker `i` and formation session `t`, define:

```text
r5[i,t] = adjusted_close[i,t] / adjusted_close[i,t-5] - 1
peer_r5[i,t] = arithmetic mean(r5[j,t] for eligible j != i with SIC2[j,t] = SIC2[i,t])
signal[i,t] = r5[i,t] - peer_r5[i,t]
```

The signal is observed at the formation close. The single execution delay is **one trading session**: the first portfolio trade is at the open of session `t+1`. For the mechanism, the forward five-session return is from adjusted open at `t+1` through adjusted close at `t+5`:

```text
adj_open[i,d] = raw_open[i,d] * adjusted_close[i,d] / raw_close[i,d]
fwd5[i,t] = adjusted_close[i,t+5] / adj_open[i,t+1] - 1
peer_fwd5[i,t] = arithmetic mean(fwd5[j,t] for eligible j != i with SIC2[j,t] = SIC2[i,t])
relative_fwd5[i,t] = fwd5[i,t] - peer_fwd5[i,t]
```

Thus the primary mechanism statistic is Spearman correlation across eligible S&P names on each formation date between `signal[i,t]` and `relative_fwd5[i,t]`. The expected sign is negative. Rebalance on every fifth U.S. trading session, anchored at the first eligible development session, from 2020-11-27 through 2026-10-30. A formation date is confirmatory only when the complete trailing signal and full forward five-session calendar window exist. Fix this date mask from the trading calendar and input availability before opening any outcome data. Do not compute a return from missing bars or impute one.

## Mechanism gate and cost floor

The confirmatory mechanism is the mean of date-wise S&P 500 Spearman ICs between `signal` and `relative_fwd5`. Compute Newey–West inference with Bartlett weights and **lag 4**, one less than the five-session forecast horizon. Use a one-sided, direction-adjusted test: equivalently, `mean(IC) < 0` and the NW t-statistic for `-IC` is **at least 2.00**. Also require the negative mean IC magnitude to meet the cost-only floor below; a smaller significant IC does not pass the requested economic floor.

The cost floor uses `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` with total traded-notional turnover 0.40 per weekly rebalance (one 20%-weight replacement: one 20% sale plus one 20% purchase), assumed weekly cross-sectional return dispersion 0.04, 0.05% cost per dollar of total traded notional, and top fraction `5/30`:

```text
break_even_rank_ic(turnover=0.40, return_dispersion=0.04,
                   cost_per_side=0.0005, top_fraction=5/30)
    = 0.0034641016
```

The mechanism must therefore have `mean(IC) <= -0.0034641016` and direction-adjusted NW t ≥2. This is the cost floor from the helper, not a forecast of expected alpha. The 4% dispersion is a planning assumption, not measured from project outcomes. Turnover is buys plus sells divided by NAV and already includes both sides; do not double it. Actual portfolio net performance and the R6 portfolio gates remain separate.

### Honest power statement

The available synthetic weekly SIC2-cluster planning run in `r6_ideas/J.md` used `r4_harness/power_sim.py` with 297 weekly dates, 54 effective SIC2 groups, NW lag 4, assumed signal autocorrelation 0.20 and assumed within-SIC2 ICC 0.15. It gave an 80% minimum detectable mean IC magnitude of about **0.0392**, effective date count about **216**, and only **26.7%** simulated probability of NW t≥2 at hypothetical |IC|=0.0183. These are design assumptions, not measured ICs or outcomes, and the simulator inflates date-level IC noise to approximate SIC2 clustering rather than performing a two-way date-and-industry bootstrap. The historical industry-reversal paper reports monthly portfolio evidence, not a weekly Spearman IC; it cannot be used as a numeric IC prior. This mechanism is materially underpowered for modest effects. Keep the registered thresholds unchanged and report a null honestly; do not reinterpret low power as a pass.

## SIC2-preserving placebo

Run the fixed placebo and save all 500 draw statistics whether or not the primary mechanism passes. Use the input-only eligible mask, preserve each formation date's SIC2 group membership and eligible-name set, and permute the **signal labels within each SIC2 group** among that date's eligible names. Groups with at least two eligible names are shuffled; singleton groups remain unchanged. For every draw, recompute the mean date-wise Spearman IC using the unchanged forward outcome panel. Use exactly **500 draws**, seed **20261111**, and save the complete IC distribution and seed/configuration.

The placebo passes only if the observed negative-direction statistic is more extreme than the 95th percentile in its registered direction and the one-sided randomization p-value is below 0.05:

```text
p = (1 + count(shuffled_direction_adjusted_mean_IC >= observed_direction_adjusted_mean_IC)) / 501
```

Here `direction_adjusted_mean_IC = -mean(IC)`, so larger values indicate stronger evidence in the registered negative direction. The placebo tests whether the actual assignment of relative-return scores to names within SIC2 adds explanatory value beyond SIC2 group structure. Do not vary the seed, mask, grouping, statistic, or number of draws after seeing outcomes.

- At each fifth-session formation close, rank Dow members with complete signal and at least one dated SIC2 peer by ascending `signal`; set an equal-weight long-only target in the **five lowest** names. No short book or alternate K is permitted. Execute at the next session's open.
- Begin from point-in-time EW30 and move pro rata toward the top-five target by the largest step that keeps **total traded notional (buys plus sells divided by NAV) at or below 50% per rebalance**, including the initial transition. Hold between scheduled rebalances; drift weights with marked prices. Report when the turnover cap binds, actual turnover, and average active share. If a date has insufficient eligible names, report the date and apply the fixed availability rule; do not substitute an unavailable security.
- Charge **0.05% per dollar of actual traded notional**, with buys and sells counted once in total turnover. Compare daily net portfolio returns with point-in-time EW30 on the same available-name basis. Report gross and net excess return, annualized net Sharpe versus EW30, average active share, maximum/mean turnover, costs, cost/gross-edge ratio, and drawdown.

The portfolio must satisfy the goal's long-only active share requirement (average active share ≥50%), have positive net excess return and higher annualized net Sharpe than EW30, and have one-sided block-bootstrap p<0.05 after Holm across the full R6 family. Use a circular block bootstrap of net excess returns with **10-session blocks, 5,000 draws, seed 20261111**. The R6 turnover/cost gate is met if turnover is no greater than 50% at every rebalance **or** costs are less than 25% of gross edge; J1 specifies the 50% turnover-cap route. Report the cost/gross-edge ratio; it is diagnostic and not an additional pass condition when the turnover route passes. Apply the required DSR using the cumulative trial count described above.

## Evidence basis and limitations

- Hameed and Mian (2015), [“Industries and Stock Return Reversals,” *JFQA* 50, 89–117](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109014000404), document monthly intra-industry reversals, including among large and liquid stocks, and link them to order imbalances/noninformational shocks and liquidity provision. That paper supports the economic motivation, but does not validate this five-day signal or post-2020 weekly effect.
- Lehmann (1990), [“Fads, Martingales, and Market Efficiency,” *QJE* 105, 1–28](https://doi.org/10.2307/2937816), documents one-week reversal. Da, Liu, and Schaumburg (2014), [“A Closer Look at the Short-Term Return Reversal,” *Management Science* 60, 658–674](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1766), and Nagel (2012), [“Evaporating Liquidity,” *RFS* 25, 2005–2039](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs066), motivate liquidity provision as one potential reversal channel.
- These results are historical. None verifies an unconditional weekly SIC2-relative anomaly in post-2020 mega-caps. Rapid liquidity provision, bid-ask bounce, and information shocks could erase or mimic the proposed signal.
