## Hypothesis and primary design

**Mechanism.** Persistent short-sale flow is an inverse FINRA short-sale-flow proxy. A higher five-session FINRA facility short-volume ratio for a firm predicts lower returns over the following five sessions. The primary mechanism is estimated cross-sectionally on the point-in-time S&P 500 development panel from `r6_data`; if it passes, the frozen strategy is traded on the point-in-time Dow 30. This ratio is not a direct measure of net short positions, investor identity, or buying pressure.

**Primary sign and horizon.** The raw short-volume ratio has a **negative** rank relationship with the next five-session return. Equivalently, the negative-pressure score defined below has a **positive** rank relationship with the next five-session return. The portfolio buys names with the **lowest** short-volume pressure. There is one primary sign and one horizon; sign reversals, additional horizons, or new flow sources cannot rescue a failed test.

### Exact Reg SHO signal

Use FINRA's CNMS daily Reg SHO files, one file per U.S. trading date, with fields `Date`, `Symbol`, `ShortVolume`, `ShortExemptVolume`, `TotalVolume`, and `Market`. Throughout this preregistration, “FINRA facility short-volume ratio” means FINRA-reported public-dissemination volume only; it is not a consolidated national-market ratio.

1. Normalize symbols using the project's point-in-time security/ticker lineage; never apply a current ticker backward across a merger or symbol change.
2. For each point-in-time security `i` and session `d`, define the raw ratio from the **same symbol-date-market row(s)**:

   ```text
   SR[i,d] = sum(ShortVolume[i,d,m]) / sum(TotalVolume[i,d,m])
   ```

   Include only rows where both `ShortVolume` and `TotalVolume` are finite, `TotalVolume > 0`, and both fields cover the same `Market` labels. If one symbol-date has multiple market rows, use the intersection of market labels with valid numerator **and** denominator, and sum both fields over that same intersection. If the file has one combined symbol-date row, use that row as published. Do not combine FINRA short volume with an independently sourced consolidated-volume denominator. FINRA `ShortVolume` already includes short-exempt sales; `ShortExemptVolume` is its subset and is reported separately for diagnostics, never added or subtracted. `TotalVolume` is volume in the same FINRA-reported facility rows, not consolidated national volume.
3. A date is valid only if the symbol has a valid paired ratio in the daily file. No interpolation, forward fill, denominator-only day, or ratio on mismatched venue coverage is allowed. A five-session score requires five valid paired daily observations for that security; otherwise it is missing for that signal date.
4. The trailing five-session ratio and its signed pressure representation at close `t` are:

   ```text
   R[i,t] = arithmetic_mean(SR[i,d]), d = the five trading sessions ending at t
   P[i,t] = -R[i,t]       # negative pressure; higher P means less short volume
   ```

The confirmatory date-wise rank IC is Spearman correlation of `P[i,t]` with the next five-session total return. Thus the predicted mean IC is positive. Report the equivalent Spearman IC of `R[i,t]` with that return as a sign check; it must be negative. Do not test either as a separate horizon or signal variant.

### Timing and return definition

Use each fifth trading session as a formation date, beginning 2020-11-27 and ending no later than 2026-10-30; only dates with the complete trailing signal and full forward outcome window qualify. The signal is calculated after the formation close. FINRA says the daily file is posted within a reasonable amount of time after regular trading hours, but historical files have no publication timestamp proving next-open availability. Accordingly, use a fixed one-session execution delay: first trade at the open of session `t+2`. The five-session forward total return is from that open through the close of session `t+6`:

```text
F[i,t] = adjusted_close[i,t+6] / adjusted_open[i,t+2] - 1
adjusted_open[i,d] = raw_open[i,d] * adjusted_close[i,d] / raw_close[i,d]
```

Use point-in-time membership and the same valid-name mask for each date's rank IC. The mask includes a member only when the point-in-time roster, valid paired FINRA ratio, and required price bars are available under the fixed price-gap rule below. Publish the scheduled-date and paired FINRA/price coverage manifest before opening outcomes. Returns are not part of the input-only feasibility gate. The one-session delay is a fixed conservative convention; do not claim archival publication timestamps establish next-open tradability.

## Mechanism gate and IC hurdle

The confirmatory mechanism statistic is the mean date-wise S&P500 Spearman IC of negative pressure `P[i,t]` against the next-five-session return. Compute Newey–West inference with Bartlett weights and **lag 4** (one less than the five-session horizon). The mechanism passes only if:

- mean IC is **positive and at least 0.0043301**;
- one-sided Newey–West **t ≥ 2.00**; and
- the fixed SIC2-stratified shuffle placebo below passes.

The cost-only IC hurdle is calculated with `r4_harness.phase3.break_even_rank_ic` for the Dow implementation: 50% total traded-notional turnover per weekly rebalance (buys plus sells divided by NAV), an assumption-based 4% weekly cross-sectional return dispersion, 5 bp per side, and top fraction `5/30`:

```text
break_even_rank_ic(turnover=0.50, return_dispersion=0.04,
                   cost_per_side=0.0005, top_fraction=5/30)
    = 0.0043301270
```

At the cap, assumed cost is `0.50 × 0.0005 = 0.00025` NAV, or 2.5 bp per rebalance. The conversion implies gross active return of approximately `0.04 × 1.587 × 0.0043301 = 0.000275` NAV, or 2.75 bp: only about 0.25 bp above assumed costs. This is a thin, cost-only planning floor, not an estimated D1 effect, measured turnover, or guarantee that the S&P mechanism IC maps into Dow portfolio edge. The 4% dispersion is an assumption carried from the D dossier. The S&P mechanism must meet this floor as well as t≥2; a statistically significant but smaller IC does not pass. The economic portfolio gate remains positive net excess return after costs, with realized cost/gross-edge ratio reported. Under the alternative rule `costs <25% of gross edge`, the same 2.5 bp cost implies at least 10 bp gross edge, or approximate planning IC `0.010/(0.04×1.587)=0.01575`; this is a sensitivity only, not a replacement threshold.

## Placebos

Use seed `20261111`; run and report the fixed placebo whether or not the primary passes.

1. **Within-date, within-SIC2 signal shuffle.** For each eligible date, permute the finite negative-pressure scores within each two-digit SIC group among eligible S&P 500 names, keeping each group's score distribution and return panel fixed. Groups with at least two eligible names are shuffled; singleton scores remain fixed. Before outcomes, report the date-wise eligible-name, eligible-SIC2-group, and singleton counts from the input-only mask. Run 500 independent shuffles with seed `20261111`, recompute the mean IC for each, and report the complete null distribution. The observed primary mean IC must exceed the 95th percentile of the null distribution and the one-sided randomization p-value `(1 + count(null_mean_IC >= observed_mean_IC))/501` must be below 0.05. Do not change group rules or seed after seeing outcomes.

Failure of this placebo kills D1; it cannot become an alternate strategy. The former five-session future-score equivalence check is removed: because the signal is persistent, it is not a defensible near-zero falsification gate. It is not required as a diagnostic and is not a Holm endpoint.

## Dow 30 portfolio, trading costs and inference

Run the portfolio only if the coverage, S&P500 mechanism, and placebo gates pass:

- Rebalance every fifth trading session. At each formation close, rank eligible point-in-time Dow names by negative pressure `P` and set an equal-weight target in the **K=5** lowest-`SR` names. No shorting. If fewer than five names have valid scores, that date is ineligible and the fixed portfolio gate fails if coverage thresholds are breached.
- Start from point-in-time EW30 and make the first trade at session `t+2` open under the fixed one-session execution delay. Move from current weights toward the top-five target by the largest pro-rata step that keeps **total traded notional (buys plus sells divided by NAV) ≤50% per rebalance**. Apply the cap from the initial transition and include all transition costs. The unconstrained EW30-to-top-five transition is 166.7% total turnover, so the cap binds at initialization. Hold between scheduled dates; no signal-triggered trades. Report actual maximum and mean turnover and dates on which the cap binds.
- Calculate daily marked net returns against the point-in-time EW30 benchmark over the same available names. Charge **0.05% per dollar of total traded notional**. Require (a) positive net cumulative excess return, (b) higher annualized net Sharpe than EW30, (c) average active share at least **50%** versus EW30, and (d) turnover no greater than 50% at every rebalance. Report gross active return and the realized transaction-cost/gross-edge ratio; the alternative R6 rule permits turnover up to 50% or costs below 25% of gross edge, but this registration chooses the stricter explicit 50% cap route and does not waive active share or net-return requirements.
- For net excess return, use one-sided circular block bootstrap with **5,000 draws, 10-session blocks, seed `20261111`**. Require raw and full-family Holm-adjusted p < 0.05. Report gross/net excess, net Sharpe, drawdown, turnover, active share, costs, cost/gross-edge ratio, bootstrap p-values, and DSR.

## Evidence basis and limitations

- Diether, Lee and Werner (2009), [“Short-Sale Strategies and Return Predictability,” *Review of Financial Studies*](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn047), document predictive information in short-sale activity but warn that trading costs can erase profitability.
- Wang, Yan and Zheng (2020), [“Shorting Flows, Public Disclosure, and Market Efficiency,” *Journal of Financial Economics*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2019.05.018), find persistent long-term shorting predicts negative returns in 2010–2015, including value-weighted evidence, while abnormal short-term flow increases do not predict returns. This motivates a fixed multi-session pressure measure while cautioning against daily spikes.

These findings motivate the sign but do not validate FINRA Reg SHO's facility short-volume ratio as net bearish pressure or establish post-2015 persistence in the Dow. FINRA's denominator is its reported `TotalVolume`, not full consolidated market volume, and reported short volume is distinct from short interest, net short positions, or investor identity. Wang et al. do not validate this exact facility ratio, five-day mean, or Dow implementation.

The sign is unchanged because it is internally consistent with the cited broad evidence: a positive-return relationship for `P=-R` is equivalent to a negative-return relationship for higher short-sale flow. The registered five-session signal and five-session return horizons and the single D1-V1 variant are unchanged; the execution delay moves the entry/exit dates but keeps the outcome window five sessions long.
