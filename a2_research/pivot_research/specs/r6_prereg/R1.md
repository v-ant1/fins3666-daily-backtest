# R1 — Own-stock predicted dividend-paying month

**Status: FROZEN 2026-11-11 (see footer). Metadata-only status-line fix before any outcome; decision 102.**

## Hypothesis and fixed design

**Mechanism.** Investors who seek dividend income or reinvest cash distributions create predictable demand for stocks approaching their recurring distribution month. If that buying pressure has a short-lived price impact, a stock whose own history predicts a dividend event in the next calendar month should outperform non-payers during that month. This is a timing signal, not a high-dividend-yield, quality, or permanent payout tilt.

**Primary direction and horizon.** Positive: predicted payers outperform non-payers over the next calendar month. The confirmatory S&P 500 statistic is a SIC2-stratified payer-minus-non-payer return spread, averaged monthly. Use a one-sided Newey–West test with Bartlett lag **3 monthly observations**; pass only if the mean spread is positive and NW t≥2.00. The lag is fixed to allow for quarterly schedule and return dependence while the measured holding window remains one calendar month. There is one sign, horizon, signal, test statistic, portfolio, and variant.

## Signal, outcome, and timing

Formation occurs at the **close of the final U.S. trading session of each calendar month M**. The prediction is for the following calendar month M+1. For every dividend event date `d` strictly before formation and no more than 12 calendar months old, project candidate recurrence dates at `d + 3`, `+6`, `+9`, and `+12` calendar months (clamp to the final valid calendar day when the source date is month-end). Define `payer[i,M+1]=1` if one or more projected dates fall in M+1; otherwise it is 0. The count of qualifying projected dates is recorded only as a deterministic tie-breaker for the Dow portfolio; it is not a second forecast. No dividend amount or price/volume characteristic enters the signal. The same rule is applied unchanged to S&P and Dow.

Use formation dates from **2021-06-30 through 2026-09-30** (the last eligible U.S. session in those months), subject to the actual exchange calendar. This gives a full 12-month allowed development lookback beginning 2020-06 and leaves the next calendar month within the staged development price window, which ends 2026-10-30. A formation is eligible only if the full next-month return window and all required input records exist. Freeze the date mask using calendars, PIT membership, SIC2 availability, dividend-history response status, and price availability before opening any forward-return values.

The forward return for S&P name `i` at formation `t` is its dividend-adjusted total return from the adjusted open of the first trading session in M+1 through the adjusted close of the last trading session in M+1:

```text
fwd_month[i,t] = adjusted_close[i,last_session(M+1)] /
                 adjusted_open[i,first_session(M+1)] - 1
```

For each formation and each SIC2 group with at least one payer and one non-payer, calculate `spread[sic2,t] = mean(fwd_month of payers) - mean(fwd_month of non-payers)`. The monthly mechanism observation is the equally weighted mean of `spread[sic2,t]` across eligible SIC2 groups. Report the number of contributing groups and names each month. This fixed sector-stratified spread is the sole primary mechanism outcome; no alternative rank-IC test may replace a failure.

## Mechanism and SIC2-preserving placebo

The S&P mechanism gate is the positive mean of the monthly SIC2-stratified payer-minus-non-payer spread with one-sided NW lag 3 and t≥2.00. Require at least 250 PIT S&P names with valid dividend flag, SIC2, and full next-month return in a formation cross-section; if not, stop before portfolio analysis and report input/power infeasibility. The 250-name threshold is a coverage gate, not an outcome-dependent date filter.

Run a within-date, within-SIC2 placebo. For each formation month, randomly permute payer flags among that month's eligible PIT S&P names **within each SIC2**, keeping that SIC2's payer count fixed. Recompute the same equal-weighted SIC2 spread and mean monthly statistic for each draw. Use exactly **500 draws** and seed **20261111**; preserve and report the entire null distribution and the eligible-name mask. The placebo passes only if the observed mean spread is above the 95th percentile of its one-sided shuffled null and the randomization p-value is below 0.05:

```text
p = (1 + count(shuffled_mean_spread >= observed_mean_spread)) / 501
```

Do not shuffle across SIC2, change group membership, or alter the number of shuffles after outcomes are visible. Report both primary and placebo raw p-values and Holm-adjusted values. R1-H12 is blocked unless H10 and H11 pass.

## Honest power statement

The 2013 study reports about **41 bp monthly abnormal return** for predicted dividend payers and **53 bp monthly four-factor alpha** for a between-company payer-minus-other-stocks portfolio, but these full-sample statistics are not a post-2011 Dow effect and do not directly estimate the SIC2-stratified R1 spread. For planning only, use the 53 bp documented spread as the main plausible effect, a monthly spread SD of 8% (assumption), about 58 effective SIC2 groups per S&P monthly cross-section, and 64 monthly dates (2021-06 to 2026-09). `r4_harness/power_sim.py` in event mode, 500 assets, 64 dates, NW lag 3 (`--horizon 4` only to set the simulator's lag), assumed temporal rho 0.20, `ic_sd=0.08`, event rate 58, cluster shape 10, event effect 0.0053, 5,000 simulations, seed 20261111 gives simulated power **0.9004** (MC SE 0.0042) and 80% MDE **0.00433**. This approximates SIC2 clusters as an overdispersed count of independent group observations; it is not a true block bootstrap and is a planning model only.

Sensitivity at the study's 41 bp long-only abnormal-return scale, using the same assumptions, gives power **0.7232** (MC SE 0.0063), below 80%. The 53 bp case is therefore powered, while the more conservative 41 bp case is borderline/underpowered under the stated dependence model. The source paper's Sharpe/alpha may not transfer to this shorter recent sample, to the within-SIC2 spread, or to a five-name Dow portfolio. Keep gates unchanged and report all failures; do not inflate the expected effect to call the design powered.

## Dow long-only portfolio, costs, and inference

Only after the input, H10 mechanism, and H11 placebo gates pass, run one portfolio on point-in-time Dow membership:

- At each eligible month-end, rank predicted payers by the number of prior event dates whose 3/6/9/12-month projections land in the coming month, descending. Let `n` be the number of eligible Dow constituents in the same-date available-name EW benchmark. Hold the top **min(15, floor(n/2))** payers at equal weight; if fewer qualify, hold all qualifying payers. Break ties by ticker ascending. If no Dow member qualifies, hold 100% cash at zero return for that month. The target holds at most floor(n/2) names, so its target active share versus the available-name EW benchmark is at least 50%; if the realized portfolio does not maintain average active share ≥50%, criterion 4 fails.
- Execute at the open of the first trading session in the coming month. Rebalance monthly, holding between rebalances. Trade toward the target with total traded notional (buys plus sells divided by NAV) capped at **50% per rebalance**, including initial transition; report when the cap binds and actual/average/maximum turnover. Measure active share as one-half the sum of absolute weight differences versus same-date EW30.
- Deduct **0.05% per side** on actual traded notional, with buys and sells counted once in turnover. Compare daily net returns against point-in-time EW30 on the same available-name basis. Report gross and net excess return, annualized net Sharpe versus EW30, average active share, turnover, costs, cost/gross-edge ratio, and drawdown.
- Criterion 4 requires positive net excess return, higher net Sharpe than EW30, average active share ≥50%, turnover no more than 50% at every rebalance (the selected cost-control route), and one-sided circular block-bootstrap p<0.05 after full-family Holm correction. Use **10-session circular blocks, 5,000 draws, seed 20261111**. Report raw and adjusted p-values and DSR at the cumulative trial count. A lower-than-50% average active share is a fail even if the portfolio return is positive.

## Evidence basis and limitations

- Hartzmark and Solomon (2013), [“The dividend month premium,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 109, 640–660](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.02.015), report abnormal returns in predicted dividend months, including a 41 bp monthly abnormal-return estimate, a 53 bp between-company four-factor alpha, and a long-only annual Sharpe of 0.421; results are not limited to small or illiquid stocks. Their monthly CRSP sample ends December 2011, so modern post-2011 own-stock evidence remains thin.
- Hartzmark and Solomon (2022), [“Predictable Price Pressure,” NBER Working Paper 30688](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30688), show that predictable aggregate dividend payments forecast broad-market returns in CRSP data through 2018, including effects in large, liquid market portfolios. This supports the cash-reinvestment pressure mechanism, not a post-2018 Dow payer-minus-EW30 return premium.
- Source definition: [yfinance `Ticker.history` API](https://ranaroussi.github.io/yfinance/reference/api/yfinance.Ticker.history.html) and [yfinance issue on dividend dates](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance/issues/568). It returns the Yahoo historical dividend event series; verify and disclose its ex-date semantics and limitations before the preregistration is frozen.
