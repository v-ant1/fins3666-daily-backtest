## 1. SEC Form 4 opportunistic purchase disclosures

**Mechanism and proposed signal.** An insider's discretionary purchase is a real buy; once disclosed, other investors may infer favorable private information and buy behind it. Candidate is a long-only issuer signal from non-derivative transaction-code `P` purchases filed on EDGAR, with trade value and filer role used only under a predeclared filter. Measure the public filing timestamp and enter no earlier than the next tradable session. Exclude grants, conversions, gifts, and sales. SEC code `P` includes open-market *or private* purchases, so the raw code alone does not identify open-market trading; the original filing exhibit/footnotes and transaction fields must support the final classification. Do not use trade date as the information date.

**Post-2005 paper evidence (effect sizes).**

- Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski (2012), *Decoding Inside Information*, report **82 bp per month value-weighted abnormal return** for a portfolio following opportunistic insiders; routine traders' abnormal returns are essentially zero. The result is conditional on separating routine from opportunistic traders, and is not an estimate for an unfiltered S&P 500 Form 4 strategy. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01740.x), [NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16454).
- Brochet (2010), *Information Content of Insider Trades Before and After the Sarbanes-Oxley Act*, reports **1.89% mean three-day cumulative abnormal return** around post-SOX insider-purchase filing dates, versus 0.59% pre-SOX. The event window begins at filing, which is closer to an executable disclosure signal, but it is an event-study average rather than a net large-cap portfolio result. [Accounting Review article record](https://openurl.ebsco.com/contentitem/gcd%3A48750131?crl=f&id=ebsco%3Agcd%3A48750131&jrnl=00014826&sid=ebsco%3Aplink%3Acrawler-gcd), [study summary with estimates](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2009/10/30/sox-and-insider-trades/).

**Data path.**

- **Development (2020-11-27..2026-10-30):** SEC quarterly Insider Transactions datasets / EDGAR Forms 3, 4 and 5; the workspace already has raw SEC quarterly archives and a filtered development table under `r6_data/insider/` covering 2020Q2 onward. Use filing/acceptance timestamp, CIK, transaction code, owner role and the PIT S&P roster. Join to the staged `r9/data/J2_DEV_FROM_20201127.npz` only after the candidate is frozen. The existing insider QA notes that its extract is as-filed, retains amendments, and that code `P` does not itself separate open-market from private purchases.
- **Clean holdout (2010-01..2015-12):** SEC's free quarterly Form 3/4/5 archive/EDGAR filing documents, parsed as filed with acceptance time, provide the transaction source. The existing archive-availability manifest reports successful HEAD checks for 2009Q1–2020Q1; those checks verify existence/size only, not correct extraction, complete Form 4 classification, or signal coverage. For prices, the intended path is a free daily history source such as Stooq/Yahoo Finance plus dated historical S&P membership and issuer/security mapping. These last three components are **not yet verified for delisted securities, corporate actions, and point-in-time membership**; L1 must establish them before the idea can pass its data gate. No licensed source is assumed.

**Overlap with prior work.** R6 screened Form 4 event/cluster ideas (`AA_event_concentrated`, `F`, `AN_insider_industry`) and rejected them on overlap, event sparsity, filing lag, and source classification. This proposal does not reuse AN's SIC2 peer-cluster signal: it targets the issuer whose insider disclosed a discretionary purchase and enters after public filing. That is a distinct portfolio mapping, but not a new source family; count it as a reuse of the Form 4 discovery family. No R6 or R9 frozen portfolio result is being repaired, and the R9 DTC/J2 outcomes are not inputs to this signal.

**Independent red-team critique.** The main threat is that published returns accrue to selected informed trades or include an event-day repricing that the public cannot capture. A free all-filer screen can dilute the effect with routine, private, planned, or low-conviction purchases; filing lag leaves the entry price after some information leakage; and the 2010–2015 S&P event count may be too small for the fixed portfolio and multiple-testing gates. Form 4 amendments and CIK/security mapping can also create look-ahead or duplicate events. Even if the event signal predicts returns, a sparse top-name portfolio may fail net Sharpe and drawdown requirements. Treat the papers as a plausible sign and mechanism, not evidence this S&P strategy will pass.

## 2. Schedule 13D activist accumulation disclosure

**Mechanism and proposed signal.** A Schedule 13D announces that an activist has accumulated a material stake and intends to influence the issuer. Other shareholders can buy alongside the activist, while managers and counterparties respond to the campaign. Candidate is a long-only event strategy for first-time activist 13D filings in PIT S&P names, using the EDGAR acceptance timestamp and an entry no earlier than the next session. Read Item 4 and amendments to separate activist intent from passive 13G filings and duplicate amendments; freeze an event definition before outcomes.

**Post-2005 paper evidence (effect sizes).**

- Brav, Jiang, Partnoy & Thomas (2008), *Hedge Fund Activism, Corporate Governance, and Firm Performance*, report **7–8% mean abnormal return over [−20,+20] trading days** around activist Schedule 13D filing/announcement. Their sample targets are disproportionately smaller firms, and the pre-filing run-up makes the full-window return unavailable to a strategy triggered by the public filing. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01373.x).
- Klein & Zur (2009), *Entrepreneurial Shareholder Activism*, independently find significantly positive announcement-period returns and positive subsequent-year returns for activist and other private-investor targets. Their initial 13D target sample reports **7.3% mean size-adjusted abnormal return over a window from 30 trading days before through 5 days after the filing**. That window is even more dominated by pre-disclosure accumulation/information leakage. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01432.x).
- Becht et al. (2017), *Returns to Hedge Fund Activism: An International Study*, find **7.0% U.S. abnormal announcement return over [−20,+20]** in a much broader international comparison. This remains an event-window benchmark, not post-filing alpha, and overlaps the same broad U.S. activism phenomenon. [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/HHX048).

**Data path.**

- **Development (2020-11-27..2026-10-30):** free EDGAR Schedule 13D/13D-A documents and filing acceptance metadata; issuer CIK joined to the staged PIT S&P panel and daily development prices after freezing the event code. EDGAR company submissions APIs and filing archives are public; use the original document, not a modern third-party activist database that could introduce backfilled classifications.
- **Clean holdout (2010-01..2015-12):** the same free EDGAR filing archive and historical acceptance metadata cover the period. A free historical-price + PIT S&P membership/security mapping path is still unverified, especially for delistings and issuer/ticker changes; use L1's verified holdout manifest before moving forward. EDGAR availability is not itself proof of a complete, tradable event panel.

**Overlap with prior work.** R6's `AM-activist` and `N` screens already covered Schedule 13D campaign pressure and rejected it for sparse events/coverage and weak pressure-to-peer mapping. This proposal is deliberately issuer-level and therefore does not reuse the prior cross-industry or J1/J2/D1/index-flow signals, but it substantially overlaps the screened AM source and campaign family. It is a bounded re-evaluation only if the new 2010–2015 PIT universe materially improves event coverage and an independently justified, post-filing outcome is registered; it is not a relabeling of the prior screen.

**Independent red-team critique.** The best-known abnormal return starts well before the filing: the activist is accumulating in private, and the 13D's filing-day jump is public repricing, not evidence that followers can capture the full 7–8%. The cited samples are not a large-cap-only S&P strategy, historical filing deadlines changed, and amendments/13D-to-13G switches complicate event dating. Event clustering and low count can make an otherwise large mean unusable under portfolio inference. A strategy restricted to post-filing returns may have little left; a strategy using the pre-filing run-up would be look-ahead. Reject unless L1 confirms enough independent holdout events and a prespecified post-filing effect is economically plausible.

## DTC evidence: material lead, not a shortlist recommendation

R9's exploratory monthly `−DTC` signal had **mean IC +0.0233, NW t=2.89 over 70 development months**, but it was unstable by year; the reported 2010–2020-style holdout design had an estimated MDE of **0.031**, above the observed mean before any winner's-curse discount. R9's own source review found no verified free listed-stock FINRA short-interest history for 2010–2016; staged consolidated records begin in late 2017, and FINRA's exposed OTC archive is not a substitute for listed securities. FINRA daily short-sale volume is a trade flow, not outstanding short interest/DTC; substituting it would recycle D1's separate, failed short-volume pathway. [R9 development evidence](../../r9/S2_DEV_EVIDENCE.md), [FINRA history audit](../../r9/BREADTH_COVERAGE.md), [FINRA short-interest archive](https://otce.finra.org/otce/EquityShortInterest/archives), [FINRA short-interest vs short-volume explanation](https://syndication.finra.org/content/short-interest-what-it-what-it-not).

The DTC thesis also needs careful naming: the empirical R9 direction is **low DTC long**, and the cited literature finds high-DTC/high-short-activity stocks underperform. That is more consistent with avoiding informed short selling / short-position exit friction than harvesting forced buy-to-cover demand. Hong et al. (2015) find a **1.19% monthly equal-weighted low-minus-high DTC spread** in 1988–2012; Boehmer, Jones & Zhang (2008) find heavily shorted stocks underperform lightly shorted stocks by **1.16% risk-adjusted over 20 trading days** using daily NYSE short-sale data. Both support a return-predictive shorting signal, but neither validates a crowded-short squeeze buy signal or solves the free historical-input gap. [Hong et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21166), [Boehmer et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01324.x).

## Decision / next gate

Rank Form 4 first because the public filing itself identifies an actual purchase and post-disclosure effects have been measured. Rank activist 13D second because the buying/coordination channel is distinct, but its literature window is mostly pre-disclosure. In both cases, treat the R6 overlap as prior search history and include it in the trial ledger. Do not compute an outcome or spend the holdout until L1 verifies free adjusted prices, delisting coverage, dated S&P membership, and filing/event availability for 2010–2015, and L2 demonstrates power under the unchanged qualification thresholds. If those gates fail, this shortlist yields no eligible candidate.
