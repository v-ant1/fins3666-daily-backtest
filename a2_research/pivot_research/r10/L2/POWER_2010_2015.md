# R10 L2 — 2010–2015 power gate and candidate ranking

**Tag:** L2  
**Period:** 2010-01 through 2015-12, approximately 1,510 U.S. sessions. This is a planning analysis only; no pre-2020-11-27 prices, returns, volumes, sealed files, or holdout result values were read. The old S&P 2016–2020 holdout is spent after J2; J2 itself failed all four confirmation gates.

## Decision summary

No candidate has a credible, well-supported probability of passing every qualification gate. The best numeric primary-gate estimates are DTC monthly IC (~34% after a 50% effect haircut), J1 signal mechanism (~28%), D1 signal mechanism (~25%), and index additions (~14–20% depending on cost tier); these do **not** equal full strategy pass probabilities. J1’s existing dev portfolio was marginal and cost-sensitive, D1 never reached its Dow portfolio test and missed its development signal threshold, DTC’s development IC is unstable and its FINRA timing is approximate, and index additions are a one-event CAR design. J2’s archived all-gate odds at a half-effect assumption were 9.6% for a shorter 1,215-session holdout; the six-year probability is not directly recalibrated here. No “verified edge” is established.

## Method

I followed the archived R7/R9 synthetic power approach: one-sided normal approximation, family size **1**, α=.05, and 80% target power. The 80%-power multiplier is `z(.95)+z(.80)=2.4865` (compared with the stricter multiple-test thresholds in earlier rounds). For portfolio shapes, I scaled the R9 hypothetical 2010–2020 tiered-cost thresholds by `2.4865 / (z(.98333)+z(.80)) × sqrt(2700/1510) = 1.1196`; this preserves its assumptions and approximate cost adjustment. The S&P portfolio threshold therefore remains an approximation, not a fresh return-path simulation.

The DSR planning count remains **15** for the J2 lineage (3 R6 plus 12 R9 variants). For other previously screened equity directions I conservatively use at least 15 cumulative trials, subject to the coordinator’s full ledger reconciliation. A family of one for this new confirmatory test does not erase exploratory trials from DSR.

## 80%-power requirements

### S&P 500 capped rank-weighted top half

Use weekly/monthly formation and the R7/R9 ρ=.8 Cap50 portfolio, the only documented rank-weighted top-half design meeting the 50% active-share floor. R9’s documented 5/10/20 bp one-way liquidity tiers are a **combined tier schedule** (equal activity across terciles), not three separate flat-cost scenarios. The underlying report provides only the combined tier estimate, so the table does not invent flat-tier recalculations.

| Design | Archived 2010–2020 tiered threshold (family 3) | Scaled 80% requirement for 2010–2015, family 1 | Eligibility / caveat |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Weekly rank-weighted top-half Cap50 | ≈.021 mean rank IC | **≈.0235** | Only ρ=.8 clears active share in the prior simulation; 50% total-notional turnover cap. |
| Monthly rank-weighted top-half Cap50 | ≈.034 mean rank IC | **≈.0381** | Same ρ=.8-only active-share caveat; fewer scheduled dates. |

These estimates inherit R7/R9’s synthetic Gaussian rank-to-return mapping, portfolio-risk assumptions, and tiered-cost treatment. The dev-measured DTC IC of .0233 is near the weekly threshold but materially below the monthly threshold after haircut; its rank IC is not a return estimate for this capped portfolio.

### Event design: S&P 500 additions

For N≈125 events and the development-measured event SD of 7.7446%, the 80%-power MDE is **1.722% net CAR per event** at one-sided α=.05. Net mean starts from the dev gross mean 1.1904%; deduct two sides at each one-way tier, then apply the mandated 50% winner’s-curse haircut before estimating power.

| One-way cost tier | Net dev mean | Haircut effect | 80%-power MDE | Primary-gate pass probability after haircut |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 bp | +1.0904% | +0.5452% | 1.722% | **19.6%** |
| 10 bp | +0.9904% | +0.4952% | 1.722% | **17.6%** |
| 20 bp | +0.7904% | +0.3952% | 1.722% | **14.1%** |

This is the probability of a positive one-sided event mean passing at 5%, not the probability of a complete tradable strategy qualifying. Event clustering, missing event dates, or a lower effective N reduce it.

## 50%-haircut primary-gate pass probabilities

The normal-approximation power formula is `Φ(effect_haircut / (SD/√N) − 1.6449)`. For D1/J1, I derived the date-level noise scale from their reported development mean IC and Newey–West t, then scaled from 297 to about 302 weekly formations. For DTC, N=72 months and SD=.0795. DTC’s measured .0233 mean falls to .01165 after the haircut, giving **34.4%** chance of a positive monthly IC significance result. D1’s .009374 becomes .004687 and gives **24.7%** chance of its mechanism t gate; J1’s .014786 becomes .007393 and gives **27.5%** chance of its direction-adjusted mechanism significance gate. Approximate power calculations are not calibrated forecasts.

The first four estimates test different primary endpoints, so their order is a screening comparison, not a common calibrated all-gates race. Only J2 has an archived all-gate probability under the existing gate set; even that is a simulation reference, not evidence of success. The data-source, placebo, turnover, active-share, net-return, Sharpe, and DSR gates all reduce full-qualification probability from the primary-gate values.

## Limitations and recommendation

The 2010–2015 interval is a clean **price/return** candidate period only under the fixed facts; this analysis did not verify all source-specific coverage. D1’s FINRA records may have gaps/partial-view history, and the full D1 test cannot be represented as clean without source-provenance review. Family=1 is used exactly as instructed for the power gate; DSR remains cumulative (15 for J2). Costs and power use prior synthetic assumptions, not measured holdout returns. No computation in this report estimates a new strategy’s returns or opens the proposed holdout.

**Ranking decision:** if continuing with existing equity evidence, the DTC monthly signal has the highest estimated primary-gate chance after haircut, but the observed effect is not a matched executable strategy and does not support a claim that it will qualify. The strongest documented portfolio path remains J2, whose actual clean S&P holdout failed. Therefore this power gate does not identify a candidate with a credible probability of producing one verified edge; any continuation needs a fresh, preregistered candidate and independent data/provenance audit before outcomes.
